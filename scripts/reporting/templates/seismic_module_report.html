2. Introduction

A single-component acceleration history is processed to obtain a motion history for engineering inspection. The calculation is described in terms of its physical measurement, numerical operations and evidence boundary.

The main workflow comprises a text-file reader, baseline correction, optional frequency filtering, and successive numerical integration to velocity and displacement. A configured route also produces a comma-separated time history and a source-declared interactive report. Public entry points and reproducible calls are given in Section 9.2.

3. Design Data and Criteria

The data basis is taken from the documented public digitization and its committed example configuration. Record processing is assessed against numerical consistency and the sampling boundary; a structural design criterion is not supplied.

3.1 Record and units

The El Centro north–south public digitization is used without changing its source bytes. Time is in seconds, and the supplied acceleration is in multiples of standard gravity. The reader converts these values to metres per second squared using g₀ = {{ g0 }} m/s². The conversion is defined in the national metrology guide, 2008 edition, Appendix B.8, entry “acceleration of free fall, standard”. Unit basis.

The source page describes attachment to a building floor rather than a free-field installation and warns of a possible high-frequency effect from foundation interaction. Different public digitizations and sign conventions are identified there. Numerical equivalence to another version of this earthquake record is not assumed. Measurement source.

3.2 Processing criteria

A uniformly increasing time coordinate and declared units are required. The sampling boundary is the Nyquist frequency fₙ = 1/(2Δt), where Δt is the sample interval. Band-pass critical frequencies are required to be positive, ordered and below fₙ. The present implementation reduces the requested upper edge when necessary; the effective edge is reported separately from the requested one. This numerical boundary is not an anti-aliasing qualification or an engineering demand limit. Filter definition; implementation record.

Zero initial velocity and displacement are the calculation conditions. The first {{ metric('baseline_samples', 0) }} samples have a mean of {{ metric('baseline_offset_m_s2', 4) }} m/s². Integration after this mean is subtracted, with filtering disabled and zero initial velocity, ends at {{ metric('unfiltered_end_velocity_m_s') }} m/s. These computed values provide evidence of baseline sensitivity; qualification of the initial window as a pre-event offset remains ASSUMED - to be confirmed. A project motion limit, acceptance spectrum and component capacity are NOT_EVALUATED because no such inputs are supplied. The data register states the action required for each gap.

3.3 Input and output contract

A time-and-acceleration text file, or an acceleration-only text file with a supplied interval, is accepted by the reader. Supported acceleration units are standard gravity, centimetres per second squared and metres per second squared. An explicit unit argument takes precedence over a comment header; the reader assumes metres per second squared when neither is supplied. Units are therefore required to be declared before engineering use. Reader contract.

The returned record contains the original time coordinate, converted acceleration and source fingerprint. The calculation returns the original, corrected and filtered acceleration, integrated velocity and displacement, the original-record summary, and the applied processing settings. The configured route writes these histories and a source-declared report. Output field names and units are defined in Section 9.2.

4. Methodology

The recorded acceleration is corrected and filtered before numerical integration is performed. Original and processed histories are preserved separately so the effect of each operation can be inspected.

4.1 Baseline and filter

b = (Σ aᵢ)/nᵦ over the selected initial samples; aᶜᵢ = aᵢ − b.

Here aᵢ is the original acceleration sample, nᵦ is the baseline-window sample count, b is the mean offset, and aᶜᵢ is the corrected acceleration. All acceleration terms are in m/s². The arithmetic mean is subtracted in the baseline helper. An alternative polynomial fit subtracts a fitted trend over the full record; this alternative is listed under Not covered. Baseline implementation.

The example uses a Butterworth band-pass design in transfer-function form. Filtering is performed forward and backward; the combined operation has zero phase and its amplitude response is the square of the single-pass magnitude. Edge transients and finite-precision effects remain possible. The order specified in the configuration is the design argument, not the total order of the combined forward-and-backward band-pass operation. Filter design; filter application.

4.2 Kinematic integration

vᵢ = vᵢ₋₁ + (ãᵢ₋₁ + ãᵢ) Δtᵢ / 2
uᵢ = uᵢ₋₁ + (vᵢ₋₁ + vᵢ) Δtᵢ / 2

The filtered acceleration is ãᵢ in m/s²; vᵢ is velocity in m/s; uᵢ is displacement in m; and Δtᵢ = tᵢ − tᵢ₋₁ is the elapsed interval in seconds. Initial velocity and displacement are set to zero. Successive composite trapezoidal integration is implemented by the scientific computing library. A residual acceleration offset accumulates into velocity and displacement, so these histories remain conditional on the selected correction and filter. Integration definition; calculation implementation.

No mass, damping ratio, stiffness or natural period enters this calculation. Relative oscillator displacement, oscillator acceleration, spectral acceleration and modal combination are not returned.

6. Validation Evidence

The available evidence is composed of repository checks and a documented public waveform. The evidence establishes bounded numerical behaviours; agreement with independent earthquake displacement or structural response is not established.

6.1 Existing checks

The baseline checks exercise initial-window mean removal, an invalid window and linear detrending. The public waveform is an example input rather than a benchmark of independently published motion outputs. The new report checks compare each displayed worked value and all embedded time-series samples with fresh public-module results. An additional constant-acceleration check compares the returned motion with the analytical integrals for zero initial motion. Check records; rebuild check.

6.2 Qualification boundary

Execution of the reader, baseline, report rebuild and direct router is recorded in the delivery checklist. General-engine dispatch and licensed-solver interaction are outside that execution set. No retained field-motion comparison, baseline-window sensitivity study, filter-band sensitivity study or published oscillator-spectrum comparison is present in the inspected seismic evidence. The model is therefore not qualified here for site-specific displacement demand or structural acceptance.

7. Key Findings and Limits of Use

Acceleration preprocessing and conditional integrated histories are available through public interfaces. The returned histories and execution status do not establish structural capacity or field accuracy.

7.1 Findings

The original peak ground acceleration is {{ metric('pga_m_s2') }} m/s². The filtered peak is {{ metric('filtered_peak_m_s2') }} m/s² under the example settings, with a sampling boundary of {{ metric('nyquist_hz', 2) }} Hz. These are motion statistics, with no supplied structural acceptance limit. Worked example.

The summary peak is calculated from the original record, whereas the integrated histories are calculated from the filtered acceleration. The reported execution status indicates that the configured operations completed; it is not a design acceptance statement. Implementation record.

7.2 Limits and safeguards

The integrated displacement is conditional on baseline treatment, filtering and zero initial motion. The record was obtained on a building floor; free-field equivalence is not established. Site-specific design demands remain NOT_EVALUATED. Measurement source; data register.

The reader checks sampling regularity but has no explicit finite-acceleration check. The calculation record type is directly constructible, so file-reader checks are bypassed when arrays are supplied directly. Non-finite values, inconsistent arrays, invalid filter edges and records shorter than the filter padding requirements require caller preflight. A bypassed reader or a successful calculation does not establish input eligibility. Reader and calculation record; padding requirements.

The present transfer-function filter is subject to the numerical sensitivity described in its library documentation. A high edge adjusted close to the sampling boundary is not evidence that the acquisition system prevented aliasing. No physical end-motion criterion, long-period reliability bound or default band suitability is established. Numerical limitations.

7.3 Not covered

Polynomial baseline fitting is available but is not exercised in this public example. Other unit and file-layout variants are represented by reader tests rather than a second earthquake case. Oscillator integration, response spectra, intensity measures beyond the original peak acceleration, multidirectional combination, soil response, structural analysis, installation envelopes and live forecasts are not covered. Marine installation controls cannot be inferred from an acceleration-only preprocessing calculation.

8. Recommendations

Further use is conditioned on the measurement basis and intended decision. The following actions are recommended before a processed history is adopted as an engineering demand.

Declare the required motion component, acquisition units, orientation, sensor condition and digitization version. Reject non-finite or inconsistent arrays before calculation, and inspect the sampling interval before selecting filter edges. These checks address the reader and direct-array limitations identified in Section 7.

Confirm that the selected initial interval represents the offset being removed, then compare baseline-window and filter-band alternatives against independent velocity or displacement evidence. Retain the effective filter band and examine residual end motion against a project-defined tolerance. Acceptance remains NOT_EVALUATED until that tolerance and its supporting measurement basis are supplied. The effect of source correction is discussed by the data distributor; filtering edge effects are described by the library documentation. Measurement source; filter application.

Evaluate a second-order-section filter implementation against the current calculation before extending the frequency range or processing longer records. The library recommends this representation to reduce finite-precision difficulties; numerical and output comparisons are required before adopting a revised implementation. Filter numerical basis.

Use a separately qualified oscillator-response calculation for spectral acceleration or structural demand. A first extension is recommended to declare oscillator properties, implement the equation of motion, compare it with analytical responses and published spectra, and define acceptance tolerances before structural decisions are made. The current calculation omits the equation of motion. Capability boundary.
