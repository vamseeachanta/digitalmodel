2. Introduction

The module is assessed through its public static riser workflow. The purpose is to give an engineer a reproducible calculation, its evidence basis and the information required before installation or operational use.

The main workflow starts with pipe dimensions and constituent densities, calculates submerged weight, and solves the suspended shape for water depth and horizontal offset. Tension, top angle, curvature and grounded length are returned. The calculation is useful for early layout and load definition; an installation decision requires dynamic loads and component capacities.

The worked example is the public repository's documented Python example. It is an illustrative input set, not a measured riser or a qualified material specification. A published cable geometry provides a separate mathematical comparison in Validation.

3. Summary

Available length is checked against the suspended length demanded by the static shape. The boundary below is calculated at fixed water depth and fixed total riser length.

The length condition is satisfied for the documented example. The calculation is not an operating envelope: vessel orientation, significant wave height, wave period and forecast triggers are NOT_EVALUATED because environmental response and installation equipment are not defined.

For use, confirm the suspended offset datum, positive net submerged weight and convergence; then assess strength, fatigue, seabed contact and vessel motion with qualified inputs. The returned top angle is measured from vertical. Angles along the riser are measured from horizontal.

5. Methodology

Uniform-weight static equilibrium is solved for a perfectly flexible, inextensible line. The horizontal tangent at touchdown defines the coordinate origin.

5.1 Weight and catenary equations

As = π(D² − d²)/4; Ai = πd²/4; Ac = π(Dc² − D²)/4;
w = g(ρsAs + ρiAi + ρcAc − ρwπDc²/4).

D, d and Dc are the steel outside, bore and coated diameters in metres. A is cross-sectional area in square metres; subscripts s, i and c denote steel, contents and coating. Density ρ is in kg/m³, g is gravity in m/s², and w is net force per unit length in N/m. The expression is the annular mass and displaced-water balance implemented by the weight model.

a = H/w; z = a[cosh(x/a) − 1]; s = √[z(z + 2a)]; T = H + wz;
θ = atan2(H, ws); κmax = 1/a.

H is horizontal tension in N, a is the catenary parameter in m, z is height above touchdown in m, x is horizontal distance from touchdown in m, and s is suspended arc length in m. T is top tension in N, θ is top angle from vertical, and κ is curvature in m⁻¹. The equations are based on Catenary Curve, 2019, equations (1), (3), (4), (9), (10) and (34), with the vertical origin shifted to the vertex. The angle is converted from radians to degrees for reporting.

5.2 Inputs, solution and outputs

The public configuration object carries dimensions, length, material and fluid objects, coating and the depth/offset datum. The weight calculator returns steel, coating, contents, buoyancy and net submerged force per unit length. The analyzer bisects for H when both depth and suspended offset are specified; alternatively, H or top tension is supplied.

The result object returns geometry, top and touchdown tensions, top angle, curvature and convergence. Tension along arc length is obtained from the public distribution method. The length boundary follows s ≤ L, giving a ≤ (L² − z²)/(2z), where L is available length in m. This inequality is derived from length closure; it is not a material allowable.

8. Key Findings, Limitations and Recommendations

The static example satisfies the available-length criterion. Further acceptance is conditional on the following inputs and assessments.

8.1 Findings and limits of use

The solution represents a uniform-weight planar line with a horizontal touchdown tangent, without axial extension, bending stiffness, current, wave loading, vessel motion or soil friction. The result is restricted to positive net submerged weight and a converged feasible geometry.

The model configuration does not fully validate physical geometry or material certificates. The weight properties use fixed gravity; changing the calculator's gravity argument does not change those properties. A caller-supplied force takes precedence over requested offset, so the returned geometry requires examination. A limits check with no supplied limits returns an empty-set aggregate; it does not establish engineering acceptance.

8.2 Recommendations

Use the static result for early geometry and load definition within these assumptions. Before adopting a layout, confirm the as-built dimensions, coating, contents state, water density, available length and touchdown datum. The evidence basis is the public configuration and weight balance described in Methodology.

Before accepting tension or curvature, supply the allowable component tension and bending criteria from the approved design basis and assess the combined loads. Before assigning any sea-state limit, include environmental response, vessel and handling-system limits, and seabed interaction. The mathematical reference contains no installation acceptance criterion.

Exclude the package's lazy-wave analyzer from calculations until a functioning equilibrium implementation and its validation are provided. The existing quarantine tests record its explicit rejection. Missing capacities and physical validation remain required inputs, not assumed passing results.
