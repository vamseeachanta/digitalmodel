Process piping calculations

digitalmodel.process_piping provides straight-pipe pressure thickness, pointwise hydrostatic targets, bolted-joint screening and line sizing. Results contain resolved citation records. The calculations do not establish complete piping-code compliance or authorize a field test or torque procedure.

Run the worked example

PYTHONPATH=src uv run --no-sync python examples/workflows/process-piping/example.py

In PowerShell, set $env:PYTHONPATH='src' first. The example prints JSON without writing a report. All material, gasket, load and friction assumptions are illustrative, with input version 1; no operating asset is represented.

Pressure and hydrostatic limits

PressureDesign requires every material factor: E is the applicable longitudinal quality factor (also called Ej), not an extra multiplier. The 2024 basis uses Table 304.1.1-1 for Y. The equation is t=PD/[2(SEW+PY)]; allowances are added before division by 1 minus the mill-tolerance fraction. P/(SE)>0.385 is rejected, and as a conservative project rule so is an ordered wall at or above D/6 (B31.3 applies D/6 to the pressure design thickness t). Mechanical loads, branches, external pressure, fatigue and high-pressure Chapter IX are outside scope.

hydrotest_pressure uses absolute temperature in kelvin and gauge pressure in pascals. For higher design temperature it applies St/S, with a 1.5P floor; the 6.5 ratio cap of earlier editions (B31.3-2012 345.4.2) is not applied. That the 2024 edition drops it is an assumption still to be confirmed against B31.3-2024 345.4.2; without the cap the target is equal or higher. The supplied minimum wall represents the condition at test, including product undertolerance and existing loss. Both elastic inner-wall hoop stress and longitudinal stress with additional loading are checked against test yield. This screen differs from the code nominal-pressure-stress calculation and is not a full stress analysis. Yield reduction is explicit. A component limit below the resulting target is reported as infeasible; it does not justify further pressure reduction. Local head, system materials, test-fluid weight, supports, brittle fracture, inspection, hold time and test records require separate assessment.

Bolt-up and leak diagnosis

check_bolt_up accepts RF spiral-wound and RTJ joints. Product-specific assembly/operating gasket stresses, maximum bolt stress, flange load limit and retained-load fraction are required. RF gasket area excludes guide rings; RTJ requires a qualified contact area. The separate m/y screen uses Appendix 2 effective width b and reaction diameter G; m/y do not establish a leak-rate limit. Full Appendix O selection/rotation, thermal operating bolt stress and fatigue are outside scope.

tightening_passes(12) gives the symmetric cross order 1,7,4,10,2,8,5,11,3,9,6,12 for snug, 30%, 60% and full target torque, followed by circular full-target passes until stable. Bolts are numbered clockwise. These generated symmetric sequences require qualification for the specific assembly procedure; they are not copied PCC-1 figures.

A universal leading cause of startup or hydrotest joint leaks is not established by population evidence. The diagnostic priority in joint_leak_checks() starts with insufficient or uneven gasket compression, then gasket selection/condition, sealing faces, alignment/external loading, thermal relaxation, pressure/rating errors and weld/thread defects. Each item includes a field check. This is a troubleshooting order, not a measured frequency ranking.

Line sizing

evaluate_line checks one catalog NPS/schedule; size_line selects the smallest NPS meeting the supplied velocity limit, optional pressure-drop budget and optional minimum ordered wall. At one NPS it tries thinner eligible schedules first. Flow is actual m³/s, lengths are metres, viscosity is Pa·s, and density is kg/m³. The existing B36.10M dimensions and shared Darcy friction helper are reused. Transition-band results are marked approximate.

Liquid loss includes fittings K and signed elevation. Gas requires inlet absolute pressure and constant inlet density/temperature/compressibility assumptions; beyond an inlet-density drop of 10% of inlet pressure, the integrated horizontal isothermal momentum equation including acceleration is solved. Outlet velocity governs. Subcritical isothermal feasibility is checked before either pressure-drop reporting method; choked conditions are rejected even below the 10% switch. Fitting K is an equivalent distributed resistance; local fitting choking, nonhorizontal gas and acoustic noise are not modeled. A service velocity limit is a screen, not a noise calculation.

Two-phase sizing is a no-slip mixture-velocity screen only: API RP 14E Ve=C/√ρ uses US-customary units internally before conversion to m/s. C and its selection basis are required; 100 for continuous clean service and 125 for intermittent clean service are common reference choices, not defaults. Solids, erosion-corrosion, slip, slugging and two-phase pressure drop are outside scope. A pressure-drop budget for two-phase flow is rejected.

service_velocity_limit(service, velocity_limit_m_s=...) requires a finite positive caller-selected limit in m/s for general or pump-suction water. No handbook numbers or defaults are embedded. The citation identifies selection guidance, not the source of the supplied number; qualify the limit for the actual service. The example supplies an assumed 2 m/s limit. There is no generic gas velocity limit.
