Pipeline integrity yield convergence analysis

Summary

The pipeline integrity entry point now takes specified minimum yield strength (SMYS) from the maintained material accessor for its existing six grades. Five yield values increase; X100 stays unchanged. The hoop design factor stays 0.72. Acceptance can change for pressures between the old and new yield-based limits. This is a change to one consumer under #1147, rather than full grade-table convergence or material qualification.

Pressure-boundary checks

The thin-wall equation is hoop stress = pressure × outer diameter / (2 × wall thickness); pressure is in MPa and both lengths are in mm. Tests use a 128.0 mm outer diameter and 8.0 mm wall thickness, giving a hoop-to-pressure multiplier of 8.0. For each grade, they check pressures just below, at and just above the unrounded canonical allowable-stress threshold. They independently pin the allowable values and the unchanged design factor. The small offsets are intentionally below the displayed utilization precision: a displayed ratio of 1.0 can correspond to either acceptance or rejection.

For each of the five changed grades, an additional case places pressure midway between the old and new limits. It exceeds the old limit and passes the new one. Increased yield therefore expands acceptance; no claim of automatic conservatism is made. The calculation formula, validation and corrosion routing are unchanged.

Verification

tests/asset_integrity/test_pipeline_skill.py and tests/materials/test_grade_matrix.py: baseline 73 passed; tests before implementation 27 failed and 70 passed; after implementation 97 passed. The original wall-thickness pass/fail, corrosion replacement and psi-adapter equality assertions remain in place. The added tests pin exactly the previous grade set, canonical values, stress limits and acceptance changes.

Known limits and way forward

Maintained engineering-basis review and material qualification remain required. The existing 0.72 hoop factor is an assessment assumption, not a universal design factor. Corrosion length is still not used in the existing calculation, and the existing comparisons do not reject all non-finite inputs. These pre-existing limits are not changed here.

SMTS policy, the other four legacy consumers, retained legacy functions and exports, psi conventions, published examples and explorer reference cases remain for later slices of #1147. The historical migration report is preserved. This change does not close the issue. Subsequent work needs an explicit current product-level policy and corresponding consumer tests.
