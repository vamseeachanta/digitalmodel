Executive Summary

The reference jacket at 60.0 m water depth is analysed with 252 anodes. Aggregate lifetime mass and initial and final current output are compared with the required quantities below, using DNV-RP-B401, Cathodic protection design, May 2021 [3]. Draft: input qualification required. The arrangement is a conditional solution on the declared basis; environmental, coating, lifetime and anode inputs are assumed. The doubled-life sensitivity changes the conclusion. Spatial protection, splash exposure, installed piles and the structural mass discrepancy remain unresolved. The way forward is input confirmation and a spatial protection assessment.

Introduction

The reference-jacket geometry is used to evaluate aggregate sacrificial-anode requirements. Public structural data qualify the geometry; they do not qualify the CP environmental or product basis. The analysis covers one reference configuration and its diagnostic lifetime sensitivity.

3

Design Data

3.1 Site data

Water depth is 60.0 m from the pinned public model [2]. The site is a reference geometry, with no installation location selected. Water-level range, splash boundaries and wave conditions are not established; above-water protection is therefore not evaluated.

3.2 Structure and equipment data

The 3-leg tubular jacket is represented by 63 members [2]. Section properties and axis coordinates are sourced; true lengths, external areas and steel mass are derived. Pile embedment is an initial estimate in the paper [1], not an installed schedule. Design life is 25.0 yr; coating category is III; both are assumed, based on previous experience [I1]. They control lifetime mass and current demand. The aluminium stand-off cylinder is 1.0 m long with radius 0.050 m, assumed, based on previous experience [I1]. Net mass is 21.6 kg, derived from π × radius² × length × the repository alloy density [I2]; no core is deducted. Dimensions and alloy performance affect both mass and output. Attachment, core, product qualification and spatial layout are not established.

3.3 Environmental data

Temperature 10.0 °C and seawater resistivity 0.3 Ω m are assumed, based on previous experience [I1]. Temperature selects current-demand and alloy-performance lookups; resistivity controls anode resistance and current output. Soil resistivity, burial condition and measured potentials are not established; buried and spatial adequacy remain conditional.

3.4 Vessel data

Not applicable: the assessed configuration is a fixed jacket; no vessel installation analysis is performed.

3.5 Criteria and source clauses

DNV-RP-B401, Cathodic protection design, May 2021 [3]: Anode mass; Anode resistance; Anode current output. The aggregate mass criterion is provided net mass ≥ lifetime required mass; initial and final output criteria are provided current ≥ corresponding current demand. Current densities, coating breakdown, capacity, utilisation and potentials are selected by the repository edition route [I2], not independently verified against the licensed standard in this analysis. Their applicability is assumed and requires confirmation; no standard table is reproduced. An aggregate PASS denotes these comparisons only; no local potential criterion is evaluated.

3.6 Method

The physical method and equations are presented in Section 4.

3.7 Load cases

The stated arrangement is the mass-sized quantity evaluated for initial, mean and final coating conditions. Water-column steel is split at the repository depth-band boundary; buried pile area is evaluated separately. A doubled-design-life sensitivity retains the same anode count and all other inputs; it is a diagnostic scenario, not a selected design.

4

Analysis Methodology

The tubular external area is calculated as A = π D L (4.1), with axis length L split by elevation and depth. Phase current demand is I = Σ A j f (4.2), where j is the edition-selected current density and f is coating breakdown. Lifetime required mass is M = I_mean × hours/year × life / (capacity × utilisation) (4.3). Individual output is i = driving potential / resistance (4.4); aggregate output is N i. Fresh and depleted resistance are evaluated separately. The required count is the upward-rounded maximum of mass, initial-output and final-output counts. Native comparisons precede display rounding. The existing CP route [I2] supplies the calculation; it is not an independent solver benchmark. The coordinate model uses metres with z positive upward from still-water level. Tubular intersections, caps, internal surfaces, shielding and current distribution are not resolved. Source dimensions and mass are compared separately from CP adequacy.

Key Findings

6.1 Mean-current lifetime mass

The stated 252-anode arrangement passes the aggregate criterion: 5442.8 kg provided against 5427.4 kg required; margin +0.3 %. Criterion: provided ≥ required [3]; the comparison applies only to the declared inputs, not local protection.

6.2 Initial current output

The stated 252-anode arrangement passes the aggregate criterion: 390.1 A provided against 11.6 A required; margin +3255.1 %. Criterion: provided ≥ required [3]; the comparison applies only to the declared inputs, not local protection.

6.3 Final current output

The stated 252-anode arrangement passes the aggregate criterion: 291.1 A provided against 106.8 A required; margin +172.6 %. Criterion: provided ≥ required [3]; the comparison applies only to the declared inputs, not local protection.

6.4 Input qualification

Increasing the assumed lifetime from 25.0 to 50.0 yr while retaining 252 anodes fails at least one aggregate criterion. Design life has no qualified source and changes the conclusion; the report remains a draft. Other assumed inputs also require confirmation; no uncertainty bound is established.

7

Limitations

Splash boundaries and formal splash/submerged/atmospheric partition are not established; water-level range, applicable wave data and the governing definition are needed.

Above-water steel is reported undivided and is excluded from the current-demand calculation; continuous electrolyte contact and applicable protection measures are not established.

The evaluated water-column load uses the seawater-exposed density rows, split at the held 30 m table boundary; it does not establish protection in intermittent splash exposure.

Buried current paths, soil resistivity, installed pile geometry and coating condition are not established; estimated pile area and declared coating are used for the aggregate load.

No measured potentials are available for this structure. Aggregate capacity does not establish protection at remote or shielded locations.

The printed 1471 MT excludes transition piece and jacket piles. Tubular steel sums to 1021365 kg (-30.6%). Adding the separately excluded 450000 kg concentrated transition-piece masses gives 1471365 kg (+0.0%); that is a different basis and does not resolve the discrepancy.

Environment, coating, design life, cylinder geometry, no deducted core and alloy applicability are assumed, based on previous experience. Aggregate outputs are approximate until these inputs are qualified; no spatial protection acceptance is established.

8

Recommendations

Confirm design life, coating condition, seawater and soil properties and qualified anode product data before selecting the arrangement. Resolve the structural mass discrepancy and installed pile geometry before finalising exposed area. Establish splash boundaries and above-water measures. Assess spacing, shielding and remote potentials against the applicable protection criterion before design acceptance. A dated monitoring interval requires installed condition and survey evidence, which are not available.
