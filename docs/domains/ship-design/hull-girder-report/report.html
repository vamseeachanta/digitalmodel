Hull-girder analysis

Internal — still-water demand and strength-input evidence

1 Executive Summary

Obtain qualified longitudinal-strength limits before accepting a loading condition. Strength acceptance is not evaluated because zero of eight strength inputs are qualified. Missing loading-manual, material and rule evidence prevents an operating-limit conclusion; no independent finite-element comparison is established.

The assumed box hull has its governing still-water sagging moment of -6.25e+04 t·m at 50 m. The maximum shear magnitude is 2488 t. The shear and moment comparisons satisfy their existing numerical criteria of 1.0 % and 0.1 %, respectively (Table 5.2). These are calculation-agreement criteria, not strength limits.

2 Introduction

This internal analysis identifies still-water demand and the evidence needed for a longitudinal-strength decision. Local strength, ultimate collapse and class compliance are outside the calculated scope.

3 Design Data

The assumed load distribution and missing strength basis define the scope below.

3.1 Site data

Not applicable: the example has no site-specific loading.

3.2 Structure data

No midship scantlings or qualified section properties are supplied; elastic stress and ultimate capacity are not calculated.

3.3 Environmental data

Only still-water equilibrium is represented. No wave, slamming, whipping or thermal demand is calculated.

3.4 Vessel data

The generic box hull has length 100 m and a central distributed load of 1e+04 t over its middle half. Geometry and loading are assumed, based on previous experience; they are synthetic inputs, not measurements.

Strength acceptance is not evaluated. The strength-input evidence is summarized in Table 3.1; its required evidence is detailed in Appendix A. Calculation comparisons use the existing analytical fixture criteria [I2].

3.7 Load cases

One still-water loading condition is evaluated at 401 integration stations and 5 reporting frames. No load case has a qualified strength limit.

4 Analysis Methodology

Digitalmodel 2.1.0 is used to integrate the distributed weight minus buoyancy along the free-ended hull [I1]. Box buoyancy balances total weight and longitudinal moment. The raw end residual criterion is 0.5 % of displacement for shear and displacement times length for moment.

With x measured from the aft end, q(x) = w(x) − b(x), V(x) = ∫q(x)dx (4.1), and M(x) = ∫V(x)dx (4.2). Positive moment denotes hogging and negative moment sagging. Trapezoidal integration uses cell-averaged weights. The continuous central-block comparator is |V|max = W/4 (4.3), |M|min = WL/16 (4.4) [I2]. No wave or strength calculation is performed.

6 Key Findings

The two analytical demand comparisons satisfy their numerical criteria in Table 5.2. No frame or section can be assigned a strength disposition because all eight strength-input entries remain unqualified.

7 Limitations

Missing strength evidence prevents allowable replacement and strength acceptance; no measured loading condition, wave combination, section stress, ultimate collapse, local buckling or independent finite-element validation is established. The consequences and required evidence are given in Appendix A.

8 Recommendations

Obtain the six loading-manual curve/property entries, qualified material evidence and the applicable rule clause before assigning a permitted loading condition. Confirm the actual weight, buoyancy and section model and obtain an independent comparison before extending this assumed example to an engineering configuration.

10 Acronyms and Symbols

SF denotes shear force; BM denotes bending moment. In the marine convention used here, t is a mass-equivalent load ordinate and t·m is its moment; no force conversion is applied. W is total distributed weight (t), L hull length (m), x longitudinal position (m), q net load (t/m), V shear (t), M bending moment (t·m), and Z section modulus (m³). Strength utilisation means demand divided by a qualified limit and is not calculated.
