Reliability program benchmarking analysis

Internal method comparison

Summary

Retain the existing analytic fatigue calculation for independent lognormal stress and S-N intercept uncertainty with fixed slope. Use OpenTURNS as an optional comparison and as a candidate for separately validated nonlinear models. Neither method establishes an accept or re-rate decision without an inspection basis and a governing reliability target; those inputs are absent from this benchmark.

1. Introduction

The analysis compares probability, reliability index, sensitivity and measured computation cost. The reference set contains a published nonlinear FORM calculation and exact linear-in-log-space fatigue cases.

2. Design basis

2.1 Asset and geometry

No inspected asset is represented. The nonlinear numerical reference is a beam deflection model.

2.2 Materials

The fatigue curve is a synthetic repository fixture, not a qualified material curve.

2.3 Loads

Mean stress range is 100 MPa; the design lives in Table 1 are cycles, not calendar years.

2.4 Environment

Environmental loading is not included because the reference model is algebraic.

2.5 Criteria

Absolute probability and index tolerances for analytic comparisons are 1e-06 and 1e-06. Sampling probability tolerance is 0.01; first-order sensitivity tolerance is 0.03. Published FORM tolerances are 1e-06 for probability and 1e-06 for index. These numerical agreement criteria are not asset acceptance criteria.

2.6 Method

See Analysis methodology.

2.7 Assumptions

Stress and intercept are independent lognormal variables, each with coefficient of variation 0.1. The power-law slope is fixed at 3; intercept is 1e+12 in cycles multiplied by MPa raised to that slope. No uncertainty in slope, correlation or rainflow history is included.

3. Analysis methodology

The existing NumPy/SciPy routine evaluates the exact normal margin in log-space. OpenTURNS 1.26 applies FORM to the same margin. Exact reference probabilities are computed independently from the stated normal mean and variance. Fixed-seed direct sampling and Jansen first-order sensitivity are also compared. The native routine is not applied to the nonlinear beam because its interface only represents power-law fatigue.

The sampling timings include different work: the native method computes fatigue-life percentiles, while the OpenTURNS method evaluates the margin and counts events through a Python loop. These observations compare the implemented workflows and do not isolate library throughput.

6. Recommendation and decision routing

The exact analytic fatigue calculation avoids sampling error under the tested assumptions and remains the recommended default for that model. Direct sampling provides a distribution check but the tested sample count does not qualify rare-event tail estimates. OpenTURNS provides a nonlinear FORM alternative, demonstrated against its published reference; an asset-specific model needs separate validation.

Accept or re-rate requires a governing target, an inspection-derived model and qualified load and material data. Measure more where those inputs or their uncertainty are missing. Escalate when variable slope, correlated inputs, nonlinear damage or rare-event probability changes the model. The present benchmark establishes numerical agreement only and supplies no asset disposition.

7. Limitations

Synthetic fatigue inputs, fixed slope and independence limit the model; see Design basis. The nonlinear reference is a FORM result, not an exact probability; see Results. One seed and one timing observation do not establish sampling coverage or transferable speed; see Computation cost. No inspection or target reliability is provided, so acceptance and re-rating remain unestablished; see Recommendation.

8. Conclusions

All retained results meet their declared numerical agreement criteria. The comparison supports retaining the analytic fatigue model and using OpenTURNS optionally where separately qualified model requirements justify it.
