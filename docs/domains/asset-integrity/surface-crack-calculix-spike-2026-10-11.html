Deepest-point signed differences are -16.017%, -13.102%, -11.907%; surface differences are -5.563%, -8.724%, -6.691%. The finest maximum absolute front difference is 11.907%. Benchmark and method

Solver: CalculiX 2.21 (as installed on the build machine; not read from the run).

The fixed flaw has a = c = 5 mm, t = 10 mm, plate full width = 50 mm and full length = 50 mm. Thus a/t = 0.5, a/c = 1, c/b = 0.2, full width/c = 10, and full length/c = 10. Remote membrane stress is 100 MPa, with no bending. The synthetic, isotropic linear-elastic material has E = 210000 MPa and nu = 0.3; no yield or toughness assumption is used.

The semicircle is a member of the semi-elliptical family. It makes the radial sampling rays normal to the front and avoids a geometric interpretation parameter. The in-repo Newman–Raju implementation requires positive a/c no greater than unity, a below t and c/b below its stated bound; this benchmark is within those bounds. The public NASA memorandum supports this shape and finite-thickness regime. The benchmark was selected before solver results and was unchanged afterwards.

Dimensional seed inputs a, b and remote stress come from the public synthetic Newman–Raju tests (a = 5, c = 25, stress = 100 in their respective cases), with t = 2a and the benchmark c = a. E and nu come from the synthetic STEEL fixture in the CalculiX tests. The refinement seed is the six beam DOFs in those tests, doubled successively. These are numerical design choices derived from repository fixtures, not measured material or geometry. File digests and the source revision are in the JSON artifact; repository terms are MIT License.

A quarter plate is meshed explicitly, without installing or invoking a mesher. The x-z cross-section uses polar layers ending on a rectangle, with the rectangle corner angle included. Crack-front nodes lie on r = a; faces between them are chords, whose angular resolution is refined. The y-direction is extruded to the loaded end. The radial coordinates inside the flaw are a[1-(1-i/n)^2]; outside the flaw they are a+(R(phi)-a)(j/n)^2, where R terminates at the plate perimeter. Extrusion coordinates are L(j/n)^2. Linear fully integrated C3D8 elements surround the front; C3D6 wedges close the polar centre. No quarter-point elements are used.

The x = 0 symmetry plane has ux = 0. The intact part of y = 0 has uy = 0; the crack face is traction-free. One uz anchor at the loaded-end origin removes rigid translation. The surface and back face remain traction-free. Negative P2 pressure imposes tensile loading at the extrusion end, following manual section 7.43 face numbering. Native summed y reactions are compared with stress times the quarter-plate end area. Tests check mesh orientation, rectangular area, front radii, crack opening and intact-face closure.

CalculiX 2.21 manual section 6.9.27 (pp. 368–372), section 10.14 and the *CRACK PROPAGATION entry describe an empirical growth route that interpolates uncracked stresses and applies shape factors. That route is not an independently solved cracked-body contour integral. No built-in contour-integral route usable for this benchmark was established by the manual inspection. Its empirical K route is not used to validate another empirical K solution.

The cracked-body FRD nodal stress syy is read through the unchanged repository parser. Roylance's public Westergaard opening-mode field (p. 7, Eq. 4) reduces on theta = 0 to sigma_yy = K/sqrt(2 pi r). Consequently apparent K is sigma_yy sqrt(2 pi r/1000) when r is in mm and K is in MPa sqrt(m). The first three exterior-ring nodes at each front station are sampled. A least squares line apparent K = K0 + slope*r supplies the intercept. The linear fit is a numerical spike choice, not a textbook guarantee of 3D extraction accuracy. The individual samples and their spread are retained. The cited leading-field range r no greater than 0.1a is flagged separately for each station. The synthetic test generates the leading stress field from a known K and also checks a derived linear apparent-K correction; it does not establish FE accuracy.

Reference values at every sampled parametric angle are obtained by calling newman_raju_k from the unchanged in-repo source file. Direct file loading avoids eager imports of unrelated assessment modules; the wrapper test compares against an independent import of the unchanged file. No coefficients or results are transcribed from a paid fitness-for-service standard.

Each run uses at most four solver threads, a bounded runtime and an address-space ceiling set by the job. Peak RSS is measured by the time tool; it is not total host memory. Runs are sequential. Meshing and file-import time are excluded from solver time. The explicit mesh avoids an external mesher setup cost, but does not establish cost or accuracy for singular quadratic or arbitrary elliptical meshes.

Limitations, recommendation and remaining leg

FRD stresses are extrapolated and averaged at nodes; the near-front result is sensitive to that recovery. Linear elements do not reproduce the square-root singularity. A fit to three distances is not a contour-independence check. The free-surface vertex has a three-dimensional corner field, so its extracted scalar K is an apparent comparison value rather than a certified endpoint SIF. Finite-length sensitivity and extraction-window sensitivity are not qualified. Only elastic mode I is tested: no J, plasticity, mixed mode, residual stress, material qualification or fitness-for-service acceptance follows from this spike.

The reported error sign depends on the node rings used in extrapolation. At the deepest point on the finest mesh, apparent K values are 7.375587, 9.495567 and 9.355543 MPa sqrt(m), at radii 0.008680556, 0.034722222 and 0.078125000 mm respectively. The first ring, the node next to the front, is the outlier. As an illustration only, extrapolating from the second and third rings gives 9.607586 MPa sqrt(m), 9.655% above the 8.761661 MPa sqrt(m) reference, instead of -11.907% for the reported fit. Both miss the 5% comparator; the route is not usable either way. This illustration adds no result and changes no reported difference.

Interim recommendation covering CalculiX only: retain the installed solver as an elastic displacement/stress engine, but do not promote this stress-extrapolation route to a Level 3 driving-force implementation. A separate investigation could compare quadratic singular elements and a domain/interaction-integral extraction. That proposed investigation is outside this time box.

The Code_Aster leg will need the unchanged geometry, load, elastic material, reference wrapper and front angles, plus native driving-force readback, contour sensitivity, refinement costs and the same comparator. Code_Aster is not installed and is not installed by this job. Licensed-solver receipts are unavailable and unused. The two-solver selection and the program note's recommendation wait for that leg and second-provider review. No follow-on issue is opened.
