Crack growth and inspection planning analysis

Summary

Thresholded fatigue growth is integrated to a supplied assessment limit and converted to a capped inspection interval. The existing surface-flaw FAD envelope can supply that limit. The governing result identifies assessment, wall-penetration or coincident limits, and the resulting plan can feed qualitative RBI. Missing LBB evidence is reported as NOT_EVALUATED; through-wall geometry alone establishes no LBB acceptance.

Method and source basis

The MIT Fatigue notes (2001), Equation (2) give da/dN = C (DeltaK)^m. The AFGROW Damage Tolerant Design Handbook, Section 5.1.2, Equation (5.1.3) gives the stress-ratio correction introduced in 1970. With R = Kmin/Kmax, the implemented form is:

Keq = DeltaK / (1 - R)^(1 - gamma)
da/dN = 0                         when DeltaK <= DeltaK_th(R, environment)
da/dN = C * Keq^m                 otherwise
N = integral from a_initial to a_limit of da / (da/dN)

C is calibrated at R = 0. The threshold is the measured raw range at service R, before correction. All material parameters and their calibration basis are required. The supported domain is 0 <= R < 1 and 0 <= gamma <= 1. These are implementation limits, not universal material limits. Compression and load-sequence effects require a different qualified model. No BS 7910 or API 579 material coefficient is supplied.

Adaptive Simpson quadrature integrates the reciprocal growth rate. The initial panels are set by steps; panel bisection compares coarse and refined Simpson estimates using the difference divided by 15. This numerical basis is described in Driscoll and Braun, Fundamentals of Numerical Computation, online Julia edition, Section 5.7, Equation (5.7.3).

The default controls are relative_tolerance = 1e-6 (dimensionless), absolute_tolerance = 1e-6 cycles and max_refinements = 20 bisection levels. The configured max_refinements must be an integer of at least 2; no upper count bound is imposed. The summed estimate is reported as GrowthResult.estimated_error_cycles against absolute_tolerance + relative_tolerance * life_cycles. Exhausting the refinement budget raises ValueError; no passing inspection plan is produced from an unconverged integral. A sampled threshold arrest returns null remaining cycles and null estimated error. History retains the initial depth and completed initial panels, without an unreached limiting-depth endpoint.

The error estimate assumes a smooth reciprocal growth rate. An arbitrary callable can contain unsampled threshold dips, singularities or oscillations; finite samples cannot certify their absence or bound the true integration error. Material calibration and a compatible DeltaK function require case-specific evidence. The legacy paris_law_life API retains its signature and DataFrame result while delegating to the same integrator and retaining only reached arrest history.

plan_crack_from_fad calls the existing critical_flaw_depth_mm. That routine can return its depth or geometry validity cap. The limit therefore denotes the end of the established assessment envelope, rather than a demonstrated fracture depth. Growth stops at the smaller of that depth and wall thickness. The FAD input length_mm = 2c is the total surface length, held fixed; the FAD routine converts it to half-length c. Aspect-ratio evolution is not modeled. A compatible depth-dependent DeltaK solution is required. Surface depth is never substituted for through-wall length. Supported membrane and bending stress components are nonnegative, with at least one positive; either component may be zero. Compression and an all-zero load are outside the FAD adapter's supported loading domain.

CrackLifeAssessment.governing_limit is WALL_PENETRATION when wall thickness is smaller than the supplied assessment depth, ASSESSMENT_LIMIT when that depth is smaller than wall thickness, and COINCIDENT_LIMITS when the two are equal. This field identifies the governing depth criterion; it does not establish a qualified fracture or LBB conclusion.

Inspection and RBI interfaces

years = remaining_cycles / cycles_per_year
half_life = years / 2
next_interval = min(years / life_divisor, supplied_max_interval)

The year, null-life and half-life semantics follow the existing inspection_planning.plan_inspection implementation. The divisor is required and cannot be less than that planner's divisor of 2. The maximum interval and policy basis are required; these inputs do not establish a crack-specific code interval. At or beyond the limit, remaining life and interval are zero and screening fails. Under threshold arrest, life and half-life are null and the supplied maximum interval governs only while the modeled loading and environment remain applicable.

Inspection policy is validated before growth integration. At RBI entry, mutable plan fields are revalidated against the cycle-to-year conversion, divisor, half-life, cap and interval, growth/screening status and policy basis; inconsistencies raise ValueError.

CrackInspectionPlan retains cycles_per_year and life_divisor with the supplied maximum interval and policy basis. screen_crack_plan scores the ratio of remaining years to the supplied reference interval against descending life-ratio edges, combines that probability category with the consequence category through the PoF x CoF matrix, and returns the smaller of the crack-plan interval and risk-band cap. Its basis records the actual life-ratio edges, reference interval, matrix band edges, per-band caps, plan cap, divisor, cycle rate and inspection policy basis. The corrosion_trend_bump subscore is null (not applicable), rather than a measured zero. Under modeled arrest, the life-ratio category uses unbounded life only for the supplied model and conditions. The caller supplies the RBI policy; default bands remain screening-practice parameters rather than API RP 581 table values.

The registered inspection router accepts inspection_planning.crack_growth instead of thickness inputs. Supply law, stress_range_mpa, geometry_factor, initial depth, integration steps, cycle rate, interval cap, divisor and policy basis. Supply either fad_inputs or explicit critical depth, wall thickness and depth basis. The router uses the existing constant-geometry SIF equation; the Python interface accepts a general compatible DeltaK callable. Results retain the ordinary result key and add crack_assessment.

Worked example: published growth problem

MIT Fatigue (2001), Table 1 and Problem 6, supplies steel C = 10-11, m = 3, an infinite-plate central crack from half-length 0.1 mm to 2.5 mm, and Delta sigma = 950 MPa. The problem's Delta sigma is treated as the range; it is not doubled. No threshold or R correction is specified, so zero threshold and gamma = 1 explicitly recover the published Paris equation. SI metres are used for half-length and MPa sqrt(m) for DeltaK.

DeltaK = 950 * sqrt(pi*a)
N = 2*(1/sqrt(0.0001) - 1/sqrt(0.0025)) / (1e-11*950^3*pi^(3/2))
  = 3351.4 cycles
Synthetic policy: 1000 cycles/year, divisor 2, maximum interval 2 years.
Remaining life = 3.4 years; next interval = 1.7 years (rounded).

The expected life is obtained by direct integration of Equation (2). The printed general expression in Problem 5 contains an extra factor m and is not used as the oracle. Synthetic policy values illustrate conversion only; they are not operating records or code limits.

Run the example from the repository root with the package dependencies installed:

PYTHONPATH=src python examples/workflows/crack-growth-inspection/run_example.py

JSON output includes the published case, a separate synthetic FAD-to-inspection example, input basis and the LBB evidence gaps. No output file is created by the script.

LBB evidence and remaining qualification

The LBB helper optionally checks the strict prerequisite length_margin * detectable_full_length < critical_full_length, with all lengths, margin and basis supplied. Equality fails the prerequisite. A geometric pass still returns NOT_EVALUATED overall. Leak-rate and detection qualification, through-wall stability under governing loads and transients, and the applicable LBB procedure and margins remain unestablished. API 579-1/ASME FFS-1 (2016), Part 9, LBB procedure is the intended qualification route; no procedure values are reproduced.

Threshold calibration, short-crack behavior, environment, residual stress effects on R, load spectrum, crack-shape evolution and inspection detection capability require case-specific evidence. The closed-form and published tests validate arithmetic and interface behavior; they do not qualify an operating component.
