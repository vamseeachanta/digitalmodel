Welded-padeye parametric demand analysis

Summary

The offline implementation evaluates nine nominal elastic demands for a caller-supplied idealized rectangular padeye, two straight base welds and in-plane loads. It produces 225 parameter cases and 375 directional calculations when all geometry is valid. Exact lookup requires a content revision and screening use. Historical reproduction remains UNVERIFIED_SOURCE; no modern-code capacity, utilization, fit, local stress, buckling or finite-element qualification is established.

Scope and source qualification

Issue 2117 identifies the historical welded-lug target. The public Design and Construction of Lifting Beams (1991), example pp. 156–158 and Fig. 16A and its 2004 erratum identify the source chain. The idealized example below is not a transcription of that drawing. No historical dimension, weld coefficient or capacity is supplied by this implementation. Source fractions, full profile, weld arrangement and erratum applicability require qualification before a historical comparison. The existing circular-lug atlas and rectangular solver example remain separate.

Inputs and coordinate system

Required geometry comprises base width B, height H, hole centre (rx, ry) measured from the base centroid, hole radius r, absolute baseline weld leg a, and a nonempty source description. All lengths are in inches. Required load inputs are unfactored gross weight in kip, the single applied load factor, integer equally loaded lug count and a source description. A previously factored load shall not be supplied as gross weight with its factor reapplied. There is no implicit historical factor or material property.

Positive X is horizontal in the plate plane; positive Y is upward; positive Mz follows the right-hand rule. Angle θ is measured from upward vertical, with 0 ≤ θ < 90°. An out-of-plane load is outside the method. Exterior scaling about the hole followed by base-centroid translation multiplies B, H, rx and ry by s while holding r fixed. Thickness t varies independently. The weld multiplier m multiplies the fixed absolute leg a, independently of t.

The rectangle is screened for positive dimensions and strictly positive hole-to-edge ligament. This establishes only geometric containment. It does not establish support fit or shackle clearance. Nonfinite inputs are rejected. Finite invalid geometry is retained as INVALID_GEOMETRY with null demands. Numerical overflow is rejected rather than issued as a finite result.

Equations and derivation

University Physics Volume 1 (2016), §12.1, Conditions for Static Equilibrium supplies force and moment equilibrium. Equal load sharing gives Fy = Wf/n; resolution gives Fx = ±Fy tan θ and resultant √(Fx²+Fy²). The load-point moment is Mz = rx Fy − ry Fx.

The two equal base welds lie on opposite plate faces and are co-projected onto y = 0, −B/2 ≤ x ≤ B/2. The projected line model excludes weld-end returns, curved welds, torsional warping and out-of-plane effects. Total line length L = 2∫dx = 2B; centroidal polar line integral J = 2∫x²dx = B³/6. The coefficient follows from the stated limits, rather than a weld capacity table. A uniform translational line force plus an elastic rotational line force gives q(x) = (Fx/L, Fy/L + Mz x/J). Its integrated force is F and its integrated moment is Mz. Convexity of the vector norm on a straight segment puts the maximum at an endpoint. qmax has units kip/in.

An equal-leg right-triangle fillet has perpendicular throat am/√2 by Pythagoras. Nominal throat stress is qmax divided by throat; this is a geometric demand conversion, not an allowable stress. No empirical weld coefficient or acceptance limit is used.

Mechanics of Materials, §4.2, Stresses in Beams (public online edition accessed 2026-10-20) supplies elastic bending stress Mc/I. For a rectangular base cut, I = t∫x²dx = tB³/12; c = B/2. Hence σleft = Fy/(Bt) − 6Mz/(tB²), σright = Fy/(Bt) + 6Mz/(tB²), and average τ = Fx/(Bt). The left cut coordinate is negative X. These are nominal transmitted section stresses, with no local hole or weld stress concentration. Thickness affects plate stress, while fixed-leg weld stress is thickness-invariant by construction.

Study and exact lookup

The grid follows the repository study plan Table 1: thickness {0.750, 1.000, 1.250, 1.500, 1.750} in; exterior scale {0.8, 0.9, 1.0, 1.1, 1.2}; weld multiplier {1.0, 1.25, 1.5}; angles {0, 30, 45}°. These are study axes, not approved fabrication sizes or design limits. The Cartesian product has 75 geometries and 225 geometry-angle rows. Each row retains two directional cells. At zero angle, negative is NOT_APPLICABLE; thus 375 directions are scheduled and 75 cells are not applicable. Invalid cases remain in the complete table.

Every response envelope retains its signed maximum-absolute demand, units and governing direction. Exact ties select positive. All valid responses are CALCULATED_SCREENING with verification NOT_EVALUATED; arithmetic regression testing does not upgrade them to historical benchmark reproduction. Lookup allows diagnostic access to invalid rows and returns an independent copy. Interpolation, extrapolation and design use are refused.

The JSON revision is SHA-256 over canonical JSON (sorted keys, compact separators, UTF-8, finite numbers). It binds method version, code digest, sources, input basis, limitations, units, axes and the complete table. It detects accidental mutation and is not an authenticity signature. The caller shall retain the expected revision independently; replacing both payload and hash defeats self-integrity checking. JSON is the authoritative typed table. CSV is a directional view: blank numeric cells mean no calculation, never a true zero; free-text reasons are not decoded as null. The HTML report includes a fixed-slice chart and complete envelopes. Reissues use a new directory; existing output files are never overwritten.

Worked example: inputs, method and answer

Synthetic inputs: B = 4.0 in, H = 6.0 in, (rx, ry) = (0.0, 3.0) in, hole radius 0.5 in, leg 0.25 in; W = 12.0 kip, f = 1.5 and n = 3. These values are chosen for independent arithmetic and are not a historical design. At t = 1.000 in, s = m = 1.0 and θ = 45.0°, Fy = Fx = 6.0 kip, resultant = 8.5 kip and Mz = −18.0 kip-in.

Independent hand calculation: L = 8.0 in; J = 32/3 in³. At x = −2.0 in, q = (3/4, 33/8) kip/in, so qmax = √1125/8 = 4.2 kip/in. The throat stress is √1125/√2 = 23.7 ksi. Axial stress is 1.5 ksi and bending contribution is −6.8 ksi after display rounding; the unrounded section calculation gives left 8.2 ksi, right −5.2 ksi and average shear 1.5 ksi. Display values are rounded to one decimal; JSON retains calculation precision. Negative loading swaps the plate-edge stresses for this symmetric hole position.

Verification and remaining work

The regression suite contains a fully written independent baseline calculation plus a 54-case, 90-direction independent force-couple reconstruction using exact piecewise integration of x². Its arithmetic tolerance is a software precision criterion, not engineering acceptance. Tests also cover symmetry and asymmetry, scale and thickness sensitivity, weld-leg sensitivity, all grid counts, null withholding, angle/load rejection, exact lookup, revision mismatch, mutation detection, immutable outputs, HTML escaping and serialized readback.

Historical profile reproduction, independently sourced historical demands and response-specific physical comparison tolerances remain NOT_EVALUATED. Their absence prevents a verified historical benchmark claim. The next engineering step is qualification of the drawing and weld arrangement, followed by a profile-specific adapter and independent baseline comparison. Modern-code capacity and a native finite-element campaign remain separate work.
