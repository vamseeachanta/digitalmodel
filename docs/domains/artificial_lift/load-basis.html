Dynacard load basis analysis

Summary

The Gibbs comparison solver propagates load and displacement through the damped wave equation for a vertical rod string. The result records downhole_load_basis.method = coupled_frequency_domain and validation_status = NOT_EVALUATED. These restrictions appear in serialized results, CLI summaries and HTML diagnostic reports.

Method and sources

With distance x positive downward and displacement u positive upward, tensile force is F = -EA du/dx. Fourier substitution in u_tt + c u_t = a² u_xx gives gamma² = (-omega² + i omega c)/a². Each ordered rod section propagates both displacement and force:

U_out = cosh(gamma L) U_in - sinh(gamma L)/(EA gamma) F_in
F_out = cosh(gamma L) F_in - EA gamma sinh(gamma L) U_in

Dynamic length L is in inches, E in psi, A in square inches, wave speed a in inches/second and damping rate c in inverse seconds. Displacement is in inches and force in lbf. The derivation and sources are recorded in gibbs-transfer.md: Applied Mechanics of Solids, section 4.3.3 and SPE-588-PA (1963), The Mathematical Model.

The static load is F_p = F_s - W_b, where W_b = sum((rho_effective - rho_fluid) A L_ft / 144) and rho_effective = weight_per_foot × 144 / A. Density is in lb/ft³; 144 is the exact square-foot to square-inch conversion. Supplied coupling-inclusive weight is retained. The implementation is physics.py with no empirical attenuation, buoyancy or stroke multiplier.

Recorded provenance

The basis records the summed buoyant_rod_weight_lbf, harmonic_count and closing_sample_dropped. A null harmonic count means all harmonics are retained; zero retains DC only. attenuation_factor is null for the coupled path. An exactly repeated first/last position and load pair is removed before FFT and harmonic validation. Both coordinates must match; near endpoints remain distinct. The output then has one fewer sample and the input is unchanged.

Synthetic static worked example

The chosen rod length is 100.0 ft and diameter is sqrt(4/pi) in, giving A = 1.0 in². Rod density is 144.0 lb/ft³, supplied weight is 1.0 lbf/ft, fluid density is 72.0 lb/ft³, E is 1440.0 psi, wave speed is 60.0 ft/s and damping is zero. Pump diameter is 1.000 in, depth is 100.0 ft and cycle rate is 1.0 stroke/min. Five identical surface samples prescribe position 0.0 in and load 1000.0 lbf; the repeated endpoint is removed, leaving four samples. These are synthetic equation inputs, not field measurements.

Independent substitution gives W_b = (144 - 72) × 1 × 100 / 144 = 50 lbf and F_p = 1000 - 50 = 950 lbf. Every non-DC Fourier coefficient is zero. Static elastic stretch is constant, so the zero-minimum position datum gives 0.0 in at every pump sample; absolute static stretch is not returned.

Qualification and way forward

At 15 harmonics, five fixture wells with zero rod damping give median 1.3% and maximum 2.0% load nRMSE against stored reference cards, compared with the old affine path's 17.3% median. The per-fixture results are 0.52%, 1.74%, 1.34%, 2.03% and 0.39%; the fifth reference also excludes its repeated endpoint. These are regression measurements, not engineering acceptance limits.

Surface/pump load correlation at shared indices reaches 0.991812 in the first fixture, so a ceiling below 0.99 is not established. The measured regression bound is below 0.995, excluding the affine path's correlation of 1.0; field accuracy is not inferred from this bound.

No openly readable published worked example with tabulated card values was found. Validation rests on closed-form cases derived from the cited equations and the five stored reference cards. A published-example check is NOT_EVALUATED.

NOT_EVALUATED: load and displacement are propagated through the damped wave equation for a vertical rod string; deviation, Coulomb friction and damping calibration are not modelled; field diagnostic accuracy is not established. Derived fillage, pressure, production, buckling and diagnoses inherit this limitation. Equation agreement does not establish field accuracy.

Legacy saved affine_surface_load results remain readable. Results without metadata deserialize with a null basis; null means unknown provenance. Other solvers do not acquire a Gibbs load basis.
