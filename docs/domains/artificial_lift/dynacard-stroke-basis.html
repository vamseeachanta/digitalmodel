Dynacard synthetic stroke analysis

Summary

The normal, tubing-movement and plunger-undertravel generators now share the same default stroke draw. Absolute stroke therefore cannot separate these labels by construction. Explicit matched pump travel and lost-motion inputs allow a reduced-travel comparison without inventing tubing compliance. Field diagnosis and the dimensioned operating envelope remain NOT_EVALUATED.

Method and source

The public SWPSC paper Over-travel Can Occur On Both The Upstroke And Down Stroke (2017), section “Definition of Over Travel and Under Travel”, Figure 1, identifies unanchored tubing as a source of stretch that consumes stroke. The matched comparison is:

pump travel = otherwise matched anchored pump travel − specified additional lost motion

The anchored input includes rod stretch and dynamic effects. It is not the surface stroke. The comparison holds those effects fixed; it does not solve coupled rod and tubing dynamics. It establishes the loss direction for matched inputs, not an ordering of absolute travel across different installations.

The otherwise matched anchored travel is reconstructed as 45.4 − 30.7 + 12.2 = 26.9 in. The generator applies the specified loss: 26.9 − 8.3 = 18.6 in, matching the source's stated downhole travel at its published precision. A normal card from another installation can also have 18.6 in of travel; absolute travel alone cannot identify tubing movement.

Scale audit and regression criteria

audit_generator_scale_ranges enumerates the generator registry and records observed minimum and maximum stroke, load range and absolute loop area for each mode. These metrics use geometric range and shoelace identities. Every pair with disjoint sampled envelopes is reported; touching endpoints count as overlap. Samples do not establish distribution support or field validity. Rounded display values are not used to decide disjointness.

Regressions require equal default positions for the three affected generators at matched seeds, equal default noise for normal and undertravel, reduced travel for a positive specified loss, rejection of incomplete or invalid loss inputs, and complete registry coverage. Independent rectangles check the audit: widths 3.0 and 4.0 in with a 200.0 lbf height have areas 600.0 and 800.0 in·lbf.

At 32 seeds per mode, the regression baseline contains 19 stroke, 53 load-range and 56 area pairs with disjoint sampled envelopes. Each current pair set shall be a subset of its baseline: removing separation passes; adding a pair fails.

Known limits and way forward

  • The retained common default travel bounds are assumed, based on previous experience. The repository's original normal-generator implementation is the provenance, not a qualified dimensional source. They shall not be used as a field operating envelope.
  • Default calls specify no physical loss. Loss magnitude is NOT_EVALUATED; default normal and undertravel cards are intentionally identical. Explicit loss requires a supplied anchored travel and cannot be inferred from the label.
  • Synthetic training and hold-out builders exclude PLUNGER_UNDERTRAVEL until a distinguishing matched-travel basis exists. They cover 19 labels. Direct generation and the 20-mode audit remain available; identical defaults shall not be trained or scored as separate labels.
  • Other generators retain their existing assumptions. Disjoint load, stroke or area envelopes reported elsewhere remain review findings.
  • The shipped classifier, stored examples and previously published confidences are not regenerated or revalidated by this change. Their earlier synthetic accuracy does not describe the revised generator population. Retraining requires an explicit data basis; field scoring remains tracked by issue 1864 on labelled real-card validation.
  • Solver round-trip classification remains a separate limitation under issue 1885 on round-trip label collapse.
