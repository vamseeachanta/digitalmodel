Drilling riser hand-check method provenance

Summary

The global hand checks implement vertical equilibrium, a tensioned beam eigenproblem and a string travel-time estimate. Their equations and independent reference calculations are mapped below. Numerical agreement checks these model identities under their stated assumptions; it does not establish API or ISO compliance. No solver result or design acceptance is established.

Vertical geometry

For a positive length L with top elevation zt, the bottom is zb = zt − L. Intersecting [zb, zt] with the region below mean sea level gives Lwet = clip(L − max(zt, 0), 0, L). This is a coordinate identity, with no standard-derived coefficient.

Weight and effective tension

The structure and bore contents have mass (μ + ρc Ai)L, where Ai = πdi²/4 is the circle-area identity. Displaced water has mass ρw V′ Lwet. Subtracting buoyancy from gravitational weight gives the inventory equation. Integrating vertical force balance from the top of each section gives Te,b = Te,t − W. The supplied tension is already effective tension; pressure end loads shall not be added again. This derivation is a mechanics identity, not a mapped standard provision.

The acceleration used by the implementation is the exact conventional standard gravity in NIST 2022 CODATA. It is not a measurement of local gravity. Fluid density, mass and displacement are inputs, not site measurements supplied by this method note.

Beam and finite-element coefficients

Euphonics §3.2.1, equations (7), (12) and (13) supplies the untensioned Euler–Bernoulli equation and its harmonic general solution. The tension term follows by varying the transverse potential energy ½∫[EI(w″)² + Te(w′)²] dz; inertia follows from ½∫μ(ẇ)² dz. The resulting equation is EI w'''' − (Te w′)′ + μ wtt = 0 for constant EI. Spatially varying properties are assembled element by element.

Let ξ = z/Le, with element degrees of freedom [w1, θ1, w2, θ2]. Cubic interpolation of displacement and slope at both ends gives the four functions:

N = [1 − 3ξ² + 2ξ³, Le(ξ − 2ξ² + ξ³),
     3ξ² − 2ξ³, Le(−ξ² + ξ³)].
Kb = EI ∫ N″ᵀ N″ dz
Kg = Te,avg ∫ N′ᵀ N′ dz
M  = μ ∫ Nᵀ N dz

Integrating these polynomials over 0 ≤ ξ ≤ 1 reproduces the implemented bending, geometric and consistent-mass matrices, including their integer coefficients and denominators. For example Kb[0,0] = 12EI/Le³, Kg[0,0] = 6Te,avg/(5Le), and M[0,0] = 13μLe/35. These factors are derived integrals, not tabulated standard values. Using the end-average tension in an element is an approximation when tension varies; mesh refinement remains necessary for a case-specific result. Point masses and translational springs add directly to nodal translation entries; rotational springs add to rotation entries. Eliminating constrained coordinates imposes pins and clamps.

The existing mass regularization is a numerical perturbation; it is not physical rotational inertia or a standards allowance. A massless region or an ill-conditioned mesh requires separate sensitivity evidence. Modes are returned in increasing frequency, hence decreasing period.

String approximation

For a uniform string, a sine mode with fixed endpoints has speed c = √(Te/μ) and period Pn = 2L/(nc), as in OpenStax (2016), §16.6. For slowly varying properties, integrating the local travel time gives Pn ≈ (2/n)∫ dz/c(z). The implementation approximates the integral using each element's average tension. No independent error bound for a variable-tension riser is established here. Positive tension is required and bending is neglected.

Lateral tensioner stiffness

For a constant line force F along a vector r of length ℓ, differentiating F r/ℓ with respect to a horizontal endpoint displacement gives the restoring stiffness magnitude (F/ℓ)(1 − ux²). Each line's force is obtained from the total vertical force and vertical direction cosine; the contributions are summed at equally spaced azimuths. For vertical lines ux = 0, giving kx = Tv/h for vertical separation h. This limiting identity is an independent derivative check; it does not qualify an actual tensioner system.

Assembly assumptions and implementation conventions

The line mass used for vibration includes structure, bore contents and Ca ρw V′ times the wet fraction. Ca is supplied by the specification. The inner barrel carries its modelled hanging weight; the existing axial-stiffness threshold selects the slip section, rather than calculating physical slip engagement. The existing subdivision epsilon, element-size defaults, mode-count defaults and mass regularizer are computational settings, without standards provenance. Flex-joint boundary conditions, ring immersion and the rigid-stack approximation require case-specific qualification. None of these conventions is promoted to a design requirement by this inventory.

For the fixed-fixed case, the published general harmonic solution is restricted by the two conditions at z = 0 to u(ξ) = A[cosh(βξ) − cos(βξ)] + B[sinh(βξ) − sin(βξ)]. At ξ = 1, the remaining displacement and slope conditions give the determinant 2[1 − cosh(β) cos(β)] = 0. This derivation establishes fixed-fixed applicability independently of the source's free-free example.

Bisection between 3π/2 and 2π excludes the zero root. With B/A = −[cosh(β) − cos(β)]/[sinh(β) − sin(β)], the test evaluates both displacement and slope at both endpoints. It also compares the independently solved root against the previous six-decimal reference at its rounding precision. The finite-element period with stiff rotational end springs is then compared against this analytical period.
