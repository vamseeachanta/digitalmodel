Installation analysis: short-horizon decision support

Engineering development notes · source review: 17 September 2026 · linked work: digitalmodel installation workflow issue.

Status: proposed engineering work. This note records recommendations, not approved operating criteria, an implementation claim or a forecast-service specification. The live analysis campaign and its inputs are unchanged.

1. Source observation and limits of the evidence

Commercial radar-based wave and vessel-motion prediction services report forecast horizons of roughly three to four minutes and example statistics on the time spent below illustrated motion thresholds. Such vendor-reported results do not establish installation-load prediction accuracy or installation acceptance limits.

Interpretation: a percentile motion threshold is a statistical measure, not an equipment allowable. Without the underlying records, threshold definitions and window-counting method, neither a reported availability percentage nor a threshold will be transferred to a jumper operation.

Published operating conditions for such services typically restrict wave height, period range, wind, vessel speed and radar line of sight. Product version, predicted quantity, usable horizon, operating domain and availability would need confirmation with any provider. Published validation claims will not be treated as certification of this installation workflow.

3. Proposed work in digitalmodel

  1. Complete and qualify the demand library. The master/change-file architecture will be retained. Results will include both ends of every crane wire and sling, whole-component demand envelopes, jumper/connector stress and bending, clearance and phase-specific criteria. Negative signed tension will remain a model diagnostic; physical slack and re-tension effects will require an appropriate line model and convergence assessment.
  2. Define tasks and recovery paths. Lift-off, splash-zone passage, suspended lowering, landing, connector engagement and recovery will be treated as distinct states. Each task definition will record required duration, preparation time, committed/non-interruptible stages, abort feasibility and time to the procedure-defined recovery condition. A one-minute quiet interval will not qualify a longer non-interruptible task.
  3. Obtain phase-resolved observations. A radar/provider adapter will be investigated before attempting a new wave-sensing algorithm. The data contract will record observation and issue timestamps, forecast valid times, reference position, coordinate conventions, units, direction convention, provider version, quality flags and uncertainty. Hs/Tp alone will not reconstruct individual future waves. A history-only autoregression will remain a baseline.
  4. Map waves and motions to installation loads. Vessel response will be translated to the actual crane-tip location using vessel geometry, heading, loading and validated RAOs or measured motion predictions. AHC, payout, crane radius, load depth, line stiffness, hydrodynamics and current will be represented where applicable. Stateful nonlinear slack/re-tension response will not be replaced by an unvalidated linear scaling of wave elevation.
  5. Build a task-window evaluator. All applicable load, motion, acceleration, clearance and minimum-tension criteria will be checked jointly. The complete candidate interval, including justified uncertainty and timing margins, will need coverage. First crossing time, governing component, remaining window duration and exclusion reason will be retained. Forecast availability outside the provider's domain will be marked invalid even where the mechanical analysis has results.
  6. Expose degraded states. Stale data, missing limits, inadequate forecast coverage, failed channels, configuration mismatch and out-of-domain states will suppress any start recommendation. If a task is underway, the display will call for its pre-agreed contingency procedure rather than issue a generic stop command that could itself be unsuitable.

Timing rule to develop: the usable horizon will be calculated from the latest observation/forecast valid times and measured end-to-end latency. It will need to cover the task's remaining required execution and contingency time with a project-approved margin. A nominal 120-second forecast does not establish 120 seconds of available action time.

Coverage implication: any provider's published operating restrictions will be evaluated independently of the existing Hs–Tp grid. Low-wave and edge-period demand cases will remain in the engineering library; they will not imply that a radar forecast is valid there. Provider restrictions will be versioned and confirmed, not installed as universal design constants.

5. Validation and report additions

  • First diagnostic run: a reviewed task definition and sourced criterion set will be applied retrospectively to one completed case. Contiguous jointly below-limit intervals and the fraction supporting the required task duration will be calculated. This will be labelled hindsight/oracle availability, not predictive performance. If criteria remain unresolved, only explicitly illustrative sensitivity results will be produced.
  • Causal replay: predictions will be generated at fixed issue times using only then-available observations. Future truth will be withheld. Calibration and evaluation will be separated by independent runs or operations; heavily overlapping forecast windows will not be counted as independent evidence.
  • Forecast metrics: wave, crane-tip motion and each critical load will receive horizon-dependent errors, bias, interval coverage and peak/phase timing errors. Persistence and history-mean baselines will remain visible. A prescribed-future-wave case will measure conditional model potential separately.
  • Decision metrics: missed exceedances, false-start indications, false alarms, warning lead time, eligible-window precision and task-completion opportunity will be reported with denominators and uncertainty. Window definitions will distinguish occupancy time, number of intervals and valid task start times, including edge censoring.
  • Forecast availability: the denominator will include all requested operating periods, including missing, stale and out-of-domain intervals. Conditional performance during valid forecasts will be reported separately; unavailable support will never count as a predicted eligible window.
  • Failure tests: stale packets, time offsets, unit/frame mismatch, changed vessel configuration, radar blind sectors, missing limits and shortened horizons will be exercised. A numerically accurate forecast with insufficient action time will not be classified as an actionable task window.
  • Field progression: authorised recorded-data replay will precede a non-controlling offshore observation trial. Prospective evidence and responsible engineering acceptance will precede any operational claim.

The engineering report will retain placeholders for forecast-validity maps, latency budgets, time-aligned measurements, jointly constrained task windows, reliability by horizon, failure-state demonstrations and provider qualification. One display will show history, NOW, the next 120 seconds, uncertainty, applicable limits and task-duration coverage; unavailable predictions will be displayed explicitly.

6. Ownership and data boundaries

Digitalmodel will own reusable adapters, analysis and validation code. Private digitalmodel-data will own reusable simulation/replay results and execution evidence. Original measured/client records will remain with their authorised private owner, referenced by stable identity, source, version, digest, units and intended-use readiness. No measured data transfer or external integration is authorised by this note.

Next implementation checkpoint: a bounded replay/task-window plan will define the task, sourced limits, data contract, latency budget and acceptance metrics. The first result will compare retrospective available windows with genuinely causal predicted windows. Numeric acceptance thresholds will remain unassigned until the applicable engineering basis is established.
