River towing

A 100.0 m wide, 3.0 m deep section with 600.0 m³/s discharge loads a generic rectangular barge: length 40.0 m, beam 10.0 m, draft 2.0 m. Section mean is Q/(WH); surface speed is mean*(m+1)/m, then multiplied by the mid-channel and section blockage factors. The current is from the bow; vessel speed is zero.

Signed quadratic velocity is integrated over height H-T to H by midpoint slices. The slot multiplier is [H/(H-T)]². Existing approximate load coefficients are reused. Nominal pull demand is hypot(Fx,Fy)*reserve/efficiency, with reserve 1.2 and efficiency 0.8. Yaw demand is compared separately with the stated 0.0 N m capacity. More nominal pull or reduced current is needed for the force criterion; actual towing requires a calibrated resistance and thrust assessment.

Assumptions and limits

  • The lateral factor 1-r+6*r*x*(1-x) has unit section average; bank retardation r is an assumed shape parameter, not a calibration.
  • The bend bias 1+a*(2*x-1) is a separate assumed outer-bank increase. Superelevation follows constant-speed radial equilibrium; it does not determine the velocity bias.
  • Channel blockage uses continuity with area width*depth minus beam*draft; rectangular section and cross-section are assumed.
  • The shallow drag multiplier (depth/(depth-draft)) squared is a full-width under-keel slot screening bound derived from continuity and quadratic drag, not a validated barge coefficient. Side bypass is ignored. Do not also use this slot model as a channel blockage correction.
  • The finite-width channel correction accounts for section-average acceleration; the slot multiplier is a separate local under-keel hypothesis. Their combined use in towing is an explicit stress scenario, not an empirical joint calibration.
  • Return levels use least-squares probability plots at (rank-0.5)/count; Weibull samples must be positive and independent, with the sampling rate specified. Gumbel input is one maximum per year.
  • Only steady current loading is considered. Tug self-resistance, wind, waves, debris, interaction forces, towline geometry and propulsion loss at speed are excluded; no operational approval follows from the synthetic decision.

Tidal reversal, unsteady river hydraulics and calibrated operational acceptance remain outside this analysis (#2000, #2002).
