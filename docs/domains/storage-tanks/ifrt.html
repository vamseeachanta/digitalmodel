Internal floating roof tank losses and vent comparison

The routine-loss calculator estimates annual evaporation from an afloat internal floating deck. The separate normal vent comparison checks entered duties against rated flow and pressure limits. Neither result establishes API compliance, structural adequacy or permission to operate.

Entry point: digitalmodel.code_checks.storage_tanks.ifrt. The runnable example prints inputs, component losses, seal comparisons and citation sidecars as JSON:

PYTHONPATH=src uv run python examples/code_checks/ifrt_example.py

Method and units

Basis: EPA AP-42 Chapter 7.1, June 2026, section 7.1.3.2, equations 2-1/2/3/4/13/14/15/18/19. API MPMS Chapter 19.2, third edition (2012), is the industry reference identified by EPA; no paid-standard table is reproduced. The EPA method is implemented from the public publication. A selected subset of table factors is supplied; other configurations require values with explicit units and resolvable citations.

Geometry is entered in metres; true vapor and atmospheric pressures are absolute kPa; vapor molecular weight is kg/kmol; liquid density is kg/m³; annual net withdrawal is m³/year. The shared Pint registry converts to the EPA native equation units. Results are kg/year. True vapor pressure is required at the average liquid surface condition; Reid vapor pressure is not substituted.

  • Rim loss uses the selected seal factor at zero wind speed. Selected seal factors assume a welded tank shell, independent of the floating deck's bolted or welded construction. Tight-fit factors require an attestation that gaps remain no greater than 1/8 inch through the complete operating level range (Table 7.1-8, footnote d).
  • Fitting loss sums actual counts times configuration-specific zero-wind factors (Table 7.1-12). Column wells, guidepoles, hatches, vacuum breakers and leg penetrations remain separate inventory items.
  • Welded deck seam loss is zero under equation 2-18. Bolted deck loss uses measured seam length, or the public default seam density of 0.20 ft/ft² when length is unknown. Gasketing or adhesive does not qualify a bolted seam as welded.
  • Withdrawal loss uses liquid clingage and net downward liquid movement. Fixed-roof support-column perimeters enter the column correction. Floating-deck support legs do not enter that correction.

Synthetic gasoline example

The assumed tank is 30 m diameter by 15 m high (gross cylindrical capacity 10,602.875 m³), with six round 0.3 m fixed-roof columns and twenty adjustable short-sleeve IFR legs. The inventory includes one bolted gasketed access hatch, one gasketed gauge hatch, one gasketed vacuum breaker, one unslotted guidepole with gasketed cover and sleeve, and six fabric-sleeve column wells. All counts are assumed, rather than taken from typical-count tables.

The assumed gasoline properties are 35 kPa true vapor pressure, 101.325 kPa atmospheric pressure, 62 kg/kmol vapor molecular weight and 740 kg/m³ liquid density. Net throughput is 100,000 m³/year. The shell is assumed to have light rust. The baseline is an average-fit vapor-mounted primary seal and a bolted deck with unknown seam length. These inputs describe an illustration, not measured plant data.

API 2000 normal vent capacity comparison

NormalVentingBasis requires a cited edition/design basis, transfer factors in Nm³ air per m³ liquid, and independently established thermal duties in Nm³ air/hour. No thermal duty, transfer factor, latitude band, insulation credit or paid table is selected automatically. The public API 2000 seventh-edition scope and contents identify the normal/emergency venting scope; the reference does not establish the current governing edition. The caller must establish the applicable edition and tank-specific method.

Inbreathing duty equals withdrawal times the entered inbreathing factor plus thermal inbreathing; outbreathing duty equals filling times the entered outbreathing factor plus thermal outbreathing. All capacities and duties must use air at 0 °C and 101.3 kPa absolute. Actual m³/h or SCFH cannot be passed as Nm³/h. Relieving differential plus connected losses is compared to each tank limit; set pressure alone is insufficient.

The independent vent illustration assumes 500 m³/h fill, 750 m³/h withdrawal, transfer factors 1 and 2, and thermal duties of 1,000 Nm³/h in each direction. Required capacities are 1,750 Nm³/h inward and 2,000 Nm³/h outward. Assumed capacities of 2,000 and 2,200 Nm³/h give margins of 250 and 200 Nm³/h. Pressure duty is 1.5 + 0.2 = 1.7 kPa versus an assumed 2.0 kPag limit; vacuum duty is 0.3 + 0.1 = 0.4 kPa versus an assumed 0.5 kPa magnitude limit.

These vent duties and curve points are synthetic arithmetic inputs. They are not API 2000 table values, thermal-duty calculations or certified vent ratings. The result retains COMPARISON_ONLY even when the entered normal checks pass. A design vent selection remains unestablished.

Support legs, landed roofs and limits

Adjustable support-leg penetrations contribute fitting losses; fixed legs or hangers without deck openings have a zero fitting factor in the selected EPA table. Leg load capacity, buckling, support pads, bottom plate loads, buoyancy, roof stability, guide clearances, electrical continuity and API 650 Annex H compliance require separate design checks.

The routine calculator rejects a landed roof. Landing on legs exposes an under-deck vapor space; standing-idle and refilling emissions require AP-42 section 7.1.3.3 and actual heel/drain-dry conditions, landing duration and landing frequency. Those emissions are not included in the routine annual total.

The routine method also rejects P/V-only fixed-roof ventilation, unstable/boiling stock and deteriorated or significantly permeated seals. A passing independent P/V capacity comparison does not remove those restrictions. API 650 open-vent circulation, blanketing, emergency fire relief, flashing, gas breakout and flame-arrester selection remain outside this calculation. No pressure protection or emission-control credit is inferred from a floating deck.
