Well intervention screening

digitalmodel.well.intervention connects problem types to intervention options, compares supplied decline fits with measured oil rates, and bounds constant-uplift payback including deferred production. Outputs support candidate investigation. They do not approve a treatment or establish well integrity.

Method and access

method_options(Problem.SAND, subsea=True) returns slickline bailing, qualified powered e-line cleaning, and coiled-tubing circulation options. Each option contains its purpose, diagnostic status, separate access classes and public sources. Tubing failure maps to HWU or rig workover. A valve is assumed tubing-retrievable unless wireline_retrievable_valve=True is supplied. Declining rate and integrity include diagnostics; no uplift is assigned to logging by default.

Subsea wireline options include riserless light well intervention vessels, riser-based intervention and rigs. CT requires riser-based or rig access in this generic screen. Special open-water CT and tubing-pull systems require separate capability checks. Sources: 2003 technical study, method and access sections and RPSEA 08121-1502 Task 2 (2010), Tables 5–6.

Public reference ranges

reference_estimate(method) provides the following sensitivity inputs. Duration bounds span different published tasks or cases. Unit day rates describe the cited supplier's 2026 US onshore reference. Their product is a derived unit-hire sensitivity envelope; it is not an observed total-job cost range. Typical duration for a particular scope is not established by these cross-case bounds.

Reference inputs only. The supplier groups slickline and wireline rates; no independent e-line rate is claimed. Rig rate refers to Eagle Ford heavy mast, while the duration evidence refers to subsea tasks. These contexts are not interchangeable.

The rate source is a supplier's budget guidance, not an independently validated market dataset. Source links and exclusions travel with each returned estimate; no currency conversion or escalation is applied. The earlier duration-comparison endpoints could not be independently verified and are no longer returned. Slickline and HWU calls without both duration_days=Band(low_days, high_days) and a nonblank duration_basis raise an error. Other methods accept the same override. Returned duration_basis and duration_readiness distinguish caller_supplied_scenario from public_reference_only. Caller-supplied durations are not independently verified; their source links contain only the retained rate citation. Neither status establishes an actual job estimate or suitability for economics without qualification.

ReferenceEstimate.unit_hire_sensitivity_usd is a USD band formed from duration [day] times unit-hire rate [USD/day] at the supplied bounds. It is not a derivative or a total-job budget. The former reference field cost_usd is removed; callers must use the new field name. The separate screen_economics(cost_usd=...) input still requires a qualified total-job cost band.

Subsea total cost and duration bands require explicit project inputs. A transferable primary-source cost range for each subsea access class has not been established. Calling reference_estimate for RLWI, riser-based intervention or a subsea rig raises an error. Supply reviewed total-job ranges directly to screen_economics. Onshore unit-hire bands exclude mobilization, offshore spread, support, services, materials, weather and contingency, and shall not be used as Gulf of Mexico job budgets.

Decline and economics inputs

DeclineFit(qi_bopd, di_per_day, b, start_date, basis) accepts supplied Arps parameters. RatePoint(date, rate_bopd, source) accepts measured history. Quantity rates and decline constants use the shared Pint registry; bare values are bbl/day and nominal 1/day. The supported classical Arps range is 0 ≤ b ≤ 1. Exponential, hyperbolic and harmonic evaluation follows Texas A&M PETE 613 Lecture 8 (2023), Appendix B.

candidate_screen requires a shortfall threshold, consecutive-point count, assessment date, maximum data age and maximum sample gap. Every point in the qualifying tail must be positive and strictly below the threshold-adjusted fit. Unsorted, duplicate, future and pre-fit dates are rejected. Empty, stale, gapped, insufficient and zero-rate histories are distinguished from positive candidate evidence. Use one well/completion and fluid stream with a consistent producing-day or calendar-day rate basis. A rate below fit does not distinguish depletion, choke changes, water breakthrough, allocation errors or removable damage.

uplift_from_shortfall(candidate, recovery_fraction) converts a positive fit-to-measurement gap to the supplied recovered-share band. Recovery fractions are required scenario inputs in [0,1], not success probabilities or treatment coefficients. No recovered fraction is supplied by this module. Alternatively supply incremental oil directly as below. Every variable in this snippet must be supplied with a qualified basis; no numerical default is implied.

from digitalmodel.well.intervention import screen_economics

result = screen_economics(
    baseline_bopd=measured_oil_rate,
    uplift_bopd=incremental_oil_band,
    cost_usd=total_job_cost_band,
    duration_days=full_outage_days_band,
    net_price_usd_per_bbl=net_realized_margin,
    horizon_days=elapsed_days_from_job_start,
    basis=scenario_basis,
)
print(result.payback_from_start_days)

The oil balance is u·max(H−d,0) − q₀·min(d,H), relative to continued baseline production. Deferred oil is q₀·d; restart payback is (C + p·q₀·d)/(p·u). Elapsed payback adds d. Cost, duration and uplift endpoints are enumerated to bound the scenarios. These are explicitly defined accounting identities using the DOE Simple Photovoltaic Economic Calculations (31 March 2010), item 1. No discount, uplift decline, success probability or later recovery of deferred oil is assumed. A None payback bound denotes no finite recovery under that constant-uplift case. Finite extrapolated payback beyond the horizon is reported, but pays_out_in_all_cases is false. Oil and net-cash pairs are ordered (minimum, maximum) and can be negative; input Band limits cannot.

Wireline conveyance hook

For deviated or horizontal slickline/e-line options, supply method_options(..., wireline_inputs=conveyance_inputs), or call wireline_conveyance directly, with measured drift, casing ID, tool OD, required clearance, pump rate, fluid density, buoyed weights, lateral length, inclination, friction, eccentricity and restriction coefficient. Physical inputs accept Pint quantities or bare SI; bare inclination is in degrees from vertical, and angle quantities are converted to degrees before the 0–180 degree check. The adapter calls the existing completion.pump_down clearance, resistance and solved-speed functions. It returns a clearance failure, stall, pump-down screen pass, or a gravity-assisted branch needing review. A positive speed does not establish retrieval, tension, weak-point, pressure, trajectory or barrier qualification. No default pump-down coefficients are introduced. Supplied wireline inputs are evaluated once and the result is attached to each line option; failed options remain visible with their status. Without these inputs, option.conveyance is None means not evaluated. method_options alone does not establish access.

Runnable example and hand-checked tests use BSEE OGOR-A Report 4081 (2023), issued 3 March 2026, pages 1–4: API 177194065600, completion D02, E018D, lease 00016, WD030. The runnable example requires --scenario scenario.json and has no default scenario. The JSON object must contain observation_dates (four ISO dates in the four source months), as_of (ISO date), shortfall_fraction, consecutive_points, max_age_days, max_gap_days, uplift_bopd, cost_usd, duration_days, net_price_usd_per_bbl, horizon_days and basis. Bands are two-element arrays. The basis must explain the representative dates and screening/economic assumptions. The exponential fit uses the first and third reported rates and elapsed days between supplied dates; it does not impose a midpoint date. Output carries scenario_readiness="caller_supplied_scenario" and the supplied basis. The CLI and Python function both reject missing required inputs.

PYTHONPATH=src python examples/workflows/well-intervention-screening/example.py --scenario scenario.json

The synthetic regression case explicitly supplies monthly day-16 dates, an assessment date of 30 April 2023, a 20.0% threshold, one qualifying point, a 31-day maximum age and a 40-day maximum gap. These are test assumptions, not source observations or recommended screening criteria. Under those inputs the fitted April rate is 53.9 bbl/day against 20.5 bbl/day measured, a 62.0% shortfall. Monthly representative dates and April's two producing days limit representativeness. The same report (pages 5–11) shows 0 bbl and 0 producing days from May to December 2023; zero-day months are not rate observations and are excluded from history.

The same synthetic test explicitly supplies uplift of 10–20 bbl/day, cost of USD20,000–40,000, outage of 2–4 days, net margin of USD50/bbl and a 180-day horizon. Conditional hand calculations give 41–82 bbl deferred oil, 24.1–92.2 days elapsed payback and USD43,900–155,950 net cash over 180 days. These values are regression evidence only; the runnable example requires the caller to supply all scenario values. The BSEE records do not establish any intervention, treatment outcome, cost, uplift or removable production loss. A candidate flag therefore does not establish that the actual well will pay out.

Integration limits and follow-ups

The external decline pipeline can pass qi, nominal Di, b, fit start and measured rate history directly; no sibling repository is read. Project-specific subsea spread ranges, historical analog uplift, success probability and decline-dependent cash-flow forecasting remain follow-ups. Explorer and website integration belong in their owning repositories. The unit day-rate source is unreviewed supplier guidance; replacing it with a stronger public cost basis is a follow-up.
