Subsea jumper calculation tools

Summary

The package digitalmodel.subsea.jumper_tools calculates annular section properties, a prescribed-tension flexible catenary, and line-mesh geometry. Inputs use SI units. Material data, gravity and geometry are supplied explicitly. The tools support preliminary calculations; neither spreadsheet parity nor structural design acceptance is established.

Scope and evidence

Three calculation slices address the section, catenary and geometry portions of issue 1645. The modules use the importable src/digitalmodel/subsea/jumper_tools/ location rather than a hyphenated source directory. Existing installation calculations and the catenary_riser engineering basis were inspected; existing APIs are unchanged. The new scalar interface avoids their material libraries, fixed gravity assumptions and solver dependencies.

The three original workbooks are absent from the inspected checkout. Their formulas have therefore not been extracted or compared. The formula extractor is tested on synthetic workbooks only. Workbook formula mapping, configuration optimization and workbook-specific FE conventions remain follow-up work under the same issue. These limitations prevent a claim of complete spreadsheet replacement.

Calculation basis

S1. Concentric circular section

Let D denote pipe outside diameter, t wall thickness, c radial coating thickness, d = D − 2t bore diameter and Dc = D + 2c coated diameter. The domain is D > 0, 0 < t < D/2 and c ≥ 0.

A_pipe = π(D² − d²)/4 = πt(D − t)
A_bore = πd²/4
A_coat = π(D_c² − D²)/4 = πc(D + c)
A_displaced = πD_c²/4
I = π(D⁴ − d⁴)/64
Z = I/(D/2)
EA = E A_pipe
EI = E I

Engineering Statics: Open and Interactive (first released 2020), section 10.2.4 derives the disk polar inertia from dA = 2πr dr. Integration between d/2 and D/2 gives A = ∫2πr dr and J = ∫2πr³ dr. Circular symmetry gives I = J/2. The factors 4 and 64 follow from integration and diameter-to-radius conversion. Z is the definition of elastic section modulus. EA and EI are stiffness products for the homogeneous linear elastic pipe; coating contributes weight and displacement only. No empirical coefficient or standard-derived material constant is introduced.

S2. Mass and fully submerged weight

m_dry = ρ_pipe A_pipe + ρ_coat A_coat
m_filled = m_dry + ρ_contents A_bore
w_submerged = g (m_filled − ρ_water A_displaced)

Masses are in kg/m, densities in kg/m³, gravity in m/s² and w in N/m. Positive w acts downward. Negative w is retained, rather than clipped. University Physics Volume 1 (2016), section 14.4 gives buoyancy as displaced-fluid weight. This model assumes a sealed external envelope, full bore contents, concentric nonporous coating, and complete submergence. Flooded/open-ended, partially filled and partially immersed configurations require a different displacement balance.

C1. Flexible catenary

H is prescribed horizontal tension, w uniform downward submerged weight, h top elevation above the flat seabed and L total available length. H, w and L are positive; h is nonnegative. No endpoint span is prescribed independently.

a = H/w
z = a (cosh(x/a) − 1)
s = a sinh(x/a) = sqrt(h(h + 2a))
x = a asinh(s/a)
V_top = w s
T_top = H + w h
θ_horizontal = atan2(V_top, H)
θ_vertical = atan2(H, V_top)
L_grounded = L − s ≥ 0
touchdown = (L_grounded, 0, 0)
top = (L_grounded + x, 0, h)

Catenary Curve (2019), equations (1), (3), (4), (9), (10) and (14) establishes force balance, arc length and tension. Its y origin is translated downward by a to place touchdown at z = 0. The hyperbolic identity cosh²u − sinh²u = 1 gives s² = h(h + 2a). Combining it with T² = H² + (ws)² gives T = H + wh. The implementation uses inverse sinh to preserve small h/a and rejects length infeasibility without silently truncating suspended length.

This is a static, inextensible, perfectly flexible line under uniform vertical load. It excludes bending stiffness, current, axial extension, soil resistance, friction, buoyancy transitions and dynamic effects. It cannot establish a rigid jumper equilibrium or a bending acceptance result. The grounded portion is straight and frictionless; H must be sustained at the first endpoint.

F1. Geometry discretization

For a straight segment from P to Q, local coordinate τ ∈ [0,1] gives P(τ) = (1 − τ)P + τQ. These coefficients follow by imposing P(0) = P and P(1) = Q on a linear polynomial; no empirical number is used. A requested integer count n places nodes at τ = i/n. Shared corners are retained once.

The catenary mesh samples equal arc increments si = is/n on the suspended portion only. C1 gives xi = a asinh(si/a) and zi = sqrt(a² + si²) − a. A rationalized height expression avoids cancellation. Node coordinates are in the x-z plane, with z upward. Connectivity is one-based. Returned element lengths are straight chord lengths, which are shorter than their curved reference arcs and are not unstretched arc lengths. The geometry does not define FE materials, element type, loads, boundary conditions, mesh convergence or bend fabrication radii. Polyline corners remain sharp.

The example prints its inputs, results and explicit NOT_EVALUATED statuses. No solver is imported and no file is written. Its unrounded JSON values support calculation reuse; the exact expressions above provide the hand-calculation basis.

Workbook extraction and remaining qualification

from digitalmodel.subsea.jumper_tools.workbook_formulas import extract_formulas
extraction = extract_formulas("supplied.xlsx")
print(extraction.source_sha256)
for record in extraction.formulas:
    print(record.sheet, record.cell, record.formula, record.cached_value)

The optional openpyxl dependency reads formula and cached-value views from one immutable input snapshot. Workbook bytes are hashed and never modified. All worksheets, including hidden sheets, are inspected. Only ordinary text formulas are supported; unsupported formula objects raise an error. Macros, external links and Excel formulas are not executed. Missing or stale cached values are not promoted to validated engineering results. Extraction records can contain private sheet names or formula text and require a separate publication review.

Spreadsheet comparison and design-code checks are NOT_EVALUATED. Qualification requires the source workbooks, formula-to-API mapping, units and assumptions, and at least two independently reviewed reference cases for each calculator. Configuration optimization and workbook-specific FE conventions require those sources before their equivalence can be established. No licensed publication values or acceptance factors are reproduced.
