#2020 implementation plan: make Holtrop-Mennen hull-form sensitivity provable

Issue #2020 · branch plan/2020-holtrop-mennen-hull-form-sensitivity · base 2a6b521e · drafted 2026-08-13 · status: awaiting adversarial review, then user approval

Goal and authorization boundary

Holtrop-Mennen will be pinned to an oracle that lives outside this repository, and the pin will be enforced by a test that runs. Today the module's only numeric checks are bands wide enough to admit almost any answer, and its declared test vectors are un-sourced numbers that no test reads.

  • G1 — The module's output will be validated against a published Holtrop-Mennen worked example with printed intermediate values, so that each coefficient function has its own oracle rather than only the total having one.
  • G2 — Every coefficient that departs from the published 1982/1984 definitions will be corrected, each behind a test that fails first.
  • G3 — tests/fixtures/test_vectors/naval_architecture/holtrop_mennen.yaml will be loaded by a parametrized test that asserts every row marked use_as_test: true, so that flag stops being decorative.
  • G4 — Every expected value in that fixture will carry a citation naming where it came from, or it will not remain in the fixture.
  • G5 — A hull-form sensitivity test will assert the direction and ordering of CT across hull forms, so a future regression that flattens hull dependence fails loudly even if every band still passes.
  • G6 — The published artifact docs/api/structural/ship-resistance-explorer.html, which is generated from this engine, will be regenerated so the public surface and the engine agree.

Boundary. This plan will change the resistance engine, its tests, its fixture and the artifact generated from it. It will not touch the CFD work in #1173, will not extend the method beyond the Fn < 0.4 regime it implements today, and will not add propulsion or wake-fraction estimation. It will not self-apply status:plan-approved.

Finding 1 — the collapse hypothesis is refuted. Every term discriminates. S differs by a factor of 3.95, iE by 5.15, c1 by 3.49, 1+k1 by 1.16. No term is form-insensitive. The near-identity in CT is a three-way cancellation at these two specific operating points: moving from the Series 60 to the tanker, CF(1+k1) rises 8.8% while CW falls 6.4% and CA falls 26.2%, and the component weights make the sum agree to 4 parts in 105. The issue's candidate mechanism — "RT and S scale together so the ratio cancels" — is confirmed as an observation (RT ratio 3.9515 against S ratio 3.9514) but refuted as a cause: that equality is the restatement of CT equality, not its explanation. The two other candidate mechanisms are also refuted: no form-dependent term is computed-but-unapplied (all six components are applied, and all six discriminate), and there is no non-dimensionalisation error (CF was re-derived from ITTC-1957 independently at both Reynolds numbers and matches). The m1 near-equality is a genuine second coincidence, unrelated to the first, and it does not propagate because c1 differs by 3.49×.

Finding 2 — one fixture value is below the floor the method can produce

CT under Holtrop-Mennen is bounded below by CF(1+k1) + CA, because CW, RB, RTR and RAPP are all non-negative. Evaluating that floor with the corrected c12 (see C-1):

The tanker's ct_approx = 0.00175 cannot be produced by Holtrop-Mennen for that hull at that speed, by any correction to this code. It sits 24% below the method's own floor of 2.2966e-3, and 18% below the bare ITTC-1957 CF multiplied by a form factor of 1.18 — the low end of the fixture's own declared form-factor band. Reaching 0.00175 would require CW < 0, or (1+k1) < 1.18 together with CA < 0. The number is not a tolerance miss. It is outside the method's range.

Finding 3 — the other fixture value has a clean model-scale interpretation

Solving for the Reynolds number at which the module's own CW and (1+k1) would give CT = 0.00385 with CA = 0 — the model-scale convention, where no correlation allowance is applied — yields:

quantityvalue
required CF3.0710e-03
implied Rn (ITTC-1957 inverted)8.746e+06
implied geometric scale λ (Froude-similar, Rn ∝ λ-3/2)20.2
implied model length6.05 m

6.05 m is an unremarkable towing-tank model length for a 122 m ship. The Series 60 value is therefore consistent with a model-scale CT recorded against a full-scale input row. The same inversion applied to the tanker requires CF = 7.62e-04, which ITTC-1957 reaches only beyond Rn ~ 1012; there is no physical scale, model or full, at which 0.00175 is that hull's CT.

Read together, Findings 2 and 3 say the fixture is not a usable oracle. One row is plausibly a model-scale number mislabelled as full-scale; the other is outside the method's range at any scale. The fixture's provenance block names the two Holtrop papers as source_type: journal_paper, but neither paper tabulates these two hulls, and the repository contains no derivation for either number. The fixture is best explained as plausible-looking values authored alongside the module rather than extracted from a source — which is exactly the failure mode use_as_test: true was supposed to prevent, and could not, because nothing read the flag.

Correcting every active coefficient defect does not close the gap to the fixture. Measured end to end: with C-1 and C-2 both fixed, series60 moves from −31.5% to −30.9% and the tanker from +50.6% to +51.8%. The two effects are worth roughly 1–3% of CT, not 30–50%. Anyone who plans a fix by chasing the percentage errors will keep finding defects that do not explain them. This is the plan's central reason for putting the oracle before the fix.

C-2 is provable without any oracle

The half-angle-of-entrance defect does not need a worked example to establish, because it breaks a property the correct formula must have. iE is an angle: two geometrically similar hulls of different absolute size must return the same value. Scaling the tanker geometry and evaluating both forms:

geometric scaleL (m)iE as implementediE published form
×0.5102.537.925538.2722
×1.0205.041.564738.2722

The published form is invariant to all printed digits across a 4:1 span. The implemented form drifts by 19% over the same span, because 100·Cb/L has dimensions of 1/m where 100·∇/L³ is dimensionless. This is the strongest single result in the inventory: it needs no source, no fixture and no tolerance, and it is the basis for test_half_angle_of_entrance_is_scale_invariant.

Implementation sequence after approval

  1. Stage 1 — obtain the external oracle. No code changes. The Holtrop & Mennen 1982 paper prints a worked example that tabulates intermediate values for every coefficient, which is what makes it a per-function oracle rather than only an end-to-end one. That example will be transcribed into the fixture as a new row carrying its own citation. This stage also settles C-7 and the form_factor_validation disagreement against the paper rather than against a secondary source. If the paper's worked example cannot be obtained, the fallback is an independent third-party implementation with published results, cited as such and labelled as a weaker oracle — and that degradation will be recorded on the issue, not absorbed silently.
  2. Stage 2 — RED. Author the failing tests. The fixture loader, the worked-example test, and the sensitivity tests, committed failing and pushed so the red state is on the record before any fix exists. The pull request will be opened at this stage; pushing to the branch alone runs no workflow and produces no evidence.
  3. Stage 3 — GREEN. Correct the coefficients. One commit per defect, each referencing its equation. C-1 and C-2 first, since they are active; C-3 through C-6 next; C-7 only if Stage 1 confirmed it; C-8's guard last. After each, the worked-example test moves closer to green, and the per-coefficient intermediate values are checked individually — a total that matches while an intermediate does not indicates two compensating errors, which is precisely the failure mode this issue is about.
  4. Stage 4 — re-source the fixture. With the engine pinned to the published example, the two synthetic rows are dispositioned: given a derivation and an explicit scale convention if one can be established, or replaced with rows that have sources. Tolerances will be set from the method's own stated accuracy, not chosen to make the current code pass.
  5. Stage 5 — regenerate the published artifact, in its own commit, with the CT delta against the current published values recorded on the issue. Confirm workflow_calculations.py needs no change.
  6. Stage 6 — closeout. Comment on #2020 with the before/after component table; comment on #1173 stating whether the CW cross-check is now usable; disposition #1682.

Stages 2 and 3 are the fix. Stage 1 exists because without it Stage 3 has nothing to aim at, and Stage 4 exists because the fixture cannot be both the target and the thing being corrected.

TDD test list

Assertions will be on relations and sourced values, never on a band wide enough to admit the defect being fixed. The existing bands are the cautionary example: 0.002 < ct < 0.005 and 0.001 < ct < 0.003 overlap, so they jointly permit two radically different hulls to return the same number — which is the reported defect, expressed as a passing test.

  • test_published_worked_example_total_resistance — the module reproduces the paper's RTOTAL within its stated tolerance. The end-to-end pin.
  • test_published_worked_example_intermediates — parametrized over every intermediate the paper prints (1+k1, S, c1…c16, iE, m1, m4, λ, and each resistance component). This is the test that would have caught C-1 and C-2 on the day they were written, and the one that distinguishes a correct total from two compensating errors.
  • test_fixture_vectors — parametrized over every row with use_as_test: true, asserting ct_approx within ct_tolerance_pct. With three vacuity guards, because a fixture-driven test that silently iterates nothing is the exact failure this issue is about: assert the YAML resolved and parsed; assert the collected parameter count is non-zero and equals the number of use_as_test: true rows in the file; and assert every row consumed carries a source field.
  • test_every_use_as_test_row_is_consumed — a separate guard asserting that no row can be marked use_as_test: true and go unread, and that no row carries an expected-value key no test asserts. The current k1_plus_1_min/k1_plus_1_max pair is read by nothing and must not be able to return to that state.
  • test_fuller_hull_has_higher_form_factor — (1+k1) strictly increases across a monotone CP sweep at fixed L/B/T. Fails on any regression that flattens form dependence.
  • test_ct_ordering_across_hull_forms — the test this issue exists for. Across a set of hulls spanning Cb and length, CT values must be separated by more than a stated minimum spread, and must order as expected. Today's engine returns two values agreeing to 4 parts in 105; this test fails on that, and would have failed on it from the beginning.
  • test_ct_exceeds_method_floor — for every fixture row, CT ≥ CF(1+k1) + CA. A cheap invariant, independent of any source, that would have rejected the tanker's ct_approx the moment it was written.
  • test_half_angle_of_entrance_is_scale_invariant — geometrically similar hulls at different absolute sizes must return the same iE. Fails on C-2 today, and pins the dimensional property rather than one hull's number.
  • test_c12_matches_published_piecewise, test_c15_matches_published_piecewise — each branch exercised, including the boundary values that today's code routes to the wrong branch.
  • test_wave_resistance_rejects_out_of_range_froude — Fn > 0.4 raises rather than silently returning an extrapolated value (C-8).
  • Component bands from the existing file — retained, tightened where the oracle allows, and explicitly documented as necessary-not-sufficient.

Acceptance criteria

  1. The module reproduces a published worked example end to end, and each intermediate it prints, each within a stated tolerance — with the source cited in the fixture and in the test.
  2. Every fixture row marked use_as_test: true is asserted by a collected test, proven by the collected parameter count equalling the row count. A row added to the fixture and marked use_as_test: true without a corresponding assertion causes a failure.
  3. Every expected value in the fixture carries a source and an explicit scale convention. No un-sourced number remains.
  4. CT across the hull-form set is separated by more than the stated minimum spread and orders correctly — stated as a relation, not as literal values, so it does not need rewriting when the numbers legitimately move.
  5. Every defect C-1 through C-8 is either corrected behind a test that failed first, or explicitly recorded as not-a-defect with the paper's equation cited against it. C-7 must resolve to one of these two, not remain "suspected".
  6. The published explorer artifact is regenerated, and the CT delta against the previously published values is recorded on the issue so anyone who consumed the old page can see what changed.
  7. The full tests/naval_architecture/ suite is green, and any newly-red test elsewhere is recorded as a pre-existing finding with its own issue rather than repaired in this branch.
  8. A comment on #1173 states plainly whether the CW cross-check is now usable, so that plan's recorded dependency decision can be revisited on evidence.
  9. #1682 is dispositioned.

Adversarial review section

Tier: T2 — multi-file, engineering-numeric, and touching a published artifact, but a bounded change to one module and its tests. Two providers.

Reviewers should attack, specifically:

  • Finding 2, hardest. Is CT ≥ CF(1+k1) + CA actually a floor under Holtrop-Mennen, or is there a regime where CA goes negative and the argument collapses? This plan checked the obvious escape and found it closed: with the third term zero (as it is for both fixture hulls, since T/L > 0.04), CA = 0.006(L+100)−0.16 − 0.00205 crosses zero at L = 722 m, so CA is strictly positive for any real ship and for the 205 m tanker it is +3.52e-04. Reviewers should look for an escape this plan did not check — a CP/lcb combination driving (1+k1) below 1, or a negative-CA regime reachable through the third term. If the floor argument fails, the rejection of Option B fails with it and the plan's whole ordering is wrong.
  • Finding 3. The model-scale inversion assumes CW and (1+k1) are scale-invariant and CA = 0 at model scale. Both are conventional, neither is stated in the fixture. Is λ = 20.2 a real interpretation or numerology that would fit any target value?
  • The refutation of the collapse hypothesis. Two operating points is a thin basis for calling a coincidence. A third and fourth hull would settle it — reviewers should demand that evidence rather than accept the two-point argument, or explain why two suffices.
