## A2 — MAJOR — The "independent second form" is the same formula rearranged; it is a tautology, not a comparator

**Where:** #1580 §Governing physics, final bullet, and Table 1 row 12, and `test_c44_second_form_agrees_with_gm_form`:

> **Independent second form.** The same coefficient is expressible without `GM_T` as `C44 = rho * g * (I_T + V * (z_B - z_G))`. The two forms will be asserted equal in the test suite, which makes the check a conservation-class comparator on top of the closed-form one and **catches a sign or datum error in `KB`/`KG` that a single-form check would pass.**

**Why it is wrong:** with the plan's own decomposition `GM_T = KB + BM_T - KG`, `BM_T = I_T / V`, `KB = z_B`, `KG = z_G`, substitute into the first form:

```
rho*g*V*GM_T = rho*g*V*(z_B + I_T/V - z_G) = rho*g*(V*z_B + I_T - V*z_G) = rho*g*(I_T + V*(z_B - z_G))
```

The two "forms" are the same expression with the distributive law applied. They agree identically for **any** values of `I_T`, `V`, `z_B`, `z_G` — including values carrying the exact sign or datum error the plan claims the check catches. The plan's own Table 1 reports the agreement as "relative difference 6.4e-16", which is the floating-point signature of an algebraic identity, and the plan reads that signature as corroboration.

This is the failure mode `.claude/rules/reproducibility-is-not-correctness.md` exists to prevent, committed in the section that cites that rule. It also inflates the claimed comparator count: the plan tells the reviewer it has a closed-form comparator **plus** a conservation comparator, and it has one comparator.

**What must change:** delete the claim that the second form is independent, delete `test_c44_second_form_agrees_with_gm_form` or demote it to a documented algebra check with no comparator status, and supply a genuinely independent comparator. A real one is available and the plan already knows where: `deck_validation.validate_mesh()` returns `k44` from Capytaine on the actual mesh (`deck_validation.py:79`), computed by a solver the plan did not write, from geometry rather than from declared `KB`/`KG`. Bind `k44` to the `rho*g*V*GM_T` target — which the plan already lists as a Files-to-Change item for `deck_validation.py` — and make **that** the conservation-class check.

## A5 — MAJOR — `test_reference_transform_body_origin_to_com_changes_c44` asserts a change the plan's own transform does not produce for the offset it specifies

**Where:**

> | `test_reference_transform_body_origin_to_com_changes_c44` | the transform is real, not a relabel | `K` about body origin, CoM offset `r=[0,0,-2.5]` | referred `C44` differs from the raw value by the predicted congruence term |

with `refer_to` defined as `T = [[I3, -skew(r)], [0, I3]]`, `return T.transpose() @ K @ T`.

**Why it is wrong:** for a purely vertical offset `r = [0, 0, r_z]`,

```
-skew(r) = [[0, r_z, 0], [-r_z, 0, 0], [0, 0, 0]]
```

whose first column (the roll column of the rotational block) is `[0, -r_z, 0]^T`. The added term in `K'[3][3]` is therefore

```
r_z^2 * K[1][1]  +  2 * (-r_z) * K[1][3]
```

— it depends **only** on the sway stiffness and the sway-roll coupling. It contains no contribution from `K[2][2]` (heave) or `K[3][3]` (roll). The plan's canonical example matrix throughout (Reproduction proofs, `test_si_input_is_identity_to_canonical`, `test_orcawave_native_is_canonical_over_1000`) carries non-zero entries at `[2][2]` and `[3][3]` only. Against that matrix, a `[0,0,-2.5]` shift leaves `C44` **exactly unchanged**, and the test as written fails.

This is not pedantry: the same algebra is why A6 below matters, and it means the plan's author did not evaluate the transform they specified against the offset they specified.

**What must change:** the test row must specify a matrix with a non-zero `K[1][1]` or `K[1][3]`, state the predicted term (`r_z^2 * K[1][1] + 2*(-r_z)*K[1][3]`) as a literal expected value rather than "the predicted congruence term", and add a companion test asserting that a purely vertical shift of a heave-and-roll-only matrix leaves `C44` invariant — which is the physically interesting fact and the one a reader of this plan will get wrong.

## A6 — MAJOR — The stated reference-point sensitivity of `C44 = rho*g*V*GM_T` is physically wrong

**Where:** #1580 §Governing physics:

> **Reference point.** The equality holds for moments taken about the **centre of gravity**, with the body free to heave. Referred to any other point the coefficient changes, so `reference_point` will be a declared field and the transform will be applied explicitly

**Why it is wrong:** the plan's own second form makes the counter-case: `C44 = rho*g*(I_T + V*(z_B - z_G))` depends on `z_B` and `z_G` only through their **difference**. Shift the vertical datum by any `delta` and both move by `delta`; `C44` is unchanged. The hydrostatic roll-restoring coefficient of a body in floating equilibrium is **invariant under a change of the vertical reference datum** — it is not "about the centre of gravity" in the sense the plan asserts, and it does not change when referred to the body origin instead.

What the coefficient *is* sensitive to is the **transverse (y) placement of the roll axis**, through the parallel-axis shift of `I_T` away from the centre of flotation, and the plan names no such sensitivity anywhere. It has identified the wrong invariance and the wrong sensitivity, and then made `reference_point` a required, no-default field on the strength of the wrong one.

This is load-bearing. A5 is the direct arithmetic consequence. The `refer_to` congruence transform is correct **for a general stiffness matrix** (a mooring matrix with sway stiffness genuinely does change `C44` under a vertical shift) but the plan presents it as necessary for the hydrostatic relation, which it is not.

**What must change:** restate the reference-point paragraph: the GM form is invariant to vertical datum and sensitive to transverse axis placement; the congruence transform is required because an *external* stiffness matrix (which may carry sway and sway-roll terms) is genuinely frame-dependent, not because the hydrostatic relation is. Keeping `reference_point` required is still right — for the correct reason.

## A7 — MAJOR — `to_native`'s uniform 1/1000 encodes an unverified vendor assumption about OrcaWave's angular convention as a tested constant

**Where:** Pseudocode `to_native`, `test_orcawave_native_is_canonical_over_1000`, `test_raw_numeric_equality_between_backends_is_not_asserted` ("AQWA native and OrcaWave native raw values differ by exactly 1000"), and AC1.

If `BodyExternalStiffnessMatrix` rotational rows are expressed per degree rather than per radian, the correct factor for the 27 cells involving a rotational DOF is `1000 * 180/pi`, not `1000`, and the plan's headline cross-backend equivalence test would pass against a deck that is wrong by 57.3x in roll.

The plan's own Standards table flags `BodyExternalStiffnessMatrix` semantics as a vendor gap — and then hard-codes `/ 1000.0` in pseudocode, in four test rows, and in AC1, as though the gap were closed. The AQWA FISK datum gap got a named "Resolution step, to be completed before the AQWA adapter is written"; the OrcaWave angular convention got nothing.

**The in-repo reference was checked for this review and does not settle it.** `PARAMETER_REFERENCE.md` establishes the mass base explicitly — `:223` "`BodyMass` | float | Body mass in **tonnes**", `:224-226` inertia tensor rows in **`t·m²`**, `:33` `UnitsSystem: SI` — and describes `BodyExternalStiffnessMatrix...` at `:233` only as "6×6 matrix rows ... The document does show OrcaWave mixing conventions — `:182` `BodyMeshAttitude` "Euler rotation angles (**degrees**)" — so a degrees-based rotational stiffness is a live possibility, not a theoretical one.

**What must change:** add the OrcaWave angular convention for `BodyExternalStiffnessMatrix` as a named blocking precondition with the same treatment as the FISK datum, and record that `PARAMETER_REFERENCE.md` was consulted and is silent on it, so the next reader does not repeat the search. Until it is resolved, `to_native` must not be written and the equivalence tests must not pin `1000`.
