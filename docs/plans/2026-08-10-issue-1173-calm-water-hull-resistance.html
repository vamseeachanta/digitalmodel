#1173 implementation plan: calm-water hull resistance by interFoam towing

Issue #1173 · branch plan/1173-calm-water-hull-resistance · base 2a6b521e · revision 2, after an adversarial REJECT · status: awaiting user approval

Revision 1 gated a fixed body against a free body's number, and then built a schedule on a tutorial's demo budget. Both errors were caught by adversarial review r1, which returned REJECT on four blockers. The reference correction is the deeper of the two: revision 1's Ct = 3.711e-3 is the Tokyo 2015 Case 2.1 value — a model free to sink and trim, with a rudder fitted, in water at ν = 1.27e-6. This plan runs a fixed, even-keel, bare hull. The matched reference for that condition is 3.55e-3 (Shen, Wan & Carrica 2015, Table 5, versus NMRI EFD from Hino 2005). Revision 1 was therefore spending 4.54% of a ±5% budget on a condition offset before a single cell was meshed. Correcting it does not merely move a number — it changes what is achievable, which changes the tolerance, which changes how many runs are worth paying for.
Every figure r1 asserted was re-derived here from the primary sources before being restated, and one of r1's own blockers did not survive that check. Blocker 4 held that mesh refinement makes the error grow, citing Wu (2025). Re-scored against the corrected referent, Wu's error shrinks monotonically with refinement — the growth was an artifact of the wrong referent, i.e. of Blocker 1. r1 did not connect its own two findings. The disposition table at the foot of this plan records that, along with three self-corrections r1 made to its earlier report. The one figure this revision could not verify is flagged as unverified in place rather than stated flat, because taking a reference on trust is the exact failure being corrected.

Goal and authorization boundary

Produce a calm-water ship-resistance capability: a hull towed at constant speed through a free surface by interFoam, with the total resistance coefficient Ct extracted from a forces function object on the hull patch and gated against published towing-tank data at a matched condition.

  • G1 — an emitted, runnable resistance case that reaches a converged force on the pinned OpenFOAM v2312 toolchain, not merely a structurally valid directory tree.
  • G2 — Ct at a stated Froude number within a stated tolerance of a stated published dataset in the same body-fixity, appendage and water condition, asserted in a test that can fail.
  • G3 (changed) — the decomposition of that Ct into computed pressure and viscous components independently gated, so a right total assembled from two wrong parts is caught. This replaces revision 1's multi-Froude trend as the primary falsifiability defence, and the justification is in Scope versus cost.
  • G4 (changed) — G3 is the independent second check. Revision 1 assigned this role to a Holtrop-Mennen cross-check; that method is now measured to be broken, so the role is reassigned rather than left resting on unvalidated code.
  • G5 — the CaseType reuse-vs-new decision documented and defended (an explicit acceptance criterion of the issue). Unchanged.

Boundary. No seakeeping, added resistance in waves, self-propulsion, propeller or rudder modelling, or full-scale extrapolation. The body is fixed and even-keel, bare hull — and unlike revision 1, that is now a match to the reference rather than a bias against it. This plan does not fix #1192's flat-plate and cylinder cases, and it no longer carves scope out of #1682.

Blocker 1 — the reference condition, corrected

Revision 1 blended two different benchmark conditions and did not notice, because both are called "KCS at Fr = 0.26" and both are hosted by the same workshop lineage. They are not the same experiment.

The diagnostic that this was condition-blending rather than a numerical slip: revision 1's Re is 1.404e7. Tokyo 2015 Case 2.1 at Fr 0.260 specifies Re = 1.26e7. So the Reynolds number and the friction line came from the 2005-family condition while Ct came from the 2015 table. Even taken on its own terms, revision 1's Cr was wrong: at Case 2.1's own Re = 1.26e7 the residuary would be 3.711 − 2.883 = 0.828e-3, not 0.879e-3.

New finding, not raised by r1: the appendage and its wetted surface. Ct is normalised on wetted surface, so the reference is not fully pinned until S is. Tokyo 2015 states its coefficients are "based on wetted surface area (S0/LPP² = 0.1803) with rudder" ⇒ 9.5527 m². Wu (2025) Table 1 gives KCS 9.4379 m² without rudder plus 0.1152 m² for the rudder — summing to 9.5531 m², which reproduces the Tokyo 2015 figure to four significant figures and so corroborates both. The two candidate normalisations differ by 1.22%. This plan runs a bare hull and will normalise on 9.4379 m². What could not be verified here is which S Hino (2005) used to publish 3.55e-3 for the bare-hull fixed case. If it was the with-rudder 0.1803·Lpp², the bare-normalised referent is 3.593e-3 instead. That 1.22% is carried explicitly in the tolerance budget below and is a Stage-0 provenance gate; it is not asserted resolved.

The gate, revised

V1 — how tight, and why not 5%

Revision 1 chose ±5% and defended it as "the issue's tighter bound". That was inertia dressed as reasoning: 4.54% of it was consumed by the condition offset, so the real physics budget was 0.46% and the number was never argued. With the offset gone the question is open, and it should be answered from an uncertainty budget rather than from the issue text.

Error sources that a correct implementation still carries, at Fr = 0.26:

  U_S   wetted-surface referent ambiguity (bare 9.4379 vs with-rudder 9.5527)   1.22%   UNRESOLVED
  U_D   EFD uncertainty, KCS resistance                                          1.00%   (Wu 2025, stated)
  U_SN  numerical/validation uncertainty, closest analogue                       1.39%   (Wu 2025, KCS static, U_V)
  U_i   iterative uncertainty over a 4000-iteration window                       0.24%   (Wu 2025)

  RSS(U_S, U_D, U_SN)                                                       =    2.10%
  V1 tolerance chosen                                                       =    3.00%   (1.43 x the floor)
  • Why not tighter than 3%. A tolerance below 2.1% asserts below the validation noise floor and would fail correct work. Had Stage 0 pinned S, the floor drops to 1.71% and 2.5% would be justified — but the gate is pre-committed at approval time, not after the fact, so it is set for the world as it is known now.
  • Why not looser than 3%. 3% on Ct is 0.107e-3, which is 14.8% of the residuary 0.718e-3. Revision 1's 5% was 24.7% of the residuary, and the issue's 10% was 49% — a gate that lets the quantity the case exists to compute be wrong by half.
  • A correct implementation passes. Both Wu grids at or above 0.57 M cells and all four Shen results pass 3% with at least 3× margin.
  • A degenerate one fails hard. A solution computing no free-surface deformation returns pure friction, Ct = 2.832e-3 ⇒ −20.2%, failing 3% by 6.7×.

V1: |Ct_CFD − 3.55e-3| / 3.55e-3 ≤ 0.03 at Fr = 0.26, fixed even-keel bare hull, Re = 1.4e7, S = 9.4379 m².

V2 — Blocker 3: a criterion that cannot fail is not a criterion

Revision 1's V2 was Cr = Ct − Cf with Cf the ITTC-57 correlation line — a deterministic function of Re, which is an input. So Cr_CFD was an affine re-expression of Ct_CFD and V2 could not fail while V1 passed. r1 confirmed this empirically: Shen's and Wu's results fail V1-as-written while passing V2-as-written. The gate advertised as "the one with teeth" had none. That is the defect class this repository has spent the week closing (#1977, #1983), and shipping another instance of it would be indefensible.

The fix is to gate on quantities the solver actually computes. The OpenFOAM forces function object writes the pressure and viscous force contributions separately. Using both turns one number into two, and the two are constrained against different references:

criterionreferenceevidence for the threshold
V2a|Cp_CFD − Cp_ref| / Cp_ref ≤ 0.15, where Cp_CFD is the computed pressure force coefficient on the hull patch (doubled for the symmetry half-domain)Cp_ref = 3.55e-3 − Cf_ITTC57(1.4e7) = 7.180e-4Published fixed-condition CFD pressure coefficients span −7.9% to +2.6% of this reference (Shen Table 14: 0.6611/0.6684/0.6988; Table 5: 0.699 present work, 0.737 CFDShip-Iowa). 15% is roughly 2× the worst published deviation.
V2b|Cv_CFD − Cf_ITTC57(Re)| / Cf_ITTC57(Re) ≤ 0.05, where Cv_CFD is the computed viscous force coefficientCf_ITTC57(1.4e7) = 2.832e-3Shen's three grids give Cv/Cf = 1.012, 1.010, 0.995 — all within 1.2%. 5% leaves 4× margin while still catching a wall-treatment defect.

Why V2b earns its place specifically here. The DTCHull tutorial ships nutkRoughWallFunction with Ks uniform 100e-6 — a 100 µm sand-grain roughness — on 0.orig/nut, verified on the host. Inheriting that onto a smooth-hull towing-tank benchmark inflates skin friction. V2b is the criterion that catches it, and it catches it without depending on whether the pressure side happens to compensate.

Independence, demonstrated rather than asserted. Ct = Cp + Cv holds exactly, so the three criteria are not algebraically independent — but neither implies the others, in either direction, and the plan proves it with two synthetic vectors that the test module asserts: 
V1 passes, V2 fails  (compensating errors — the case V2 exists for)
  Cv = 2.945e-3  (+4.0%   -> V2b PASS)
  Cp = 0.605e-3  (-15.8%  -> V2a FAIL)
  Ct = 3.550e-3  ( 0.0%   -> V1  PASS)

V2 passes, V1 fails  (both parts individually tolerable, total is not)
  Cv = 2.945e-3  (+4.0%   -> V2b PASS)
  Cp = 0.818e-3  (+14.0%  -> V2a PASS)
  Ct = 3.764e-3  (+6.0%   -> V1  FAIL)
Revision 1's V2 admitted no such pair, which is precisely how the defect should have been detected before review.

V3 — withdrawn, with reasons that are not only about cost

Revision 1 required Fr ∈ {0.227, 0.260, 0.282} with Ct(0.282)/Ct(0.260) ≥ 1.10 and Ct(0.260)/Ct(0.227) ≥ 1.03. Three problems, in ascending order of seriousness:

  1. It was one-sided (r1 standing finding, accepted). A solution returning ratios of 5.0 passes both inequalities. A trend criterion must be a two-sided band.
  2. It cost two extra production solves — see the budget below — to constrain a shape, when V2a/V2b constrain the composition of the gated number itself at zero additional solver hours.
  3. The reference series is the wrong condition, and cannot be made right. The EFD ratios 1.213 and 1.069 come from Tokyo 2015 Case 2.1 — free in heave and pitch, with a rudder. Sinkage and trim grow with Froude number, so the free-condition Ct(Fr) slope is steeper than a fixed body's by construction. Requiring a fixed body to reproduce a free body's slope is a criterion a correct implementation is expected to fail. No fixed-condition multi-speed KCS Ct(Fr) series was located; Tokyo 2005 published the fixed case at a single speed.

V3 moves to a follow-on issue, with two conditions attached: it must be two-sided, and it must be scored against a fixed-condition series or else run free-to-sink-and-trim so that the body condition matches the data. Point 3 alone would justify the withdrawal at zero cost saving.

The issue's own wording is affected and that is stated openly. #1173's prose asks for the "wave-making resistance trend with Fr captured (humps/hollows)". Its six-item acceptance checklist does not. This plan proposes to close #1173 against the checklist and to record the trend as follow-on scope in a comment on the issue, so the descope is visible where the issue is read rather than buried here.

Kelvin half-angle (issue criterion 4) stays qualitative: a rendered free-surface elevation field with the 19.47° wedge overlaid in the verification report. Visual confirmation, not a gate.

Vacuity guards, per #1977: each of V1/V2a/V2b asserts its reference is non-null and was loaded from the committed fixture rather than defaulted; a known-negative control must fail V1; and the two independence vectors above must produce exactly the stated verdicts.

Blocker 2 — the schedule, re-budgeted

The iteration budget was a tutorial artifact

Revision 1 budgeted 4 000–6 000 iterations, reading the DTCHull tutorial's endTime 4000 as a convergence requirement. It is a demo budget — the tutorial exists to produce a picture in an afternoon. Wu (2025), on the same solver family and scheme ("the first-order Euler method implicit with local time stepping for static mesh simulation"), ran 20 000 / 40 000 / 30 000 iterations for KCS static-mesh fine/medium/coarse, and reports iterative uncertainty U_i = 0.24% over a window from 26 000 to 30 020. (Precision note: that window belongs to Wu's KCS dynamic-mesh S1 case, which ran 30 020 iterations; the static-mesh counts are the three above. The conclusion — tens of thousands, not thousands — is unaffected.)

This plan budgets 25 000 LTS iterations with the force mean taken over the final 4 000, and makes the window an explicit, recorded convergence check rather than an assumption.

Revision 1's 2.5e-5 core-s per cell-iteration was back-derived from a narrative markdown file whose mesh size was not recorded — revision 1 flagged this itself as the claim most likely to be wrong. Wu (2025) Table 14 supplies a direct measurement for exactly this configuration:

r1 established the cost and stopped there. The consequential question is whether three Froude numbers plus two mesh levels is still the right thing to buy at that price. Four options, ranked:

Recommendation: option C, and why

  1. The falsifiability that two extra Froude numbers were buying is bought more sharply, and for free, by the force decomposition. V3 spent roughly 160–215 h to constrain the shape of Ct(Fr). V2a/V2b constrain the composition of the very number being gated, from the same single run, using data the forces function object already writes. Compensating errors between an over-predicted friction and an under-predicted wave field — the failure mode V3 was aimed at — are caught directly rather than inferred from a slope.
  2. Refine downward, not upward. Revision 1's AC 6 went finer, which is where cost lives: 2.83× the cells for 304 h. A coarser companion at r_G = √2 gives the same two-level ΔCt for ~25 h — a twelvefold saving for the same self-consistency evidence. The literature already establishes that going finer than 1.6 M barely moves Ct (Shen: 1.68 → 10.58 M moves it 0.34 percentage points).
  3. Grid convergence for this exact case is published twice and #1173 does not ask for it. Wu covers 0.21/0.57/1.64 M on interFoam-LTS-static; Shen covers 1.68/4.26/10.58 M on an overset code. Neither of #1173's six acceptance criteria mentions mesh convergence — AC 6 was self-imposed by revision 1. Spending the single largest line item in the plan to reproduce a published convergence study is the weakest expenditure available.
  4. The three-Froude sweep is unsound independently of cost. Restated from V3 above because it is decisive: the only multi-speed EFD series is free-heave-and-pitch with a rudder. Even with unlimited compute, running three fixed-body points against it would produce a comparison that cannot be made clean.
  5. Risk of ruin. A 26-day exclusive campaign over a flapping link, with a serial runner and a two-hour default timeout, has a poor chance of completing without a restart that invalidates part of it. A 72-hour run is recoverable inside a week. If Fr 0.26 passes cleanly, a follow-on issue for the sweep and the fine-grid study is trivially justified and can be scheduled against a dedicated host. If it fails, the 215 h would have been spent on a broken case anyway.

Blocker 4 — acceptance criterion 6, reformulated

r1's objection: AC 6 requires V1 on the finer mesh, but in Wu's KCS results the error grows monotonically with refinement (−1.19% → 4.20% → 4.97%), the coarse grid being accurate by error cancellation. A criterion a correct implementation fails is as bad as one that cannot fail.

The premise does not survive Blocker 1's correction. Wu's errors were published against 3.711e-3. Re-scored against 3.55e-3 they read −5.77% → −0.14% → +0.65% — error shrinking monotonically with refinement, which is the textbook behaviour. The apparent divergence was the condition offset: 4.54 of Wu's ~4.6% average error is Blocker 1, which r1 itself observed one section earlier without joining the two. Blocker 4's evidence is therefore rejected. Shen's Table 14 is a mild counterweight — against 3.55e-3 its error grows from 0.62% to 0.96% across 1.68→10.58 M, and Shen's own V&V classes Ct convergence as oscillatory — but a 0.34-point drift is far below any defensible tolerance and does not support the criterion r1 proposed to strike.

AC 6 is nevertheless reformulated, for a different and better reason: under option C the plan no longer performs a refinement study at all, and a criterion should describe what is actually done.

AC 6 (revised). Grid sensitivity is established in two parts. (a) By citation: the verification artifact records Wu (2025) Table 5 and Shen (2015) Table 14 re-scored against the fixed even-keel referent, and states that the production mesh is sized at or above the density at which both reach sub-1%. (b) By measurement: two levels are run at Fr 0.26 — the production mesh and a companion coarser by r_G = √2 — and the criterion is |Ct_fine − Ct_coarse| / Ct_fine ≤ 0.03, i.e. the levels agree with each other to within the V1 tolerance. It is explicitly not required that V1 hold on both, because on a two-point study the coarse level's agreement with EFD is not the property under test — self-consistency is. A coarse level that misses EFD while tracking the fine level is informative; a pair that disagree with each other means the answer is mesh-dependent and the run does not support a validation claim regardless of which level happens to match.

Execution blocker: the runner cannot run this

This is a hard blocker on execution and revision 1 would have discovered it at Stage 5, after paying for it. src/digitalmodel/solvers/openfoam/runner.py executes each stage as a single subprocess.run(...) over a fixed argv with timeout=self._config.timeout_seconds, whose default is 7200 — two hours (runner.py:92; the same default recurs at case_definition.py:372, prebuilt_mesh.py:80, smoke.py:239). There is no mpirun anywhere in the runner, so it cannot use the eight ranks the whole cost model assumes, and it has no surfaceFeatureExtract / topoSet / refineMesh loop — the DTCHull Allrun performs surfaceFeatureExtract, blockMesh, then six topoSet+refineMesh pairs before snappyHexMesh. A 72-hour eight-rank solve is outside every one of these.

Scoped response, in the order it matters:

  1. Fail closed on the budget, first. Add a preflight to OpenFOAMRunner that refuses to start a stage whose estimated wall-clock exceeds timeout_seconds. Today an over-budget solve is killed at two hours and surfaces as a crash; the two-hour cap is configurable (workflow.py already documents timeout_seconds: 43200), which makes the silent-truncation failure mode worse, not better. Test: test_runner_refuses_solve_exceeding_timeout.
  2. Add MPI and detachment as explicit config — ranks: int and detach: bool on OpenFOAMRunConfig, with detach wrapping the remote command in setsid nohup and logging under the external work root per #1565. Progress is polled by short, idempotent, re-connectable calls; no held session.
  3. Drive the mesh pipeline as a declared stage list rather than a hardcoded sequence, so the six-pass topoSet/refineMesh loop is expressible. Each stage keeps its own timeout.
  4. Stage 1 proves survival against a deliberate mid-solve disconnect before any long run starts. Unchanged from revision 1 and still correct.

  • The wetted-surface resolution. This plan normalises on 9.4379 m² (bare) while the referent 3.55e-3 may have been published on 9.5527 m² (with rudder). This is the single largest open item.
  • The V1 tolerance of 3%. Built from an RSS of four terms, one of which (U_SN = 1.39%) is borrowed from Wu's grid rather than measured on ours, and one of which is the unresolved item above. Argue the number down or up, but argue it from the budget.
  • V2a's 15% threshold. Set at roughly 2× the worst published deviation of a computed pressure coefficient from a derived residuary. The two are not the same decomposition — Cv_CFD ≠ Cf_ITTC57 exactly — and the tolerance is absorbing that definitional slack as well as physical error. A reviewer may reasonably argue the two effects should be separated rather than pooled.
  • The descope to one Froude number. The strongest counter-argument is that the issue's prose asks for the trend and the plan is closing it without. The rebuttal — the only reference series is the wrong condition — should be attacked directly: if a fixed-condition multi-speed KCS series exists and was missed, the recommendation weakens considerably.
  • The mesh sizing at 1.5 M half-domain. Argued from Wu's 1.64 M and Shen's 1.68 M full-domain grids, both unstructured or overset. A snappyHexMesh hex-dominant cell is not equivalent, and the half-domain-doubling equivalence is an assumption.
  • Whether Stage 1 is early enough — carried forward from revision 1 and still open. It now sits before roughly 97 h of committed solver time rather than before 625 h, which reduces the exposure but does not eliminate it.
