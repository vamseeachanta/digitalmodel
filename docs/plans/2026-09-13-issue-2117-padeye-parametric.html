The local x axis will follow the weld baseline, y will be perpendicular to the weld baseline in the plate plane, positive toward the lug, and z will follow plate thickness. Positive angle will rotate the load toward +x. The source procedure will define the chain: 16 kip gross × 1.8 = 28.8 kip, shared equally by two upper lugs under the historical symmetric lifting arrangement, giving Fy = 14.4 kip per lug, Fx = 14.4 tan(theta) kip and resultant = 14.4/cos(theta) kip. The 1.8 historical factor will already be included and will not be applied again. Weld moment will follow Mz = rx Fy − ry Fx, using the verified vector from the weld-group centroid to the pin-hole centre. Separate signed unit tests will verify this convention.

The baseline material, hole, pin, shackle envelope and supporting beam will remain fixed. Each row will check positive ligaments, self-intersection, hole containment, pin clearance, jaw clearance, shackle-body interference, footprint support and weld-size applicability before dependent calculations. Missing envelope information will produce an unresolved check, not an assumed fit. Geometry changes will record plate-volume and weight changes; the fixed gross load will remain a benchmark assumption, not a revised payload rating.

3. Calculation and readiness contract

The baseline will first be independently reproduced using the corrected historical method. Each applicable check will retain its own demand, capacity, utilization, criterion and source location: tension, corrected shear/tear-out, bearing, geometric/dishing limitations and weld-group loading. The implementation will verify each formula's geometric and directional domain before evaluating a varied profile. No unsupported generalization of a circular-lug formula will be permitted.

The historical beam calculation will be reproduced separately. Local beam/flange and weld-transfer qualification for changed footprints will remain unresolved unless a verified method and its inputs are available. Pin/shackle strength, fatigue, fracture, out-of-plane loading, cheek plates and fabrication qualification will remain explicit exclusions. No overall assembly capacity or current-code compliance verdict will be issued from partial checks.

Source dimensions and equations will require a visual readback and independent transcription check. Printed numerical results will be compared using their displayed rounding interval; independent unrounded arithmetic will use a predeclared relative tolerance of 1e-8 with unit-specific absolute tolerances recorded before evaluation. Any unresolved published discrepancy will remain a discrepancy, with affected reproduction status withheld. Tests will not tune constants to recover a target.

Every attempted row will carry separate geometry, source, calculation, reproduction and intended-use readiness fields. Invalid or unresolved rows will retain reasons and null unavailable outputs; null will not become zero. A row may support documented historical screening of named checks while remaining unqualified for engineering design.

4. Repository artifacts and lookup behavior

Proposed paths will be examples/parametric/padeye_ricker/ for the reproducible study configuration and go-by, data/benchmarks/padeye_ricker/<revision>/ for schema-conforming derivative tables and manifests, and docs/reports/ for the HTML calculation report. These paths will define a separate dataset and will not change atlases/lifting_lug/default.txt.

A single authoritative table will produce CSV and JSON exports plus report charts. The manifest will contain stable case IDs derived from canonical input content, units, source editions and page references, source/input/output SHA-256 digests, code revision, profile definition, study selections, rejected-row count and method version. Source copying/publication rights will be verified before derived source content is saved to this public repository. Unknown rights will block affected publication and completion of the requested dataset; no private-copy workaround will be presumed to confer rights. A rights-related scope change will require a revised review and owner decision. Vendor-licensed standards PDFs will never be committed; restricted source locators will remain in the authorized private owner.

Exact-case queries will require every varied parameter and will return the case's checks, readiness and limitations. Missing or off-grid values will be rejected; no nearest-case substitution, extrapolation or interpolation will occur. Queries will evaluate readiness per requested response: verified historical demands will remain available with limitations even when assembly qualification is unavailable; unresolved capacities and invalid calculations will return null with reasons. No response will imply an assembly design verdict. Export roundtrips will preserve IDs and finite numeric values. Completeness will require unique membership of all 225 Cartesian combinations, not row count alone.

The HTML report will follow the calculation-report schema and engineering register, including assumptions, equations, source references and limitations. It will include a baseline comparison, complete results link, rejection summary and plots of check utilization against thickness, grouped by profile scale and selected weld/angle. Every displayed trace will derive from the same table; no missing check will appear as passing. Thickness will display three decimals and captions will follow tables and figures. Chart concepts in this plan will remain descriptions, not simulated results.

5. Implementation, validation and review sequence

  1. The source/rights and geometry-definition record will be resolved first. Unresolved source inputs will block dependent calculation, while deterministic configuration and validation work may proceed.
  2. RED tests will precede implementation for invalid dimensions, incompatible geometry, missing provenance, double factoring, coordinate signs, independent baseline results, parameter coverage, duplicate/missing rows, deterministic IDs and off-grid refusal.
  3. A bounded pure calculation adapter will generate the 225-row study with an independently checked baseline. Source criteria will emit Citation sidecars and fail closed where the required authority cannot be resolved.
  4. Independent checks will compare the baseline, each one-factor extreme and mixed corner cases before the tables are reported. An unchanged exact baseline will appear within the matrix; additional comparison records will not inflate its row count.
  5. Schema, data roundtrip, legal/security, engineering-register and HTML visual checks will precede publication. Path-specific commits will preserve concurrent work.
  6. Claude and Codex will perform adversarial plan and artifact reviews. T2 review will use two providers because the first slice will be an offline benchmark adapter and report, with no fleet or licensed execution changes. Blocking findings will be corrected before acceptance; invalid review output will block, and unavailable providers will be reported explicitly.

Final inline review corrections will further bind the study. Each inclined-angle row will evaluate both signed horizontal directions, Fx = ±14.4 tan(theta), with the same vertical component and signed eccentricity. It will retain both weld-demand results and their envelope rather than assume a governing lug orientation. The zero-angle row will contain one direction only: 225 parameter rows will therefore contain 375 directional evaluations. Baseline reproduction will compare only the published orientation; the opposite-direction response will be an independently derived sensitivity result, not a published comparator.

Results will distinguish method-computed from independently verified. Before implementation, a versioned verification register will enumerate the 54-case minimum subgrid specified in section 7 and both directional evaluations for its inclined cases (90 evaluations total). A separately derived weld-group calculation will verify those demands without importing production response functions. Additional rows will be marked method-computed unless separately checked. Minimum usable coverage will require independent verification for those 54 rows; all other valid rows will still require complete method-computed responses. Lookup will expose the verification state per response.

Two identifiers will be distinct: a parameter key stable across method revisions, and a row content hash incorporating method/profile revision. Lookup will require an explicit manifest revision; no implicit latest alias will be created in this first slice. Superseded revisions will be refused by normal lookup and will require an explicit historical-audit mode that labels the output superseded. Grid completeness will be checked per revision. Tests will verify that a revision change cannot mix old and new rows, and that equal parameter keys in different revisions cannot produce an ambiguous answer.

6. Later finite-element verification

A subsequent reviewed campaign will propose the baseline plus four thickness/profile corners, each at 0 and 45 degrees, at the baseline weld multiplier. It will first define the pin-load, weld and supporting-beam model and mesh-convergence criteria. Native invocation budgets and convergence tolerances will require approval in that campaign plan. This initial implementation scope will include zero native solver invocations. Selected FE cases will not qualify the full analytical table; local singular peaks will not be lookup responses.

7. Acceptance and handoff

The following review controls will bind implementation. The local weld baseline will be horizontal with +y aligned to global vertical and gravity along −y; source verification will confirm this mounting assumption. The loading scenario will represent fixed gross weight with varying sling angle, not a fixed-resultant direction sweep. Tests will verify the complete load-sharing chain and an independently hand-calculated eccentric moment with nonzero rx and Fx.

If the verified baseline thickness or angle is absent from the proposed grid, generation will stop for a revised reviewed matrix; no hidden extra row or nearest-point replacement will occur. The procedure load reading adopted by the user will govern. Every other disputed source quantity will block its dependent check until independently resolved. Baseline comparison will include intermediate areas, section properties, demands and capacities, with the number of matched quantities and unmatched comparators reported.

At 30 and 45 degrees, net-section, tear-out and direction-dependent plate capacities will remain unresolved for 150 rows until a separately reviewed directional method is specified; vertical formulas will not be projected onto those cases. Inclined rows will expose verified weld-group demands and applicable independently established weld checks only. For all directions, each strength criterion will require a versioned applicability record with explicit geometric limits and source clause before calculation; absence will block the check. Changed-footprint support checks will remain unresolved for the 180 rows with profile scale other than 1.00. Whole-assembly qualification will remain unavailable for all 225 rows. Tests will guard these counts; clearing a withheld mode will require a reviewed method update.

Weld multipliers will define continuous geometric sensitivity rather than fabrication selections. The table will record whether each exact leg lies on a 1/16-in fabrication grid, with non-grid counts reported without rounding the analysis. Minimum and maximum weld limits will reference both joined thicknesses, including the fixed supporting flange, and will be hard applicability checks. Minimum usable coverage will apply to verified weld-group demand responses; fabrication or capacity applicability failures will remain visible and will not invalidate otherwise verified statics. Any advertised capacity lookup will independently satisfy its baseline and applicable-domain gates.

Canonical case inputs will use decimal strings: thickness in inches to three places, profile/weld multipliers to two places and angle in integer degrees; canonical serialization will use sorted field names and a fixed method/profile revision before SHA-256 hashing. Lookup will validate exact canonical input without rounding off-grid requests into the domain. Responses, including rejected cases, will carry method and manifest revisions, source/input hashes and code revision. CSV null will be the literal NULL token; the explicit reader will restore it to null and reject missing fields, NaN and infinities. JSON will use null. Roundtrip tests will verify rejected and unresolved rows.

Charts will break traces at null/rejected values, show omitted counts and reasons, and label each capacity axis with its historical method edition. Citation sidecars will distinguish historical reproduction from present applicability; historical authority will remain usable solely for the declared historical purpose. The 2004 erratum will be checked for its short-sling formula on p.149 and allowable shear formula on p.152. Changes to consumed corrections will create a new method version and supersede prior affected rows. BV NR595 will remain an offshore-handling-systems gap checklist; ASME BTH-1 will enter the candidate current-design-basis register, with applicability still unresolved.

The study adapter will be pure offline calculation. Tests will patch every reachable solver-dispatch entry point to raise, proving that the study cannot consume a licensed solver seat. T2 review will deliberately use two providers in deviation from the three-agent default: the scope will be one isolated benchmark adapter and its data/report consumer, reusing existing schema and citation gates without changing their shared contracts. Broader infrastructure changes will require separate scope and review. General atlas-validation findings will be tracked in issue 2118.

Acceptance will require a reproducible command, all 225 attempted cases accounted for, independently verified baseline quantities for every response exposed through lookup, mode-specific result criteria, no silent interpolation, a verified report and a documented lookup example. Usable verified responses will cover at least the complete 54-case subgrid of thickness 0.750/1.250/1.750 in, profile scale 0.80/1.00/1.20, weld multiplier 1.00/1.50 and all three angles, including the unchanged baseline comparison at weld multiplier 1.00. Utilization above unity will remain a valid computed failure, not an invalid row. If source or geometry exclusions prevent this minimum usable coverage, completion will remain blocked pending a reviewed scope revision. Every geometrically valid row among the 225 attempts will require all offered lookup responses to be method-computed, with independent verification limited to the versioned verification register and reported separately. Any unresolved design check will remain visible. The study will be described as a historical parametric go-by until a separately established current-code basis and assembly qualification support broader use. The closeout will name retained artifacts, source gaps, review evidence and the next checkpoint.
