Connected-tank sloshing analysis

Illustrative U-tube anti-roll response and a single-tank tuned-absorber comparison.

Summary

An invented, illustrative U-tube system comprises two tank legs connected by a conduit. Analysis is performed to determine the connected-tank response and compare it with a single-tank tuned absorber. The geometry represents no particular vessel or client. The results are summarized below.

Analytical predictions are evaluated using the connected-tank model and the tuned-absorber model, with published aggregate calibration results. Archived computational fluid dynamics (CFD) evidence is presented separately from the illustrative predictions. The predictions support screening; design qualification is not established.

The published moment-fit band limits the use of the aggregate error statistic. Transfer to the invented geometry remains an assumption, including inside that band. The single-tank comparison illustrates peak splitting and merging; its amplitudes do not establish a design ranking.

Computed curves

The connected-tank curves are evaluated using the analytical model at the illustrative inputs listed above. Frozen-fluid prismatic roll inertia is calculated in the inertia curve; a validation error for rigid-column conduit inertia is not established by that curve.

Damping is evaluated at forcing period 20.0 s with imposed level-difference amplitudes from the published damping inputs. The calculated damping depends on amplitude; it is not a fixed tank property.

The moment is calculated at 5.0° imposed roll and the illustrative conduit area, using the tank-moment model.

The coupled response is evaluated with the published response inputs: vessel roll period 20.0 s, hull damping 5.0 % and tank authority 12.0 %. Tank authority denotes a normalized stiffness scale, distinct from a mass ratio. In the coupled-response model, the forcing reference is fixed by the bare-vessel roll amplitude. The calculated response is roll per unit wave moment at that finite reference; quadratic loss makes it amplitude-dependent. Shading marks periods outside the published moment-fit band. Wave-spectrum and dimensional vessel calibration are not established.

Validity band

The moment model degrades below about 16 s and the CAUSE IS NOT ESTABLISHED. An earlier explanation was withdrawn. The residual also depends on roll amplitude.

The reported moment RMS applies at forcing periods of 20 s or longer for the published calibration. Intermediate periods are not qualified by that aggregate statistic. Even inside the band, transfer to the invented geometry is an assumption. No new physical mechanism is proposed.

Loss scaling and independent area check

The fitted area exponent is n = 2.138, with 8.6 % RMS over 35 cases. The naive inverse-area form has 36.5 % RMS after its own coefficient refit: it underpredicts damping at the smallest area (group RMS 55.1 %) and overpredicts at the largest (group RMS 108.3 %). Way forward

Further assessment is required before the illustrative response is applied to a design. The geometry assumptions, comparison limits and calibration limits define the outstanding work below.

  • Not done: The new geometry's natural period requires measurement before effective conduit length is recalibrated.
  • Not done: Moment and loss require validation across roll amplitudes and the short-period band.
  • Not done: Single and connected tanks require comparison at matched mass, tuning, hull damping and forcing.
  • Not done: The residual cause requires resolution before the empirical validity band is widened.
  • Not done: Operating fill, freeboard, structural loads and dimensional vessel coupling require assessment.

Left

The archived period checks do not establish a direct frozen-fluid roll-inertia error bound. Raw CFD fields, meshes, unstaged case quantities and the archived geometry are omitted. Continuous fill coverage, matched single-tank versus connected-tank performance, and anti-roll phase or benefit remain unestablished. The earlier campaign does not qualify the invented illustrative geometry.
