1 Executive summary

The coupled model can be used to compare generic hull roll with and without the connected tank. The results are screening estimates; no result represents a real hull.

The implications of the model's stated moment fit are listed in Section 7.

  • At an 18 s hull roll period, alpha 0.05, and conduit area A_c = 6.8 m², the peak roll reduction screening estimates are 46.1 % at 2 % hull damping and 23.3 % at 5 %. See Figure 5.1.
  • At 60 s forcing and 18 s hull period with 2 % hull damping, there is 6.0 % more roll at alpha 0.05 and 29.5 % at alpha 0.20, compared with the 25.0 % slow-forcing limit at alpha 0.20, from 1/(1−α). See Section 5.2.
  • The moment fit has 4.3 % RMS error for forcing periods of 20 s and longer; it degrades below about 16 s for a reason not established. See Section 6.
  • The reference tank's natural period is 14.3 s [I4]; the 18 s sweep starts at 12.6 s. Every sampled forcing period below 20 s lies outside the model's stated moment-fit band (≥20 s), including parts of the 21 s sweep. The 46.1 % peak at 18.5 s lies outside it. See Appendix A.

2 Introduction

The generic hull roll comparison describes the effect of a connected anti-roll tank for engineers considering the simplified coupled model. The scope is a normalized hull and the public reference tank, with no actual hull mass, inertia or restoring.

These screening estimates exclude sea-state weighting, structural loads and design qualification. No full-scale roll reduction has been calculated here.

4 Analysis methodology

The model links hull roll to water transfer between connected tank legs. Tank moments feed back into the hull roll equation. Alpha is the tank's quasi-static free-surface moment stiffness divided by the hull's roll restoring. Alpha is dimensionless.

The public U-tube model is used; a software version is not declared. Its exact source is identified in [I2]. Kₛ is the quasi-static tank moment per roll angle. The public model includes vertical-centroid and local free-surface terms in Kₛ. Kₛ also includes water redistribution between the legs. Alpha therefore follows the complete public moment convention.

(4.1) The normalized bare-hull impedance is D = 1 − (T₀/T)² + 2iζ(T₀/T).

(4.2) The coupled impedance is D − αH/Kₛ. H is tank moment per roll angle; Kₛ is its quasi-static limit.

T₀ is the hull roll period (s), T is the forcing period (s), ζ is the hull damping ratio (1), and i is the imaginary unit. H and Kₛ use the same moment-per-angle units, so both impedances and alpha are dimensionless [I2].

The hull equation uses normalized restoring of 1, with no units. The model sets forcing to give 5° bare roll at the nominal resonance (ASSUMED - to be confirmed). Forcing stays fixed within each tank-on and tank-off comparison. Forcing is rescaled between damping groups to keep the 5 degree bare-roll reference, so the comparison also changes the tank amplitude.

The calculation finds each response peak separately over 0.7 to 1.3 times the hull roll period. Reduction equals 100 % times one minus the coupled peak divided by the bare peak. The input basis and calculation are recorded in [I1] and [I4].

5 Results

All charts select the existing 18 s hull roll period and conduit area 6.8 m². The wave-period chart uses 2 % hull damping and alpha 0.05. Alpha is normalized stiffness as defined in Section 4. The calculated tank natural period is 14.3 s [I4]. The reference geometry is retained rather than tuned to the 18 s hull; the tank and hull natural periods therefore differ.

5.1 Peak roll reduction

Figure 5.1 shows peak reduction against hull damping; Figure 5.2 shows the dimensionless roll amplification factor against forcing period; Figure 5.3 shows peak reduction against alpha. The full sensitivity matrix and damping comparison are in Appendix A.

The amplification factor multiplies the normalized wave moment to give roll in radians. The bare factor is 1/(2ζ) at the nominal hull resonance; a normalized moment of 2ζ times 5° in radians gives the 5° bare roll used here.

5.2 Long-period penalty

Slow water transfer reduces the effective hull restoring. In the slow-forcing limit, the roll ratio approaches 1/(1−α). At alpha 0.05, that limit gives 5.3 % more roll.

At 60 s forcing and 18 s hull period with 2 % hull damping, there is 6.0 % more roll at alpha 0.05 and 29.5 % at alpha 0.20, compared with the 25.0 % slow-forcing limit at alpha 0.20, from 1/(1−α). The slow-forcing limit understates the penalty at 60 s. A near-resonance reduction does not remove this penalty. The comparison is recorded in [I1].

6 Validation and benchmark

The model's stated moment fit and the mathematical hand checks address different quantities. The moment fit has 4.3 % RMS error for forcing periods of 20 s and longer; it degrades below about 16 s for a reason not established [R1].

No tank-on and tank-off real-vessel comparison establishes these reductions. No full-scale roll reduction has been calculated here.

Alpha = 0 gives zero reduction, or 0 %. An undamped box hand check copies the model's own moment formula, so it checks arithmetic and the coupling denominator, not the physics of the moment model. It also checks both natural frequencies [I5].

For that mathematical check, each leg measures 2 m by 1 m, with 1 m depth and 1 m offset. The conduit has 1 m² area and 1 m effective length. Fluid density is 1 kg/m³; gravity is 1 m/s².

The hand calculation gives Kₛ = 53/6 N m/rad and H = 359/24 N m/rad at 0.5 rad/s. At alpha 0.1, the response is 2120/1231 times normalized forcing, with no units [I5].

7 Limitations

The model's stated moment fit does not validate coupled hull reduction.

The reductions are screening estimates for generic hulls, with no real-hull evidence; the missing comparison is described in Section 6.

  • The reference tank's natural period is 14.3 s [I4]; the 18 s sweep starts at 12.6 s. Every sampled forcing period below 20 s lies outside the model's stated moment-fit band, including parts of the 21 s sweep; see Appendix A notes.
  • The damping comparisons rescale forcing between damping groups and therefore change tank amplitude; see Appendix A damping comparison.
  • Calibration transfer and the reference tank dimensions remain assumed; their basis is in Table 3.1.
  • These screening estimates exclude sea-state weighting, structural loads and design qualification; the assessed scope is in Section 2.
  • A near-resonance reduction does not remove the long-period penalty; see Section 5.2.

8 Conclusions and next steps

The peak reduction depends on the assumed hull damping and alpha; the separate peak comparisons are shown in Section 5 and Appendix A. The near-resonance reduction does not remove the long-period penalty in Section 5.2.

Calibration transfer and the bare-roll reference remain ASSUMED. The missing real-vessel tank-on and tank-off comparison in Section 6 remains the evidence gap for coupled hull reduction.
