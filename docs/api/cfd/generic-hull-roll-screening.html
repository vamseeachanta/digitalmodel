Coupled method: screening estimate

The model links hull roll to water transfer between connected tank legs. Tank moments feed back into the hull roll equation. Alpha is the tank's quasi-static free-surface moment stiffness divided by the hull's roll restoring. Alpha has no units.

The public model includes vertical-centroid and local free-surface terms in that quasi-static stiffness. Its denominator also includes water redistribution between the legs. Alpha therefore follows the complete public moment convention.

The normalized bare-hull impedance is D = 1 − (T₀/T)² + 2iζ(T₀/T). The coupled impedance is D − αH/Kₛ. H is tank moment per roll angle; Kₛ is its quasi-static limit.

The hull equation uses normalized restoring of 1, with no units. The model sets forcing to give 5° bare roll at the nominal resonance. Forcing stays fixed within each tank-on and tank-off comparison. Forcing changes between hull-damping groups because tank loss depends on amplitude.

The calculation finds each response peak separately over 0.7 to 1.3 times the hull roll period, with no units. It refines all sampled local peaks. Reduction equals 100 % times one minus the coupled peak divided by the bare peak.

Tank basis: screening estimate

Long-period penalty: screening estimate

Slow water transfer reduces the effective hull restoring. In the slow-forcing limit, the roll ratio approaches 1/(1−α), with no units. At alpha 0.05, with no units, that limit gives 5.3 % more roll. A near-resonance reduction does not remove this penalty.

Validity limits: screening estimate

The 18 s cases mildly extrapolate below the moment model's validated band of 20 s and longer. Every sampled forcing period below 20 s lies outside that band, including parts of the 21 s sweep. The published moment fit does not validate coupled hull reduction.

No tank-on and tank-off real-vessel comparison establishes these reductions. No full-scale roll reduction has been calculated here. These screening estimates exclude sea-state weighting, structural loads and design qualification.
