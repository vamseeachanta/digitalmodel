Fixed-platform air-gap analysis

Summary

Subsidence reduces the underside clearance against a supplied design basis. The engine reports dated clearances, first daily breaches, horizontal wave-contact drag and three remediation routes. Results below are for an invented example platform; they do not establish structural acceptance or lifting feasibility.

Clearance and wave contact

Deck elevation = original underside minus cumulative subsidence. Still-water air gap = deck elevation minus HAT minus surge. Crest clearance = still-water air gap minus crest height. Margin = crest clearance minus required clearance. Equality meets clearance; crest clearance at or below zero flags wave contact. Comparisons allow 1e-9 m numerical round-off, which is not an engineering clearance tolerance.

Wave-in-deck screening

Status: screening_only. Method: API RP 2A-WSD, 21st edition, December 2000; Supplement 1, December 2002, C17.6.2; Table C17.6.2-1. The historical edition is identified; applicability to an actual assessment needs confirmation.

For this rectangular screen, A = immersion times weighted area ratio times (width cos heading + orthogonal width sin heading). Immersion is capped at the silhouette depth. Nonuniform framing requires separate area integration. F = 0.5 rho Cd A (aw V + ab U)^2; division by 1000 converts N to kN. V is the unmodified maximum horizontal wave speed at the lower of crest and silhouette top. U is in-line current; opposing/oblique currents are unsupported. Force acts halfway up the immersed envelope. Original underside must coincide with the lowest silhouette elevation; depth includes the solid equipment envelope.

Invented input geometry: width 20.0 m; envelope depth 3.0 m; orthogonal width 0.0 m; heading 0.0 degrees; weighted area ratio 0.5. Invented input wave speed 4.0 m/s; current 0.0 m/s; density 1025.0 kg/m3. Deck classification: moderately_equipped; storm: hurricane. The empty light-framing portion is represented by the uniform half-area ratio; the deck classification is an independent invented assumption for the overall deck.

Remediation options

Target service end: 2032-01-01; governing case: design. Minimum lift restores all supplied target clearances; this example adds 0.2 m allowance. It is not a jack stroke or approval to lift.

live_with_it

  1. Assess local/global wave-in-deck loads and residual structural capacity.
  2. Define weather, manning and shut-in limits through a full risk assessment.
  3. Monitor subsidence and revisit the design basis and operating limits.

raise_topside

Geometric lift including allowance: 2.7 m.

  1. Assess jacket, foundations, topside and interface feasibility.
  2. Design and install temporary supports and jacks at the legs.
  3. Isolate and disconnect affected interfaces under an engineered plan.
  4. Lift in engineered small increments with synchronized monitoring.
  5. Install designed leg extensions and connections.
  6. Transfer load to the permanent structure and inspect connections.
  7. Reconnect interfaces and verify clearances, loads and commissioning.

decommission

  1. Define cessation, isolation, removal and foundation/site obligations.
  2. Develop structural removal and environmental assessment inputs.

Engineering inputs for the business case

  • Survey datum, subsidence forecast uncertainty and target service end date.
  • Return-period levels, crest kinematics and site-specific wave-in-deck assessment.
  • Local and global load paths, jacket/leg/foundation reserve and fatigue life.
  • Topside weight and centre of gravity, jack reactions and temporary support capacity.
  • Riser, conductor, piping, cable, access and shutdown interface constraints.
  • Operating exposure, manning/shut-in criteria and decommissioning constraints.

Costs and NPV are outside the engine. Manning and shut-in limits need risk assessment; the checklist supplies no operating permission or numerical lift procedure.
