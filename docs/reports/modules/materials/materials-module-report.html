1. Executive Summary

The materials module is a library of steel-grade properties and line-pipe section calculations. Analysis is performed to determine the information available for preliminary material selection and bare-pipe quantity estimates; key results are summarized below.

  • Use the documented pipe-size example to obtain section geometry and nominal bare-pipe mass.
    • The computed plain-end mass is 42.5 kg/m. Its difference from the public reference is +0.1 %, within the existing numerical-check tolerance of ±0.3 %; this criterion checks a tabulated mass, not structural adequacy.
  • Keep the strength basis separate from the dimensional catalogue.
    • The selected grade supplies specified minima; procurement qualification and allowable operating pressure are NOT_EVALUATED.
  • Obtain the missing design basis before release for engineering use.
    • Confirm dimensional editions, material certification, tolerances, corrosion allowance and operating loads. The displayed geometric boundary only requires a positive hollow bore.

2. Introduction

Steel-grade lookup and pipe-section calculations are examined as a connected workflow. A public catalogue example is used to show how material identity, dimensions and derived quantities remain distinguishable.

The module stores minimum strengths for line-pipe, hull, structural, austenitic stainless and duplex steels. It also exposes an indexed material database, lower-bound toughness estimates and monotonic true stress–strain curves. Only the grade-to-line-pipe workflow is evaluated numerically in this document.

Nominal pipe size is a catalogue label rather than a measured outside diameter. Product specification level is the quality and testing level attached to a line-pipe strength query; the pipe-section object does not itself certify either level.

3. Basis and criteria

The calculation basis is separated into published nominal geometry, a selected grade and an assumed density. Numerical agreement is judged against existing repository assertions; design acceptance is not assigned.

3.1 Source and material basis

The selected dimensions are the public standard-wall pipe example in the dimensional reference. The independently selected X52 grade is listed in the public grade reference. Their combination demonstrates the module interface; availability of that exact product is not established.

The grade record cites API Specification 5L, Line Pipe, edition 46 (2018), clauses 9.3.1 and 9.3.2, tensile-property tables for product specification levels 1 and 2. The dimensional catalogue cites ASME B36.10M, Welded and Seamless Wrought Steel Pipe; its edition is not recorded. Licensed source tables have not been independently requalified by this analysis.

The mass calculation uses a density of 7850 kg/m³ from the method record: ASSUMED - to be confirmed for the ordered material. The line-pipe mass implementation uses a fixed carbon-steel density; a different grade density is not automatically substituted.

3.2 Acceptance criteria

The catalogue dimensions are required to match the public dimensional entries. The mass comparison uses the existing plain-end mass assertion, with a relative tolerance equivalent to ±0.3 %. This is a rounding and numerical-consistency criterion, not an operating limit. The geometry requires D > 0, t > 0 and t < D/2, where D is outside diameter and t is wall thickness. Equality at the upper boundary removes the hollow bore and is rejected.

Pressure, external collapse, local buckling, fracture, fatigue, weld qualification and corrosion-life criteria are NOT_EVALUATED. No loads, manufacturing tolerances, inspection measurements or material certificates are supplied for this example.

4. Inputs and outputs

A grade and a complete diameter-and-wall specification are required by the public pipe factory. The returned immutable section contains nominal dimensions, a grade record and derived cross-section properties.

4.1 Required inputs

A canonical grade name or recognised alias is supplied. Diameter is entered as nominal pipe size or explicit outside diameter in inches. Wall thickness is entered as a catalogue schedule with nominal pipe size, or as an explicit value in inches. Providing both alternatives for either dimension is rejected; missing or unlisted combinations are rejected.

Grade strengths are stored in megapascals. A tensile-strength query selects the product specification level explicitly; the default query selects level 2. An absent level is rejected rather than replaced with another level. Exact grade identities are retained: a bare line-pipe grade and a hull grade with the same letter remain different materials.

4.2 Returned information

Outputs include inside diameter, diameter-to-wall ratio, metal area, bore area, plain-end mass per length and bore volume per length. Metric dimensions and customary mass units are available. The text product reference is an identifier assembled by the module; it is not a certificate of compliance.

The indexed database adds source citations, nullable edition fields and explicit missing links to other material datasets. A matching source fingerprint establishes agreement with the inspected repository data only. Original-standard qualification and purchase-order suitability are separate requirements.

5. Theory and equations

A concentric, uniform circular section is used to calculate the geometry. Mass per length is obtained by multiplying metal area by the fixed implementation density.

5.1 Section relationships

Inside diameter is denoted d, metal area Am, bore area Ab, density ρ, mass per length m′ and bore volume per length V′. All lengths in each equation use the same unit.

d = D − 2t
Am = π(D² − d²)/4 = πt(D − t)
Ab = πd²/4
m′ = ρAm
V′ = Ab
Diameter-to-wall ratio = D/t

These are annular and circular area identities, implemented by the method record; π is the circle constant, and the factors follow directly from the radius-to-diameter relation. Areas are converted to square metres before multiplying by density. Bore volume is reported in litres per metre using the metric volume relation in the unit reference.

5.2 Representation

The cross-section is shown with the calculated wall-to-diameter relationship. No pressure boundary condition, finite-element mesh, coating, contents, weld reinforcement, end fittings or corrosion loss is modelled.

6. Worked example and results

The public example is computed by calling the module with nominal pipe size 8, schedule 40 and grade X52. The size and schedule reproduce the dimensional reference; the grade is selected independently from the public grade reference.

The module returns outside diameter 8.625 in and wall thickness 0.322 in, with inside diameter 7.981 in. Plain-end mass is 28.6 lb/ft, equivalent to 42.5 kg/m. These are nominal bare-pipe quantities.

Every numerical result is generated from the module call; the complete output is given in Tables A.1a and A.1b. The figure varies explicit wall thickness at the same outside diameter to illustrate the hollow-section constraint. Sensitivity sections are mathematical examples, not available catalogue products.

The upper wall boundary is 109.537 mm. A wall at or above that value is outside the hollow-section domain; no bore capacity is reported there.

7. Validation evidence

Repository validation is assessed from the shipped tests and public references. The evidence supports numerical consistency for the exercised cases, with the qualification limits stated below.

The line-pipe tests compare nominal outside diameters and published plain-end masses, check area-to-mass consistency, exercise schedule aliases and reject ordinary missing, conflicting and invalid geometry. The grade tests check published minima, distinguish hull and line-pipe aliases and preserve legacy conversions. The database tests check identity, nullable edition metadata and source fingerprint behaviour.

The selected outside diameter, wall and bore match the public dimensional table. The computed mass differs from the public mass by +0.1 %, meeting the existing ±0.3 % numerical criterion. The public dimensional page is internally mixed in its product-standard labelling; it is used only as a dimensional and mass reference. The selected grade minima agree with the public supplier table; this is not independent certification against the licensed specification.

The existing toughness tests exercise equation anchors, material-class restrictions and input units; the stress–strain tests exercise yield, hardening and curve-table relationships. These test records do not establish material-specific fracture performance. No measured pipe, mill certificate, laboratory curve or field benchmark is held for this example. Historical validation notes are retained as supporting records, but current source and test assertions govern this report.

8. Limits and recommendations

Use is limited to nominal material lookup and circular-section calculations within the stated geometry. Engineering release remains conditional on a supplied design basis and qualified material data.

8.1 Recommended actions

For preliminary quantities, retain the bare-pipe basis and identify the density as assumed. Before procurement, confirm the dimensional-standard edition, product level, delivery condition and mill certification. Before pressure or structural assessment, supply tolerances, corrosion allowance, temperature, pressure and other loads to the applicable design assessment. This follows the missing inputs and unqualified fields identified in the data register and material qualification record.

Reject non-finite dimensions before calling the pipe factory; its comparison-only checks do not establish rejection of every non-finite value. Restrict this catalogue workflow to its intended line-pipe grade family: the factory also accepts other material records, yet its generated product reference retains the line-pipe label and its mass uses the fixed density. Source: method record. A positive hollow bore alone does not establish a manufacturable or structurally adequate section.

Use canonical grade and explicit product-level queries for new work. Legacy adapters intentionally preserve earlier conversion conventions; equality with the canonical minimum is not established for every adapter. Review the adapter basis before combining its outputs with canonical values.

8.2 Not covered

Hull and general structural-grade selection, stainless and duplex product suitability, fracture-toughness estimation, monotonic stress–strain curve generation, fatigue-curve associations and structural-shape associations are not evaluated. Temperature-dependent strength reduction is not applied by the grade lookup. Toughness and hardening require their own measured or qualified inputs; no such inputs are inferred from grade identity.

9. References and evidence

Public references and repository records are identified separately. Web references were inspected on 2026-10-21; an absent edition remains an evidence gap.

9.1 Source qualification

Manufacturer and supplier publications are used as openly accessible numerical references, not as substitutes for the applicable licensed standard or purchase specification. The dimensional page is undated; no dimensional-standard edition is assigned. Repository records describe implementation and test coverage, not original-document approval.

10.2 Quality checks and document comments

The existing materials tests pass at the time of issue. Rebuild checks compare every marked example quantity and chart point with fresh module calls, require deterministic output and detect a changed module result. Style inlining, local links, source-fingerprint mismatch rejection and visible wording are checked. These checks establish reproducibility of this numerical example; browser interaction and material-source qualification are separate evidence.

The public reference mass is 28.55 lb/ft. Its signed percentage difference is calculated from unrounded values as 100 × (computed − reference) / reference. The nominal geometry uses the stated positive hollow-bore domain. No numerical result outside that domain is returned.
