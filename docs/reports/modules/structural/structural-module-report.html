1. Executive Summary

The structural module is a collection of analytical stress, stability, strength and fatigue calculations. Analysis is performed to determine the membrane stresses in a documented closed-end cylinder example; key results are summarized below.

  • The membrane stress calculation can be reproduced for the public cylinder.
    • With the source diameter interpreted as the internal diameter, the hoop stress is 413.7 MPa and the equivalent stress is 358.3 MPa at 10.3 MPa internal pressure. The diameter convention is ASSUMED - to be confirmed. Detailed results.
  • A pressure rating cannot be assigned from this example.
    • Material strength and the governing allowable-stress criterion are required inputs. Strength acceptance is NOT_EVALUATED. Design criteria.

2. Introduction

The structural module is used to calculate stresses and selected structural resistances from supplied geometry, materials and loads. Its public stress interface is assessed here through the documented calculation classes and their existing unit tests. Calculation records.

Stress recovery is the main workflow covered. Pressure, internal radius and wall thickness are supplied; hoop and longitudinal stresses are returned. The resulting stress tensor is then used to obtain equivalent stress, principal stresses and maximum shear stress. The calculation does not determine the pressure or the structural boundary conditions. Interface records.

The published cylinder problem provides an open calculation basis without a project-specific component. Its radial displacement question is outside the worked example because elastic constants are not supplied. Separate strength, buckling and fatigue assessments remain necessary for a component design. Public example.

3. Design Data and Criteria

A uniform cylinder with closed ends is assessed under internal pressure. The source dimensions and pressure are retained in the appendix, and the diameter is interpreted as the internal diameter for this calculation. Source basis.

3.1 Inputs and outputs

Pressure is supplied in megapascals and both radius and thickness in metres. Membrane stresses are returned in megapascals. The tensor interface accepts and returns stresses in the same units; megapascals are retained throughout this example. Other structural interfaces use different unit conventions, so their input definitions remain controlling. Interface records.

The material object required by the stress calculator is stored by the constructor but is not read by the selected membrane stress methods. The supplied material preset therefore contributes no strength value to this example. Material acceptance is assessed separately from stress recovery. Implementation record.

3.2 Criteria and rationale

The calculation criterion is equilibrium of a thin, uniform cylindrical shell under differential internal pressure. The pressure is treated as internal pressure relative to the exterior, with tensile normal stress positive. The closed ends transmit pressure thrust to the shell. These conditions are required for the longitudinal membrane equation. Pressure Vessels, Stresses.

The wall-to-radius ratio is 2.5 %. The source requires a radius large relative to thickness but does not specify a numerical acceptance ratio. This reported ratio is a geometry descriptor, not a pass criterion. Material allowable stress, its temperature basis and the governing design rules are missing; strength acceptance is NOT_EVALUATED. Thin-wall basis.

4. Methodology

Membrane stresses are calculated from force equilibrium, and stress invariants are calculated from the assembled symmetric stress tensor. The public equations are applied through the module rather than reimplemented as reported results. Membrane basis; equivalent-stress basis.

4.1 Membrane equilibrium

The hoop and axial stresses are σθ = pr/t and σa = pr/(2t), respectively. Here p is differential internal pressure, r is the internal radius and t is the uniform wall thickness. The coefficient in the axial equation follows from closed-end force equilibrium. Pressure Vessels, equations (2) and (3), 2001 edition.

The local axes are axial x, circumferential y and radial z. Normal stresses σx and σy are assigned the axial and hoop results. Radial stress and all shear components are set to zero in the membrane idealisation. The source identifies the neglected radial stress as the reason that the thin-wall condition is required. Thin-wall discussion.

4.2 Stress tensor reduction

The equivalent stress is σe = √{[(σx − σy)² + (σy − σz)² + (σz − σx)² + 6(τxy² + τxz² + τyz²)]/2}. The symbols τ denote the shear components on the named planes. This is a stress measure; a yield criterion also requires material strength. Yield and Plastic Flow, Multiaxial stress states, 2001 edition.

Principal stresses σ1 ≥ σ2 ≥ σ3 are the eigenvalues of the symmetric stress tensor. Maximum shear stress is (σ1 − σ3)/2. The radial principal stress is retained when finding the maximum shear; restricting the comparison to the tangential plane omits the governing shear plane for this loading. Tensor basis; closed-cylinder shear example.

Hoop stress governs the normal-stress components in this load case. The equivalent stress remains below the hoop stress because both tangential normal stresses are tensile. This ordering describes the calculated demand and does not establish a margin to material strength. Stress-state basis.

6. Validation

The repository evidence consists of analytical unit tests, documented examples and the rebuilt public example. This evidence checks numerical behaviour; no measured cylinder response or independent pressure-rating benchmark is supplied for the worked example. Evidence records.

The existing unit tests cover uniaxial stress, pure shear, principal-stress ordering, maximum shear and hoop and longitudinal stress equations. They also exercise the separate plate, column and member-capacity calculations. Those broader tests do not establish the suitability of this cylinder for service. Existing test record.

The accompanying example test compares every reported tensor output and every pressure sample with a fresh module call. Independent equilibrium expressions and a closed-form equivalent-stress expression provide additional numerical comparators. The hoop-stress difference relative to the equilibrium reference is 0.0 %. This is an equation comparison, not a measured validation uncertainty. Rebuild evidence.

The legacy command-line tests require the installed command-line entry point. That executable is absent from the calculation environment used for this issue; those tests did not reach the calculations. The worked example uses the documented Python interface. Command-line evidence.

7. Limits of Use and Not Covered

The membrane equations are restricted to a thin, uniform cylinder away from geometric discontinuities. Local bending, through-wall stress variation, openings, weld details and end-closure stresses are outside this idealisation. Membrane assumptions.

The selected stress functions do not reject zero or negative wall thickness, negative radius or non-finite pressure. Dimensional consistency and finite, physically meaningful inputs are therefore required before use. No equivalent-stress limit or material acceptance flag is returned by these stress functions. Implementation record.

7.1 Not covered

The module also contains plate and stiffened-panel buckling, column and member capacity, pressure and wall-thickness strategies, pipe cross-section weight and buoyancy, fatigue counting and damage, spectral fatigue, nonlinear stress response, finite-element adapters, connection checks, parachute frames, offshore containers and lifecycle screening. These sub-areas have distinct assumptions and interfaces; their engineering qualification is not established by this stress example. Scope records.

Installation dynamics, rigging, environmental response and sea-state limits are outside this calculation. No installation decision is issued. Radial displacement is not calculated because the elastic constants required by the public problem are missing. Displacement basis.

8. Recommendations

The stress result is suitable for reviewing the stated membrane equations and interface units. Design use requires the missing material and geometric evidence to be resolved before a pressure limit is assigned. Applicability basis; strength basis.

Confirmation of the diameter convention is recommended as the first engineering action. A component drawing or dimensional record is required to identify the inner radius and effective wall thickness. The present result remains conditional on that convention. Radius definition.

Selection of an applicable design code, edition, allowable stress at temperature, corrosion allowance, weld basis and load combinations is recommended before pressure-rating assessment. Material yield alone does not cover all pressure-component failure modes. No numerical pressure limit is issued until that criterion is supplied. Material-strength dependency.

An independent analytical benchmark with fully specified geometry, material and acceptance criteria is recommended for each additional sub-area selected for use. Guarding the stress interface against non-finite and non-physical inputs is recommended before automated assessment of externally supplied data. The maintainer can use the existing tests and the public example as the initial regression basis. Input behaviour; example checks.

9.2 Calculation and evidence locators

The public Python interface is documented in the structural-analysis guide and exported by the public package. The selected methods are StressCalculator.hoop_stress and StressCalculator.longitudinal_stress in the stress calculator; tensor methods are StressState.von_mises, principal_stresses and max_shear in the stress models.

Existing evidence: analytical unit tests, command-line tests, and the command-line entry-point definition. Additional stress and nonlinear tests exist in the stress test directory; they are outside the numerical example.

Module boundaries are identified by the structural source tree and the routing registry. These are implementation inventories rather than evidence of compliance with any design standard.

The retained input record is public-cylinder.json. The report generator calls the public module and embeds the exact calculation data; the report test rebuilds the HTML and checks numerical equality. The existing assessment report supplies the inlined stylesheet and page layout.

from digitalmodel.structural.structural_analysis import (
    StressCalculator, StressState, STEEL_S355,
)

# p, r and t are read from the documented input record and converted.
calculator = StressCalculator(STEEL_S355)
hoop = calculator.hoop_stress(p, r, t)
axial = calculator.longitudinal_stress(p, r, t)
state = StressState(sigma_x=axial, sigma_y=hoop)
equivalent = state.von_mises()
principals = state.principal_stresses()
maximum_shear = state.max_shear()

10.2 Symbols and status legend

p denotes differential internal pressure; r, internal radius; t, wall thickness; σ, normal stress; τ, shear stress. Subscripts θ and a denote circumferential and axial directions; x, y and z denote the local axial, circumferential and radial axes. Subscript e denotes equivalent stress; ordered subscripts 1, 2 and 3 denote principal stresses. MPa denotes megapascal; m denotes metre; mm denotes millimetre; in denotes inch; psi denotes pound-force per square inch.

Strength acceptance is NOT_EVALUATED when the material criterion is missing. The diameter convention is ASSUMED - to be confirmed. Zero radial stress is a membrane idealisation. These statements apply to the figure, table and interactive readout.
