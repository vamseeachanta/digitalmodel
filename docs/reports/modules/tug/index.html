Tug analysis: duty-cycle energy and hybrid sizing

Draft for maintainer review

1 Executive Summary

The tug analysis module is a set of preliminary performance calculations. Analysis is performed to determine the ideal energy and battery discharge required by a public duty-cycle example; key results are summarized below.

  • The duty cycle can be used as a calculation example.
    • The cycle requires 13,200.000 kWh, with peak demand of 3,600.000 kW.
    • At the assumed engine baseline of 2,000.000 kW, peak battery discharge is 1,600.000 kW and discharge energy is 1,600.000 kWh.
  • A battery specification cannot be selected from these results alone.
    • Recharge, conversion losses, reserve and ageing are not evaluated; installed battery capacity remains NOT_EVALUATED.
    • No tug operating approval is established.

The numerical basis is the public example; the interpretation is conditional on the data register.

2 Introduction

Preliminary tug calculations are provided for bollard pull, escort force, girting stability, fender contact and energy, towline loads, and energy and emissions. The energy workflow is evaluated here because its public example can be checked directly by an energy balance without adopting unsourced empirical coefficients.

The example is a software regression case, not measured operating data. No named craft, equipment selection, drawings or field measurements are represented. Entry points and evidence locators are given in the source register.

4 Design data and assessment basis

The public example supplies installed propulsion power of 4,000 kW and an engine baseline fraction of 0.5. These are illustrative inputs, marked ASSUMED - to be confirmed for any physical application.

The duty cycle contains transit, assist and standby phases. The complete phase inputs are recorded in Table A. The engine baseline is deliberately below the peak demand to demonstrate battery discharge during assist.

4.1 Criteria and rationale

The calculation criterion is agreement with the piecewise constant energy balance: energy is power integrated over time. The reference is the public power definition. Agreement tests arithmetic, not equipment suitability. A physical acceptance criterion requires a battery discharge rating and usable capacity after reserve, loss and ageing allowances; those data are absent.

5 Methodology and equations

Phase demand is calculated from installed power and the phase power fraction. Phase energy is calculated by integrating that constant demand over its duration, following the power definition.

Pj = Pinstalled fj; Ecycle = Σ Pj Δtj.

P denotes power in kW; j identifies a phase; f is the dimensionless phase power fraction; Δt is phase duration in h; E is energy in kWh; Σ denotes summation over the phases. The installed-power relation defines the input fraction, rather than introducing an empirical coefficient.

Pbase = b Pinstalled; Pbattery,j = max(Pj − Pbase, 0); Ebattery = Σ Pbattery,j Δtj.

b is the assumed dimensionless engine baseline fraction. The positive difference assigns only demand above the baseline to battery discharge. This dispatch assumption is combined with the same energy integral; it is not a class rule. Peak demand and peak battery power are the maximum phase values. Battery energy share is discharge energy divided by cycle energy. The equations are implemented by the documented energy functions.

The model does not credit charging between discharge phases. Energy above the baseline is accumulated without a time-dependent state of charge. The output therefore represents total ideal discharge over the selected cycle, not necessarily the minimum battery capacity with managed recharge.

6 Worked example and results

The public regression example is evaluated by calling the module with the shared illustrative duty-cycle inputs. Each phase is also evaluated separately to obtain the plotted power demand and battery contribution.

The cycle requires 13,200.000 kWh, with peak demand of 3,600.000 kW. At the assumed engine baseline of 2,000.000 kW, peak battery discharge is 1,600.000 kW and discharge energy is 1,600.000 kWh. All returned sizing quantities are recorded in Table B.

Assist governs peak demand and battery discharge. Transit and standby do not exceed the chosen baseline; that condition establishes neither available charging power nor a recharge schedule. Battery share is reported as a fraction in Table B, not as a fuel-saving percentage.

7 Validation evidence

Existing regression checks are inspected as software evidence. The tug test file contains 34 test definitions distributed across the calculation areas in Table C; this inventory is not a count of field trials or passed tests.

The energy tests contain literal expected cycle energy and peak-shaving results. Other checks exercise propulsion dependence, force scaling, stability boundaries, fender overlap and energy, towline sizing, and emissions. No measured battery discharge record or separately published hybrid benchmark is identified in the inspected tug source, tests and documentation.

The report checks compare all sizing fields with fresh module calls, compare displayed values at their printed precision, and include hand-calculated energy, peak power, baseline and battery-share anchors. A separate case uses an assist duration of 2.5 h to check the duration factor in discharge energy. Numerical tolerance is applied to arithmetic comparisons. Field validation remains NOT_EVALUATED; confirmation requires measured power and time histories at a declared electrical or mechanical boundary.

8 Findings, limits and recommendations

The calculation establishes an ideal discharge demand for the assumed profile. Installed battery capacity remains not evaluated because its governing data are absent.

8.1 Recommendations

A measured phase profile at the selected power boundary is recommended before equipment sizing, because energy depends directly on phase power and duration under the energy integral. A time-dependent charge and discharge assessment with efficiency, usable capacity, reserve and ageing inputs is recommended before selecting a battery, because those terms are absent from the sizing calculation.

Input validation is recommended at the application boundary. The underlying energy function checks installed power and an empty profile, but does not reject negative phase durations, out-of-range phase fractions or all non-finite inputs. This report checks the selected profile before calculation. The module is not modified by this assessment.

8.2 Not covered

Bollard pull and escort estimates, girting stability, fender optimization and berthing energy, towline strength and recoil, and fuel and emissions are not evaluated numerically here. Their implementations include empirical coefficients or simplified criteria whose cited source editions and clauses are incomplete in the inspected code. Those values require a separate basis review before engineering use. No nominal class factor is adopted in this example.
