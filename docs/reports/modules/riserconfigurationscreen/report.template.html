1. Executive summary

The steel catenary riser is a suspended pipe with a mobile touchdown and a grounded tail. Analysis is performed to determine the static configuration ranking under prescribed horizontal excursions, and the key results are summarized below.

  • The public synthetic example provides a provisional configuration shortlist.

    • The static utilisation chart below compares each configuration with the assumed limit of [[settings.constraints.max_static_utilisation:2]]. A point below this limit alone does not establish suitability for service.

    • Fatigue damage and life are not computed. The curvature range is a ranking proxy.

2. Introduction

Riser analysis is performed for the public synthetic steel catenary example at a water depth of [[settings.water_depth_m:1]] m. A static configuration is screened before any detailed environmental or operational assessment is attempted.

The module ranks an explicit grid of initial hangoff angles and retains configurations that violate a criterion. The return value contains shapes, forces, constraint margins and a provisional shortlist. This report uses the module's International System of Units throughout to preserve the published input contract; it is not a field operating report in customary units.

4. Analysis methodology

The inextensible catenary is solved in a vertical plane with a mobile touchdown and a frictionless grounded tail. The static reference equations are taken from the public mooring-system formulation identified in section 9.1, in its infinite axial-stiffness limit.

4.1 Equilibrium and fixed length

Let h denote water depth, w submerged weight per unit length, H horizontal tension, a the catenary parameter H/w, s suspended arc length, x horizontal distance from touchdown, L total installed length and g grounded length. Let α denote the top tangent angle from vertical. The equations are:

a = h sin α / (1 − sin α); s = √(h² + 2ah); x = a asinh(s/a); g = L − s; T = w(a + h).

Here T is top tension and asinh is the inverse hyperbolic sine. The suspended profile is z = a[cosh(x/a) − 1], with elevation z measured from the seabed. These expressions follow from the suspended-segment equations with zero vertical force at touchdown and infinite axial stiffness. The public source and the recorded equation check are identified in section 9.1.

At an excursion, a is varied until x + g equals the new horizontal endpoint separation while L remains fixed. A transverse excursion changes that separation by the planar distance formula; the returned shape remains in the radial plane. Three-dimensional bending is not represented.

4.2 Ranking and stress screen

Touchdown curvature is w/H. The objective is the largest excursion curvature range at touchdown or, for a lazy-wave configuration, at the sag bend. Feasible configurations are ranked first, in ascending objective order. Ties are resolved by ascending grid coordinates. Violated and unavailable configurations remain visible in the returned table.

The stress screen samples the calculated shape, adds pressure thrust to effective tension, and combines axial, elastic curvature-bending and thin-wall hoop stresses using a plane-stress von Mises expression. The public mechanics references and implementation locator are given in section 9.1. With area A, wall thickness t, outside diameter D, Young’s modulus E, local curvature κ, wall tension T_wall and yield strength σ_y, the implemented expressions are:

σ_a = T_wall/A ± EκD/2; σ_h = (p_i − p_o)D/(2t); U = √(σ_a² + σ_h² − σ_aσ_h)/σ_y.

The bending term follows from linear elastic strain ε = κD/2 and σ = Eε. Radial and shear stresses are omitted. The references support the component equations, not an independent validation of their combined implementation. The output remains a software screening indicator with no design-code resistance factor or compliance claim.

The underlying catenary is not a beam model. Bending stiffness is absent from equilibrium even though curvature is used to estimate a bending stress. The lazy-wave route preserves buoyancy at fixed material arc coordinates; that route is not the worked example.

4.3 Static benchmark and loading order

A separate analytical check uses an assumed depth of 100 m, a top angle of 30 degrees from vertical, submerged weight of 10 N/m and grounded length of 50 m. Since sin α equals one half, a equals the depth; H equals weight times depth, top tension equals twice H, and touchdown curvature equals the inverse of the depth. The calculated values are [[anchor.horizontal_tension_n:1]] N, [[anchor.top_tension_n:1]] N and [[anchor.touchdown_curvature_per_m:4]] per metre respectively. Literal hand values are asserted in the report tests.

The existing module test also compares a bare catenary with a separately evaluated closed-form expression. This establishes an ideal-catenary numerical check, not a field validation. Still water is the only load basis evaluated here. Current loading, waves plus current, and host-motion dynamics are not subsequent stages of this module.

The hand reference and the module values for horizontal tension, top tension and touchdown curvature {{ANCHOR_AGREEMENT}} within a relative tolerance of 1e-7. This numerical test tolerance is not a design allowance.

5.2 Not covered

Statics with current and environmental operating envelopes are not evaluated. Wave and host-motion dynamics, vortex-induced vibration, fatigue life, installation loads, hangoff component stresses, seabed interaction and local buckling are not evaluated. Environmental records, qualified host excursions and component acceptance data are absent.

The lazy-wave configuration route, buoyancy sensitivity and its larger example grid are not reproduced in this report. Their existing tests are identified in section 9.2. No claim is made that this assessment qualifies those sub-areas for service.

6. Key findings

The static screening result is a provisional choice of initial geometry under synthetic inputs. The first-ranked angle of [[result.ranked_candidates.0.configuration.hangoff_angle_deg:1]] degrees is a grid choice, not a continuously optimised solution. The curvature range decreases monotonically across the sampled grid, from 1.8803e-3 per metre at 8 degrees to 4.9461e-4 per metre at 20 degrees. The selected angle is the upper grid boundary: the minimum is not bracketed. Extend the grid within qualified geometry and component limits before claiming an optimum.

The criterion margins are evaluated over the supplied positions only. An unexamined position between or beyond those positions is not demonstrated to meet the criteria. An allowable host-excursion envelope and structural capacity under environmental loading remain unestablished.

7. Limitations

The calculation is limited by its assumed inputs and ideal cable mechanics. Elastic extension, seabed friction, contact stiffness, local ovalisation, pressure-dependent stability, hydrodynamic loading and host rotations are excluded from the static geometry.

The stress estimate samples the shape and does not prove that every local maximum has been captured. Positive criterion margins are conditional on the supplied thresholds and material values. The synthetic zero-pressure case cannot represent an operating submerged pipe without additional pressure and contents data.

No measured response, physical test, independent beam-model result or field qualification for this example is held in the module assessment. The repository's adjacent closed-form benchmark is supporting mathematical context, not a native result for this example. Evidence from that separate benchmark is not substituted for missing validation.

8. Recommendations

The provisional shortlist is suitable for selecting candidates for further assessment. Acceptance for service remains conditional on qualified input data and a separate environmental, structural and operational assessment.

Replace the assumed pipe, pressure, weight and acceptance data with the design basis before interpreting feasibility. Establish pressure variation, actual material resistance, component bending limits and installation geometry. The rationale is the input-dependent screening contract described in sections 3 and 4.

Assess still-water equilibrium against the analytical equations first, then introduce current, waves and host-motion response in a qualified model. Establish allowable host excursions and component acceptance limits before installation or in-service decisions. This sequence addresses the load and operating information absent from the present calculation.

For any criterion violation, exclude the candidate from the provisional shortlist and reassess geometry or the governing input against the same explicit limit. An unavailable result requires resolution of the missing data or failed shape closure. Reducing the curvature proxy alone does not resolve a stress or operational limit.

Appendix B. Numerical assurance and interpretation

The report checks compare displayed values and chart data with a fresh module call. The analytical anchor checks horizontal tension, top tension and touchdown curvature against literal hand calculations. Independent fixed-length hand values also check the first-ranked angle, curvature range, radial top tensions and grounded lengths, and the far-excursion utilisation. The existing ideal-catenary check compares tension, suspended length, span and angle.

Shape and fixed-buoyancy tests address numerical invariants. Export tests address preservation of the screening basis. These checks do not establish environmental performance or design acceptance. Test execution results accompany the technical delivery rather than being inferred from the existence of test files.

Displayed inputs retain the supplied significant digits. Result tables use engineering display precision; the embedded payload includes only plotted fields at fixed decimal precision (forces to 0.1 N, lengths to 0.000001 m, curvature to 12 decimal places and utilisation to 8 decimal places). Closure diagnostics remain in the workflow export. Tests perturb depth by one unit in the last place in both directions and perturb closure margins to check byte-identical presentation. Sub-nanometre numerical residues are not engineering results and are omitted from the visual interpretation.
