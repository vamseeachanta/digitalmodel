1. Executive summary

The steel catenary riser is a suspended pipe with a mobile touchdown and a grounded tail. Analysis is performed to determine the static configuration ranking under prescribed horizontal excursions, and the key results are summarized below.

  • The public synthetic example provides a provisional configuration shortlist.
    • The first-ranked initial hangoff angle is 20.0 degrees from vertical, with an excursion curvature range of 0.00049461 per metre. The ranking applies only to the supplied static criteria.
    • The static utilisation chart below compares each configuration with the assumed limit of 1.00. A point below this limit alone does not establish suitability for service.
  • Environmental response and service acceptance are NOT_EVALUATED.
    • Current, wave and host-motion loading are not supplied. The static excursion grid does not establish an SCR operating envelope or acceptance for service.
    • Fatigue damage and life are not computed. The curvature range is a ranking proxy.

2. Introduction

Riser analysis is performed for the public synthetic steel catenary example at a water depth of 1500.0 m. A static configuration is screened before any detailed environmental or operational assessment is attempted.

The module ranks an explicit grid of initial hangoff angles and retains configurations that violate a criterion. The return value contains shapes, forces, constraint margins and a provisional shortlist. This report uses the module's International System of Units throughout to preserve the published input contract; it is not a field operating report in customary units.

3.3 Excursions and criteria

The near, initial, far and cross excursions are static positions. The radial offsets are −50.0, 0.0 and 50.0 m. The cross excursion is 0.0 m along the initial span and 50.0 m transverse to it. The original pipe length is preserved at each position.

Top tension is limited to 5000000 N and bend radius is limited from below by 50.0 m. These thresholds are illustrative input criteria, not limits taken from a design standard. The static utilisation threshold divides an elastic stress estimate by the supplied yield strength. It is used to reject highly stressed candidates before detailed assessment; it does not include a design-code resistance factor.

Hog clearance is not applicable to the bare steel catenary. The lazy-wave route additionally checks net buoyancy and hog elevation above the sag bend. Numerical closure is checked internally; the tolerance is an algorithm setting, not an engineering acceptance band.

4. Analysis methodology

The inextensible catenary is solved in a vertical plane with a mobile touchdown and a frictionless grounded tail. The static reference equations are taken from the public mooring-system formulation identified in section 9.1, in its infinite axial-stiffness limit.

4.1 Equilibrium and fixed length

Let h denote water depth, w submerged weight per unit length, H horizontal tension, a the catenary parameter H/w, s suspended arc length, x horizontal distance from touchdown, L total installed length and g grounded length. Let α denote the top tangent angle from vertical. The equations are:

a = h sin α / (1 − sin α); s = √(h² + 2ah); x = a asinh(s/a); g = L − s; T = w(a + h).

Here T is top tension and asinh is the inverse hyperbolic sine. The suspended profile is z = a[cosh(x/a) − 1], with elevation z measured from the seabed. These expressions follow from the suspended-segment equations with zero vertical force at touchdown and infinite axial stiffness. The public source and the recorded equation check are identified in section 9.1.

At an excursion, a is varied until x + g equals the new horizontal endpoint separation while L remains fixed. A transverse excursion changes that separation by the planar distance formula; the returned shape remains in the radial plane. Three-dimensional bending is not represented.

4.2 Ranking and stress screen

Touchdown curvature is w/H. The objective is the largest excursion curvature range at touchdown or, for a lazy-wave configuration, at the sag bend. Feasible configurations are ranked first, in ascending objective order. Ties are resolved by ascending grid coordinates. Violated and unavailable configurations remain visible in the returned table.

The stress screen samples the calculated shape and combines axial force, elastic curvature-bending and thin-wall hoop stresses. The zero-pressure example uses pipe metal area A = π[D² − (D − 2t)²]/4, longitudinal stress σa = T/A ± EκD/2 and hoop stress σh = 0. Both bending signs are examined. The plane-stress identity is σvm = √(σa² + σh² − σaσh), and utilisation is the largest sampled σvm divided by the supplied yield strength. The elastic bending relation and von Mises identity are supported by the public teaching references in section 9.1; the input values are assumptions from Table B.

The complete application to an ideal cable, finite-pressure response and local component stresses remains NOT_EVALUATED. The stress result is a software screening indicator and does not establish design-code compliance. Curvature along the ideal SCR follows κ = wH/T², derived by differentiating the tangent angle under vertical equilibrium; this geometric reduction does not include bending stiffness.

The underlying catenary is not a beam model. Bending stiffness is absent from equilibrium even though curvature is used to estimate a bending stress. The lazy-wave route preserves buoyancy at fixed material arc coordinates; that route is not the worked example.

4.3 Static benchmark and loading order

A separate analytical check uses an assumed depth of 100 m, a top angle of 30 degrees from vertical, submerged weight of 10 N/m and grounded length of 50 m. Since sin α equals one half, a equals the depth; H equals weight times depth, top tension equals twice H, and touchdown curvature equals the inverse of the depth. The calculated values are 1000.0 N, 2000.0 N and 0.0100 per metre respectively. Literal hand values are asserted in the report tests.

The existing module test also compares a bare catenary with a separately evaluated closed-form expression. This establishes an ideal-catenary numerical check, not a field validation. Still water is the only load basis evaluated here. Current loading, waves plus current, and host-motion dynamics are not subsequent stages of this module.

Horizontal tension, top tension and touchdown curvature agree with the hand references within a relative tolerance of 1e-7, checked before display rounding. The numerical tolerance is an assurance criterion, not a design allowance; insignificant floating-point residuals are not reported as physical results.

5.2 Not covered

Current and wave loading, host-motion response, vortex-induced vibration, touchdown-zone cyclic bending, weld fatigue, seabed interaction, local buckling and installation loads are NOT_EVALUATED. No environmental time series, soil properties, fatigue curves or qualified component resistance data are supplied. Service acceptance requires those inputs and separate assessments against the governing criteria.

The lazy-wave configuration route, buoyancy sensitivity and its larger example grid are not reproduced in this report. Their existing tests are identified in section 9.2. No claim is made that this assessment qualifies those sub-areas for service.

6. Key findings

The static screening result is a provisional choice of initial geometry under synthetic inputs. The first-ranked angle of 20.0 degrees is a grid choice, not a continuously optimised solution.

The objective decreases monotonically over the supplied initial-angle grid from 8 to 20 degrees, with curvature ranges of 0.00188028 and 0.00049461 per metre respectively (Table C, synthetic input grid in section 9.2). The selected 20-degree point is the upper grid boundary: the minimum is not bracketed. Extension of the grid requires a qualified geometric and operational basis.

The criterion margins are evaluated over the supplied positions only. An unexamined position between or beyond those positions is not demonstrated to meet the criteria. Environmental response and an SCR operating envelope remain NOT_EVALUATED.

7. Limitations

The calculation is limited by its assumed inputs and ideal cable mechanics. Elastic extension, seabed friction, contact stiffness, local ovalisation, pressure-dependent stability, hydrodynamic loading and host rotations are excluded from the static geometry.

The stress estimate samples the shape and does not prove that every local maximum has been captured. Positive criterion margins are conditional on the supplied thresholds and material values. The synthetic zero-pressure case cannot represent an operating submerged pipe without additional pressure and contents data.

No measured response, physical test, independent beam-model result or field qualification for this example is held in the module assessment. The repository's adjacent closed-form benchmark is supporting mathematical context, not a native result for this example. Evidence from that separate benchmark is not substituted for missing validation.

8. Recommendations

The provisional shortlist is suitable for selecting candidates for further assessment. Acceptance for service remains conditional on qualified input data and a separate environmental, structural and operational assessment.

Replace the assumed pipe, pressure, weight and acceptance data with the design basis before interpreting feasibility. Establish pressure variation, actual material resistance, component bending limits and installation geometry. The rationale is the input-dependent screening contract described in sections 3 and 4.

Assess still-water equilibrium against the analytical equations first, then introduce current, waves and host-motion response in a qualified beam model. Establish touchdown-zone fatigue, termination stresses, seabed interaction and installation load cases against the applicable design basis. This sequence addresses the SCR load and response information absent from the present calculation.

For any criterion violation, exclude the candidate from the provisional shortlist and reassess geometry or the governing input against the same explicit limit. An unavailable result requires resolution of the missing data or failed shape closure. Reducing the curvature proxy alone does not resolve a stress or operational limit.

10. Acronyms and symbols

Abbreviations and mathematical symbols are defined here for reading the calculation. A stress utilisation is dimensionless; curvature is inverse length.

SCR means steel catenary riser. SLWR means steel lazy-wave riser. SI means International System of Units. NREL denotes the National Renewable Energy Laboratory. NOT_EVALUATED means that the evidence needed for a result is absent. It does not mean a zero result.

H is horizontal tension; T is total top tension; w is submerged weight per unit length; h is water depth; a is catenary parameter; s is suspended arc length; x is distance from touchdown; z is elevation above seabed; L is installed length; g is grounded length; α is top tangent angle from vertical. N denotes newton, m metre and Pa pascal.

Appendix B. Numerical assurance and interpretation

The report checks compare displayed values and chart data with a fresh module call. The analytical anchor checks horizontal tension, top tension and touchdown curvature against literal hand calculations. Separate hand-worked assertions pin the first-ranked angle and curvature range, three excursion tensions and grounded lengths, and the far-position hangoff utilisation. The existing ideal-catenary check compares tension, suspended length, span and angle.

Shape and fixed-buoyancy tests address numerical invariants. Export tests address preservation of the screening basis. These checks do not establish environmental performance or design acceptance. Test execution results accompany the technical delivery rather than being inferred from the existence of test files.

Displayed inputs retain the supplied significant digits. Embedded plot fields use fixed decimal precision: forces to 0.1 N, geometry to 0.000001 m, curvature to 1e-12 per metre and utilisation to 0.000001. These are presentation resolutions, not accuracy claims. Solver closure diagnostics are excluded from the embedded data. The unrounded results determine ranking, feasibility and the analytical agreement check. Tests require identical HTML after one-ULP changes to computed floats and to the analytical check depth.
