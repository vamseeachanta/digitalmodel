1. Executive summary

The power module is a set of electrical-network calculation tools. Analysis is performed to determine bus voltages and power transfers for specified loads and generation; key results are summarized below.

  • A converged electrical state is calculated for the public four-bus example.
    • Voltage magnitudes lie inside the supplied case limits; the plotted limits are input data rather than a general operating standard.
  • Both specified generator operating points exceed their supplied reactive-power limits.
    • Bus four reactive generation is 181.4 Mvar against an upper limit of 100 Mvar. Reference-bus reactive generation is 114.5 Mvar against an upper limit of 100 Mvar. The fixed-voltage operating point is excluded pending a generator-limit study.

The application limits and required next analysis are stated in Sections 7 and 8. The case basis is cited in Reference 2.

2. Introduction

The alternating-current load-flow workflow is described for an engineer specifying a balanced steady-state network. The calculated state is separated from equipment acceptance.

Bus and branch objects are assembled into a network and passed to the documented solver. Bus voltages, angles, injections, branch flows, losses, convergence and iteration count are returned. No external engineering program is required for this workflow.

Not covered

Data-center power-path redundancy, microgrid energy management, battery dispatch, governor and voltage droop, island detection, relay coordination, communication configuration and commissioning sequences are present elsewhere in the power package. Their input models and tests are distinct; numerical conclusions in this report apply to load flow only. Fault levels, contingencies and dynamic stability are not implemented by the load-flow entry point.

3. Design data and criteria

The public four-bus case is used as the numerical basis. Input values are taken from the open case tables rather than estimated from an installation.

The common power base is 100 MVA. Active and reactive powers are divided by this base before entry; positive net injection means generation minus demand. The generator voltage setpoint takes precedence over the bus starting magnitude. Angles enter in radians and are returned in degrees. The supplied case input tables and their source are reproduced in Appendix 10.1.

At the reference bus, voltage magnitude and angle are fixed and net powers are solved. At the generator bus, active injection and voltage magnitude are fixed and reactive injection is solved. At demand buses, both net powers are fixed and voltage magnitude and angle are solved (Reference 1, Section 4.1).

The mismatch tolerance is 1.0e−12 pu and the iteration cap is 50. These explicit calculation settings are numerical controls; the tolerance is selected to support the hand-reference assertions and is not a plant acceptance criterion. Voltage bounds and generator reactive bounds come from the case tables. Branch ratings are compared at both ends outside the solver. A converged state is not an acceptance decision.

4. Methodology and theory

The nodal admittance matrix is assembled from pi-equivalent branches. Power-balance equations are solved by Newton iteration in voltage magnitude and angle.

The reciprocal-impedance and nodal-assembly basis is given in Reference 3, Chapter 5, Section 3. For a real sending-end tap, the branch equations are taken from Reference 1, Section 3.2, equations (3.1)–(3.2) with zero phase shift:

y = 1/(r + jx), h = jb/2; Yff = (y + h)/t²; Yft = Ytf = −y/t; Ytt = y + h.

Here r is series resistance, x is series reactance, b is total charging susceptance, t is real tap ratio and j is the imaginary unit. Subscripts f and t denote the sending and receiving terminals. Half the charging is placed at each pi-section end; the ideal tap precedes the entire pi section. The public case encodes a nominal tap as zero; unity is passed to the module (Reference 1, Appendix B, branch data).

The complex-voltage vector V, injected-current vector I and complex injected-power vector S satisfy Reference 3, Chapter 5, equations (1), (6) and (8), also expressed in Reference 1, Section 3.6, equations (3.8)–(3.17):

I = YV; S = diag(V) conjugate(YV); S = P + jQ.

Y is the assembled admittance matrix; P and Q denote active and reactive injection. For terminal flows, Sf = Vf conjugate(If) and St = Vt conjugate(It); the branch complex loss is Sf + St. These are terminal power definitions in the same source. Charging can make net branch reactive loss negative without implying active generation.

The unknown vector u contains non-reference angles and demand-bus magnitudes. With scheduled-minus-calculated mismatch Δs, the update is J Δu = Δs, followed by u ← u + Δu (Reference 4, Section 4.5.2, equations (4.5.8)–(4.5.11)). J contains the derivatives of calculated active and reactive injections. Only active equations at non-reference buses and reactive equations at demand buses enter the solve (Reference 1, Section 4.1, equations (4.2)–(4.5) and Newton-method paragraph).

A result is marked converged when the maximum absolute scheduled-power mismatch is strictly less than the supplied tolerance. Voltage and equipment bounds are not part of that stopping test. The system base is stored by the module; dimensional conversion is performed explicitly in this report.

6. Validation evidence

Numerical evidence is provided by the existing admittance and solved-circuit tests. The public example demonstrates a supported workflow; agreement with a separate execution of the source program is not assessed.

The existing tests exercise bus and branch construction, input rejection, admittance assembly, convergence flags, output fields, power balance and nonconvergence. The hand-reference tests additionally compare a solved state against prescribed rectangular phasors, reject a deliberately incomplete iteration, and check terminal powers for tapped branches with charging. Test locations are listed in Section 9.2.

For the documented hand case, V1 = 1 + j0, V2 = 0.96 + j0.28 and V3 = 0.90 − j0.12 pu. Series reactances are 0.5 and 0.25 pu, with zero resistance and charging and unity taps. The scheduled injections are obtained from S = V conjugate(I), giving generator-bus active injection 2.0288 pu and demand-bus consumption 1.4688 + j0.024 pu. These are constructed circuit data, not an installation assumption (Reference 5).

The module returns reference-bus reactive injection 0.0800 pu, generator-bus reactive injection 0.7584 pu and final demand-bus voltage 0.907965 pu. Hand arithmetic gives the same values within the absolute numerical criterion of 10⁻⁸ pu. The arithmetic and literal assertions are provided in the report test; the theory is cited in Section 4.

The report consistency checks compare every displayed numerical binding with fresh module outputs or the cited inputs. Those consistency checks do not replace the hand-worked literals. Field measurements, certified equipment results, a separate program solution and a published solved-output comparison are not evaluated.

7. Key findings and limits of use

The supplied operating point is limited by reactive generation at both sources rather than bus-voltage bounds. Numerical convergence is established only for the stated inputs.

Both source voltage setpoints are held even after their supplied reactive bounds are exceeded. Reactive-limit enforcement, conversion of generator buses to demand buses, tap control, voltage-dependent loads, phase-shifting transformers, explicit bus shunts and unbalanced circuits are outside the implemented load-flow model. The case has no unsupported bus shunts or phase shifts.

Exactly one reference bus and unique bus identifiers are required. Constructor checks reject zero branch impedance, nonpositive tap and inverted voltage bounds. The caller also needs to check finite numbers, existing branch endpoints, positive power base, a connected network, sensible starting voltages and valid iteration settings; comprehensive checks are absent. A singular linear solve can raise an exception. An iteration-limit result still contains numerical values and requires rejection when convergence is false.

The dense admittance and Jacobian matrices are used. Large-network performance and ill-conditioned operating points are not evaluated. Voltage bounds stored on buses do not reject a solved voltage violation. The reported branch flows omit explicit receiving-end records; receiving powers in Table 3 are reconstructed from sending powers and reported losses.

8. Recommendations

A constrained operating study is required before the public example is used as an accepted generator operating point. The recommendations follow the exceeded case limit and the method boundary in Reference 1, Section 4.1.

Apply both supplied reactive bounds in a limit-aware analysis with an explicit reference-control arrangement. Release an affected voltage setpoint and solve the resulting demand-bus equations, as described in the cited source. Both regulated sources exceed their bounds in this circuit; conversion of both to fixed-demand buses leaves no voltage reference. A feasible reference-control arrangement and reactive-support or redispatch study are required before that conversion is used. Report the revised source powers, voltage deviations and both terminal branch loading checks before accepting operation. No reactive-support rating or acceptable revised operating point is inferred from this unconstrained state.

For an engineering network, supply the actual equipment ratings, voltage criteria and applicable operating duration. Reject a nonconverged result and any exceeded supplied limit before use. Add finite-input and connectivity validation, followed by a published solved-state comparison and field-data comparison when measured data are available.

10.2 Symbols and units

Per unit (pu) denotes a quantity divided by its consistent system base. MW is megawatt, Mvar is megavolt-ampere reactive and MVA is megavolt-ampere. Alternating current is the sinusoidal steady-state basis. A reference bus fixes voltage and angle; a fixed-active-power-and-voltage bus is conventionally called PV; a fixed-active-and-reactive-power bus is called PQ. V is complex voltage; I is complex current; Y is complex admittance; S is complex power; P is active power; Q is reactive power; Qg is generator reactive power; Qd is local reactive demand; Sbase is the power base. J is the Jacobian, u is the unknown vector and Δ is an increment. Remaining branch symbols are defined immediately after their equation.
