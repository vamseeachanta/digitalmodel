1. Executive Summary

The diffraction module is a preparation, conversion and assessment toolkit for frequency-domain hydrodynamics. Analysis is performed to determine the reproducibility of its public cylinder radiation example and the evidence available for wider use; key results are summarized below. [M] [W]

  • The cylinder example is available as a reproducible radiation reference.
    • 75.0 % of the sampled non-zero refinement comparisons meet the repository target benchmarks: added-mass change below 0.5 % and damping change below 0.1 %, against the finer expansion. [C]
    • The damping refinement target is not met at 2.0 rad/s; further mode refinement is required before that coefficient is adopted as a converged reference. [C]
    • The computed mass and damping coefficients are tabulated in Section 6; these describe forced heave radiation, not a freely responding body. [M]
  • A wider diffraction design is assessed only after its model basis is supplied.
    • Independent program agreement, preferred program, panel size relative to body length and comparative runtime are NOT_EVALUATED. No paired independent program result is computed here. [E]
    • Surge, sway, heave, roll, pitch and yaw long-wave comparators are shown, with no observed motion arrays. Long-wave agreement is NOT_EVALUATED for every mode. [L]

2. Introduction

A common model description is converted into hydrodynamic inputs, and completed results are organized for engineering assessment and motion-data transfer. The main workflow is explained from preparation to interpretation; the executable example is restricted to the independent numerical cylinder reference. [W] [M]

Geometry and mass properties are first described in a canonical specification. Frequency and heading axes, environment, solver options and output selections are supplied explicitly. Preparation is followed by an external diffraction solution, result extraction, unit and convention checks, physical screening and export. Native solver execution remains outside the evidence demonstrated by this report. [W] [I] [O]

A motion response amplitude operator, abbreviated RAO, relates complex displacement to incident wave amplitude. Radiation added mass and damping instead describe forces generated by prescribed body motion. Radiation coefficients alone do not provide excitation forces or a motion RAO. [T] [M]

3. Summary of Results

Computed radiation coefficients are presented against angular frequency. The plotted points are the documented frequencies; joining lines are visual guides and carry no interpolation accuracy claim. [C]

Positive heave damping is returned at each sampled frequency. A nonnegative damping baseline is drawn because radiation removes energy from this prescribed heave motion; it is not a numerical accuracy tolerance. The meaning and applicable geometry are bounded by the cylinder physics tests. [C] [T]

3.1 Long-wave comparators for all motion modes

The small-body comparator example uses ω = 0.05 rad/s and depth 500.0 m from its separate public test case. The body follows the surface excursion or slope under the stated idealisation; these curves are expected magnitudes, not computed diffraction motion results. [L]

Surge and sway use finite-depth surface particle excursions, heave uses the surface elevation, and roll and pitch use the wave slope without dynamic amplification. Yaw uses the module’s slender-body approximation; an irrotational excursion field gives a different result for a body symmetric in plan. No yaw acceptance claim is made. [L]

5. Analysis Methodology

Linear inviscid, incompressible and irrotational flow is assumed in the frequency domain. A flat impermeable bed, a linear free surface and small body motion are imposed. [T]

∇²φ = 0; Φ(r,z,t) = Re[φ(r,z) exp(−iωt)]
g ∂φ/∂z = ω²φ at z = 0; ∂φ/∂z = 0 at z = −h
ω² = g k tanh(kh); T = 2π/ω

The complex potential is φ, the physical potential is Φ, time is t, angular frequency is ω, wavenumber is k, water depth is h and period is T. The imaginary unit satisfies i² = −1 and defines the stated time convention. The cylindrical coordinates are radius r and vertical elevation z, positive upward. Gravity is g. The equations follow the open theory manual’s linear boundary-value problem and the module’s dispersion implementation. [T] [M]

For the cylinder, the water below the base and the water outside the radius are expanded separately and matched in potential and radial velocity. The body-base condition is unit heave velocity, the cylindrical wall is impermeable and the exterior wave is outgoing. The expansion is truncated at N modes, with the same count used in both regions. A sharp lower edge produces slow local velocity convergence; an algebraic residual alone is not a force-error bound. [M] [C]

A₃₃ = ρ Re[∫base φ dS]; B₃₃ = ρω Im[∫base φ dS]
ΔQ = 100 |Qcoarse − Qfine| / |Qfine|

The subscript 33 denotes heave–heave coupling. Added mass is A₃₃, radiation damping is B₃₃, water density is ρ and dS is the horizontal base area element. The force integral and signs are those of the implemented unit-velocity convention. The refinement quantity Q is either added mass or damping. The finer expansion supplies the reference denominator, which is nonzero in every reported comparison. [M] [C]

[−ω²(M + A) − iω(B + Bext) + C] ξ = Fexc; RAO = ξ / aw

For interpretation of a full motion analysis, M is structural mass and inertia, A is radiation added mass, B is radiation damping, Bext is externally supplied damping, C is restoring stiffness, ξ is complex motion, Fexc is complex wave excitation and aw is incident wave amplitude. This is the stationary linear motion balance with the stated negative-exponential convention; no such motion system is solved in the cylinder example. [T] [D]

In the main workflow, a canonical specification is checked before mesh packaging and deck conversion. Frequency units, heading direction, reference point, density and rotational units are reconciled before completed results are compared or exported. Missing coefficient tables are distinguished from measured zeros because a spreadsheet bridge can supply placeholder matrices. [W] [I] [O] [B]

Preparation returns solver input files and associated meshes, not solved coefficients. Completed motion arrays are held by frequency and heading for each motion component; added-mass and damping matrices are held by frequency with per-block unit labels. Optional hydrostatics retain displacement, centres and stiffness. Export produces motion-data configuration, comma-separated coefficient tables, a workbook and a summary; each recipient convention is checked before those files are used. [W] [O]

6.1 Existing evidence and its meaning

The analytical reference tests inspect dispersion roots, density scaling, damping behaviour, boundary conditions, matching continuity, truncation sensitivity and regression values. The continuity test deliberately zeroes solved coefficients to show that a trivial solution fails; free-surface and bed conditions alone are weaker evidence because the basis satisfies them by construction. [C]

The long-wave tests exercise all motion comparators, including finite-depth dispersion and zero-reference handling. Roll-damping and equilibrium tests inspect their separate arithmetic contracts. Schema, unit and canonical-example tests support software behaviour, while synthetic comparison fixtures exercise decision logic without proving physical agreement between programs. [E] [L] [D]

The published unit-box report is a synthetic report-pipeline example: its coefficient series are generated with seeded perturbations rather than native diffraction solutions. The canonical unit-box preparation example demonstrates specification parsing and portable mesh packaging in its source tests. Both are useful software evidence and neither establishes a motion-accuracy bound. [P]

The stored example reports under the solver validation tree are not treated as independent reference-program outputs. The module source explicitly records this evidence limitation. Historical solver-proximity statements in its docstring are not recalculated or promoted to an error bound here. [M]

6.2 Runtime and computation

The recorded first reference computation takes 0.8 s for 4 coefficient cases and the comparator curves, with one numerical thread. The measured environment is aarch64 with 20 logical processors and 119.7 GiB total memory. Imports and input extraction are included; rendering and test execution are excluded. This measurement is specific to these small reference systems. Native program runtime and comparative speed are NOT_EVALUATED. [R]

7. Key Findings

A reusable heave radiation reference is provided for the stated circular cylinder, with explicit expansion sensitivity. The evidence supports a reference-case check, while the complete diffraction workflow remains dependent on supplied geometry, inertia, conventions and independent result evidence. [M] [W]

Coefficient units are mixed by matrix block: translation, rotation and their coupling have distinct dimensions. Translational RAOs use m/m, while unified rotational RAOs use degrees/m. The long-wave functions return rotational slopes in rad/m, which are converted to degrees/m in this report. Native solver units and phase signs require explicit reconciliation; a common schema does not prove that reconciliation. [O] [L]

The calculated displaced-water mass is 6.440 t, based on volume 6.283 m³. This is a geometric reference only: structural mass, centre of gravity and stability are not supplied for this prescribed-motion case. Weight–buoyancy equilibrium and stability are therefore NOT_EVALUATED. [M] [Q]

8. Limitations

Use is bounded by the linear potential-flow idealisation and by the evidence actually calculated. Viscous separation, breaking waves, large motion, impact and nonlinear roll response are absent from the cylinder reference. [T] [D]

No physical panel mesh is used by the matched-eigenfunction cylinder, so no mesh-size recommendation follows from its mode count. A panel-refinement sequence, wave coverage and independent reference evidence are required for a body-specific diffraction design. Regional sea-state periods and natural periods are NOT_EVALUATED because no metocean analysis or mass/restoring basis is supplied. No regional period values are assumed. [M] [I]

8.1 Not covered

Native solver installation and execution, multibody interaction, control surfaces, quadratic transfer functions, mean drift, mooring coupling, pressure fields, detailed mesh generation and downstream dynamics are not evaluated. The module contains interfaces for several of these areas, but the unified output schema does not contain every native excitation or second-order result. [I] [O] [B]

9. Recommendations and Way Forward

The cylinder reference is retained as a small, reproducible radiation check. Wider application is advanced through a complete input basis and independently sourced comparisons with stated acceptance criteria. [C] [A]

  • Use the cylinder refinement check when changes affect dispersion, eigenfunction matching or force integration. Recalculate the reported cases when those implementations change. [C]
  • Increase the retained expansion count where a refinement target is not met, then repeat the coefficient comparison at the same frequency against a finer expansion. Accept that reference coefficient only after its stated target is met. [C]
  • Complete the model basis before assessing body motion: geometry, mass, centre of gravity, inertia, environment, external damping and operating wave range. The missing-data register identifies the outstanding quantities. [I] [Q]
  • Define a body-specific panel-refinement sequence and target benchmarks before comparing programs. Preserve unit, origin, phase and heading evidence with the result arrays. No universal panel-length ratio is inferred from this reference. [A] [O]
  • Establish roll damping from an explicit frequency-dependent inertia and restoring basis. If resonance lies in the operating range, obtain damping evidence from decay or forced-motion tests before assigning a design roll limit. [D] [T]
  • Extend the reference evidence in sequence.
    • Add a cited public excitation and motion benchmark with the original result arrays.
    • Compare converged panel results with the present cylinder radiation reference.
    • Complete all motion-mode long-wave checks, mesh quality, equilibrium and operating-range damping sensitivity before accepting a body-specific design.
    [A] [C] [L] [Q]

The generator reads the public source-test inputs, calls the numerical files directly and builds this page. Completed calculation cases are retained in an optional external scratch directory with input and result digests; changed inputs produce distinct records. Chart series are selected with the checkboxes; point details are available by keyboard focus and hover. Comments are entered by section and saved beside the report through a selected folder. 10.3 Roll resolution and damping sensitivity

A resonant roll grid and damping-sensitivity curve are NOT_EVALUATED. Roll restoring stiffness, structural and added inertia, natural period and operating frequency range are not supplied. An arbitrary grid or damping value is not substituted. The required grid resolves the eventual resonance peak and is refined until the selected motion target is stable. [D] [A]

Bcrit = 2√[Croll(Iroll + Aroll(ωn))]; ζ = Btotal/Bcrit
ωn² = Croll/(Iroll + Aroll(ωn))

Critical roll damping is Bcrit, roll restoring stiffness is Croll, structural roll inertia is Iroll, added roll inertia is Aroll, natural angular frequency is ωn and the damping ratio is ζ. Total damping is Btotal. The relations apply to an uncoupled mode, and the inertia is evaluated at the same frequency as the damping basis. An undamped resonance has no finite linear motion amplitude; it is not an operating prediction. Damping percentages and warning limits remain required design inputs until their source and application basis are recorded. [D]
