1 Executive Summary

The geotechnical module is a collection of soil-resistance and screening calculators. Analysis is performed to determine axial clay-pile resistance and the margin against an illustrative applied load; key results are summarized below.

  • The public example passes its illustrative axial load check.
    • The allowable axial resistance is 8,069.0 kN against 5,000.0 kN applied load, using the example design divisor of 2.0. The stated load is the acceptance boundary for this illustration. Input basis; criterion.
    • The full circular tip area is used. Plugging and an independent pile load-test comparison are not evaluated. Calculation basis; qualification record.
  • The calculation is available for reproducible screening.
    • Design release is withheld until the soil profile, tip condition, method edition and design factors are qualified. The numerical load check alone does not establish design acceptance. Limits and recommendations.

2 Introduction

The main axial clay-pile workflow is described from input selection through the returned capacities and load verdict. A documented public repository example is replayed by calling the module; it is not represented as a published field benchmark. Example record.

Pile primitives and composite clay-pile functions are provided alongside anchor, foundation, scour, sediment-motion, liquefaction, parameter-database and optional slope interfaces. The interfaces are assessed as software entry points, rather than as a common design certification. Scope record; slope interface.

3 Engineering summary

The applied load is compared with ultimate resistance divided by the supplied design factor. Sensitivity to embedded length is evaluated with diameter, soil strength, effective stress and design factor held at the public example values. Criterion and implementation.

The failing shorter-length cases are arithmetic sensitivity cases. For the illustrative check, the load is limited to the calculated allowable resistance; design use remains conditional on qualification of the physical pile and soil profile. Lengthening is not accepted as a design recommendation without depth-dependent soil evidence. Load criterion; constant-property model.

4 Design basis and inputs

A uniform undrained clay idealisation and concentric compressive loading are adopted from the public example. The source calls the pile open-ended, while the calculator uses the full circular tip area; this discrepancy is retained explicitly as a limitation. Geometry description; tip-area expression.

The design factor is treated as an example input, not a prescribed code factor. Load acceptance means applied load does not exceed the calculated allowable resistance; equality is accepted by the workflow. Geometry, strength, effective stress, bearing factor, design factor and demand are required engineering inputs for a qualified use. Load comparison.

The public example inputs are marked ASSUMED - to be confirmed for any physical application. They are not measured site properties. No property is selected from the parameter database in this calculation; the data register is provided in the appendix. Input qualification register.

5 Theory and methodology

Shaft adhesion and tip bearing are summed in the public composite pile function. The following equations describe the implementation being replayed; normative attribution and the applicable edition of American Petroleum Institute Recommended Practice 2GEO (API RP 2GEO) remain NOT_EVALUATED. No coefficient is asserted as a qualified standard value. Composite function; coefficient source.

ψ = sᵤ / σ′ᵥ; f = α sᵤ; qᵦ = N꜀ sᵤ
α = min(αcap, a ψb)
ψ ≤ 1.0: a = 0.5, b = −0.5
ψ > 1.0: a = 0.5, b = −0.25
αcap = 1.0. Implemented alpha expression.

Here ψ is the dimensionless strength-to-effective-stress ratio; sᵤ is undrained shear strength and σ′ᵥ is effective vertical stress, both in kPa. The symbols a, b and αcap denote the implemented coefficient, exponent and adhesion cap, and α is dimensionless adhesion. The exact upper exponent is the negative quarter-power in the cited implementation. N꜀ is the supplied dimensionless tip bearing factor, f is unit shaft resistance and qᵦ is tip pressure in kPa. Definitions and expressions.

Aₛ = πDL; Aᵦ = πD²/4; Qₛ = f Aₛ; Qᵦ = qᵦ Aᵦ
Qᵤ = Qₛ + Qᵦ; Qallow = Qᵤ/F; U = P/Qallow. Geometry and resistance; design division.

D and L are pile outside diameter and embedded length in metres. Aₛ and Aᵦ are shaft and full circular tip areas in square metres; π is the circle constant. Qₛ, Qᵦ, Qᵤ and Qallow are shaft, tip, ultimate and allowable resistance in kN. F is the supplied design divisor, P is applied axial compression in kN, and U is dimensionless load utilization. Acceptance is P ≤ Qallow. Resistance definitions; acceptance expression.

For layered clay, shaft resistance is summed over the supplied layer thicknesses and the final layer supplies tip strength. Returned adhesion and unit shaft resistance refer to the final layer. Layer thicknesses are not reconciled with a separate embedded-length field by the composite function. Multilayer interface.

7 Validation evidence

Arithmetic regressions, component identities, input-domain checks and optional public slope benchmark reproductions are held in the repository. These evidence classes are distinguished because replaying a formula does not qualify its soil model or establish field performance. Pile tests; evidence inventory.

For piles, component summation, single/multilayer equivalence, final-layer tip selection and response to changed dimensions are tested. Existing fixed-value assertions are retained as regressions; their external numerical provenance is not established here and their reference values are not reproduced. An independent pile load-test comparison is NOT_EVALUATED. Existing pile assertions.

Independent rectangular-area checks are held for foundation eccentricity. Sediment-motion tests hold independent arithmetic checks of threshold stress and friction velocity. Neither validates the pile calculation. Public slope workbooks and optional reproduction tests are documented separately; slope results are not recalculated for this report. Area checks; threshold checks; public benchmark record.

The report rebuild test compares every result field with a fresh public-function call and every plotted case with a fresh workflow call. Printed engineering values are checked against their unrounded calculation fields. Byte equality is required for the rebuilt report and change record. The appendix inventory counts source test functions, rather than executed parameterized cases. Rebuild contract.

8 Limits and recommendations

Design acceptance is not assigned beyond the illustrative load inequality. The following gaps are material to physical pile resistance and are not resolved by a passing software regression. Model boundary; qualification record.

8.1 Recommendations and limits of use

The full-area tip result is conditional on the model idealisation; wall thickness, annular bearing and plugging are not represented. Before design release, establish the applicable tip condition and compare an appropriate tip model with independent evidence. For a failing load check, limit applied compression to the calculated allowable value while the physical model is qualified. Tip formulation; load criterion.

Strength and effective stress are constant within each supplied layer; the calculation does not derive a stress or strength profile. Before extending embedment, establish depth-dependent soil properties and reconcile the supplied layer thicknesses with geometry. Settlement, installation effects, cyclic degradation, tension, lateral response and group interaction require separate assessments. Supported inputs and equations.

The composite entry point does not enforce a complete finite, positive input domain. Validate dimensions, stresses, strengths, layer order and the positive design divisor before use; the optional range checker in the lower-level pile interface is not a site-specific validity envelope. Before design release, record the selected standard edition, formula attribution and independent acceptance evidence. Input validation; composite contract.

8.2 Not covered

Sand piles, drag and suction anchors, mudmat bearing and sliding, legacy pipeline and monopile scour, armour sizing, liquefaction, sediment-motion thresholds, database property selection and optional slope/seepage calculations are outside the worked example. The legacy scour attribution is not qualified by the separately sourced sediment-motion helper. Future calculations require an explicit method basis, qualified inputs and independent reference cases for each sub-area. Module boundaries; threshold scope; slope boundaries.
