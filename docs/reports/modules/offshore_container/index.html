1. Summary

The public package calculates ideal SHS properties, a centred sling angle and reduced member demand-to-allowable ratios. The independently derived section and sling example is reproduced: A = 0.03 m², I = 0.000125 m⁴, Z = 0.00125 m³ and v = 45.0° [D1/D2]. An explicitly synthetic usage scalar gives 1e+08 Pa [A3]. These are arithmetic checks, not accepted design factors or a resistance assessment.

2. Context and scope

Decision: determine which software outputs have independent arithmetic support and which claims require further engineering evidence. The exact reviewed source revision is 09089a121a16069c4b657d1a320a630a23ab1dd5. No calculation source is changed by this report. It is a single synthetic example; range coverage, physical validation and acceptance-policy review are NOT_EVALUATED.

The load path assumed by the implementation is mass → factored weight → attachment vertical load → resolved sling tension and inward horizontal load → top-rail axial stress. Separately, a factored internal weight becomes a uniform load shared between long floor rails, then simple-span bending stress. A padeye receives only a scalar net-area stress check [API].

3. Input basis

A1 is the synthetic sharp-cornered, concentric section in D1: outer side b and uniform wall t. A2 is the centred rectangle and link height in D2. All four attachments are at the plan corners. The origin is the plan centre at the attachment plane; x follows length, y follows width and z points upward. The sling angle v is measured from positive vertical toward a corner. Stress and returned ratios are unsigned scalar magnitudes; signed force equilibrium is not solved.

A3 consists of arbitrary mass, yield stress, net area and explicit factor inputs chosen only to exercise arithmetic. Every DesignFactors field is supplied. Synthetic factors are not prescribed by a standard and are not an accepted design basis. The unused angle and single-attachment fields are retained to expose their lack of effect. Inputs and outputs, units and their bases are all in the numbers table in Results.

The SI prefix milli gives mm = m × 10³; centi gives cm = m × 10²; kilo gives kN = N / 10³ [SI]. The public gravity definition is 980.665 cm/s², giving G = 9.80665 m/s² [GRAVITY, SI]. G is conventional gravity, not site gravity.

4. Method

Let R be mass in kg, g acceleration in m/s², kS the structure scalar, kP the attachment scalar, fT the tare fraction and u the usage scalar. The following equations describe the existing algorithm, with scalar factors provided by the caller [API].

T = fT R; FL = kS R g [N]; Fi = (kS R − T) g [N]; σallow = u fy [Pa].
n = clamp(ninput, 2, 4); Q = kP R g / (n − 1) [N]; RSL = Q / cos(v) [N]; H = Q tan(v) [N].

The count bounds are the implementation policy [API], not a standard prescription adopted here. A single requested attachment is mapped to the lower bound; there is no separate single-attachment calculation.

Utop = (H/A) / σallow; w = Fi/(2L) [N/m]; Mmax = w L²/8 = Fi L/16 [N m]; Ufloor = (Mmax/Z)/σallow; Upad = (RSL/Apad)/σallow; Ugov = max(Utop, Ufloor, Upad).

The rail share divisor represents two long rails. For a simply supported beam, reactions are wL/2 and M(x) = wLx/2 − wx²/2; setting its derivative to zero gives x = L/2 and the displayed maximum moment. This independently derives the beam coefficient, but does not establish that the actual floor behaves as that beam. N/m² = Pa; N m/m³ = Pa; stress divided by allowable stress is dimensionless. Loads returned in kN divide the calculated newtons by the SI kilo scale. Angle in degrees is radians × 180/π by the definitions of a full turn and a degree [D2].

5. Worked calculation

D1 subtracts the inner square from the outer: bi = b − 2t = 0.1 m. Integrating y² over each concentric square gives I = ∫ y² dA = (b⁴ − bi⁴)/12. Thus A = 0.2² − 0.1² = 0.03 m², I = (0.2⁴ − 0.1⁴)/12 = 0.000125 m⁴ and Z = I/(b/2) = 0.00125 m³. The integral of y² over a centred rectangle gives the denominator; it is not a fitted coefficient [D1].

D2 gives r = √((6/2)² + (8/2)²) = √(3² + 4²) = 5 m. Height is also 5 m, so tan(v) = r/h = 1 and v = π/4 rad. Both SI constructor routes are checked against the same integrals.

A3 sets u = 0.5 and fy = 200 × 10⁶ Pa, hence σallow = 100 × 10⁶ Pa. With the explicit mass and factors in Results, Q = 400g N, RSL = 400g√2 N, H = 400g N, Fi = 1500g N, Mmax = 562.5g N m and FL = 1600g N. These coefficients follow solely from A3 substituted into Method. Independent assertions use these closed expressions and the D1 section integrals. They establish arithmetic, not a strength or acceptance basis.

6. Checks

The test file [TEST] checks the normal public API against D1/D2 and the explicit A3 allowable scalar, separately from production replay. The synthetic load expressions check every returned Utilization field. Section properties use relative tolerance 1e−12 with zero absolute allowance; angles use absolute tolerance 1e−12 in the relevant angular unit with zero relative allowance. Other synthetic arithmetic uses relative tolerance 1e−12 with zero absolute allowance. These tolerances permit floating-point rounding only; they are not measurement uncertainties or design margins.

Separate tests generate two clean external deliveries, compare all bytes with the committed delivery, read every manifest digest independently, and check local links, sections and embedded JSON. Changed values and reversed signs fail the analytical oracle even when builder and report agree. Altering a delivered result also fails the byte manifest. Rebuilding or replaying the same calculation is a freshness check, not independent numerical evidence.

No claim of physical validation, input-domain coverage or design acceptance follows from these software checks.

8. Limits and NOT_EVALUATED findings

TopicStatusOpen finding
StrengthNOT_EVALUATEDThe scalar axial and bending demands omit frame action, projection of horizontal pull onto individual rail axes, member buckling, combined stress, real-section effects, joints, welds, corrosion and other load cases.
PadeyeNOT_EVALUATEDNet-area axial stress omits pin bearing, tear-out, bending, welds, shackle fit, out-of-plane load and local geometry.
SharingNOT_EVALUATEDAttachment count is silently clamped to the implemented range; equal sharing over n minus one attachments is a model assumption, not an equilibrium solution for actual rigging.
Input domainNOT_EVALUATEDPublic methods do not reject invalid dimensions, nonpositive areas, material stresses or inappropriate factors. The worked inputs are positive with a nonzero hollow core; wider domain behaviour is not qualified.
Unused fieldsNOT_EVALUATEDsingle_padeye_load_factor and default_sling_angle_to_vertical_deg do not affect utilization. A single attachment is mapped to the lower supported count; geometry always determines angle.
Factor provenanceNOT_EVALUATEDutilization does not call factor_citations. The citation registry has its own values, validates metadata rather than clause content and does not bind caller overrides. No licensed standard was accessed.
CertificationNOT_EVALUATEDNo accepted design basis, independent design review, fabrication evidence, prototype test, operational qualification or certification is supplied.

9. Way forward

Before physical use, establish an accepted load and resistance basis with the applicable standard and material data; resolve attachment count, rigging geometry and eccentric load sharing; qualify frame, floor and padeye resistance with the relevant failure modes; and add input-domain validation with reviewed boundary cases. Obtain independent engineering review and required test evidence. Treat these as open method and acceptance-policy findings; this report does not replace algorithms or select a design basis.
