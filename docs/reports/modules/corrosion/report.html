2. Introduction

Compatibility is screened from material-group indices and an environment category. The main workflow is evaluated through the public pair-screening interface and its matrix companion.

Purpose and scope

The report is used to identify pairs requiring engineering attention, reproduce the public zinc–steel example and locate the evidence needed before design use. Repository tests and the published explorer provide software evidence; an independent exposure experiment is not supplied by those records.

Not covered

Corrosion-rate models in the adjacent cathodic protection package, anode sizing, impressed-current design, stray-current assessment, inspection-based remaining life, pitting and stress-corrosion cracking are outside this module and this analysis.

3. Design data and basis

The public regression example is represented as a zinc coating electrically connected to a steel substrate. The physical arrangement is used to explain the screening result; dimensions and exposure history are absent from the example.

Physical arrangement and boundary conditions

Inputs and acceptance basis

Material keys, environment key, an optional environment-to-voltage mapping and an optional marginal-band width are accepted. The shipped catalogue contains 16 material groups and 3 environment classes, counted directly from the repository tables.

The input indices are 1.25 V for zinc and 0.85 V for carbon steel, relative to the module's gold reference. The marginal band is 0.05 V. These are implementation data, not independently established electrochemical properties or approved criteria. Their external edition and table provenance are NOT_EVALUATED.

ASSUMED - to be confirmed: the wetted coating and substrate remain electrically connected. Area ratio, alloy condition, water chemistry, temperature, coating thickness and design life are required for a physical corrosion assessment and remain NOT_EVALUATED here. No numerical substitute is introduced.

The software limit is retained to reproduce existing behaviour, not to set a design acceptance criterion. A qualified environment limit is required before an engineering disposition is made. The public physical guidance supports considering electrical contact, electrolyte and relative surface areas.

4. Analysis methodology

An absolute index difference is calculated and compared with an environment-dependent allowable. The returned category is symmetric in material order; the anodic designation follows the larger index.

Equations and category definitions

ΔV = | Ia − Ib |

ΔV is potential difference in volts; Ia and Ib are the material-group anodic indices in volts. L is the environment allowable in volts and B is the marginal-band width in volts. The index definition and category equations are the repository screening algorithm; they are not a mixed-potential or current-balance solution.

  • The difference is within the software allowable when ΔV ≤ L; the returned category is “OK”.
  • The difference exceeds the software allowable within its marginal band when L < ΔV ≤ L + B; the returned category is “MARGINAL”.
  • The difference exceeds the software marginal band when ΔV > L + B; protection is requested and the returned category is “PROTECT”.

A floating-point comparison allowance and output rounding are applied by the implementation. They are numerical conventions, not physical uncertainty bounds; their exact definitions are recorded in the repository algorithm reference.

Outputs and use sequence

A result record contains both material keys, environment key, potential difference, allowable difference, category, anodic and cathodic members, and explanatory notes. A square table of categories is returned by the matrix interface. The fastener and coating notes are qualitative and are not an area-ratio calculation.

The documented material keys are selected, an environment basis is qualified, the pair is screened and the returned difference is compared with the returned limit. Unknown material or environment keys raise an error. Custom threshold mappings are supported; numerical input validation for negative or non-finite custom values is not established by the existing tests.

5. Detailed results

The zinc–steel public regression case is calculated by calling the module. Environment variation is then calculated with the same material pair; no measured corrosion behaviour is inferred.

Worked example: zinc coating and carbon steel

The source is the public regression example. The supplied indices give |1.25 − 0.85| = 0.40 V. The returned harsh-marine allowable is 0.15 V and the marginal-band width is 0.05 V.

The difference exceeds the allowable by 0.25 V and exceeds the upper marginal boundary of 0.20 V. The difference exceeds the software marginal band; protection is requested. Zinc is returned as anodic and carbon steel as cathodic.

The returned request is a generic dissimilar-metal category. In an intentional galvanized coating, zinc consumption is part of the protection mechanism; coating adequacy is NOT_EVALUATED by this screen. For an unintended conductive joint, electrical isolation is the recommended next design action, supported by the public physical guidance.

6. Key findings

The zinc–steel difference exceeds the harsh-marine software marginal band, so protection is requested. The anodic direction is reproduced across material order and matrix presentation.

The example establishes deterministic software behaviour against repository data. External provenance for numerical acceptance criteria, coating adequacy and physical rate prediction remain NOT_EVALUATED. The harsher environment label changes the threshold rather than predicting the effects of salinity, wetting time or temperature.

7. Limits of use

Use is restricted to material-pair screening with explicit qualification of the inputs and criteria. No structural, coating-life or maintenance interval decision is established.

Passivation, local chemistry, polarization, electrical resistance, current distribution and exposed area are not model inputs. The public physical guidance notes that service conditions can alter potential differences and even relative ordering. Material-group indices therefore do not establish a site-specific galvanic driving voltage.

The source description attributes the defaults to a dissimilar-metals standard without an edition or clause. That attribution is retained as an unresolved source claim; no numerical value is asserted as a requirement of that standard. The marginal category is a software convention and supplies no physical safe-use band.

Changing the optional marginal width does not update the fixed-width wording in the module's marginal note. Use the returned category and numeric fields when custom settings are applied, and obtain a corrected explanatory note before relying on that text.

8. Recommendations

Engineering acceptance is deferred until the material condition, exposure and governing criterion are established. Actions are assigned to the evidence gaps and to the calculated protection request.

  • For an unintended zinc–steel conductive joint, introduce electrical isolation and confirm separation of the conductive path at detailed design. This action addresses the protection request and is based on elimination of electrical contact in the public physical guidance.
  • For intentional galvanizing, retain the coating intent and assess coating thickness, exposed steel area, environment and depletion before defining an inspection interval. This recommendation follows the missing physical inputs and the area effect described by the public physical guidance; no unsupported service date or depletion rate is assigned.
  • Before adopting a numerical default as an acceptance criterion, obtain its public edition and table or replace it with an approved required input. This action resolves the missing external provenance recorded in the data register.
  • As the first extension, add sourced criterion metadata, finite non-negative custom-input checks and notes derived from the selected marginal width. Preserve the existing public examples as regression checks and add tests at the selected boundaries. The scope follows the interface limitations and existing repository evidence.

10.3 Symbols and status legend

V denotes volt. ΔV denotes the absolute difference of the material-group indices. Ia and Ib denote those indices; L denotes the environment allowable and B the marginal-band width. Anodic denotes the member assigned the larger index by the software. Cathodic denotes the other member. NOT_EVALUATED means that a physical conclusion or its evidence is absent.

The difference is within the software allowable for “OK”. The difference exceeds the software allowable within its marginal band for “MARGINAL”. The difference exceeds the software marginal band and protection is requested for “PROTECT”. These sentences describe software categories, not engineering acceptance.
