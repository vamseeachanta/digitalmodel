1. Executive Summary

The hull-girder screening module is a longitudinal loading-condition calculator. Analysis is performed to determine still-water shear force, bending moment and comparisons with supplied allowable curves; key results are summarized below.

  • Loading conditions are ranked using calculated demand and raw equilibrium closure.
    • The synthetic central-cargo example gives a maximum sagging moment of −62,496.9 t·m at 50.0 m.
    • The numerical comparison is limited by the analytical-fixture tolerances in Table 7.1; it does not establish strength acceptance.
  • Illustrative checks satisfy the supplied example limits.
    • The maximum bending utilisation is 50.0 % against the 100.0 % illustrative limit; strength acceptance is NOT_EVALUATED.
    • Approved loading-manual limits and an independent loading-instrument comparison are required before an operational strength decision is made.

The demand and illustrative boundary are shown in Figure 6.1. The scope and disposition are supported by References 9.2(a), 9.2(b) and 9.2(d).

2. Introduction

The main still-water workflow is documented for engineers reviewing loading conditions. A documented public synthetic box-barge example is recalculated through the module entry point; it is not a measured asset or a transcription of a textbook numerical example.

The implementation builds distributed and concentrated weights, obtains buoyancy, integrates the net load and compares demands with position-dependent allowable magnitudes. The result is exported as station, frame and summary tables. Source functions and their test records are listed in Section 9.2.

3. Summary

The loading calculation is used as a screening aid. Controls are applied to the input basis and the reported coverage before any loading condition is referred for an operational decision.

Weight units, positions, total weight and longitudinal centre of gravity shall be checked against the loading record. The buoyancy method shall be selected for the actual hull; the box method is restricted here to the prismatic example. Raw end residuals shall be reviewed before corrected load curves are interpreted.

Both sign-specific allowable curves shall be supplied from the approved loading manual. Missing negative curves inherit positive magnitudes; this symmetry is an example assumption, not evidence of an approved limit. All reporting frames and expected demand peaks shall be included. A returned pass status alone does not establish that every strength check was evaluated: omitted curves produce no-allowable frame results, while the overall status can still be pass. These behaviours are recorded in Reference 9.2(f).

5. Methodology

The longitudinal hull is treated as a free-floating beam. Net load is integrated along the hull using cumulative trapezoidal quadrature, with the sign convention defined below.

5.1 Equilibrium, integration and signs

q(x) = w(x) − b(x); V(x) = ∫₀ˣ q(s) ds; M(x) = ∫₀ˣ V(s) ds

The coordinate x is measured forward from the aft end in metres; s is the integration coordinate. Weight density w and buoyancy density b are in tonnes per metre. The net density q is weight minus buoyancy; shear V is in mass-equivalent tonnes and moment M is in mass-equivalent tonne-metres. Positive M is hogging; negative M is sagging. The general beam equilibrium relations are given in Reference 9.2(g), equations (3.68) and (3.72). That reference uses the opposite load sign: its q is b − w when mapped to this convention. The implemented convention is stated in Reference 9.2(b).

For the box method, a linear buoyancy distribution is fitted to the discrete weight and longitudinal moment. Optional linear closure correction is applied after the raw residuals are recorded. Distributed weights are represented by cell-averaged linear densities constrained by total weight and centre of gravity; a centre outside the middle third of a block is rejected. Concentrated weights are spread rather than treated as exact force jumps. These implementation choices are documented in Reference 9.2(b).

5.2 Allowable interpolation and analytical comparator

uV(x) = |V(x)| / Vallow(x); uM(x) = |M(x)| / Mallow(x)

Utilisations u are dimensionless. Allowable magnitudes are linearly interpolated at the reporting frames, and the allowable matching the demand sign is used. Outside the supplied allowable knot range, the end allowable is held constant. The module does not search a continuous utilisation envelope between reporting frames; the chart markers identify the positions actually screened.

|V|reference = W/4; Mreference(L/2) = −WL/16

For the documented central block over the middle half of a hull of length L and cargo weight W, these continuous solutions follow by integrating the piecewise constant net density. Their derivation and numerical comparison criteria are held in Reference 9.2(c) and 9.2(d). A spread block edge changes the sampled shear extreme; the continuous reference is therefore a comparator rather than an expected identical discrete value.

5.3 Entry point and outputs

The public dictionary entry point is imported and called as follows. Configuration is deep-copied before the call because the router replaces the settings block with results.

from copy import deepcopy
from digitalmodel.hull_girder_screening import router
result = router(deepcopy(config))["hull_girder_screening"]

The configuration contains a hull-girder settings mapping and an explicit output directory. Station tables contain position, weight density, buoyancy density, shear and moment. Frame tables contain demand, allowable, utilisation and status. Summary tables contain extremes and positions, raw residuals and maxima of the evaluated utilisations. Comma-separated-value files and the returned dictionary carry these results; numerical output paths are kept out of this report.

6. Detailed Results

The public example is computed through the router with its still-water inputs. Optional wave loads, total allowable curves and section-modulus inputs are excluded from this worked example.

6.1 Worked example

The calculated displacement is 10,000.0 t and its longitudinal centre of gravity is 50.0 m. The negative shear peak is −2,487.5 t at 25.0 m; the positive shear peak is 2,487.5 t at 75.0 m. Their magnitudes tie in this symmetric example. The maximum sagging moment is −62,496.9 t·m at 50.0 m. Raw end shear and moment residuals are respectively 0.0 % and 0.0 %, against the 0.5 % closure gate. The box buoyancy fits the discrete weight force and moment, so raw closure holds by construction for this example; it is not independent validation of the buoyancy basis.

The maximum frame shear utilisation is 49.8 % and the maximum frame bending utilisation is 50.0 %. Both use the 100.0 % illustrative threshold. These comparisons demonstrate arithmetic with supplied curves; operational strength acceptance remains NOT_EVALUATED.

The legend is interpreted as follows. The numerical gate is satisfied when raw closure is inside its tolerance. An illustrative check is satisfied when evaluated utilisation is inside its supplied limit. Strength acceptance is not evaluated when qualified limits or independent reference evidence are absent.

8. Key Findings, Limitations and Recommendations

The implemented still-water workflow is supported by a synthetic analytical comparison and automated regression records. Engineering acceptance remains dependent on a qualified loading basis.

8.1 Findings and recommendations

The central-block result has the documented sagging sign and satisfies the numerical comparison criteria in Table 7.1. Approved loading-manual curves, their sign conventions and frame coverage are required for an asset-specific screen. A comparison with an approved loading instrument is required before the result is used to set an operational limit, consistent with Reference 9.2(h).

When a raw closure gate fails, the weight and buoyancy force and moment balance is reconciled before any corrected result is used. When an illustrative allowable is exceeded, the loading condition is referred for engineering assessment and load redistribution is evaluated using approved limits; this report does not assign an operating allowance. These actions follow the closure and screening basis in References 9.2(b) and 9.2(h).

Station refinement and additional reporting frames are required to resolve load discontinuities and utilisation peaks; no refinement study is performed here. Finite numeric values, units, monotonic direct-buoyancy positions, curve coverage and boolean settings shall be checked before calling the router: the implementation does not comprehensively validate all such inputs. A missing allowable is treated as missing evidence rather than acceptance.

8.2 Limits of use and not covered

The box buoyancy idealisation does not replace hydrostatics for a shaped hull. Load discontinuities are spread by cell averaging; local stresses and exact concentrated-load jumps are not represented. The closure correction is a numerical device, not evidence that supplied hydrostatics are physically valid. Results are sampled at stations and frame gates; no continuous maximum-utilisation search is made.

The optional hydrostatics-table waterline solve and direct-buoyancy import are described but not worked here. Rule wave envelopes, total still-water-plus-wave screening, scantling-derived section properties and section-modulus yield screening are not covered by the worked example. The supporting strength module also contains an ultimate-strength estimate, which is outside this router workflow. Fatigue, buckling, torsion, horizontal bending, slamming, whipping and installation sea-state envelopes are not evaluated. Orientation limits, crane rigging and forecast support are not applicable to this still-water module analysis; no sea-state operating limit is inferred.

The implementation names IACS UR S11, Longitudinal Strength Standard, Rev.7 (2010), wave bending moment and wave shear force clauses. Edition equivalence and the permissible-stress clause basis are not established here. No licensed numerical rule coefficient or limit is reproduced.

10.3 Quality evidence

The rebuild test recalculates the public example independently, compares every published station and frame field with the router, checks numerical text and verifies reproducible HTML and change records. The test-definition inventory is derived from the cited files. These assertions establish reproducibility of this report; they do not establish independent validation of an operational hull.

The quality pre-flight basis is stated in Tables 4.1, 7.1 and 10.1: input provenance, numerical criteria, sign mapping, missing data and intended use. Every example number is generated from the configuration, router output or analytical comparator. No finite-element calculation, mesh or licensed solver is used.
