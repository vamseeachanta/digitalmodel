2. Introduction


The metallic-line fatigue calculation is described for tension ranges supplied at a mooring component. A screening analysis is performed; platform motion, water depth and mooring equilibrium are not solved by this module. [Reference 2]


The documented public example is used without changing its loads, areas, curve choice or design fatigue factor. It is a published software example rather than a measured loading record or an experimental benchmark. [Reference 1]


The main workflow is tension-range bins to nominal stress ranges, cumulative damage, fatigue life and screening margin. Other input routes are listed under Not covered.




3. Design Data


The public example inputs are retained as the calculation basis. Their suitability for a physical mooring system is not established. [Reference 1]

Criteria and data status


The screening criterion is defined by the implemented margin: predicted life divided by required life is at least unity. Required life is design life multiplied by the supplied design fatigue factor. 4. Analysis Methodology


The supplied tension ranges are converted to nominal axial stress ranges before the existing stress–life helper is called. Damage is accumulated line by line and the line with greatest damage is selected as governing. [Reference 2]



Stress–life and cumulative damage equations


Nominal stress range is calculated as Δσ = ΔT / A with force and area expressed in consistent units. A tension in kilonewtons is converted to newtons before division by area in square millimetres; the resulting stress is in megapascals. No additional stress concentration or thickness correction is applied in this route. [Reference 2]


The helper represents the selected curve by N = Nₖ(Δσ / Sₖ)−m. Here N is allowable cycles, Nₖ is the knee cycle count, Sₖ is the knee stress range, and m is the applicable segment slope. The below-knee segment is anchored continuously at the knee. Both segments remain finite; an endurance cut-off is not applied. The stored curve source and the helper implementation are distinguished in the reference record. [Reference 3] [Reference 4]


Miner damage is calculated as D = Σ(nᵢ / Nᵢ), where nᵢ is applied cycles and Nᵢ is allowable cycles for bin i. Fatigue life is calculated as L = T / D, required life as Lᵣ = T × F, and screening margin as M = L / Lᵣ. T is the supplied design life and F is the supplied design fatigue factor. The decision M ≥ unity is equivalent to D ≤ the reciprocal of F. These are the implemented equations, conditional on the assumed lifetime exposure. [Reference 2]


Inputs, execution and outputs


The documented command-line entry calls the engine with an input configuration. The same mooring workflow is called directly for the example calculation in this report. Required settings are positive design life, positive design fatigue factor, and a non-empty list of metallic lines with non-empty cycle bins. For tension bins, effective area is required. Curve and environment are selected explicitly here. [Reference 1] [Reference 2]


The returned configuration contains the governing line, damage, life, margin, screening status and line summaries. Separate comma-separated-value files contain per-bin tension range, stress range, applied cycles, allowable cycles and damage, and per-line total damage, predicted life, required life, margin and screening decision. The caller configuration is modified in place. [Reference 2]


Reproduction instructions and exact interface locators are retained in section 9.2. Engineering inputs and results are kept separate from the comment file.

6. Key Findings


The workflow is available for metallic-line screening and its example results are reproducible through the direct module interface. The greatest total damage controls the reported line because the same design life and design fatigue factor apply to every line. [Reference 2]


The material labels control eligibility only: the same curve is passed to chain and wire. This is the governing qualification gap for physical use. The result is sensitive to effective area, exposure cycles, corrosion protection and curve applicability; none is established for a real installation by the example. [Reference 1] [Reference 2]


The public example is suitable for checking input and output handling. It does not establish mooring resistance, a watch circle, an inspection date or a permissible environmental envelope.




7. Limitations


The calculation is limited to the supplied stress basis and cycle exposure. Acceptance of a physical mooring is deferred until that basis is established. [Reference 1] [Reference 2]


Global equilibrium, pretension response, seabed contact, bending, chain interlink contact, out-of-plane bending, fretting, mean-stress correction, local stress concentration, corrosion wastage and thickness correction are not evaluated in this workflow. Neither synthetic rope damage nor residual strength is evaluated. Synthetic material labels are rejected. [Reference 2]


Input checks reject missing or nonpositive required scalars and empty line or bin lists. Finite-value checks and rejection of simultaneous input representations are not comprehensive. When several representations are supplied, stress bins take precedence over tension bins, then stress history, then tension history. Supply one representation and independently check finite input values. [Reference 2]


The exposure period is not inferred or annualised. Increasing design life while holding lifetime cycle bins fixed changes the reported life without changing damage; design life is not an automatic loading multiplier. Raw calculated life is therefore not a service-life recommendation. [Reference 2]


Not covered


Stress-range bins, stress histories, tension histories, history repetition factors, histogram sensitivity, alternative environments, parametric atlases and synthetic-rope workflows are not demonstrated here. The history route uses rainflow counting followed by bin-centre histogramming; binning convergence and repeat-block boundary effects require separate checks before use. [Reference 2] [Reference 4]




8. Recommendations


Acceptance is to be based on component evidence and a documented exposure model. The public example remains a screening calculation until those records are supplied. [Reference 1] [Reference 2]


Obtain a component-specific stress–life or tension–life basis, effective area and local stress definition for each metallic component. Confirm the selected environment and corrosion protection, then assess the applicability of the cited fatigue method before evaluating service loads. The present material labels do not perform that selection. [Reference 2] [Reference 3]


Document the period represented by each cycle bin and scale the loading to the design exposure before calculation. Retain the original counts, scaling basis and effective area with the assessment. This action addresses the exposure assumption underlying L = T / D. [Reference 2]


Suspend acceptance when calculated damage exceeds the reciprocal of the adopted design fatigue factor, or when the adopted curve, protection condition or exposure basis ceases to apply. Recalculate using measured or qualified loads and component data; reduce the permitted loading or replace the affected component before accepting a result that exceeds that limit. This trigger follows the implemented margin criterion and does not assign an unsupported calendar deadline. [Reference 2]


Add an independent component benchmark with documented geometry, load range, exposure duration and permissible damage before adopting this workflow for an installation. Add explicit finite-input and mutually exclusive input checks, and qualify the history binning separately. No passing software check substitutes for these data. [Reference 2] [Reference 5]

10. Acronyms and Symbols


The symbols used in the equations and tables are defined below. Stress and tension are ranges throughout; no amplitude conversion is applied.


A is effective load-bearing area. ΔT is cyclic tension range. Δσ is nominal stress range. N is allowable cycles. Nₖ and Sₖ are knee cycles and knee stress range. m is the active stress–life slope. The subscript identifies a cycle bin. nᵢ is the applied cycles in that bin. D is cumulative Miner damage. T is design life. F is design fatigue factor. L is extrapolated fatigue life. Lᵣ is required fatigue life. M is screening margin.


MPa means megapascal; kN means kilonewton; mm² means square millimetre. DFF means design fatigue factor. S–N means stress–life. CP means cathodic protection. CSV means comma-separated values. API means application programming interface. JSON means JavaScript Object Notation. NOT_EVALUATED means that an engineering result has not been established from the available data.

Appendix C. Quality assurance pre-flight


The calculation is rebuilt through the module rather than a parallel fatigue formula. All displayed worked-example observations are checked against a separate module call. Per-bin stress conversion and damage summation are checked independently, followed by the life and margin identities. Material rejection and a damage-limit exceedance are exercised by the report tests. These checks are arithmetic and software evidence only. [Reference 6]


The document checks cover the ten-section frame, revision location, unique anchors, working internal references, unchanged exemplar stylesheet, absence of remote script dependencies and visible wording. Browser interaction and component fatigue performance require separate assessment.


The screening criterion is met when damage does not exceed its adopted limit. The screening criterion is not met when that limit is exceeded. NOT_EVALUATED is used when the engineering basis is absent. No engineering status is inferred from a successful rebuild.
