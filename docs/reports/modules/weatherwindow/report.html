Weather-window screening

Draft for maintainer review

A fixed-interval significant-wave-height record is assessed for available continuous operating time.

1. Executive summary

The weather-window module is a record-based screening tool for marine operations. Analysis is performed to determine whether a supplied wave-height record contains sufficient continuous operating time; key results are summarized below.

  • The illustrative record fails the duration screen.
    • The longest accepted spell is 36 h against a required 48 h. An operation start is not supported by this record under the supplied duration criterion.
  • Accepted exposure alone does not establish a usable weather window.
    • Accepted exposure is 73.2% against an input threshold of 50%, but 0 spells meet the required duration.

These are results for a synthetic public repository example, not a forecast or a location-specific installation envelope. Heading, wave period, structural response and a live-support trigger are NOT_EVALUATED.

2. Introduction

The main screening workflow is described from supplied environmental samples to the returned decision. The result is intended for preliminary planning by an engineer who supplies the operational limits.

Significant wave height, denoted Hs, is accepted in metres. A fixed interval is assigned to every sample. No wave transformation, response analysis or environmental data retrieval is performed by this workflow. The public example is used unchanged for calculation; its descriptive case identifier and output directory do not affect the calculation. [E]

The Python entry point is assess_weather_window; window_lengths_hours returns the continuous spells, and router accepts a configuration mapping and writes a summary. Their exact locators are provided in Section 9.2.

4. Design data and criteria

The supplied example is treated as illustrative design data. Its limits are input choices rather than values derived from a standard.

Input contract

The required inputs are a nonempty list of Hs samples in metres, a positive Hs limit in metres and a positive operation duration in hours. A positive time step in hours and an optional required workability in percent are also accepted. The default interval is overridden explicitly here by 3 h, as supplied by the example. [E]

The example contains 56 samples representing 168 h. Its Hs limit is 2 m and its workability threshold is 50%. All sample values and their elapsed-time coordinates are provided in the appendix. [E]

Criteria and rationale

A sample is accepted when Hs is less than or equal to the input limit. A spell is viable when its unrounded duration is at least the required duration. The overall screen requires a duration fit and, when supplied, the workability threshold. The separate duration criterion prevents a fragmented calm record from being accepted solely on its exposure percentage. [E]

ASSUMED - to be confirmed: each sample describes its complete following interval and all intervals are equally spaced. No interpolation across a threshold is applied. A physical Hs limit, direction tolerance, wave-period limit, wind limit, current limit and forecast uncertainty allowance require an operation-specific basis; none is inferred here.

System boundary

The represented system is the environmental screening calculation. Geometry, crane capacity, rigging, structural response, hydrodynamic response functions and installation stages are outside its inputs, so installation acceptance is NOT_EVALUATED.

5. Theory and methodology

Accepted exposure is calculated by relative frequency, and persistence is calculated by counting consecutive accepted samples. The elementary statistical definitions are sourced below; the acceptance inequalities are supplied screening conventions. [S1] [S2]

Discrete exposure and persistence

Let N be the sample count, Δt the input interval in hours, Hs(n) the wave height at sample index n in metres and L the input wave-height limit in metres. The indicator b(n) is unity when Hs(n) ≤ L and zero otherwise. Define T as represented exposure in hours and W as accepted exposure in percent.

T = N Δt; W = 100 Σ b(n) / N.

The relative-frequency definition is given in the cited statistics textbook; multiplication by 100 expresses a fraction as percent. Assigning Δt to each sample is the declared interval convention, not an estimate of the measured transition time. [S1]

A run is a consecutive sequence of identical classifications. Let k(j) be the number of accepted samples in run j, d(j) its duration in hours and M the number of accepted runs. Run terminology follows the cited handbook. [S2]

d(j) = k(j) Δt; mean duration = Σ d(j) / M; longest duration = max d(j).

The mean is the arithmetic mean defined in the statistics textbook. The longest duration is the largest observed run, not a prediction of future availability. For an all-rejected record the implementation returns no runs and zero duration statistics. [S3] [E]

Percentiles and the returned decision

For ordered durations y, the linear percentile convention uses rank r = 1 + p(M − 1), k = floor(r), f = r − k, and Q(p) = y(k) + f[y(k+1) − y(k)]. Here p is the requested fractional percentile and Q is in hours; at the last rank the largest duration is used. This is the R7 alternative listed in the handbook; the handbook's default method is R6. [S4]

The reported lower and upper percentiles use p = 0.1 and p = 0.9 respectively. They describe the sampled spells; neither is a confidence bound nor a forecast exceedance probability. [S4] [E]

Let D be required duration in hours and Wmin the optional input workability threshold in percent. The intended duration decision is max d(j) ≥ D; viability is counted separately with d(j) ≥ D. The screen passes only when duration fits and W ≥ Wmin, with the exposure test omitted when Wmin is absent. [E]

The implementation rounds persistence statistics and total accepted hours to a decimal place before calculating fit and exposure. The viable count instead uses unrounded durations. This distinction matters near a decision boundary and is demonstrated in the appendix. [E]

No standards-derived coefficient is used in this calculation. The software's standards label lacks an edition and clause-qualified criterion; standards compliance is NOT_EVALUATED. A cited statistical definition does not qualify a marine operating limit.

8. Findings, limitations and recommendations

The demonstrated calculation returns the duration rejection for the supplied illustrative record. Engineering acceptance remains dependent on qualified data and unrounded decision quantities.

Limits of use

Negative and non-finite wave samples are not explicitly rejected. The workability threshold is not constrained to a physical percentage range. No timestamps are supplied, so gaps, irregular sampling and duplicate observations are undetected. Record-edge spells are counted in full even though their continuation outside the record is unknown. [E]

Rounded persistence maxima and accepted exposure can disagree with unrounded viability or exceed a physical percentage for short intervals. The diagnostic in the appendix demonstrates that a passing status is not sufficient at such boundaries. [E]

The example represents a short illustrative record. Its exposure fraction is not annual operability, a start-time distribution, a waiting-time estimate or a probability of completing an operation. Forecast uncertainty, safe suspension, contingency duration and recovery criteria are not represented.

Recommendations

For the failed illustrative duration screen, retain the input Hs limit and require a qualified continuous forecast interval meeting the required duration before a start decision. A revised operation duration requires a separately justified execution and recovery sequence; accepted exposure alone does not remove the duration restriction. [E]

Before operational use, calculate decisions from unrounded durations and exposure, and add boundary tests requiring fit to agree with viable count. Reject non-finite and negative samples, require consistent timestamps and constrain any percentage threshold to its defined range. These actions address the observed input and rounding limitations. [E] [S1]

Supply approved limits for heading, wave period, wind, current and structural response before installation acceptance is assessed. Define the forecast validity interval, uncertainty allowance, suspension criteria and forecast-support trigger from the approved operation basis. No numerical installation envelope is assigned in their absence.

Decision-boundary diagnostic

The existing rounding diagnostic uses a single Hs sample of 1 m, a limit of 2 m, an interval of 0.96 h and a required duration of 1 h. The raw spell is 0.96 h, but the rounded longest spell is 1 h. The returned fit is Yes, the viable count is 0, and accepted exposure is 104.2%. The returned status is pass. This inconsistent result is rejected as an operating basis; decisions require unrounded quantities and consistent counts. [E]
