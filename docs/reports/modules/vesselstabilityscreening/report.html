1. Executive Summary

Vessel stability screening is a table-based calculation of loading equilibrium and righting-arm criteria. Analysis is performed to determine the effect of loading and free surface on the supplied stability data; key results are summarized below.

  • The synthetic reference condition meets the evaluated general intact criteria.
    • The fluid metacentric height is 5.3809 m against the general minimum of 0.15 m. The result applies to supplied synthetic cross-curves, including their nonphysical extension beyond deck-edge immersion.
  • The public cross-curve example is calculated from its published inputs.
    • The righting arm at 30 degrees is 0.9449 m. No downflooding angle or operational sea-state limit is established by that example.
  • The excessive-free-surface condition does not meet the initial stability criterion.
    • The fluid metacentric height is −4.5691 m. Slack-tank exposure is to be reduced and the loading condition recalculated before acceptance, as required by the free-surface basis in the Code.

2. Introduction

Loading conditions are screened using supplied hydrostatic and cross-curve tables. Geometry-based hydrostatics and an approved stability booklet are not produced by this module.

The documented workflow accepts a configuration mapping and returns its results with comma-separated-value tables. The underlying calculation functions also accept typed weight items, hydrostatic rows and righting-arm arrays. The main intact workflow is covered here; the public example exercises the cross-curve correction directly.

3. Summary

The controls needed for a loading decision are identified from the supplied data and evaluated criteria. An operational envelope is not established by the static screen.

Mass, centres of gravity, tank moments, table displacement range, opening immersion angles and criterion applicability are to be established before interpreting acceptance. Heading, significant wave height, peak period, crane dynamics and forecast support are NOT_EVALUATED; no sea-state or heading limit is inferred.

The initial metacentric-height criterion alone does not establish acceptance of every righting-arm criterion. The excessive-free-surface condition fails the combined intact screen; reduce free-surface moments, then repeat every applicable check. Code, Part B, free surfaces.

4. Design Data

The physical system is represented by a loading inventory and supplied stability tables. The public example and synthetic reference condition are separate data sets.

4.1 Physical system and assumptions

The synthetic geometry has length 100 m, beam 20 m and depth 10 m. Water density is a supplied input of 1.025 t/m³. These data are assumed, based on previous experience; ASSUMED - to be confirmed for physical use. Synthetic fixture.

4.2 Inputs, outputs and criteria

Inputs use metres, tonnes and degrees; righting-arm areas use metre-radians. Longitudinal positions are measured forward from the aft perpendicular. Trim is positive by the stern. The keel datum is used here for vertical heights; all supplied data require a common datum.

Hydrostatic rows require draft, displacement and metacentre height. Longitudinal buoyancy and flotation centres and the moment to change trim enable end-draft estimates. Weight items require mass and vertical and longitudinal centres; tank free-surface moments are supplied directly or calculated from rectangular dimensions and density. A supplied righting-arm table is already corrected; cross-curves require correction for the fluid centre of gravity.

Returned outputs include total displacement, solid and fluid gravity centres, mean draft, metacentric height, trim, criterion values and margins, righting-arm samples and limiting fluid gravity-centre values. Conditional outputs cover lifting and damage. The overall status includes only configured checks; absent analyses do not become accepted results.

The general intact criteria are used as a calculation demonstration, with the minima shown in Detailed Results and their basis in the public Code. Applicability to a vessel type is not established. Optional weather pressure, lifting and damage criteria remain required cited inputs when those analyses are requested. Code, Part A, righting-lever properties.

5. Methodology

Weight moments are summed, hydrostatics are interpolated and free-surface correction is applied before the stability criteria are evaluated. Numerical acceptance is reported for the supplied tables.

5.1 Loading and equilibrium

W = Σwᵢ; KG = Σ(wᵢzᵢ)/W; LCG = Σ(wᵢxᵢ)/W.

W is displacement mass, wᵢ is an item mass, zᵢ is its height above keel and xᵢ is its position from the aft perpendicular. KG and LCG are the resulting vertical and longitudinal gravity centres. These are mass-moment balances. Course Notes, Summer 2020, section 3.2.2.1.

FSM = ρₜlb³/12; FSC = ΣFSM/W; KGfluid = KG + FSC; GMfluid = KM − KGfluid.

FSM is free-surface moment; ρₜ is tank-fluid density, l is free-surface length and b is transverse breadth. FSC is the virtual gravity-centre rise. KM is metacentre height above keel; GMfluid is its height above the fluid gravity centre, also called initial fluid metacentric height, GM0. The divisor follows from the second moment of a rectangular free surface. Course Notes, Summer 2020, sections 4.8.3–4.8.3.1.

q = q₁ + (W − W₁)(q₂ − q₁)/(W₂ − W₁); trim = W(LCB − LCG)/(100 MCT).

q is any interpolated hydrostatic property between rows indexed by masses W₁ and W₂. LCB and LCF denote the longitudinal buoyancy and flotation centres. MCT is moment to change trim per centimetre; the factor converts centimetres to metres. End drafts distribute trim about LCF over the length between perpendiculars. Interpolation is restricted to the tabulated range. Course Notes, Summer 2020, sections 1.6 and 3.6.2.

5.2 Righting arms and limiting gravity centre

GZ(φ) = KN(φ) − KGfluid sin φ; A = Σ[(GZᵢ + GZᵢ₊₁)/2](φᵢ₊₁ − φᵢ).

φ is heel angle, GZ is the righting arm, KN is the cross-curve arm relative to the keel reference and A is area under the curve. Angles in the integration are radians. The area expression is derived by integrating each straight-line segment exactly. Piecewise linear interpolation and trapezoidal integration are used. Course Notes, Summer 2020, sections 1.6, 2.8 and 4.5.

The limiting fluid gravity centre is searched by bisection between the keel and the metacentre for each configured criterion. The lowest finite limit governs; an infeasible criterion remains a failure, and a result capped at the metacentre is identified. The synthetic example uses search tolerance 0.0001 m as a numerical input, not an engineering allowance. The criteria are sourced to the Code; bisection is an implementation method.

6.2 Published cross-curve worked example

The published tabulated cross-curve arms are passed to the module after unit conversion. The published gravity-centre height is 23.84 ft, equivalent to 7.266432 m. The exact length conversion used is 0.3048 m/ft. Course example; unit conversion source.

At heel 30 degrees, the published cross-curve arm is 15.02 ft. Subtracting the sine correction and converting to metres gives 0.9449 m. This is the headline result calculated from the displayed inputs.

The complete input and comparison table is provided in the appendix. Differences are calculated against the published corrected-arm table, which contains an arithmetic inconsistency as well as rounded corrections, and increase in percentage magnitude near a zero righting arm. The published input gravity centre and comparison values are preserved; the arithmetic inconsistency is described in Validation. Negative arms at the largest heel indicate a restoring-moment reversal in the supplied curve; that range is excluded from any positive-stability interpretation. A flooding limit, full loading inventory and opening geometry are not supplied for this calculation, so operational acceptance is NOT_EVALUATED. Course example and stability interpretation.

8. Key Findings, Limitations and Recommendations

The evaluated criteria are met by the synthetic reference inputs. Use is limited to static screening of supplied tables and does not authorize a loading or installation operation.

8.1 Findings and actions

The excessive-free-surface condition has fluid metacentric height −4.5691 m against 0.15 m minimum and fails the initial-stability check. Reduce the sum of free-surface moments by an approved tank-management arrangement, update weights and centres, and repeat every applicable criterion before acceptance. Tank subdivision and accurate tank-state correction are addressed in the Course Notes, minimizing free-surface effects and the Code, free surfaces.

The public curve becomes negative at large heel. Keep any operating interpretation within approved opening and stability limits; obtain the downflooding information before establishing that range. The sign change is shown for numerical interpretation only. Course Notes, range of stability; Code, righting-lever properties.

Hydrostatic lookup rejects extrapolation, but the shared righting-arm interpolator holds endpoint values outside its supplied angular range. Supply curves spanning every requested evaluation angle and reject truncated curves before screening. The maximum-arm angle is taken from tabulated samples, so angular discretization affects that criterion and the limiting gravity-centre result.

8.2 Not covered

Weather, crane lifting, added-weight flooding, direct damaged hydrostatics and damaged righting-arm criteria are available sub-areas but are not calculated in this report. The added-weight route reuses intact hydrostatics with a free-surface approximation; it is not a geometry-based lost-buoyancy solution. Waves, response operators, sea-state envelopes, progressive flooding, rigging, transient sloshing and field comparisons are NOT_EVALUATED.

The method accepts externally supplied numerical data without establishing their physical provenance. Complete finite-value screening, datum reconciliation, criterion applicability, survey uncertainty and angular convergence are to be reviewed before engineering acceptance. The general criterion set alone does not establish full compliance with the Code.
