Fatigue analysis

Response amplitude operator spectral fatigue module analysis

Draft for maintainer review

Illustrative public example; screening arithmetic with unresolved method limitations.

1. Executive Summary

The response amplitude operator spectral fatigue module is a frequency-domain calculation for structural hot spots subjected to wave loading. Analysis is performed to determine the available calculation capability and the limits of the published illustrative example; the key results are summarized below.

  • The public example can be reproduced from its supplied wave spectra and scalar stress transfers.
    • The splash-zone detail gives a screening arithmetic life of 30.8 years against the configured 75.0 years. The arithmetic criterion is not met.
    • The keel-joint detail gives a screening arithmetic life of 193.8 years against the configured 75.0 years. The arithmetic criterion is met; engineering acceptance is not established.
  • Design or operating decisions are deferred pending calculation qualification.
    • The cycle-rate convention and the default fatigue estimator require correction and independent comparison. The illustrative stress transfer also has an unresolved amplitude-versus-range convention. [1–4]

2. Introduction

The system is represented by supplied scalar stress responses at a splash-zone detail and a keel-joint detail. Spectral fatigue analysis is performed for the documented illustrative wave environments; water depth, a physical riser stack-up and an actual host response are not supplied. [1]

The intended use is reproducible calculation demonstration. Structural life, allowable offset and permission to remain connected are not established by these inputs.

3.2 Criteria and required data

The implemented criterion compares reciprocal annual damage with design life multiplied by the design fatigue factor. The purpose of this criterion is to reserve a fatigue-life margin against the configured exposure; passing the arithmetic test alone does not qualify the physical design. The criterion and damage weighting are implementation definitions, rather than an independently checked standards requirement. [2]

Geometry, stiffness, boundary conditions, stress concentration, material environment, stress amplitude convention and an independently supported stress transfer are required inputs for engineering use. These quantities are NOT_EVALUATED; no substitute field values are introduced.

4. Analysis Methodology

The wave spectrum is transferred to a stress spectrum and is evaluated by the downstream fatigue library. Linear response, stationary exposure within each sea state and a scalar stress response are assumed by the calculation; applicability to an actual structure is not established. [2–4]

4.2 Transfer, moments and fatigue equations

Sσ(f) = |H(f)|² Sη(f); ω = 2πf; Sη(f) = 2π Sη(ω).

The symbol f is cyclic frequency in hertz and ω is angular frequency in radians per second. H is stress amplitude per unit wave amplitude in megapascals per metre. Sη is wave-elevation power spectral density, per hertz or per angular frequency as indicated. Sσ is stress power spectral density in square megapascals per hertz. The density conversion preserves the integral when the frequency axis changes. [3]

mₙ = ∫ fⁿ Sσ(f) df; σ = √m₀; α₂ = m₂ / √(m₀m₄); ε = √(1 − α₂²).

The symbol mₙ is a stress spectral moment of order n; σ is root-mean-square stress, α₂ is the irregularity factor and ε is the bandwidth measure. With a cyclic-frequency axis, the zero-upcrossing rate is √(m₂/m₀) and the peak rate is √(m₄/m₂), in cycles per second. The library divides these rates by an additional angular-to-cyclic conversion. Its returned damage and life therefore remain screening arithmetic. [4, 5]

N = A / Δσᵇ; d = ν E[(Δσ)ᵇ] / A; Dannual = Σ pᵢ dᵢ Tyear; L = 1 / Dannual; Lrequired = Ldesign F; M = L / Lrequired.

The symbol N is cycles to failure, Δσ is stress range in megapascals, b is the supplied stress-versus-cycle slope and A is its intercept in a consistent stress unit. The symbol d is damage per second, ν is the estimator's cycle rate, E is the cycle-range expectation, pᵢ is sea-state occurrence, Tyear is the library's annual duration, Dannual is annual damage, L is life in years, F is the configured design fatigue factor and M is the dimensionless life margin. The annual conversion follows the library convention. [2, 4]

The default estimator represents cycle ranges by an exponential and Rayleigh mixture. Its parameter implementation uses the bandwidth measure where the published method uses the irregularity factor. Agreement with another route sharing this estimator does not establish fatigue accuracy. [4, 5]

A separate diagnostic uses α₂ in the mixture parameters and cyclic-frequency rates without the extra 2π division, on the same finite stress-spectrum grid and single-slope input. The resulting splash-zone life is approximately 66.2 years instead of 30.8 years, an increase by a factor of 2.1; it remains below 75.0 years. The keel-joint life is approximately 567.7 years instead of 193.8 years, an increase by a factor of 2.9, against 75.0 years. These diagnostic estimates quantify the combined direction and magnitude of the two implementation defects; the amplitude-versus-range and curve-basis limitations remain. Correction is tracked by #2583. [1, 4, 5]

6. Key Findings

The wave-to-stress transfer and occurrence-weighted damage sequence are available through a documented public function. Reproduction of the example is established as calculation-output consistency by the accompanying rebuild test; physical calibration and fatigue-method accuracy are not established. [1–4, 6]

The splash-zone detail governs the current screening arithmetic. Its life is below the configured required life; the other detail's arithmetic pass is insufficient for engineering acceptance while the common estimator remains unqualified. [2, 4]

7. Limitations

The applicability boundary is set by the supplied scalar transfer and the unresolved numerical conventions. Project design life, inspection intervals and allowable operations cannot be established from the present example. [1–5]

The wave grid is fixed by the spectrum generator because the router does not expose grid controls. The spectrum is normalized over that finite band; this preserves a target variance without demonstrating that omitted tails or resonant responses are insignificant. Transfers are linearly interpolated and clamped at their endpoints outside their supplied frequency band. Frequency convergence and transfer-band coverage are required before engineering use. [3, 7]

The input checks do not comprehensively reject non-finite values or enforce a normalized occurrence distribution. Independent checks of finiteness, units, ordering, amplitude convention and total exposure remain necessary. The public example calls its response a stress range per wave amplitude, while the module describes stress amplitude per wave amplitude; that ambiguity is unresolved. [1–3]

Not covered

Alternative wave spectra and fatigue estimators are available but are not assessed here. Complex phase, heading combinations, multiaxial fatigue, nonlinear response, corrosion allowance, thickness effects and local stress concentration are not resolved by the demonstrated scalar transfer. Still-water statics, current loading, offset envelopes, stroke, drift-off, recoil, weak-point strength and vortex-induced vibration are not evaluated by this report. [2, 3]

8. Recommendations

Engineering acceptance is conditioned on removal of the identified calculation and input-basis gaps. The next assessment is defined by an independent reference calculation and a documented physical stress-transfer basis. [1–5]

  • Correct and independently test the frequency-unit convention and estimator parameters before using life or arithmetic pass results to establish an inspection or design decision. A single-frequency analytical rate and an independently integrated cycle-range distribution provide separate checks. [4, 5]
  • Resolve whether the supplied response is stress amplitude or stress range; provide calibrated structural transfers covering the energetic wave band, with material, detail category and environment stated. [1, 3]
  • Establish wave-grid and response-grid convergence, retain omitted-tail evidence, and enforce finite inputs and a stated exposure normalization. [2, 3, 7]
  • After correction, recalculate the splash-zone detail against the configured required life. If that criterion remains unmet, assess stress reduction or a revised operating exposure with the same qualified calculation basis. No numerical operating limit or retrofit date is established by this example. [1, 2, 5]

10. Acronyms

The terminology is defined for reading the calculation basis and the source records. Symbols are defined beside their equations.

A response amplitude operator is the frequency-dependent stress transfer per unit wave amplitude. Power spectral density describes variance distributed over frequency. JONSWAP means Joint North Sea Wave Project. A stress-versus-cycle curve relates cyclic stress range to cycles to failure. The design fatigue factor multiplies the required design life. CSV means comma-separated values.
