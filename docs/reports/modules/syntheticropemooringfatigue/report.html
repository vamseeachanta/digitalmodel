2. Introduction

Synthetic rope service life is screened from specified tension ranges, cycle counts and load ratios. The calculation is performed without a global mooring model or a licensed solver.

The main workflow covers tension-range fatigue, a supplied creep-life law and a minimum-tension ratio check for each synthetic span. The least margin governs each line, and the least line margin governs the returned screening result. An overall pass is returned only when every line meets every supplied criterion.

The documented public example is used as a numerical demonstration. It is not a published physical experiment or a qualified site design. The module is classified as development maturity in the module registry.

3. Design data and acceptance basis

The design basis is supplied as a configuration mapping with consistent force and time units. Loads and material laws are prescribed inputs; site conditions are not calculated.

3.1 System and input basis

The example design exposure is 25.0 years. ASSUMED - to be confirmed: the supplied cycle counts represent this complete design exposure, with no additional annualisation. This interpretation is required because the module divides design life by the accumulated damage.

Water depth, span length, rope diameter, installation state, host motion, metocean weighting and measured tension histories are not supplied. They are not invented. Geometry is shown only as a labelled load-path schematic in the system figure. The physical input values and complete bin set are recorded in Table A and Table C.

3.2 Criteria and rationale

The fatigue margin is L / (T × DFF). The creep margin is Lcreep / (T × creep safety factor). The minimum-tension margin is Tmin / (MBL × minimum allowable tension ratio). Every margin is required to reach unity; a life below its factored requirement or a tension below its prescribed minimum therefore fails the supplied criterion. These definitions and the unity comparison are implementation acceptance rules, not an assertion of code compliance.

The public example attributes its polyester curve and fatigue factor to Position mooring, October 2010, polyester fatigue provisions. That edition has not been opened for this analysis. The public configuration values are reproduced in Table F, including its attributed curve and factor; source qualification is NOT_EVALUATED. The example's mean-load modifier, creep parameters and low-tension threshold are demonstration inputs; their material applicability is NOT_EVALUATED. The complete unchanged configuration remains the input record identified in Section 9.2.

4. Analysis methodology

The documented workflow is called directly on the public configuration, with only the output directory redirected. Prescribed loads are screened at the rope-span boundary shown below.

4.1 Fatigue theory and supplied material functions

Linear fatigue accumulation is represented by Miner's rule:

D = Σ(nᵢ / Nᵢ); L = T / D.

D is accumulated fatigue damage, nᵢ is the cycle count in bin i, Nᵢ is the allowable count at that range, T is the design exposure in years, and L is the extrapolated fatigue life in years. The damage sum and its approximation limits are described in the open institutional fatigue teaching note, May 2001, cumulative-damage section, equation (1). The life expression follows by repeating the same exposure block: damage is proportional to elapsed exposure under that assumption. This bookkeeping does not qualify a rope material curve.

The required fatigue input function maps tension range divided by breaking load to allowable cycles. Its power-law intercept, slope and mean-load modifier are supplied in the public configuration, rather than selected by the module. The implemented relation is N = aD × exp(−k × mean tension / MBL) × R−m, where R is tension range / MBL. The public configuration attributes aD and m to the cited 2010 Table F3; k is an unsourced demonstration coefficient (Table F). The public example disables that modifier; no mean-load fatigue sensitivity is demonstrated. The claimed curve basis is identified in the cited standard edition and clause titles; qualification is NOT_EVALUATED because the primary text is not in hand.

The required creep input function maps mean tension divided by breaking load to creep life in years. The supplied demonstration relation is Lcreep = Lref × 10(rref − mean tension / MBL) × c. Lref, rref and c are the unsourced demonstration coefficients in Table F; no standard calibration is claimed. No open material calibration is held for that input law. Its coefficients are required inputs and its physical qualification is NOT_EVALUATED; the calculated creep life is conditional on that law. The minimum-tension threshold is likewise a required input, not a predicted axial-compression fatigue life.

4.2 Interface, outputs and arithmetic checks

The public workflow accepts a nonempty line list, material names, breaking load, mean and minimum tension, positive tension ranges and cycle counts, explicit fatigue and creep models, and separate life safety factors. Global model settings are required even when line overrides are provided. Polyester and generic synthetic material labels are admitted. Nylon requires a line-specific fatigue curve; high-modulus polyethylene requires line-specific fatigue and creep models. Chain and steel wire are rejected.

The same configuration mapping is mutated and returned. Its input settings are replaced by line summaries, the governing line and mechanism, screening status and paths to the bin and line-summary comma-separated-value files. A copy of the input mapping is therefore required when the original input record is needed after the call. The bin file contains range, normalised range, count, allowable count and damage. The summary file contains calculated lives, margins and governing results.

Arithmetic is checked separately against hand-derived range and minimum-tension normalisation, allowable cycles, both line damages and fatigue lives, fatigue margin, required creep life, governing creep margin and minimum-tension margins. These are arithmetic anchors, not physical material tests. The rebuild test also checks every marked example value against a fresh call.

6. Key findings

The screening outcome is controlled by the least of the separate mechanism margins. Fatigue damage alone is insufficient to determine the overall result.

The large calculated fatigue lives are extrapolations of the prescribed exposure and input curve, not recommended service lives.

Existing repository tests provide workflow status and curve-wiring checks. No physical rope benchmark or source-qualified creep calibration is established by those assertions. The evidence inventory and the checks performed for this report are separated in the quality-assurance appendix.

7. Limitations

The calculation is limited to prescribed scalar loads and supplied material functions. Engineering acceptance remains not evaluated until material and exposure data are qualified.

7.1 Limits of use

Temperature is passed to the outputs without changing the fatigue or creep calculation. There is no moisture, abrasion, ageing, bedding-in, bend-over-sheave, termination or load-sequence model. The minimum-tension margin is a threshold screen, not an axial-compression damage model. Normalisation by breaking load does not establish a characteristic-strength or residual-strength basis.

Positive-value guards do not establish a finite numerical domain or physical ordering of minimum, mean and peak tension. Nonfinite inputs, duplicate line identifiers and extrapolation outside calibrated material ranges require checking before the call. Extreme coefficients can cause overflow or underflow; a numerical return does not establish validity. Output files are written sequentially, without transactional recovery, and repeated input stems can overwrite earlier results.

7.2 Not covered

The separate fatigue interpolation atlas, material database ingestion, stiffness and creep-elongation design utilities, global station-keeping, host response, anchor design and metallic-line fatigue are not covered. No dynamic solver, rainflow counting, tension-history generation, static equilibrium, recoil or geometric mesh is used in this workflow.

8. Recommendations

Material qualification and exposure definition are required before these margins are used for service decisions. The following actions are based on the failure and the missing-data entries recorded in this analysis.

Overall screening does not meet the supplied criteria. Minimum tension governs. First polyester line: Minimum tension criterion is met; calculated 1,500.0 kN against 900.0 kN required, margin 1.67 against unity (1.000). Basis: supplied model parameters and prescribed loads. Creep rupture governs. Second polyester line: Creep rupture criterion is not met; calculated 3.162 years against 75.0 years required, margin 0.0422 against unity (1.000). Basis: supplied model parameters and prescribed loads.

Second polyester line requires reassessment for creep rupture. Obtain qualified supplier creep data at the intended load, temperature and environment, then reassess mean tension and breaking capacity. Reducing tension or changing capacity also changes global stiffness, offsets and anchor loads; those effects require a global mooring assessment outside this calculation.

A design-exposure tension histogram is required with explicit environmental weights and cycle-counting conventions. The distinction between full tension range and half-range amplitude is recorded before evaluating the curve. Minimum tension is checked against a material-qualified low-tension criterion, with monitoring tied to that criterion once established. No operational trigger or inspection interval is invented while those data are absent.

The numerical interface is strengthened next by finite-value checks, physical load ordering, unique line identifiers, explicit exposure units, provenance for material laws and non-overwriting output handling. Additional reference cases then cover each mechanism at its acceptance boundary and outside its calibrated domain. These actions follow the observed implementation limits, rather than a claim of standard compliance.

10. Acronyms and supporting records

Symbols and supporting records are provided for interpretation of the screening results. The complete example values and data gaps are recorded in the appendices below.

MBL means minimum breaking load as named by the input interface. T-N means tension range versus allowable cycle count. HMPE means high-modulus polyethylene. CSV means comma-separated values. QA means quality assurance. The symbols D, nᵢ, Nᵢ, T and L are defined in Section 4.1. A margin is the calculated quantity divided by its required minimum; unity is the acceptance boundary. NOT_EVALUATED means that no qualified engineering conclusion is established for the named item.

The existing workflow registry test asserts the two line statuses, overall creep failure, positive bin outputs and decreasing allowable cycles with increasing range. The existing curve-wiring test compares calculated allowable cycles with the same input power law; it is a regression assertion, not an independently sourced physical benchmark. Both named workflow nodes in Section 9.2 passed with their application dependencies installed; they run in the tests-workflows CI shard. The two rope-specific atlas nodes, test_synthetic_rope_fatigue_atlas_is_log_log_exact and test_fatigue_bins_handler_shared_by_both_ropes, also passed. No solver integration is imported by this report.

The report test suite checks the full rebuild, every marked example value, hand-derived arithmetic anchors, the section and reference structure, offline assets, interactive chart bindings, public wording and explicit UTF-8 text input/output with line-feed output. The report tests are run with the interpreter's UTF-8 override unset. The mathematical anchors are range divided by breaking load, minimum tension divided by breaking load, minimum tension divided by required minimum tension, independent power-law cycle counts and damage sums, exposure divided by damage, the reciprocal factored damage, the factored creep exposure and the square-root form of the second line's prescribed creep calculation divided by its requirement. The test source records each arithmetic derivation.
