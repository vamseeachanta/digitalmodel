1. Executive Summary

The drilling riser is a tensioned conduit between a floating host and a seabed wellhead. Analysis is performed to determine the capabilities and limits of the drilling riser module; the key results are summarized below.

  • The public stack-up example is reproduced as an axial screening calculation.
    • The applied top-force estimate is 2,304.3 kip and the lowest effective force is 460.9 kip against the zero-effective-tension reference.
    • The pressure-only wall estimate is 0.984 in; actual wall thickness and allowances are not supplied, so a wall acceptance result is not established.
  • No operating window is established. The allowable drilling and connected offsets are not established.
    • A watch circle and equipment acceptance criteria are absent; do not connect on the basis of this calculation.
    • Dynamic response, recoil and fatigue are not evaluated.

2. Introduction

The drilling riser calculation package is used for component assembly, axial sizing, static response and operating assessments. Riser analysis is performed here for the registered public stack-up example; no site water depth is identified.

The main workflow accepts aggregate weight, pressure and geometry, calculates force and pressure-only thickness, and writes an effective-tension profile. The documented router is called directly, without a licensed solver. The example is public repository documentation, not an independently measured field benchmark.

3.3 Metocean, criteria and load cases

The illustrated profile is still-water axial equilibrium. Current-dominated and wave-dominated environmental sets are absent. No return-period, sea-state, heading or offset matrix is supplied.

The zero-effective-tension line is a force-sign reference from the implemented axial equilibrium, not a drilling acceptance limit. The weight and wall multipliers are example assumptions, not verified standard acceptance criteria. The default slip-ring weight and buoyancy factors are inherited implementation constants; their recorded source and qualification boundary are given in References.

4. Analysis Methodology

The axial calculation is evaluated first by hand equilibrium and then by the module. The boundary-condition idealisation is shown beside the physical stack-up; no finite-element mesh, centralisers, lateral supports or stops are represented.

4.1 Equations and symbols

The applied top force is T = W F, and the effective force at depth fraction d is E(d) = T − W d. W is the example submerged string weight, F is the example weight multiplier, T is the calculated top force, d is the fraction measured downward from the surface, and E is effective tension. The profile endpoints are surface and lower end. These equations are implemented screening algebra, sourced to the calculation functions in References.

The pressure-only wall estimate is t = P D S / (2 Y). t denotes required wall thickness, P is design internal pressure, D is outside diameter, S is the example wall multiplier, and Y is specified minimum yield strength. The coefficient follows the implemented Barlow thin-wall hoop-equilibrium relation; external pressure, bending, corrosion, wear and manufacturing allowances are not included.

The slip-ring expression is R = W f − B b + A(γm Hm − γw Hw). R is minimum slip-ring force, B is buoyancy uplift, f and b are weight and buoyancy factors, A is the internal area, γm and γw are mud and seawater weight densities, and Hm and Hw are their vertical heads. The implementation expects flooded-steel apparent weight with fluid head counted once. The example does not document that weight convention, so its slip-ring result remains illustrative.

4.2 Calculation sequence and outputs

The public input is read, the documented router is called, and its profile CSV is read back. The returned scalars are top force, pressure-only wall thickness, optional slip-ring force and profile point count. The CSV contains depth fraction and effective force. The module uses kilonewtons, millimetres and megapascals internally; engineering displays use the component adapter's conversion convention.

The build-up beyond still water is not performed. Static current response needs distributed drag and section stiffness; wave-current dynamics need response amplitude operators, sea-state spectra, headings, initial conditions and travel limits. Regular heave alone is not substituted for host-motion response.

5. Detailed Results

The scalar results and axial profile are calculated from the public example. The effective-force chart precedes environmental and dynamic assessments because it is the available static arithmetic check.

5.1 Statics against hand equilibrium

The module returns 2,304.3 kip top force and 460.9 kip lowest effective force against the zero-effective-tension reference. The maximum absolute relative difference from hand equilibrium is 0.0 %, using hand equilibrium as the reference at the supplied profile stations. This checks arithmetic consistency of the assumed linear profile; it is not independent qualification of the physical model.

The wall estimate is 0.984 in. The illustrative slip-ring force is 1,610.9 kip. The router reports these separately and does not reconcile them into a governing tensioner setting. The flat weight multiplier omits surviving tensioner count, efficiency and angular allowances; its result is not the package's separate minimum-tensioner-capacity calculation.

Wall tension is not evaluated. Effective tension and true wall force are different quantities; a wall-force curve is not fabricated from the aggregate weight profile. Foam-zone locations are also absent.

5.2 Current and operating envelopes

Static current response is not evaluated. Current-dominated environments and wave-dominated environments have no supplied case sets or acceptance criteria, so no offset envelope is established for drilling or staying connected. The static tension chart is not an operating-envelope chart.

5.3 Dynamics, drift-off, recoil and weak point

Dynamic stroke, tension, angles and stop strikes are not evaluated. Host-motion response data and equipment travel limits are absent.

Drift-off under the annual current is not evaluated; drift-off under the decadal current is not evaluated. No disconnect time or watch circle is established; do not connect on this basis.

Recoil is not evaluated: data needed: closure curves. Weak point analysis is not performed. Vortex-induced vibration and fatigue are not evaluated.

6. Key Findings

Public calculation entry points and test evidence are present. Their existence supports reproducible arithmetic, while operating acceptance remains outside the supplied example data.

The effective-force profile stays above the zero-effective-tension reference in this example. That force-sign observation does not establish tensioner capacity, structural strength, travel margin or safe connection.

The separate pressure-only wall and slip-ring estimates are returned without a combined design decision. The mass, buoyancy and fluid-head conventions require reconciliation before the slip-ring value is used as a setting.

7. Limitations

The example is an aggregate axial screen. Its output is limited by the supplied weight convention, unverified acceptance factors and absent component and environmental data.

The scalar helpers perform arithmetic without comprehensive finite-value, positivity or geometry validation. The router checks depth-grid ordering and range and rejects incomplete optional slip-ring fields, but scalar plausibility remains a caller responsibility. No standard compliance or equipment certification is asserted.

7.1 Not covered

Component-registry assembly, schedule reconciliation, taper stress-joint sizing, static beam and conductor response, operating envelopes, telemetry monitoring, drift-off screening, response correction, damping, tool passage, stack-up drawing reconciliation, global-model preparation and result aggregation are separate capabilities. They are identified from the module source but are not exercised by the worked stack-up example.

The paper-based drive-off fixture is explicitly recorded as incomplete and incompatible with the scalar drift-off screen. Its reproduction test is skipped; it is not a successful published benchmark. The repository holds closed-form static-beam tests, but those tests do not qualify the operating envelope or coupled dynamic response.

8. Recommendations

A complete operating assessment is recommended before a connection decision is made. The required work follows the missing inputs and model boundaries documented above.

Reconcile flooded-steel weight, buoyancy and fluid-head accounting against the actual component schedule, then apply the separate surviving-tensioner calculation with documented capacity, efficiency and angular allowances. The source for this separation is the assembly and slip-ring function documentation in References.

Establish the actual wall, corrosion and wear allowances, pressure differential and combined-load criteria before comparing thickness. Populate component stiffness, buoyancy zones, support locations, host response amplitude operators, metocean sets, joint travel, disconnect duration and closure curves before calculating operating limits. Retain the zero-force screen as a diagnostic and require a complete watch-circle assessment before connection.

Extend automated input checks to reject non-finite values and physically inconsistent geometry. Retain the independent equilibrium comparison and add publicly sourced component and dynamic benchmarks with complete inputs, compatible model assumptions and declared tolerances before extending the stated validation scope.

9. References

9.1 Basis and source status

The registered public stack-up example supplies every case input and the explicit weight and wall multipliers. These are example assumptions, not measured design data. The workflow registry identifies the example as an offline calculation.

The stack-up calculation functions and router define all equations and output conventions used here. The component adapter defines the display conversions. The default weight factor 1.05 and buoyancy factor 0.96 are read from the implementation, without substituting a value from a publication.

The implementation cites American Bureau of Shipping, Guidance Notes on Drilling Riser Analysis, 2017, Section 3, Subsection 3, item iii), equation (3.2), and American Petroleum Institute Recommended Practice 16Q, Design, Selection, Operation and Maintenance of Marine Drilling Riser Systems, second edition, 2017. The latter clause text is recorded as pending a held-edition check. No licensed table or clause values are reproduced as independent acceptance criteria.

Appendix: quality assurance pre-flight

The router is called with the full example, and temporary CSV output is read back for the report. Top force, wall estimate, slip-ring force and every profile station are reconciled to direct module calls in the rebuild tests. Independent decimal arithmetic checks equilibrium and the pressure relation. A changed-input test checks propagation through the summary, chart and displayed values. Byte-for-byte rebuilding checks the delivered HTML against its generator.

The stylesheet, chart data and interaction code are embedded; no external script, image or stylesheet is needed. Test-definition counts identify evidence held, not a whole-package pass rate. No licensed solver or physical validation is used.
