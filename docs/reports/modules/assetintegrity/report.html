1. Executive Summary

The asset integrity module is a collection of metallic-component screening and assessment calculations. Analysis is performed to determine the remaining pressure resistance of a blunt corrosion defect and the evidence required before a component decision is made. The key results are summarized below.

  • The published shallow pipe example meets the stated pressure comparison.
    • The capped reference pressure is 1,093.2 psi against an operating-pressure demand of 910.0 psi. The implemented relative-depth boundary is 0.80 of the nominal wall; the example lies within that boundary. This is an example reproduction, without operating-asset qualification. [A][D]
  • The intact design-pressure cap changes the interpretation of the module result.
    • The module returns an uncapped factored pressure of 1,099.1 psi for the shallow defect. The public example also caps pressure at the intact design pressure. That cap is applied separately for the reference comparison. [C][E]
  • An operating limit requires the component record.
    • The adopted standard edition, design factor, sizing uncertainty, full defect boundaries, other loads and degradation history are required before an operating decision is established. Remaining life and a next inspection date are not evaluated from this example. [F][G]

2. Introduction and scope

Remaining strength, measurement sufficiency and the disposition of a damaged component are addressed by the module. The pressure-resistance calculation for an isolated longitudinal corrosion defect is examined in detail because documented callable functions and public numerical comparisons are present. [D][E]

2.1 Main workflow

Outside diameter, nominal wall, maximum loss depth, axial flaw length and specified minimum yield strength are entered. Original B31G is called with an explicit pressure demand and a factor derived from the public example. Failure pressure, factored pressure and the separate applicability record are returned. The intact design-pressure cap is then applied only for comparison with the published example. [D][C]

The general measured-grid route is also available: the grid is parsed, damage morphology is classified, screening calculations are applied, a disposition is produced and measurement sufficiency is assessed. This orchestration route is distinct from the standalone corrosion-pressure function used here. [H]

2.2 Not covered

Modified B31G, the effective-area profile search, circumferential projection and the separate DNV corrosion-pressure method are present but are not calculated in this worked example. Crack assessment, pitting, dents, shell distortion, hull wastage, mooring-chain dimensions, composite-repair screening, inspection planning, campaign comparability and riser-joint assessment are additional surfaces. Their presence does not extend the qualification of this pressure example to those damage mechanisms. The escalation interface prepares assessment information; it does not establish a numerical higher-level solution. [I][J]

3. Design basis and criteria

A straight pipe with isolated blunt metal loss is assessed under internal pressure. The example geometry and material strength are taken from the public program documentation; no measured component is represented. [A]

3.1 Governing basis

The implemented original method carries the ASME B31G-2012 label. The historical example is attributed by its public documentation to ASME B31G-1991, Nonmandatory Appendix A. The numerical comparison therefore establishes reproduction of that public example, rather than edition-matched qualification of every requirement of the later standard. Equations and example values below are obtained from the open program documentation and the repository implementation; a licensed publication is not reproduced. [B][A][D]

3.2 Comparison criteria and rationale

The pressure comparison is met when the capped reference pressure is at least the stated operating-pressure demand and no implemented applicability flag is raised. The cap prevents the example calculation from exceeding the intact design-pressure basis. The relative-depth boundary is 0.80 of nominal wall in the implementation; the recomputed depth ratio controls the flag, and binary rounding can place the nominal boundary sample just above the limit. This check alone does not establish complete material, flaw or loading applicability. [C][D]

Agreement with the published shallow-example pressure is checked within 1.0 psi. The calculation at the published rounded depth boundary is checked within 3.0 psi of the operating-pressure demand. These are the existing numerical test tolerances, rather than measurement allowances or operating margins. [E]

The factor entered for this example is the reciprocal of its published combined design factor, 0.72. A different design basis requires an explicit corresponding factor. The module's default factor is not adopted implicitly. [A][D]

4. Inputs and outputs

Pipe geometry is entered in inches and strength and pressure are entered in pounds per square inch. Loss depth is positive into the wall; remaining wall and loss depth are different quantities. The selected function performs no automatic unit conversion. [D]

4.1 Required inputs

Outside diameter is 30.0 in, nominal wall is 0.438 in, axial corrosion length is 7.5 in and specified minimum yield strength is 52,000.0 psi. The shallow corrosion depth is 0.100 in. Pressure demand is 910.0 psi. These values describe the published example; they are not site design data. [A]

The low-level function rejects nonpositive geometry, wall at least as large as diameter, negative length and depth outside the wall. Complete finite-value, strength, pressure and factor checks remain the caller's responsibility. The broader pipeline-screen adapter has stronger input validation; its input contract is listed in the appendix. [D][F]

4.2 Returned quantities

Predicted failure pressure, intact flow-stress pressure, flow stress, the bulging factor, lost-area fraction, remaining strength factor and uncapped factored pressure are returned. A pressure comparison boolean is returned when demand is supplied. Applicability is returned separately; a positive pressure comparison with an applicability flag is not an established operating result. Exact field names and the call example are confined to the reference appendix. [D]

5. Theory and methodology

Pressure resistance is estimated by reducing the intact flow-stress pressure for longitudinal loss of load-carrying area and local bulging. The original method uses a parabolic area idealization for the shorter-defect branch and a remaining-ligament expression for the longer-defect branch. [B]

5.1 Equations and symbols

D is outside diameter, t is nominal wall, d is maximum loss depth and L is axial loss length. Sy is specified minimum yield strength, Sf is flow stress, z is the dimensionless geometry parameter, a is fractional lost area, M is the dimensionless bulging factor and Pf is predicted failure pressure. [B]

z = L² / (D t); Sf = cf Sy; a = ca d / t; M = √(1 + cm z)

The implemented coefficients are cf = 1.1, ca = two thirds (0.667 when displayed), and cm = 0.8. The bulging expression applies for z at or below 20.0. The coefficient display is rounded; full precision is retained by the called module. [B][D]

Pf = (2 Sf t / D) (1 − a) / (1 − a / M)

For z above the stated branch boundary, the implemented remaining-ligament expression is used instead. [B]

Pf = (2 Sf t / D) (1 − d / t)

5.2 Pressure factor, reference cap and disposition

F is the combined design factor taken from the public example. Pmodule is the module's uncapped factored pressure, Pdesign is the intact yield-based design-pressure cap and Preference is the capped comparison pressure. The remaining strength factor is Pf divided by the intact flow-stress pressure. [A][C][D]

Pmodule = F Pf; Pdesign = 2 Sy t F / D; Preference = min(Pmodule, Pdesign)

The reference cap is a comparison adapter outside the selected function. The published program applies additional intermediate rounding; exact equality of rounded boundary values is not inferred. At an operating asset, independent design and applicability checks remain necessary even when the example pressure comparison is met. [C][E]

6. Worked example

The public shallow-defect example is calculated by calling the original corrosion-pressure function. A second call uses the published rounded depth boundary with the same geometry, material strength and pressure factor. Every displayed result in this section is bound to those returned quantities or to the stated reference-cap calculation. [A][E]

6.1 Calculated pressure resistance

For the shallow depth of 0.100 in, flow stress is 57,200.0 psi, the bulging factor is 2.103, and the fractional lost area is 0.152. Predicted failure pressure is 1,526.5 psi and the remaining strength factor is 0.914. The uncapped factored pressure is 1,099.1 psi. [D]

The intact design-pressure cap governs at 1,093.2 psi against the operating-pressure demand of 910.0 psi, leaving a calculated pressure difference of 183.2 psi. The capped pressure is within 0.1 % of the published rounded pressure of 1,093.0 psi. The example lies within the implemented depth boundary; this result does not establish an operating limit for a measured component. [A][D]

At the published rounded depth boundary of 0.249 in, predicted failure pressure is 1,265.2 psi and the factored pressure governs at 910.9 psi. That pressure is within 0.2 % of the published pressure demand, within the stated numerical tolerance. The rounded depth boundary is not an inspection trigger because sizing uncertainty and future loss are absent. [A][E][G]

6.2 Depth sensitivity

Depth is varied while pipe geometry, material strength, flaw length and pressure factor are held fixed. Pressure resistance decreases as depth increases. The chart in the validation section draws the demand and the implemented depth boundary; each pressure point is obtained from a separate module call. No corrosion rate or time projection is assigned. [D]

7. Validation evidence

Numerical agreement with a public example is checked separately from physical qualification of an operating component. Existing tests, previous-calculation anchors, synthetic workflow examples and inspection-fixture tests are held in the repository; their evidence scopes differ. [E][J]

7.1 Public and repository evidence

The existing published-example tests cover capped original-method pressure and the rounded depth boundary. The shallow comparison alone is governed by the intact cap; the deeper call exercises corrosion resistance directly. Separate published single-defect comparisons are held for the DNV capacity method. Those capacity checks do not establish the choice of operating factors or qualification of interacting defects. [E]

Modified-method pressure and uniform-profile effective-area results in that test file are previous-calculation anchors, without independent published outputs for those exact cases. The focused corrosion tests also check limiting geometry, depth and length trends, invalid geometry and critical-profile segment selection. Their passing assertions establish those checks, without field calibration. [E][K]

Documented synthetic pipeline grids exercise the wider screening workflow. Separate inspection-fixture tests exercise parsing and the general assessment chain; no inspection record is incorporated in this report. The existing source audit identifies unresolved edition and formula bindings in the general and local metal-loss routes. A screening status from those routes is not treated as complete standard qualification. [F][L][M]

7.2 Interactive calculated comparison

The point dispositions are: green, the pressure criterion is met; red, the pressure criterion is not met; amber, method applicability is exceeded. A calculated pressure shortfall requires review of a lower operating pressure or repair before the stated demand is adopted. An applicability exceedance requires a different qualified assessment or a repair disposition; extrapolated pressure is not used. [F][D]

The nominal depth-boundary sample raises the native applicability flag because binary representation places its recomputed relative depth just above the implemented comparison. That sample is reference-only; no tolerance is added to force a passing status. The returned flag requires examination before component screening is interpreted. [D]

8. Limitations and recommendations

Numerical behavior is established for the example's stated input basis. Operating-asset acceptance, measurement uncertainty, future degradation and complete standard applicability are not established. [E][F]

8.1 Limits of use

Crack-like loss, dents with associated damage, defect interaction, weld flaws, external pressure, bending, compression and combined-load failure are outside this calculation. Material applicability is not inferred from a supplied yield-strength number. The reference implementation applies additional restrictions that the selected low-level function does not enforce. The implemented depth flag is therefore a necessary software check, rather than a complete method-selection decision. [B][D][F]

The function's pressure-comparison field is independent of its applicability record. Both records require examination. The factored pressure field is not automatically capped at the intact design pressure. A returned number or a positive boolean does not authorize operation. [D]

Thickness-sizing uncertainty, future corrosion allowance, complete flaw boundaries, pressure history and other loads are absent from the public example. Remaining life, inspection interval and a repair design are recorded as NOT_EVALUATED. No assumed site value is substituted for these required inputs. [G]

8.2 Recommendations and way forward

Establish the original design basis and the adopted assessment edition before selecting a factor or interpreting a pressure margin. Retain the intact design cap when reproducing this public comparison. For an operating component, apply the restrictions of the adopted method rather than transferring the example's factor. [A][C][D]

Resolve the full axial defect extent and inspection sizing accuracy before a pressure decision is issued. Where the required pressure exceeds calculated resistance, assess a lower pressure or a qualified repair. Where applicability is exceeded or another damage mechanism is present, refer the component to the relevant assessment route rather than extending this curve. These actions follow the documented screening disposition and applicability handling. [F][D]

Obtain comparable dated inspections before assigning a corrosion rate or inspection interval. For the general measured-grid workflow, resolve the existing source-audit limitations before a complete general- or local-loss acceptance claim is issued. The next engineering deliverable is a component-specific input and applicability record, followed by a calculation on that confirmed basis. [N][M]
