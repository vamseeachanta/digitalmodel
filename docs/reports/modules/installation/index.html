1. Executive Summary

The installation module is a collection of screening calculations for offshore lifting and pipe laying. Analysis is performed to determine the foundation splash-zone wave-height limits under the illustrative catalog basis; key results are summarized below.

  • The foundation screen is available for all catalog sizes.
    • Calculated wave-height limits span 1.76 m to 5.70 m across the supplied period grid, with the catalog inputs retained. Calculated results.
    • Slamming is the largest individual hydrodynamic term in this example. The sum of the terms is compared with the reduced submerged weight. Calculation basis.
  • Installation acceptance is NOT_EVALUATED.
    • Vessel orientation, crane capacity at radius, sling tensions, station-keeping tolerance and the combined sea-state limit require a qualified vessel basis.
    • Vessel motions, local forecasts and lowering dynamics remain required before an installation decision is made.

2. Introduction

The foundation splash-zone calculation is examined as the main workflow in this analysis. The public installation example is reproduced through the existing calculation interface, without a dynamic installation simulation.

The module also contains a rigid-jumper screening workflow and scalar pipe-laying checks. Their functions, input contracts and test evidence are identified below; numerical reproduction is limited to the foundation workflow. Module scope.

3. Summary

The installation controls are separated into calculated foundation demands and required vessel information. A combined envelope is not assigned while any governing system remains unevaluated.

Vessel motions, crane capacity, rigging capacity, station keeping and deeper lowering are NOT_EVALUATED. The foundation curve is therefore retained as a screening result only; no heading-specific operating envelope is issued.

Live forecast support is offered after the vessel-specific operating threshold is established. The trigger wave height and a two-minute forecast acceptance basis remain NOT_EVALUATED. No forecast data is supplied. Forecast scope.

4. Design Data

The physical basis is taken from the public foundation catalog. Every inherited engineering input is marked ASSUMED - to be confirmed; a sourced input is not treated as a qualified installation value.

4.1 Vessel, crane and rigging

Vessel particulars, crane radius and capacity, hoist stiffness, reeving, lift-point locations and allowable sling capacities are required inputs. Vessel orientation and station-keeping tolerance are NOT_EVALUATED.

The catalog describes sling counts, nominal sling lengths, nominal angles and hook elevation. Attachment and spreader coordinates are absent, so a dimensionally consistent rigging arrangement and a checked three-dimensional rigging view are NOT_EVALUATED. No sling-load distribution is calculated. Catalog basis.

4.2 Structures

The open-frame foundation envelope, bottom plate and skirt are shown for each catalog size. These drawings represent supplied envelope dimensions; member layouts and approved fabrication drawings are unavailable.

4.3 Environment, stages and criteria

Peak periods from 6.0 s to 16.0 s are supplied by the calculation's default grid. Water depth, directional wave scatter, wave steepness checks, currents and vessel response amplitude operators are not supplied; a response amplitude operator is the vessel response per unit incident-wave amplitude. No regional wave climate is inferred. Calculation basis.

Lift-off and in-air handling precede splash-zone crossing; submerged lowering and landing follow it. Only the splash-zone force balance is calculated here. Static submerged weight is reduced by the inherited reserve divisor before comparison with the sum of slamming, drag and inertia.

The criterion is an illustrative module force balance. The reserve divisor is a caller input, not a substantiated dynamic amplification or minimum sling-tension criterion. Standard-attributed coefficients are inherited from the public catalog as assumptions; the licensed clauses have not been checked. Standards basis.

5. Methodology

The calculation is performed for every foundation and every supplied peak period. The surface wave velocity and acceleration are inserted into a quadratic force balance, from which the nonnegative significant wave-height root is returned.

5.1 Theory and equations

Hₛ is significant wave height in m, Tₚ is peak period in s, vₗ is lowering speed in m/s, ρ is water density in kg/m³, A is bottom projected area in m², V is displaced volume in m³ and W is submerged weight in N. Cₛ, Cᵈ and Cₐ are dimensionless slamming, drag and added-mass coefficients; D is the dimensionless reserve divisor.

v = πHₛ/Tₚ + vₗ; a = 2π²Hₛ/Tₚ²
Fₛ = ρCₛAv²/2; Fᵈ = ρCᵈAv²/2; Fᵢ = ρ(1 + Cₐ)Va
Fₛ + Fᵈ + Fᵢ ≤ W/D

Fₛ, Fᵈ and Fᵢ are upward slamming, drag and inertia forces in N. The integer coefficients arise from the linear-wave harmonic relations and the force definitions implemented in the cited calculation. Hₛ is used as twice the representative wave amplitude; this substitution does not describe an extreme irregular-wave crest distribution. Implemented equations.

K = ρ(Cₛ + Cᵈ)A/2; J = ρ(1 + Cₐ)V
α = K(π/Tₚ)²; β = 2K(π/Tₚ)vₗ + 2Jπ²/Tₚ²; γ = Kvₗ² − W/D
Hₛ = max[0, (−β + √(β² − 4αγ))/(2α)]

K and J collect the quadratic-drag and inertia coefficients; α, β and γ are the quadratic coefficients. A zero result also occurs when the implemented quadratic or discriminant is invalid. Zero therefore requires input review before it is interpreted as a calm-water operating limit.

5.2 Public interfaces and outputs

The foundation function accepts a structure record, a period list, a lowering speed and a reserve divisor. It returns the limiting wave height and governing individual force term at each period, plus submerged weight, bottom area, speed and divisor. Returned wave heights are rounded to hundredths of a metre.

The set interface selects lift records with hydrodynamic data. When a vessel heavy-lift envelope is supplied, the smaller vessel or foundation limit is returned at each period; missing vessel points remain unavailable. The full configuration interface resolves catalogs and a vessel basis, calculates lifts and envelopes, and writes HTML and structured results. No missing-vessel substitute is introduced here. Interface definitions.

7. Validation

Existing numerical evidence is distinguished from physical validation. The public example is supported by regression assertions and analytical consistency checks; field correlation is not supplied.

The foundation example contains 3 structures and 18 foundation-period results. Existing splash-zone tests check a small-foundation numerical reference, monotonic period response, weight-to-area sensitivity, combination with the supplied vessel envelope and omission of records lacking hydrodynamic data. Envelope tests also retain unavailable vessel points. Existing test evidence.

The existing pamphlet test file contains 39 test definitions. Adjacent pipe-laying integrity and stress files contain 20 and 14 definitions respectively. These are source-inventory counts, not a count of passed engineering cases. The integrity file includes a strict expected failure for the unresolved weld-repair interpretation. Test inventory.

The accompanying rebuild check compares every displayed result with a fresh module call and brackets the force-balance root within the module's rounding interval. Force contributions in the chart are independently reconstructed from the documented equations; they are not additional return fields of the module.

Validation against field measurements is planned using synchronized incident waves, vessel and hook motions, line tension, lowering speed and structure immersion. Acceptance tolerances and a representative validation case remain required before installation use. No field-calibrated accuracy is claimed.

8. Key Findings, Limitations and Recommendations

The calculated foundation-only limits are available for comparative screening under the inherited assumptions. Complete installation acceptance remains dependent on vessel motions, rigging behaviour and site conditions.

8.1 Limits and operating recommendations

For a red force-balance result, reduce incident wave height or change lowering speed and recalculate before proceeding with the lift assessment. A reduction in lowering speed reduces the quadratic hydrodynamic demand in the implemented equation; the speed change also changes exposure duration, which is not modelled. Force-balance basis.

Before an operating limit is issued, replace the illustrative hydrodynamic inputs with a reviewed structure basis, establish crane capacity at the working radius, resolve sling geometry and allowable loads, supply directional vessel responses and assess the complete lowering sequence. A screen limit that exceeds the qualified environmental or vessel-motion coverage is referred for engineering review and is not extrapolated. Envelope boundary.

The scalar force balance excludes vessel-hook motion, irregular-wave extremes, buoyancy variation during immersion, sling elasticity, line resonance, current-induced offset and landing interference. No time integration, mesh or time-step convergence is performed. A larger static reserve does not establish a minimum dynamic line tension.

8.2 Not covered

The rigid-jumper workflow screens lift-off, in-air bending, splash crossing, lowering and tie-in, then exports case and summary records. Its configured empirical inputs require separate qualification. The pipe-laying package provides scalar buoyancy, tension, route-radius, coating-strain, weld-repair and signed longitudinal-stress checks; combined pressure effects and full contact dynamics are outside those checks. Neither workflow is numerically assessed in this foundation example. Other module areas.

The wider installation module's generic weather and risk-reference features are not adopted as site forecasts or measured failure probabilities. A sample forecast is not substituted for missing live observations.
