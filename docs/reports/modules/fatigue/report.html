Fatigue module analysis

Draft for client review

1. Executive summary

The fatigue module is a stress-based fatigue assessment library. Analysis is performed to determine cumulative damage and exposure utilisation at a supplied stress evaluation point, and the key results are summarized below.

  • The stress-history workflow is available for an explicitly defined load block.
    • Counted stress ranges, cumulative damage and duration-scaled life are returned together.
    • The public counting example is reproduced; the life result remains conditional on the assumed stress scale, exposure and selected resistance curve.
  • The assumed exposure satisfies the calculation's utilisation limit.
    • The boundary is a dimensionless utilisation of unity, including the supplied fatigue factor.
    • No component acceptance or service-life forecast is established from this illustrative loading.

2. Introduction

Fatigue damage is assessed from local stress histories or pre-counted stress ranges. A single stress evaluation point under repeated loading is represented; water depth and global structural response are not represented.

The main workflow is a duration-aware load-block assessment. Ordered histories are counted without replacing ranges by histogram-bin centres. Additive load cases are accumulated in one block; alternative operating scenarios are assessed separately. Inputs, native outputs, existing numerical evidence and the engineering decisions that remain open are described below.

3. Design data

The calculation basis is supplied as local stress, material-detail selection and exposure duration. The geometry and boundary conditions that produce the stress are established outside the module.

3.1 System and stress boundary

A conceptual welded strip is shown to locate the evaluation point. Neither a finite-element model nor an applied-force-to-stress conversion is included.

3.2 Inputs and outputs

A history case contains a name and ordered stress samples in megapascals. A histogram case contains paired nonnegative stress ranges and counts. The complete set of cases is assigned a positive block duration, a positive design duration, a selected stress-life curve, an environment and a design fatigue factor. Nonfinite values, unequal histogram lengths and duplicate case names are rejected by the assessment input contract (native calculation basis).

Outputs comprise per-range allowable cycles and damage, case contributions, total block damage, unfactored life, factored utilisation, an acceptance flag and the largest case contribution. An unbounded life under zero damage is returned as a null value; it is not a measured service life. Native export support also provides structured data, a workbook and a report, with a portable document available only when a renderer is present. Export rendering is not exercised here.

3.3 Resistance and criteria

The demonstration selects class D in air from the native curve library. The library identifies DNV-RP-C203, Fatigue design of offshore steel structures, with a 2021 label; its parameter tests record comparison with the October 2011 edition, S-N curves in air. Equivalence to the later edition is not established by this report. No licensed parameter table is reproduced (curve evidence).

The calculation criterion is utilisation less than or equal to unity after the supplied fatigue factor is applied. This is the native assessment boundary, rather than a project-specific design approval criterion. The factor, curve classification and environment remain engineer-supplied inputs. The counting comparison uses the public output distribution, with exact count agreement and a relative range tolerance of one part in a trillion as a numerical comparison choice; it is not a material or design tolerance (numerical checks).

3.4 Public waveform and assumptions

The waveform and sample construction are taken from the Usage example of rainflow release 3.2.0. Its coordinate and signal are dimensionless. The sample span is 4.0 and the sample count is 201, including both endpoints.

q(x) = 0.2 + 0.5 sin(x) + 0.2 cos(10.0x) + 0.2 sin(4.0x)

The symbols x and q denote the published sample coordinate and signal ordinate respectively. Every coefficient in this expression is taken from that public example; no metocean or material loading is implied.

The complete record represents 1.0 year of loading, the requested exposure is 25.0 years, and the design fatigue factor is 1.0. These are demonstration inputs recorded in the data register, not values obtained from a physical installation.

4. Analysis methodology

Cycles are extracted by the native history counter and are evaluated by the native stress-life and cumulative-damage functions. The validated assessment interface is called for both the worked result and every exposure-chart point.

4.1 Counting and resistance

Closed loops are counted as full cycles and adjacent residual reversals as half cycles. The cycle range is the absolute difference between the two stress extrema; the cycle mean is their arithmetic mean. The half-cycle convention is the repository's retained-residual policy and is checked against the open published example. ASTM E1049-85, Standard Practices for Cycle Counting in Fatigue Analysis, Rainflow Counting, is named by the implementation; its licensed clause text is not checked here.

N(S) = a / Sm

S is the local stress range in megapascals, N is allowable cycles, m is the selected segment slope, and a is its intercept in cycles multiplied by megapascals raised to m. The native curve switches segments at its knee; its second segment is anchored continuously at the knee. The knee is not an infinite-life cutoff in this assessment. The catalogue endurance interface has a different cutoff convention and is not substituted for the native assessment curve (curve evidence).

4.2 Cumulative damage and exposure

D = Σ ni / N(Si)
L = Tb / D
U = D × (Td / Tb) × F

D is dimensionless block damage, n is the counted cycles in range i, L is unfactored life in years, Tb is block duration in years, Td is requested exposure in years, F is the supplied design fatigue factor, and U is dimensionless utilisation. The calculation passes when U does not exceed unity. These equations are the implementation's linear cumulative-damage and duration-scaling model (native calculation basis).

Sequence effects, damage interaction and evolving material resistance are omitted by the linear sum. Histogram-bin centres are avoided in this example because endurance is a nonlinear function of stress range. The lower-level convenience life function has no explicit record duration and interprets reciprocal damage as years; the explicit block-duration interface is used here to retain the exposure basis.

5. Detailed results

The public waveform is counted and its exact ranges are passed through the native assessment. Results are interpreted only on the assumed stress scale, curve and duration basis.

5.1 Public counting comparison

The returned full-cycle equivalent count is 7.0 cycles and the largest stress range is 143.4 MPa. Every range is within 0.1 % of the public reference, with the percentage bound rounded upward from the unrounded differences; the corresponding counts agree exactly. The open example qualifies counting for this one record; it supplies no independent material-life result. The complete range and damage breakdown is retained in the appendix table.

5.2 Native damage and utilisation

Block damage is 1.3e−6. Unfactored mathematical life is 7.7e+5 years. Requested-exposure utilisation is 3.3e−5, against a dimensionless limit of 1.0. The assumed exposure satisfies the calculation's utilisation limit. The reported life is an extrapolation of a sparse illustrative block and is not a service-life prediction.

The exposure at the factored utilisation boundary is 7.7e+5 years. The chart extends through that boundary to expose the limit and its exceedance, rather than to establish a permitted operating duration.

6. Key findings

The native stress-history assessment is demonstrated end to end against a public counting distribution. The design decision remains conditional on the supplied resistance and exposure inputs.

Exact counted ranges, per-range damage and explicit duration scaling are retained by the assessment interface. Input validation and numerical regression checks provide evidence about implementation behaviour. Physical validation of a welded component, a measured load spectrum or a design fatigue factor is not established by those checks.

The recorded library edition distinction and differing cutoff conventions are material to method selection. A catalogue endurance value and the assessment's continuous low-stress branch are not interchangeable without an explicit design basis.

7. Limitations

Engineering acceptance is limited by the absence of a qualified component, measured loading and a confirmed resistance basis. The public record demonstrates numerical counting rather than the fatigue strength of a real structure.

Mean stress is reported by counting but no mean-stress correction is applied by the selected assessment. Thickness, stress concentration, hot-spot extrapolation, multiaxial loading, environment adjustments beyond the selected curve, residual stress, overload interaction and corrosion evolution require separately justified treatment. A generic thickness-correction default is not a universal detail-category basis.

7.1 Not covered

The wider package includes curve catalogues and fitting, stress concentration, hot-spot stress, frequency-domain damage, weld classification, environmental corrections, multiaxial criteria, crack growth, legacy spectral calculations and report exporters. These sub-areas are not qualified by this worked example.

The repository's ancillary evidence record identifies unresolved frequency-unit consistency in spectral rates, an unsupported fallback for unknown weld details, and incomplete environment slope transfer. Those are recorded concerns, not newly measured error magnitudes in this report; the related methods are excluded from its recommendations (ancillary evidence). Crack-growth inspection intervals, global structural response, dynamic motion, stability and physical operating envelopes are NOT_EVALUATED.

8. Recommendations

The duration-aware assessment is recommended for numerical evaluation after the local stress and resistance basis is established. Component acceptance is withheld until the open engineering inputs are resolved.

Supply a qualified weld-detail classification, a selected standard edition and environment, the represented block duration, the required design exposure and the design fatigue factor before using the result for disposition. Confirm the need for thickness, hot-spot and mean-stress corrections independently and supply a stress basis consistent with the chosen curve (input contract; resistance evidence).

When utilisation exceeds unity, reduce the assessed stress exposure or revise the component detail and repeat the assessment against the same limit. No retrofit date or inspection trigger is specified without a qualified physical model and inspection basis. Resolve the ancillary evidence concerns and add independent reference cases before extending this report's scope to those methods (recorded method limits).

10. Acronyms

Notation and units are defined to support interpretation of the calculation. Unexplained internal labels are excluded from the engineering narrative.

MPa denotes megapascal. S-N denotes stress range versus allowable cycle count. The symbols D, L, U, F, n, N, S, a, m, Tb and Td are defined in the methodology. NOT_EVALUATED means that the stated engineering result is not established from the available inputs.
