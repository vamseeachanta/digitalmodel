    1. Executive Summary

    The spectral fatigue module is a frequency-domain screening tool for local structural details. Analysis is performed to determine the occurrence-weighted fatigue life of the supplied stress spectra, and the key results are summarized below.

    • The public example is reproduced as a screening calculation.
      • The splash-zone detail returns 25.5 years against the required 75.0 years. Its life margin is 0.3, below the workflow acceptance boundary.
      • The keel joint returns 443.4 years against the required 75.0 years. Its life margin is 5.9, above the workflow acceptance boundary.
    • Design acceptance is withheld pending numerical qualification.
      • The documented hertz input convention and the implemented crossing-rate convention differ. The implemented Dirlik bandwidth substitution also differs from the open equations.
      • The failed splash-zone screen requires a revised stress response and a repeat calculation after the rate convention and estimator coefficients are resolved.

    These values describe the current module, not the service life of an assessed structure. Basis: Public example, Implementation and Open theory. The governing life comparison is shown below; the detailed results retain both location summaries.

    2. Introduction

    Fatigue screening is performed from local stress power spectral density, without a time history or a rainflow count. The public workflow is evaluated from the supplied spectra through the reported life and governing location.

    The spectrum describes the distribution of stress variance over frequency. It does not describe riser geometry or calculate stress from waves. The example is openly documented in the repository and is used as a software demonstration; no measured structural response or independently published physical benchmark is supplied with it. Basis: Public example.

    3. Design Data

    The supplied stress spectra are treated as the complete input boundary. Design life, design fatigue factor, stress-life coefficients and sea-state occurrences are read directly from the public example.

    System and input basis

    A splash-zone detail and a keel joint are identified. Site, water depth, material, weld geometry, support restraint, mean stress and stress concentration treatment are not supplied. A single-sided, stationary Gaussian local stress spectrum is ASSUMED - to be confirmed; this is a method-applicability assumption, not a measured property. Basis: Public example and Open theory.

    Frequency is specified in hertz, stress spectral density in square megapascals per hertz, and time in years. The design life is 25.0 years and the supplied design fatigue factor is 3.0. These values are demonstration inputs. They are not project-specific acceptance criteria. Exact numeric inputs are retained in the Appendix input register and the embedded calculation data.

    Criteria and rationale

    The required life is the design life multiplied by the design fatigue factor. The life margin is calculated life divided by required life. The workflow accepts a location when this margin is at least unity. The factor provides the supplied life reserve; its engineering selection is a required input for an actual assessment. A passing screen does not establish acceptance while the numerical qualification gaps remain. Basis: Implementation.

    4. Analysis Methodology

    Moments are obtained by trapezoidal integration on the supplied frequency grid. A damage estimator is applied to each sea state, and annual damage is weighted by its occurrence fraction before location lives are compared.

    Spectral basis and equations

    Let f be frequency in hertz and S(f) be the single-sided stress spectral density. The stress variance is m₀ and its square root is the root mean square stress. The moment order is n. The irregularity factor is α₂ and the complementary bandwidth parameter is ε.

    mₙ = ∫ fⁿ S(f) df; σ = √m₀; α₂ = m₂ / √(m₀m₄); ε = √(1 − α₂²).

    For moments formed on a hertz grid, the mean upcrossing rate is ν₀ = √(m₂/m₀) and the peak rate is νₚ = √(m₄/m₂), in events per second. For moments formed with angular frequency, the rates include division by 2π. The two moment conventions are related by mₙ(angular) = (2π)ⁿmₙ(hertz). These expressions follow from the frequency definition and the moment equations in Open theory.

    The implemented library integrates powers of the supplied hertz values but divides both rates by 2π. For the public example, the difference in returned peak rate is −84.1 % relative to the rate obtained from the documented hertz convention. This is a convention discrepancy, not a validated correction to the reported fatigue lives. Basis: Implementation; the Appendix shows both rates.

    Damage and life

    Let Δσ denote stress range, N allowable cycles, m the supplied stress-life slope, and A the stress-life intercept. The single-slope relation is N = A/(Δσ)ᵐ. The intercept A is obtained by exponentiating the supplied decimal logarithm. The narrow-band range-based expression is d = ν₀ (2√(2m₀))ᵐ Γ(1 + m/2)/A, where d is damage per second and Γ is the gamma function. This is the range form of the amplitude expression in Open theory; amplitude is half the stress range.

    The default estimator represents the range distribution by an exponential and Rayleigh mixture. In the open formulation the first mixture weight is D₁ = 2(x − α₂²)/(1 + α₂²), with x = (m₁/m₀)√(m₂/m₄). The implementation uses ε in place of α₂ throughout the coefficient calculation, and clips several parameters. The difference is retained in this reproduction and requires an independent estimator benchmark before design use. The open equation and the code are identified in References.

    Let pᵢ be the occurrence fraction and aᵢ the full-time annual damage in sea state i. Annual location damage is a = Σpᵢaᵢ; calculated life is L = 1/a; required life is Lᵣ = YF; and life margin is M = L/Lᵣ. Here Y denotes design life and F denotes design fatigue factor. The implementation converts seconds to years using its fixed calendar convention; that conversion is reproduced directly rather than replaced. Basis: Implementation.

    No bilinear stress-life knee, environment correction or thickness correction is applied by this workflow. The supplied coefficients are used as a single slope at all stress ranges.

    6. Key Findings

    The main workflow is available and its calculation chain is exercised by repository tests. The evidence establishes software behavior within those tests, with defined limits on engineering interpretation.

    The estimator test file contains 15 test definitions covering moment properties, positive damage, stress sensitivity, method labels and selected inter-method relationships. The workflow test compares its narrow-band annual damage with a direct call to the same library. That is a wiring regression, not an independent physical validation. Basis: Test evidence.

    The public example supplies stress spectra but no independent measured-life result or qualified physical benchmark for the default estimator. A hand calculation from the open equations is retained in the report tests to distinguish reference damage from the reproduced implementation. It does not qualify structural life. Other fatigue test files exercise separate structural implementations; those results do not qualify this workflow automatically. No experimental accuracy band is established by the evidence reviewed here.

    The unqualified hand reference places both details below the required 75.0 years, including the keel joint that passes the reproduced workflow screen. Numerical correction and physical qualification remain outstanding. Basis: Test evidence and Open theory.

    7. Limitations

    The outputs are restricted to reproduction of the supplied scalar spectra. Engineering acceptance is not evaluated until the frequency convention, estimator coefficients and input suitability are resolved.

    Input guards are incomplete: frequency ordering, finite values, nonnegative spectra and occurrence fractions summing to unity are not checked. Positive occurrence fractions above unity are accepted. A zero-damage location is rejected by the workflow, so the current route does not return infinite life for a zero spectrum. Basis: Implementation.

    Numerical convergence of the coarse example frequency grid is not demonstrated. Gaussian stationarity, stress concentration, mean stress, structural restraint, corrosion environment, load simultaneity and the fatigue-factor selection remain unqualified. The single-slope result is not a complete standards assessment.

    Not covered

    The wave-to-stress response workflow, legacy closed-form workflow, alternate structural fatigue libraries, time-history rainflow counting, multiaxial criteria and crack-growth inspection planning are outside this report. Alternate spectral estimators are listed as available options; their individual empirical coefficients and accuracy are not evaluated here. Basis: Implementation and Open theory.

    8. Recommendations

    Numerical qualification is required before the returned life is used for design or an operating decision. The actions below follow the documented calculation differences and the method assumptions.

    • Resolve the frequency convention with a dimensionally consistent hertz and angular-frequency equivalence test. Compare crossing rates and annual damage with an independent narrow-band calculation. Basis: Open theory.
    • Compare the default mixture coefficients with the open formulation and add an independent narrow-band-limit and broad-band damage benchmark before accepting the estimator. Qualify the alternative estimators separately. Basis: Open theory.
    • For the failed splash-zone detail, reassess the local stress-transfer model and weld detail, then evaluate the revised stress spectra against the required life after numerical qualification. The keel-joint screen is retained as a numerical result only. Basis: the stress-life and damage equations in Open theory.
    • Require finite increasing frequencies, finite nonnegative spectra and a complete occurrence distribution before calculation. Add frequency-grid convergence and application-specific stress-life checks. Basis: the integration and probability assumptions in Open theory.
    • Supply the missing physical and material data in the Appendix register, select an applicable fatigue factor, and obtain independent engineering review before design release.

    10. Acronyms and Symbols

    The symbols and units used in the assessment are defined here. Stress is expressed in megapascals and frequency in hertz throughout the input and result tables.

    Power spectral density (PSD) is stress variance per unit frequency. Root mean square (RMS) stress is the square root of stress variance. The design fatigue factor (DFF), denoted F, multiplies the design life Y to obtain required life Lᵣ. The life margin M is calculated life L divided by Lᵣ.

    f is ordinary frequency; ω is angular frequency; S is a single-sided stress spectrum; mₙ is a spectral moment of order n; σ is RMS stress; α₂ is the irregularity factor; ε is its complementary bandwidth parameter; ν₀ and νₚ are upcrossing and peak rates. Δσ is stress range; N is allowable cycles; A and m are stress-life coefficients; Γ is the gamma function; x is the moment combination used in the mixture; D₁ is its first weight. d is damage per second; a is annual damage; aᵢ is full-time annual sea-state damage; pᵢ is sea-state occurrence fraction; i indexes the sea states. The symbol π is the circle constant. MPa denotes megapascal; Hz denotes hertz.
