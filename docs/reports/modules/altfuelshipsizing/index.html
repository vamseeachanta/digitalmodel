Alternative fuel ship sizing analysis

Liquid-hydrogen inventory, cylindrical tank volume and voyage boil-off

Draft for maintainer review

1. Executive Summary

Alternative fuel ship sizing is a concept-design calculation of fuel inventory and storage volume. Analysis is performed to determine the effect of supplied propulsion demand and voyage dwell on liquid-hydrogen storage; key results are summarized below.

  • A preliminary storage allowance can be selected for the documented synthetic example.
    • The sea-only inventory requires 1,856.3 m³ gross tank volume; the example with 240.0 h in port requires 1,903.9 m³ under the constant heat-leak model.
  • The consumed-vapor assumption can be screened against the sea fuel draw.
    • At the example heat flux, generation is 304.7 kg/day for the sea-only tank and 309.9 kg/day for the port-dwell tank. Sea fuel draw is 7,920.0 kg/day. The sea-only tank reaches that draw at 39.0 W/m² in Figure B; above that boundary, venting or reliquefaction is required and the reported inventory is optimistic.
  • A detailed design cannot be released from these calculations.
    • Tank pressure, structural adequacy, route powering accuracy and physical validation remain NOT_EVALUATED.

2. Introduction

The fuel-chain workflow is used to translate supplied shaft demand into fuel mass and gross storage volume. The electrical drivetrain and fuel-cell plant are represented by fixed efficiencies on a lower-heating-value basis.

Liquid hydrogen is represented by constant liquid density and latent heat. Hydrogen vapor generated by heat ingress is either credited to the plant at sea or treated as a loss. Port dwell is represented by zero fuel burn with continuous vapor generation. This report uses a public repository example with synthetic inputs; it is not a measured ship or an externally published operating trial. The calculation and example records are identified in the source register.

3. Summary and operating controls

Inventory allowances are determined by the burn reserve and by the periods in which vapor cannot be absorbed. The available calculation boundary is an average sea-leg vapor balance; an operating approval envelope is not established.

The port-dwell case carries 3,098.6 kg of additional boil-off allowance. Port hotel demand is not represented, even though hotel demand is included at sea. Shore power and vapor handling therefore require a separate balance.

For the consumed mode, compare heat-leak generation with consumption for each sea leg. A value above the boundary in Figure B requires a vapor-disposal or reliquefaction design and a revised inventory calculation. An empty warning list does not establish pressure control. The governing warning and port-loss semantics are documented in the calculation record.

4. Design data and acceptance basis

The documented public example is adopted as a synthetic calculation basis. Powering, efficiencies, reserve and insulation are marked ASSUMED - to be confirmed for application to an actual design.

The supplied sea shaft demand is 4,750.0 kW and the hotel electrical demand is 500.0 kW. The drivetrain and fuel-cell efficiencies are held constant. The complete numerical input and property registers are provided in the appendix; the source of each value is stated there.

Criteria and rationale

The sea consumed-vapor criterion is generation no greater than fuel draw, because surplus vapor has no sink in that model. Equality is the balance boundary; generation greater than draw sets the module warning. All port vapor is added as lost inventory. These are software accounting criteria, not class-rule acceptance criteria. Available arrangement volume, maximum pressure, minimum reserve endurance and relief capacity are required design inputs and remain NOT_EVALUATED.

The numerical stopping criterion is the change in required fuel mass between successive tank estimates. The API defaults and the exhaustion exception are recorded in the source register. That tolerance controls iteration closure only; it provides no measure of physical accuracy.

5. Methodology and theory

Energy conservation and cylindrical geometry are applied with the units defined below. The equations reproduce the documented library method rather than a resistance or pressure-vessel calculation.

Energy and inventory balance

t = s / v; Pₑ = Pₛ / ηₑ + Pₕ; P𝒇 = Pₑ / η𝒇; ṁ = cₕ P𝒇 / H

The route distance s is in nautical miles, speed v in knots and sea duration t in hours. Shaft demand Pₛ, hotel demand Pₕ, electrical demand Pₑ and fuel power P𝒇 are in kW. The dimensionless efficiencies ηₑ and η𝒇 represent the drivetrain and the lower-heating-value plant efficiency. H is lower heating value in MJ/kg and ṁ is fuel flow in kg/h. The unit conversion cₕ is the seconds-per-hour to kilojoules-per-megajoule ratio; it is defined in the unit implementation.

Mᵥ = (1 + r) Σ(ṁᵢ tᵢ); M = Mᵥ + Mₗ; Vₙ = M / ρ; V𝓰 = Vₙ / (1 − u)

The subscript ᵢ identifies each sea burn period. Reserve fraction r is applied to sea burn only. Voyage fuel including reserve Mᵥ, lost vapor Mₗ and required inventory M are in kg. Liquid density ρ is in kg/m³, ullage fraction u is dimensionless, and net liquid volume Vₙ and gross tank volume V𝓰 are in m³. Lost vapor is added without a further reserve multiplier, as implemented in the voyage balance.

Heat leak and vapor generation

λ = L / D; V𝓰 = πD²L / 4; A = πDL + πD² / 2; Q = qA; ṁᵥ = Q / hᵥ

Tank length L and diameter D are in m; slenderness λ is dimensionless. Surface area A is in m². Uniform heat flux q is in W/m², heat ingress Q is in W, latent heat hᵥ is expressed in J/kg for this equation, and vapor generation ṁᵥ is in kg/s. The reported vapor rate is converted to kg/day; its percentage rate is referenced to the full-load liquid mass. The alternative heat-flux input is q = U(Tₐ − Tₛ), with thermal transmittance U in W/(m² K) and ambient and storage temperatures Tₐ and Tₛ in K.

The heat balance uses constant saturation properties, uniform heat flux and the entire cylinder area throughout the voyage. The public monograph defines latent heat as the saturated vapor–liquid enthalpy difference; the repository property approximations are retained, with their external qualification limits stated in the property source register.

Inventory is iterated when lost vapor increases tank size and hence surface area. In consumed mode, sea vapor is credited without adding inventory; surplus generation is flagged rather than removed from the credit. Port vapor is always added as a loss. Leg sequence does not change heat leak, because no depletion-dependent liquid level or pressure state is solved.

8. Key findings, limitations and recommendations

The principal inventory effect is the loss of vapor during periods without fuel consumption. The reported volumes are conditional on the supplied powering and on the constant heat-leak approximation.

A separate vapor-handling balance is required whenever a sea leg exceeds the Figure B boundary or any port dwell is present. Venting or reliquefaction, pressure protection and port energy supply are to be resolved before the consumed-mode assumption is used for design. This action follows the module warning and zero-port-burn treatment identified in the calculation record.

Replace the synthetic shaft-demand, efficiency and insulation inputs with qualified route and supplier data before selecting storage capacity. Obtain arrangement space, tank closure geometry, supports and relief design before treating the calculated cylinder as an installable tank. Comparison with a measured heat-leak test and a route energy balance is required to establish physical accuracy for the intended storage condition.

Not covered

Wind-assist thrust integration, fuel-price comparisons, alternative-fuel property selection and tonnage are available sub-areas but are not engineering assessments in this report. Supplied wind polars and a route wind distribution are required for a wind assessment. The speed sweep uses an aggregate sea passage and excludes dwell; it is not a schedule calculation. Explicit leg shaft powers bypass the default wind reduction, so the enriched summary is not a substitute for checking each leg. These behaviors are identified in the workflow source.

Refrigerated ammonia and ambient methanol require separate property and plant bases. The methanol preset does not establish an ambient boil-off model. Resistance, sea-state limits, heading limits, crane rigging, structural stress and stability are not represented; installation envelopes and forecast controls are therefore not assigned. General numerical finiteness is not comprehensively checked by the module, so nonfinite inputs require rejection before use.

Quality pre-flight evidence

The reconstruction check compares both delivered files byte for byte with a fresh build. Each displayed example number carries a source key; the API consistency test calls the same public functions as the builder and compares every key and displayed value, including all numeric result fields and leg records. Separate hand-worked literal assertions check the sea flow, reserve inventory, port-dwell inventory, gross volume, lost vapor, inventory increase and heat-flux boundary without using the sizing functions as the expected-value oracle. The export check also compares the configuration workflow with the voyage calculation in a temporary output directory.

The chart check compares every plotted generation value with the module heat-leak function and checks that the absorption boundary is crossed. Document checks cover section count, reference targets, inlined house styles, public wording, precision and local comment controls. These checks establish reconstruction consistency; physical validation and browser interaction testing remain NOT_EVALUATED.
