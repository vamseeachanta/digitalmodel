1. Executive summary

The bottom hole assembly database is an illustrative catalogue and assembly checker for drilling components. Analysis is performed to determine assembly dimensions, static weights and the scope of the available checks; key results are summarized below.

  • The documented synthetic building assembly is available for calculation teaching and interface trials.
    • The computed length is 22.5552 m and the maximum outside diameter is 0.3048 m, equal to the assumed hole-size limit. Nominal equality does not establish running clearance. [source] [source]
  • The weight-on-bit advisory is within its assumed limit for this example.
    • The planned load is 444.8 N and the available load is 11277.2 N after the assumed margin. This is not an equipment rating or a drilling acceptance result. [source] [source]
  • Equipment selection remains outside the evidence supplied.
    • The separate nominal-table comparison is NOT_EVALUATED. Published-source comparison records and qualified operating criteria are required before design use. [source]

2. Introduction

A drilling bottom hole assembly is represented as an ordered sequence from the bit upward. The database is assessed as a data-handling and elementary mechanics module, with the documented public example used as the numerical basis.

The catalogue contains 3 synthetic assemblies, 9 components and 3 source records. The rotary building workflow is covered in full; the pendulum and motor examples are identified under “Not covered”. None of the quantities establishes a manufactured component property. [source]

Inputs are stored with units and provenance. Outputs preserve the calculation formula and resolving source keys, allowing an engineer to distinguish a supplied quantity from a computed quantity. A digest checks file consistency; it does not authenticate the source. [source]

3. Main workflow and physical representation

The building assembly is loaded, checked and summarized through the documented interface. The physical sequence is shown with nominal cylindrical geometry and an explicit bit datum.

The bit is followed by a near-bit stabilizer, a measurement while drilling tool, a drill collar and heavy-weight drill pipe. The final pipe marks the end of this modelled assembly. The names “building” and “pendulum” classify the examples; directional drilling response is not calculated. Density units below use the United States (US) liquid gallon. [source]

Source-checkout use is illustrated in the reference appendix. An installed caller supplies an explicit catalogue directory containing the expected data files. Loading rejects invalid geometry, unknown component references and incompatible internal connections before the summary is obtained. [source]

The input set contains source records, component records and ordered assembly records. Each physical quantity requires a value, unit, source identity, basis and the original representation and date; a stored computed quantity also requires a formula. Joint counts and positions are integers. A per-joint length override is optional. The loader requires the fixed catalogue and manifest files and rejects unexpected files, aliases, duplicate keys and symlinks. [source]

The summary returns total length, maximum outside diameter, air weight, buoyed weight when density is available, an advisory state and warnings. Sections return length, diameters and distance from the bit, with the first blade centre where supplied. Calculated quantity records contain their unit, formula, computed basis and source references. Invalid input raises a database error; a successful call still requires inspection of the advisory state. [source]

4. Design basis and criteria

All physical inputs in this example are treated as ASSUMED - to be confirmed. The catalogue assumptions are used unchanged so that the published teaching example is calculated without assigning an equipment rating.

The hole diameter is 12 in; the fluid and material densities are 10.0 and 65.5 lb/US gal. The advisory margin is 15.0 %, selected in the catalogue rather than prescribed by a standard. The planned weight on bit is 100 lbf. The appendix records the complete component inputs and their basis. [source]

The nominal bit diameter is required to match the hole diameter, and the body, maximum and blade diameters are limited by the hole diameter. These are consistency criteria with the rationale that the represented assembly fits the nominal modelled hole. They are not clearance criteria. A relative numerical tolerance is used by the implementation for equality; no physical tolerance is assigned. [source]

Collars precede heavy-weight pipe and drill pipe in bit-up order, while intervening tools are permitted. Internal interfaces require matching labels and opposite pin/box genders. External end interfaces and actual thread ratings are not qualified. The weight-on-bit check is advisory because contact, inclination and neutral-point position are absent. [source]

5. Methodology and equations

Section lengths and distances are accumulated from the selected joint lengths and counts. Static buoyancy is applied through the uniform-material approximation, and the assumed margin is applied only to weight below the first heavy-weight pipe.

The geometric definitions use section length ℓi, joint count ni, selected per-joint length Li, total length L and bit-up coordinate zi. Indices i and j identify sections counted upward from the bit. Counts are dimensionless and lengths are in metres. Scalar lengths with common units are added by ordinary algebra. These expressions define the aggregation performed by the module, rather than an empirical drilling relation. [source] [source]

ℓi = niLi; L = Σ ℓi; zi = Σj<i ℓj.

The first blade centre is located by adding the section coordinate, the supplied lower blade offset and half the supplied blade length. A repeated section reports the blade on its first joint only. These are coordinate definitions, with common-unit scalar addition as the basis. [source] [source]

With weight per length wi in newtons per metre, the air weight Wa is the sum of the section weights. The buoyant force B equals the displaced-fluid weight. The uniform-material reduction follows by dividing B = ρfgV by Wa = ρsgV, where ρf is fluid density, ρs is material density, g is gravitational acceleration and V is material displacement volume. The density ratio is dimensionless; the same units are required for both densities. [source] [source]

Wa = Σ ℓiwi; Wb = Wa − B = Wa(1 − ρf/ρs).

The buoyed weight Wb is in newtons. This reduction represents a uniform material with hydrostatic fluid access consistent with material displacement. Sealed cavities, differing internal fluid density and mixed component displacement require a separate pressure and displaced-volume model. No pressure field is solved here. [source] [source]

The available advisory load Wlim is defined from the below-pipe air weight Wbelow and the dimensionless assumed margin m. The planned load Wp is compared with this value. The expression is a catalogue policy, not a sourced design-code safety factor; the physical buoyancy term has the basis given above. [source] [source]

Wlim = Wbelow(1 − ρf/ρs)(1 − m); advisory within assumed limit when Wp ≤ Wlim.

Heavy-weight pipe and all sections above it are excluded from Wbelow. When no heavy-weight pipe exists, the whole modelled assembly is included. Missing density, planned load or margin produces NOT_EVALUATED. Total buoyed weight still includes the entire assembly when density is supplied. [source]

Unit conversion uses the module unit registry. The factors are 0.3048 m/ft, 0.0254 m/in and 4.448222 N/lbf at the displayed precision; the public units reference provides the conversion basis. No separate gravity value is selected by this report. [source]

6. Worked example

The documented synthetic building assembly is calculated by calling the module. Inputs, returned values and plotted diameters are generated from those calls, with the component detail placed in the appendix.

The returned length is 22.5552 m. Air weight is 26333.5 N and buoyed weight is 22313.1 N. The near-bit blade centre is 0.6096 m from the bit datum. [source] [source]

The planned load of 444.8 N is below the returned advisory limit of 11277.2 N after the assumed margin of 15.0 %. The returned state means that the assumed static comparison is met; buckling, contact, fatigue and equipment strength remain NOT_EVALUATED. [source] [source]

7. Validation evidence

The repository evidence is separated into calculation checks, input-rejection checks and source-comparison metadata. No field measurement or equipment-performance benchmark is supplied in the module evidence set.

The existing suite contains 36 test functions before parameter expansion. It covers synthetic loading, repeated-joint and override calculations, blade positions, unit round trips, geometry and connection rejection, provenance, bounded reads and restricted-record refusal. The function inventory is listed in the appendix; the count describes source inspection rather than an execution result. [source]

The nominal-table tests pin the supplied transcription and exercise comparison metadata. The audit reports 138 cells: 0 with comparison records, 1 pending and 137 without a comparison status. The overall source-comparison status is NOT_EVALUATED. Counts describe repository metadata, not the contents of an authenticated standard. No nominal-table dimension or coefficient is reproduced here. [source]

The report tests add literal hand-calculation anchors for geometry and static weights, compare displayed example values against fresh module calls and rebuild the page. These checks establish consistency of this example and its presentation. They do not establish physical calibration or qualification for drilling. [source]

8. Key findings, limits and recommendations

Use is limited to illustrative catalogue assembly and elementary static calculations under the stated assumptions. The required next evidence is identified separately for data, source comparison and engineering acceptance.

Recommendations

For catalogue evaluation, retain the supplied assumptions and inspect both the returned state and its warnings. If the planned load exceeds the returned advisory value, reduce the illustrative planned load or revise the modelled below-pipe weight and recalculate; neither action establishes an operating limit. Missing inputs are supplied before interpreting the advisory. This response follows the documented advisory definition. [source]

For engineering selection, replace synthetic geometry, weights and densities with reviewed equipment data and establish a displacement model for the actual fluid boundaries. The uniform-material buoyancy reduction is restricted to its stated physical assumptions. A qualified load, clearance, strength and operating basis is required before acceptance is determined. [source] [source]

For the nominal reference table, obtain authorized source access and attach a comparison locator to every published field before any correction or equipment use is assessed. The pending source discrepancy is left unchanged. Comparison status alone does not authenticate the source evidence. [source]

Not covered

The pendulum and motor examples are not worked separately in this report. Motor performance, directional response, torque and drag, buckling, vibration, hydraulics, fatigue, connection ratings, non-magnetic measurement-tool spacing and bending strength ratio criteria are not calculated. Field-name correspondence to drilling data standards is present; an interchange writer is absent. Required criteria remain NOT_EVALUATED. [source] [source]
