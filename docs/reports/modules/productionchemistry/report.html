Executive summary

Production chemistry is a mineral-scale screening module for produced water. Analysis is performed to determine the change in mineral saturation along a specified production-string temperature and pressure path. The key results are summarized below.

  • The public synthetic example can be reproduced through the documented interface.
    • Calcite saturation index changes from −0.6 at the bottomhole to 0.3 at the wellhead, against the saturation boundary of 0.0 [1, 2, 4].
  • The screening results can guide the selection of conditions for further chemical assessment.
    • Quantitative design suitability is not established because the supplied coefficients are not calibrated against an independent physical dataset [2].

Introduction

Mineral-scale tendency is assessed from water composition and local thermal and pressure conditions. The calculation is used to identify supersaturation along the specified path; deposited mass and treatment dosage are not calculated [2].

The main workflow comprises composition entry, a single-state calculation and an ordered production-string profile. Calcite, gypsum, hemihydrate, anhydrite, barite, celestite and halite are returned. The public example is a synthetic published repository example, not a measured operating case or an external experimental benchmark [1–3].

Not covered

Ideal volumetric water mixing and compatibility sweeps are available and are addressed by existing tests [3]. Their calculated results are outside this report. Mixing is not followed by carbonate re-equilibration or precipitation. In particular, when gas carbon dioxide is defined for only one endmember, the gas route is dropped at interior mixture fractions; endpoint and interior calcite indices can then follow different carbonate routes [2]. Inhibitor selection, corrosion, organic deposits, reactive transport and fluid hydraulics are outside the implemented screening method.

Design data

The water composition and path are taken directly from the public example [1]. The composition is held fixed while the stated pressure and temperature are applied; depletion of dissolved ions is not calculated [2].

The bottomhole is specified at 250.0 °F and 9000.0 psia. The wellhead is specified at 120.0 °F and 250.0 psia, with linear intermediate states [1]. The gas carbon dioxide mole fraction is 0.020 (dimensionless). The supplied pH is 6.6, but the positive gas fraction selects the gas route throughout this path [2].

Ion concentrations are entered in milligrams per litre. Temperature is entered in degrees Fahrenheit and pressure in pounds per square inch absolute (psia). The full input register is given in Appendix 10.1. pH is the negative decimal logarithm of hydrogen-ion activity; the mixing approximation instead uses hydrogen-ion concentration [2]. The synthetic composition is marked ASSUMED - to be confirmed for transfer to any actual service.

Analysis methodology

Saturation is evaluated from a conditional solubility correlation. Dissolved concentrations are used in place of explicit activities, with activity effects assigned to the conditional constants [2]. The physical definition uses activities; this distinction limits direct comparison with full speciation models [4, 5].

Calculation basis and criteria

SI = log10(Q / Ksp)

SI denotes saturation index, Q the ion activity product and Ksp the solubility product for the stated mineral reaction. The ratio is dimensionless. The boundary SI = 0 corresponds to equality of the product and solubility constant. A positive index indicates supersaturation; a negative index indicates undersaturation. This is a thermodynamic boundary, not an operating alarm limit [2, 4].

ci = Ci / (1000 Mi); I = ½ Σ ci zi²

Ci is an entered ion concentration in mg/L, Mi is its molecular mass in g/mol, ci is molar concentration in mol/L, zi is signed ionic charge; ionic strength is denoted by I (mol/L). The unit conversion and one-half weighting are implemented directly [2]. The molecular masses and charges actually used are listed in Appendix 10.1.

F(T,P,I) = a₀ + aTT + aT²T² + aT³T³ + aPP + a√I√I + aII

F is a conditional logarithmic constant; T is temperature in °F and P is pressure in psia. Each a denotes the coefficient of its associated term, with reciprocal units required to make F dimensionless. Distinct coefficient sets are used for each mineral and carbonate route. Their values are read from the implementation in Appendix 10.1; no coefficient is represented as a value transcribed from a publication [2].

SIcarbonate,gas = log10(cCa cHCO₃² / fCO₂) + Fgas; fCO₂ = yCO₂P

cCa and cHCO₃ are calcium and bicarbonate concentrations in mol/L. fCO₂ is carbon dioxide fugacity approximated by partial pressure in psia; yCO₂ is its gas mole fraction. The unit-specific scaling is absorbed into Fgas; pressure units cannot be substituted without recalibration [2].

pHestimated = log10(cHCO₃ / fCO₂) + FpH-from-gas

The estimated in-situ pH uses a separate conditional constant FpH-from-gas. It is a model output, not a measurement at the stated conditions [2].

SIcarbonate,pH = log10(cCa cHCO₃) + pH − FpH;
SIsalt = log10(cM cX) + Fsalt

The measured-pH route is used only when a positive gas fraction is unavailable. For the salt expression, M denotes the mineral cation and X the anion: calcium, barium or strontium with sulfate, or sodium with chloride. Water activity and hydration effects are absorbed into the constants [2].

Inputs and outputs

A BrineComposition instance accepts the listed ions, optional total dissolved solids, measured pH and gas carbon dioxide mole fraction. A carbonate route is required even when only sulfate results are of interest. Negative concentrations, non-positive pressure and out-of-range gas or mixing fractions are rejected; finite values, charge balance and coefficient applicability require separate checks [2, 3].

The saturation_indices single-state output gives the index, logarithmic concentration product, conditional constant, method basis and route details for every mineral. The si_profile output gives an ordered table of temperature, pressure, each mineral index and its change from the first state. A missing participating ion produces a non-finite index, which means not evaluated rather than a zero tendency [2].

Detailed results

The endpoint results are calculated with the supplied default coefficients and the public synthetic water. Every result in Table A is obtained by calling the module; a field acceptance decision is not established [1, 2].

The computed ionic strength is 1.593 mol/L. Calcite saturation index changes from −0.6 to 0.3, against the saturation boundary of 0.0. The wellhead change relative to the bottomhole is 0.9 index units. These are screening results from uncalibrated coefficients [2].

At the wellhead, the carbon dioxide partial pressure used by the module is 5.0 psia. The logarithmic concentration term is −5.2, and the conditional term is 5.5. Their unrounded sum gives the calcite index 0.3. The estimated in-situ pH is 6.6; it is derived from the same uncalibrated model, not an independent measurement [2].

Key findings

The calcite index at the synthetic wellhead is 0.3, above the saturation boundary of 0.0. The conditional model therefore indicates supersaturation at that state; deposited quantity, onset time and treatment demand are not established [2, 4].

The existing suite contains 18 automated checks [3]. Arithmetic checks cover ionic strength, coefficient overrides, profile order, changes from the first state and mixing endpoint identity. Qualitative checks cover temperature trends, depressurization, calcium sulfate phase ordering, halite tendency and an interior barite mixing peak. Other checks address input rejection and result contracts. These checks support calculation consistency and expected trends; they provide no measured error bound for the default coefficients [2, 3].

The published explorer provides a reproducible example [1]. An independent experimental benchmark dataset, calibration residuals and a demonstrated domain of quantitative accuracy are not supplied by the inspected chemistry module, its tests or its example. Quantitative design suitability remains not established.

Limitations

Use is restricted by coefficient provenance and the simplified chemical representation. The default coefficient sets are described by the implementation as configurable approximations rather than verified published correlation values; calibration is required before quantitative use [2].

Molar concentration is treated as interchangeable with molality. Density, ion pairing, explicit activity coefficients, charge-balance correction and water activity are not resolved. The high-salinity public example therefore carries an unquantified chemical-model error. The supplied total dissolved solids does not replace the individual ions in the ionic-strength calculation [2].

The gas mole fraction is fixed, fugacity is approximated by partial pressure, and composition is unchanged along the path. Gas liberation, nucleation, precipitation, redissolution, residence time and deposition kinetics are not solved. Neither an inhibitor dose nor an operating alarm limit is available from this result [2].

Temperature, pressure and salinity applicability envelopes are NOT_EVALUATED: no calibrated bounds are supplied. Uncertainty, physical dimensions, hydraulic conditions and observed deposit records are also NOT_EVALUATED. Those gaps are recorded with their consequences in Appendix 10.4.

Recommendations

Further assessment is directed at the evidence gaps that prevent quantitative design use. The following actions address the supersaturation screen and the limitations of its chemical basis [2, 5].

For calcite and every other mineral with a positive wellhead result in Table A, obtain representative water and gas analyses at the relevant conditions and assess the thermal and pressure path with a qualified speciation basis. Use those results to define laboratory conditions for scale and inhibitor assessment before setting a treatment requirement. The current positive index alone does not size a treatment [2, 5].

Replace the default coefficients with a documented calibration dataset and a stated temperature, pressure and composition envelope. Retain independent reference cases outside the fit and record errors against an agreed quantitative criterion. The implementation permits coefficient replacement, but that capability does not establish calibration or accuracy [2, 3].

Define operating triggers from qualified chemistry and observed deposit or performance evidence. No numerical pressure restriction, sampling interval or inhibitor dosage is specified here because its required basis is missing. For concentrated brines, select an activity model and thermodynamic dataset suited to the intended composition, with their own limitations assessed [2, 5].

10.3 Calculation evidence and quality pre-flight

The existing suite comprises 18 checks [3]. The arithmetic and behavioural evidence is described in Section 6. The rebuild check compares the complete document with its generated form, compares all state and profile values with direct module calls, and reads back every annotated printed number against its computed origin. This establishes numerical reproduction; an independent physical validation is NOT_EVALUATED.

Quality pre-flight covers section and reference integrity, input units, the saturation boundary, source records, reproducibility and the distinction between synthetic inputs and measured data. The chart uses the same embedded profile as Table F. Table precision is selected for readable screening results; unrounded arrays are retained for numerical readback. The saturation criterion is not an acceptance tolerance for the chemical model.
