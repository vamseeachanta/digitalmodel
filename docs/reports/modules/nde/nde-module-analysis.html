1. Executive Summary

The nondestructive evaluation module is a set of acoustic data utilities and a sampled pulse-timing function. Analysis is performed to determine which timing results are supported by the available evidence, and the key results are summarized below.

  • Use the timing function to identify the unique largest absolute pressure sample in an isolated pulse.
    • The synthetic example returns 3.0 s for either polarity, matching the constant-speed target of 3.0 s with a 0.0 % difference relative to that target. This example establishes the sampled peak convention only. Worked example
  • Supply an inspection basis before interpreting the time as a material or defect measurement.
    • Measured accuracy and inspection acceptance remain NOT_EVALUATED because calibration records, material properties and an acceptance criterion are absent from the example. Findings and recommendations

2. Introduction

The acoustic timing capability and its data-loading interface are described for an inspection engineer selecting tools for a calculation. The available analytical evidence is examined before any interpretation of the result as a physical measurement.

Nondestructive evaluation, abbreviated NDE, examines a component without intentional damage. The present calculation accepts a receiver trace; it does not simulate a transducer or classify a defect. The acoustic data loader is described separately because its tests use substitute datasets. [A] [C] [D]

3. Summary of Results

The sampled receiver pressure is displayed against time, and its peak is compared with a constant-speed analytical target. Positive and negative pulses are evaluated using the same timestamps and the same magnitude convention.

The returned time is 3.0 s for either polarity. The target is 3.0 s, and the difference is 0.0 % of the target. This agreement is specific to a pulse whose peak is sampled exactly. [B]

4. Data and Basis

The public synthetic example is used as the calculation basis. Its parameters are numerical test inputs, and no measured material or inspection acceptance data are assigned.

4.1 Inputs and outputs

The timing function is supplied with finite timestamps in seconds and signed, baseline-corrected pressure samples in consistent units. The sequences are required to be nonempty and equal in length; timestamps are required to increase strictly. The pressure requires a nonzero, unique absolute maximum. A timestamp in seconds is returned. Invalid or ambiguous input raises a value error. [A]

The loader accepts a dataset variant and a requested sample count. Its returned mapping contains the collected frames, variant, dataset name, actual count and attribution. The frames are passed through without a receiver-coordinate or pressure-unit conversion. The optional dataset package is required for access to real data. The requested count is not validated; reject non-positive requests before calling the loader. [C]

4.2 Analytical basis and criteria

For an isolated, nondispersive pulse emitted with its peak at the time origin, the ideal peak time is the one-way travel distance divided by constant speed. This relation is obtained by rearranging the velocity definition in College Physics, second edition, Time, Velocity, and Speed. [G]

treference = L / c

The symbol L denotes the one-way path length in metres; c denotes the assumed constant speed in metres per second; treference denotes the analytical peak time in seconds. The example supplies L = 6.0 m and c = 2.0 m/s. The speed is a numerical fixture value, not a sound speed assigned to any material. [B]

The target benchmark is agreement with the analytical peak when that peak lies on the sample grid. The rationale is to check time selection independently of material properties. This is an algorithm check, not an engineering acceptance criterion. The inspection limit remains NOT_EVALUATED. [A] [B]

5. Methodology

The timestamp of the unique largest absolute sample is selected by the timing function. The straight propagation path is used only to provide an independent analytical comparison.

k = arg maxi |pi|; tpeak = tk

The index i identifies a sample; pi is its signed pressure; k is the unique index with the largest magnitude; ti is its timestamp in seconds; tpeak is the returned timestamp. Taking the absolute value makes the result independent of polarity. This is a sampled peak convention, not a first-arrival detector. [A]

The schematic identifies the source, the ideal one-way path and the receiver record.

The example's Gaussian is evaluated at the recorded sample times. In dimensionally explicit form, its exponent is the negative square of (L − ct) divided by a length scale, with relative amplitude and length scale set by the synthetic fixture. These choices describe the test waveform only. [B]

The difference is calculated as the returned time minus the reference time, divided by the reference time and expressed as a percentage. A different emission reference requires subtraction of that reference from the returned timestamp. [A] [G]

6. Worked Example

The public synthetic pulse example is evaluated by calling the timing function for both polarities. Every input and result below is inserted from that calculation.

The one-way distance is 6.0 m and the numerical speed is 2.0 m/s. The record contains 6 samples from 0.0 s to 5.0 s. The Gaussian exponent has power 2; these are fixture inputs rather than engineering coefficients. [B]

The positive pulse returns 3.0 s against a reference of 3.0 s. Its difference is 0.0 % of the analytical reference. The negative pulse returns 3.0 s against 3.0 s, with a difference of 0.0 %. The result establishes peak selection for the documented sample grid only. [A] [B]

7. Validation

The available validation evidence is separated into analytical timing checks and interface checks with substitute datasets. Inspection performance is not established by either evidence category.

The timing checks cover both polarities, an irregular timestamp record with a shifted emission reference, and rejection of empty, mismatched, unordered, nonfinite, zero-signal and tied-peak inputs. A single sample with a negative time origin is also checked. The analytical target is computed from the fixture distance, speed and emission reference. [B]

The loader tests check construction, variant selection, attribution, returned mapping and optional dependency behaviour using substitute data objects. These checks establish the tested interface behaviour, not compatibility with a real release of the external dataset package. [D] [E]

The published loader example demonstrates the intended access pattern but contains a defect-detection placeholder. No measured receiver benchmark or accepted defect result is supplied by that example. A real-data validation requires a versioned receiver trace and a calibration reference with stated uncertainty. [F]

8. Findings and Recommendations

The demonstrated calculation and the evidence gaps governing inspection use are identified below. The recommendations are tied to the documented function contract and available tests.

  • The synthetic peak is selected at the analytical target for either polarity.
    • Retain the analytical example as a regression check when changing the timing calculation. [B]
  • Timing accuracy between samples is not established; interpolation is absent.
    • Provide a calibrated timing reference and a sampling-resolution assessment before assigning a measurement uncertainty. [A]
  • Noise, multiple echoes, baseline offset and dispersion are not resolved by the timing function.
    • Supply an isolated, baseline-corrected pulse and a known emission reference. Reassess the record when a maximum is tied or a pulse cannot be isolated. [A]
  • Measured accuracy and inspection acceptance remain NOT_EVALUATED because calibration records, material properties and an acceptance criterion are absent from the example.
    • Obtain those inputs and the governing inspection procedure before accepting a material or defect result. [A] [F]
  • Real dataset compatibility is not established by the substitute-data tests.
    • Check the installed dataset version, constructor arguments, frame schema, receiver selection, units and timestamps against an actual sample before coupling the loader to timing. [C] [D] [E]

9.3 Quality assurance pre-flight evidence

The rebuild checks compare the saved report with its reconstruction and compare the calculation with a direct call of the timing function.

Every displayed worked-example value is checked against the computed inputs or results. The chart samples are checked against the public fixture, and the signed percentage difference is calculated from unrounded times. The printed pressure values retain nonzero small samples. Internal section links, source paths and standalone delivery are checked by the rebuild tests.

The analytical peak falls exactly on a sample in this example. Sampling sensitivity away from that grid point, measured noise and calibration uncertainty are not evaluated. No finite-element mesh, structural load or buoyancy calculation is involved.

10. Not Covered

The excluded capabilities are listed to bound the timing result and prevent its use as an inspection acceptance decision.

Real-data execution of the basic, maze and inclusions loader variants is not performed. Scattering field solution, receiver extraction, material characterization, defect detection, echo discrimination, wall-thickness calculation and probability of detection are not evaluated. The calculation supports selection of a sampled peak from a supplied trace only. [A] [C] [F]
