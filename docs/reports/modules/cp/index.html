1 Executive summary

The cathodic protection design module is a calculation library for sacrificial-anode demand, mass and current output. Analysis is performed to determine the aggregate anode requirement for a published jacket example; key results are summarized below.

  • The aggregate requirement is read as 547 anodes on the declared assumption basis.
    • The published 569-anode arrangement passes the aggregate mass, initial-current and final-current checks. Mass governs the required count; no aggregate increase is required.
    • Field protection remains NOT_EVALUATED by this calculation. A distributed potential survey and local allocation assessment are required before a retrofit is specified.
  • The scope of evidence is identified before use.
    • Published phase demands and fresh individual output are reproduced to their printed precision; the assumed depletion model differs from the paper.
    • Design life, net alloy interpretation and material assumptions require confirmation. The calculation is not an installed-design acceptance.

2 Scope and capability

The main offshore sacrificial-anode workflow is described from zone definition through a governing anode count. The calculation is bounded to an electrically connected structure with declared current demands and anode geometry.

Seawater and buried-steel areas are grouped by exposure, coating and depth. Initial current is used to establish polarisation capacity; mean current is used to size consumable alloy; final current is checked against the depleted anode geometry. A sufficient total mass alone does not establish sufficient initial or final current.

The engineering inputs are areas, exposure definitions, coating condition or effective phase densities, connected loads, design life, electrolyte resistivity, alloy capacity and utilisation, anode dimensions, net alloy mass and provided count. The outputs are phase demands, required mass, fresh and depleted resistance and output, separate governing counts, a recommended total and aggregate pass or fail checks. The library returns calculation data; report presentation is supplied separately.

3 Design basis and acceptance criteria

The published example is evaluated on its own area and effective current-density basis. The original standard edition is not identified in the paper; the module is explicitly evaluated with the May 2021 edition selector.

The published effective densities already represent each surface category. A unity coating multiplier is therefore applied; a second coating deterioration factor is excluded to avoid counting coating effects twice. The connected-well allowance is applied at each phase. This phase interpretation is an assumption, recorded in the appendix.

For an aggregate pass, provided alloy mass is at least the mean-demand mass requirement, fresh total output is at least initial demand, and depleted total output is at least final demand. Each criterion is necessary because charge capacity and instantaneous output constrain different failure modes. The corresponding standard references are DNV-RP-B401, Cathodic protection design, May 2021: the anode material design values, anode resistance and utilisation tables and the design protective-potential clause. Numeric standard tables are not reproduced.

The active capacity and utilisation are checked against the existing example's declared assumptions before this report is written. Their applicability is not established by that equality check. Source editions, equation locators and assumption provenance are listed in the Source register.

4 Theory and calculation sequence

Current demand and consumed charge are evaluated before the current-output constraint is applied. The equations below describe the implemented main workflow; edition-specific applicability remains subject to engineering review.

Iₚ = Σ(A𝓏 i𝓏,ₚ f𝓏,ₚ) + Iconnected
M = Imean t H / (ε u)
f(t) = min(a + bt, unity)
fmean = min(a + bt/2, unity)
c = w₁ + w₂ + 2√(h² + ((w₂ − w₁)/2)²); r = c / (2π)
rfinal = r √(unity − u)
R = ρwater [ln(4L/r) − 1] / (2πL), for L ≥ 4r
Ianode = ΔE / R
N = max(ceil(M/m), ceil(Iinitial/Ianode,initial), ceil(Ifinal/Ianode,final))

Phase p is initial, mean or final, and z denotes a surface zone. Area A is in square metres; density i is in amperes per square metre. It is a bare-steel design density, or an effective density when f is unity; f is the dimensionless coating multiplier. Current I is in amperes. Required net alloy mass M and individual mass m are in kilograms; life t is in years; H is the hours-per-year conversion used by the library; ε is electrochemical capacity in ampere hours per kilogram; u is the usable alloy fraction.

Length L and equivalent radius r are in metres; electrolyte resistivity ρwater is in ohm metres and alloy density ρalloy is in kilograms per cubic metre. Resistance R is in ohms and driving voltage ΔE is in volts. The ceiling operator rounds upward to an integer anode count N. The resistance expression is the long slender stand-off form; the module selects a different form outside its length-to-radius domain. Fresh radius is derived from the published symmetric trapezoid perimeter c, with parallel widths w₁ and w₂ and height h. The depleted radius assumes similar cross-section shrinkage at fixed length; the paper does not establish that depletion model.

For the general linear coating model, a is the initial bare fraction and b is its annual increase. The library's mean factor is the capped midpoint value, not the time integral of a curve that saturates during the design life. A qualified coating model or explicit phase inputs are required for that condition. In this worked example, phase-specific effective densities are used directly. All equation and conversion records are identified in the Source register.

5 Worked public example

The public jacket example is calculated by calling the production module for the installed arrangement and for the required arrangement. Published effective phase densities and the section-periphery radius are supplied explicitly; the material-default equality check is retained.

The example mudline is at −84.1 m relative to still-water level; member coordinates are not supplied. The reported installed arrangement contains 569 anodes of nominal mass 323.4 kg and length 2.020 m. The section perimeter is 1.051 m; the equivalent radius is derived from that perimeter. Seawater resistivity is 0.22 Ω·m; the module driving voltage is 0.25 V. Nominal mass is interpreted as net alloy for the calculation.

The module returns initial, mean and final total demands of 2,688.3, 1,452.5 and 1,892.1 A. The net alloy requirement is 176,714.8 kg against 184,014.6 kg when nominal mass is treated as net alloy. The mass, initial-current and final-current counts are respectively 547, 537 and 529 anodes.

The fresh and depleted individual outputs are 5.01 A and 3.58 A. The supplied arrangement provides 2,851.8 A initially, above demand of 2,688.3 A. The mass requirement governs. The required total of 547 anodes is recalculated by the same module; that arrangement provides 2,741.5 A initially and passes all aggregate checks.

The area, density, individual resistance and source-comparison records are retained in the data register. The public paper, its tables and the exact reproduction entry point are identified in the source register.

6 Detailed results and validation evidence

Each required count is shown against the supplied anode-count limit. Mass, initial output and final output pass for the stated assumptions; mass governs the required count.

Each zone contributes to the initial, mean and final demand listed in the appendix. Anodes are shared across the connected structure in this calculation; required and provided anode mass and count per zone are NOT_EVALUATED because anode allocation and the connected-load split are not supplied. An aggregate pass does not establish a local protection margin.

The repository holds independent arithmetic tests of current demand, mass, resistance and selection of the largest criterion count, as well as edition and input-boundary checks. The public jacket tests separately check source transcription, phase-demand reproduction, geometry-derived outputs and explicit assumptions. The report tests also pin literal hand-calculated values for the section-based arrangement.

Published demands and fresh output are reproduced to their printed precision. Depleted output differs because similar-section shrinkage is assumed. The required count differs from the installed count; material and life assumptions do not establish the original design method. Signed percentage differences are recorded against the published reference values in the appendix. Agreement of functions that share a formula kernel is consistency evidence; it is not an independent field validation.

7 Limits of use

Aggregate current and alloy charge capacity are evaluated; spatial potential distribution is not evaluated by this workflow. Release of a final design is withheld until the missing design and survey evidence is resolved by the responsible engineer.

The module's use status requires a responsible engineer to check each deliverable. The standard edition and shape applicability require a separate design-basis review.

The published cross-section establishes the fresh perimeter radius independently of mass or core deductions. Core deductions affect net alloy mass and charge capacity. The actual net alloy mass and the assumed similar-section depletion at fixed length remain unresolved; product geometry, anode-to-steel separation, shielding, electrical continuity, sediment current paths and local depletion are not resolved here. The paper reports lower-level under-protection despite the original aggregate design, illustrating the spatial limitation.

Not covered

Pipeline bracelet and terminal-bank design, ship-hull calculations, multiple anode families, component continuity and phased depletion are separate capabilities in the module. Impressed-current supply and cable design, surveys, monitoring, stray-current models and corrosion-rate estimates are also outside this analysis. Deprecated and experimental functions require their own use-status and source review. No comparison with a private historical design is included.

8 Recommendations

No aggregate increase is required for the installed arrangement on the published-section basis. A field retrofit remains conditional on local demand, continuity, condition and potential evidence.

The installed arrangement exceeds the required 547 anodes by 22 anodes on the assumed life and net mass basis. The required total is recalculated and passes the aggregate mass and current criteria. The earlier aggregate increase recommendation is withdrawn; this calculation does not justify removing installed anodes or changing the spatial arrangement. The calculation basis is the module implementation of the May 2021 resistance, material and utilisation definitions listed in the source register. Spatial layout remains NOT_EVALUATED.

In service, obtain potentials, continuity and anode-condition evidence at the lower levels and the buried-steel connections before a retrofit is sized. Under-protection at a location is the trigger for a local demand and distribution assessment. A numeric potential trigger is a required design input for the selected reference electrode and service conditions; it is NOT_EVALUATED here. The public paper's remedy places additional sacrificial anodes at the affected lower levels; completion and post-retrofit performance are not established by the supplied evidence.

Confirm design life, alloy capacity and utilisation, nominal-to-net mass and anode geometry before the aggregate count is released. Retrofit quantities by level and a completion date are NOT_EVALUATED. The public remedy and the calculation criteria are located in the source register.

10 Symbols and units

The symbols used in the equations are defined with their units. Phase suffixes identify the demand and anode condition used in each calculation.

Cathodic protection is the reduction of corrosion by shifting the steel potential through externally supplied current. A sacrificial anode supplies that current by consuming alloy. A stand-off anode is separated from the protected steel by its supports.

A: steel area (m²); i: phase current density (A/m²); f: coating breakdown fraction; I: current (A); M: total required net alloy mass (kg); m: individual net alloy mass (kg); t: design life (years); H: hours per year; ε: alloy capacity (Ah/kg); u: utilisation fraction; L: anode length (m); r: equivalent radius (m); c: section perimeter (m); w₁ and w₂: parallel section widths (m); h: section height (m); ρwater: electrolyte resistivity (Ω·m); ρalloy: alloy density (kg/m³); R: anode resistance (Ω); ΔE: driving voltage (V); N: anode count; a: initial coating breakdown; b: annual breakdown rate (per year). Initial means fresh-anode polarisation demand, mean means life-average demand and final means end-of-life demand and depleted geometry.

The count chart uses anodes. Current-density conversions between amperes and milliamperes use the metric-prefix definition. A pass means only that the stated aggregate inequality is satisfied. A fail means that it is not satisfied. NOT_EVALUATED means that the required evidence is absent. An assumption marked ASSUMED - to be confirmed remains an input requiring engineering confirmation.
