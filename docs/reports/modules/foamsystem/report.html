1 Executive Summary

A low-expansion foam system is a water and concentrate supply feeding discharge devices over a protected area. Analysis is performed to determine solution demand, concentrate inventory, proportioner duty and pipe-network head demand; key results are summarized below. The public synthetic example is evaluated as a screening calculation [5].

  • The calculation is used to identify the governing supply basis.
    • The largest tank sectional area governs at 3000 L/min, with 1800 L of concentrate at the example duration and concentration [1] [5].
  • The example is used to identify equipment data required for design review.
    • The returned head margin is 25.6 m against the assumed 130 m pump rating; installed-system acceptance is NOT_EVALUATED. Pressure losses through the proportioner and discharge-device performance are absent [5] [6].

2 Introduction

The documented sizing workflow is assessed from its public example and existing tests. The calculation is used for preliminary low-expansion foam sizing; a fire-extinguishment prediction is not made [5] [6].

The main workflow comprises protected-area demand, concentrate quantity, rated proportioner checks and a single-scenario tree network. Criteria are selected by library key or supplied inline with a standard, edition and clause. Missing citation fields and library-key collisions are rejected. A citation string records the selected basis; its presence does not establish applicability [6].

Inputs comprise protected areas and criteria, the single-fire or simultaneous-area policy, optional concurrent hoses, concentration and reserve, optional proportioner ratings, and optional hydraulic geometry and terminal duties. The returned configuration contains per-area demand, concentrate quantities, envelope flags and hydraulic margins. Three comma-separated tables are written for demand with citations, pipe flow and friction loss, and terminal path heads; the latter two are written only when hydraulics is supplied. The public example also demonstrates transfer of these tables into a report pack [5] [6].

3 Design Data and Criteria

The documented public example is used without altering its engineering inputs. Its areas, geometry, concentration and equipment ratings are synthetic inputs, marked ASSUMED - to be confirmed; the source tables are in the appendix [5].

3.1 Physical arrangement and boundary conditions

Water is mixed with concentrate upstream of the pipe header. Flow is prescribed at both monitors and terminal pressure is imposed; no nozzle discharge curve or pump operating-point intersection is solved. The diagram shows connectivity and elevation, not a fabrication drawing.

3.2 Acceptance basis

The greatest of the cargo deck, largest tank section and largest monitor area demands is selected. The opened Fire Safety Systems Code resolution, adopted in 2000, sets these supply bases and an additional monitor minimum in Chapter 14, paragraph 2.2.1.1; the inert-gas-fitted duration is addressed in paragraph 2.2.1.2 [1]. The repository library labels its basis as the 2015 consolidated edition and uses different clause numbering. That consolidated edition was not opened; equivalence to later amendments is NOT_EVALUATED [5] [6].

The example assumes inert-gas-fitted service. The monitor minimum of 1250 L/min is stated in the opened resolution, Chapter 14, paragraphs 2.2.1.1.3 and 2.2.2.2 [1]. This minimum is not enforced by the area-rate multiplication. Each example monitor is assigned 1500 L/min; monitor coverage and the required fire-main simultaneity are outside this calculation. A nonnegative pump margin and a proportioner point inside the assumed envelope are screening criteria selected from the example, with actual equipment suitability NOT_EVALUATED [5] [6].

4 Analysis Methodology

Area demand and concentrate conservation are calculated before equipment duty and pipe-network head are assessed. The solution is treated as water for friction screening, using the synthetic density and pipe coefficient in Table A.5 [5].

4.1 Demand and concentrate

Qᵢ = Aᵢ rᵢ; Q = max(Qᵢ) + Qₕ; V꜀ = c [max(Qᵢ tᵢ) + Vₕ] (1 + R); Q꜀ = Q c.

Aᵢ is protected area in m²; rᵢ is solution application rate in L/min/m²; Qᵢ and Q are area and design flows in L/min; Qₕ is concurrent hose flow in L/min; tᵢ is discharge time in minutes; Vₕ is hose solution volume in litres; c is concentrate volume fraction; R is reserve fraction; V꜀ is total concentrate in litres; Q꜀ is injection in L/min. Rate times area and flow times duration implement Chapter 14, paragraphs 2.2.1.1–2.2.1.2 of the opened 2000 resolution [1]; volume fraction and percent conversion follow NIST Special Publication 811, 2008 edition, Sections 8.6.6 and 7.10.2 [3]. Reserve and hose allowances are explicit inputs.

The maximum-flow and maximum-volume scenarios are selected separately: different discharge durations can make different areas govern flow and inventory. For simultaneous protected areas the sum policy replaces each maximum with a sum. Hose streams are always added. In this example no hoses or reserve are specified [5] [6].

4.2 Equipment and pipe network

Solution flow is checked between the proportioner's input minimum and maximum; concentrate injection is checked against its input maximum; concentration is checked against its input rating. Utilization is design flow divided by maximum rated solution flow, expressed as a percentage. These are envelope comparisons with assumed inputs, not vendor performance calculations [5] [6].

hꜰ = 10.67 L q^1.852 / (C^1.852 d^4.87); H = Σhꜰ + (zₜ − zₚ) + pₜ/(ρg).

hꜰ is friction loss in m; L is physical plus equivalent pipe length in m; q is pipe flow in m³/s; C is the dimensionless Hazen–Williams pipe coefficient; d is internal diameter in m; H is required pump head in m; zₜ and zₚ are terminal and pump elevations in m; pₜ is required terminal pressure in Pa; ρ is solution density in kg/m³; g is standard gravitational acceleration in m/s². The required head is the largest terminal-path head. Flow in each pipe is the sum of downstream terminal flows.

The friction equation is the implementation's rounded SI form of Hazen–Williams. The public EPANET 2.2 manual, 2020 edition, Section 3.1, Table 3.1 gives the customary-unit coefficient 4.727, flow exponent 1.852 and diameter exponent 4.871 [2]. SI conversion gives approximately 10.67; the implementation rounds the diameter exponent to 4.87. Exact equality to the EPANET form is not asserted. Unit conversion is based on NIST Special Publication 811, 2008 edition, Appendix B.8 [3]. Hazen–Williams is empirical for water in turbulent flow; its use for foam solution is an assumption requiring fluid data [2].

Pressure head and static lift follow the energy balance in University Physics, Volume 1, 2016 edition, Section 14.6 [4]. The implementation uses standard gravity 9.80665 m/s² and 100000 Pa per bar; the unit bases are NIST Special Publication 811, 2008 edition, Appendix B.8 and Section 5.2, Table 8 [3]. Mixing-device loss, velocity-head changes and nozzle curves are omitted.

6 Validation Evidence

The repository holds analytical arithmetic tests, hydraulic screening tests and a report-pack example. This evidence exercises software behavior; physical validation against a fire test or measured hydraulic network is not established [6].

The sizing tests cover demand, concentration and reserve arithmetic, proportioner flags, Hazen–Williams arithmetic, laminar Darcy–Weisbach agreement with Hagen–Poiseuille, a turbulent cross-method comparison, tree validation and routed outputs. The criteria tests cover citation fields, explicit source-review flags, selection and collisions. The report-pack tests compare checked-in sizing tables with fresh calculation output and exercise rendering. The inventory in Table A.4 counts test functions, without implying execution or engineering acceptance.

The accompanying report tests assert hand-worked supply, inventory and injection values, compare every tagged worked-example value with the workflow return, recalculate chart points and rebuild the committed page at displayed precision. These latter checks are consistency checks. The hand arithmetic does not validate the regulatory inputs. Execution results and checks not performed are listed in the appendix; no field-validation record is held with this report.

7 Limits of Use and Not Covered

Use is limited to preliminary low-expansion sizing with cited, applicable criteria and a connected tree network. Installed-system acceptance is NOT_EVALUATED because equipment, coverage and governing-edition evidence are absent.

Area-rate multiplication does not enforce the fixed monitor minimum. Optional pump and proportioner checks use input ratings, and hydraulic terminal flow is not reconciled automatically with the demand calculation. Concentrate hose allowances rely on the caller passing the same hoses and durations used in demand. Nonfinite input rejection is not established across all input fields [6].

Not covered: loop balancing; medium- or high-expansion foam; fuel compatibility and environmental approval; foam quality and extinguishment; monitor throw and coverage; fire-main simultaneity; suction losses and cavitation; vendor mixing-device losses; transients; and detailed equipment selection. The alternative Darcy–Weisbach branch, storage-tank criteria and helideck criteria are described in the module documentation but are not worked examples here [6].

The alternative friction branch uses an abrupt laminar-to-turbulent switch, whereas the public EPANET manual describes a transition interpolation. This report supplies no accuracy claim for that transition region [2] [6].

8 Recommendations

Input qualification and omitted physical losses are resolved before design acceptance. The recommendations below address the specific evidence gaps exposed by this calculation.

  • Obtain the applicable governing edition, fuel basis, coverage drawing and inert-gas status; review each supply basis and duration against the opened resolution and applicable amendments before issuing a design [1].
  • Enforce the monitor minimum as a separate input check before accepting any area-based result; review monitor coverage and fire-main simultaneity separately. The opened Chapter 14 monitor and installation requirements provide the basis [1].
  • Replace assumed equipment ratings with supplier pressure-flow curves and include mixing-device, suction and fitting losses. Reassess head margin whenever the selected flow, geometry or fluid properties change; the energy balance and empirical water-friction limitations provide the trigger [2] [4].
  • Reconcile terminal supply with the governing area demand in every scenario. If a required minimum or equipment envelope is exceeded, revise device allocation or equipment rating and repeat the screening before acceptance [1] [5].
  • Extend analytical input-rejection and transition-region tests before expanding the supported hydraulic use range. Compare a measured network or qualified public benchmark before making a physical-performance accuracy claim [2] [6].
