1 Executive Summary

Fluid-column hydrostatics is a static pressure calculation for a liquid of uniform density. Analysis is performed to determine the pressure increase below a stated surface datum; key results are summarized below.

  • The published water-column pressure is reproduced with the stated gravity.
    • At 40.0 m, the computed gauge pressure is 392.0 kPa; the difference from the published reference is 0.0 %. Public example basis.
  • The result is a pressure demand for subsequent component assessment.
    • Allowable pressure and absolute pressure are NOT_EVALUATED because a component rating and an absolute surface pressure are not supplied. The pressure diagram carries the surface boundary; it establishes no operating limit.

2 Introduction

The pressure increase in a stationary liquid is calculated from density, gravity and vertical depth. The calculation is applied to the pressure part of a published reservoir example; component strength and installation conditions are outside this assessment.

The public interface contains 8 functions: pressure increase, bottom pressure and conversions for density, length and pressure. The pressure calculation has no dependency on a licensed analysis program. The existing implementation and tests are identified in the evidence register.

3 Summary and calculation controls

A consistent pressure datum is required for the surface and bottom of the column. Engineering acceptance is left unevaluated until the component rating is supplied.

Density is entered in kilograms per cubic metre, height in metres and gravity in metres per second squared. Pressure is returned in pascals. A supplied gauge surface pressure produces a gauge bottom pressure; an absolute surface pressure produces an absolute result. The default surface datum is gauge despite the bottom helper's absolute-pressure wording.

The caller is responsible for finite values, physical density and a non-negative depth when depth is measured down from the surface. Input validation, datum tagging and unit checking are not implemented. Negative inputs and non-finite arithmetic are not rejected.

The conversion helpers accept density in pounds per US gallon (ppg), length in feet (ft) and pressure in pounds per square inch (psi), bar or pascals (Pa), as appropriate to the selected helper. Each returns the converted numerical value without a unit wrapper.

4 Design data and criteria

The calculation basis is taken from the published example without substituting missing engineering inputs. Equality to its pressure result is the reproduction criterion; structural acceptance is not inferred from that equality.

The liquid density is 1000.0 kg/m³ and gravity is 9.80 m/s². Reservoir depth H is 80.0 m and evaluation depth h is 40.0 m. Public example basis.

The acceptance comparison uses the source gravity explicitly. The module default is 9.81 m/s², as recorded by the implementation; it is not a location-specific gravity measurement. No density or depth range is enforced.

5 Theory and methodology

Hydrostatic equilibrium is integrated for a constant-density liquid under uniform gravity. The resulting pressure increment is added to the supplied surface pressure.

dp/dz = −ρg; Δp = ρgh; pb = p₀ + Δp

The upward coordinate is z, downward depth is h, density is ρ, gravitational acceleration is g, surface pressure is p₀ and bottom pressure is pb. Δp is the pressure increment. These relations and the constant-density restriction are given by the public theory source.

The pressure function evaluates Δp directly. The bottom-pressure function adds the surface value. No atmospheric pressure is added automatically. Conversions are scalar multiplications; they do not identify the datum or change the physical model.

Numerical arrays with multiplication and addition semantics are supported by the arithmetic and are exercised by an existing test. Ordinary Python lists are not a general array input contract. Stratified liquids require pressure increments to be integrated using their actual density distribution; that integration is not supplied by this module.

6 Worked public example

The pressure part of the published reservoir example is reproduced by calling the public pressure function. The mean wall pressure equals the pressure at mid-depth because the pressure distribution is linear in depth.

With the input basis in Table A, the function returns 392.0 kPa at 40.0 m. The published reference is 392.0 kPa, giving a difference of 0.0 %. The bottom pressure at 80.0 m is 784.0 kPa gauge. Public example basis.

The module default gravity gives 392.4 kPa at the same evaluation depth, a difference of 0.1 % from the published reference. The explicit source gravity is retained for reproduction.

Pressure units other than pascals are obtained through the module converters. The converted values and default-gravity comparison are recorded in Table B. The force part of the published example is excluded because wall-force integration is not a module function.

7 Validation evidence

Calculation-level evidence is provided by the existing focused tests and the public pressure comparison. Field measurements and pressure-rating qualification are not held in the inspected module test set.

The existing set contains 10 tests: unit conversions and round trips, the fluid-column relation, array arithmetic, a worksheet pressure comparison and the zero-surface gauge convention. The worksheet inputs are described as generic in that test file; the underlying spreadsheet is not provided as independently inspectable evidence. These checks establish the stated assertions only. They do not establish a design envelope.

The report is checked by 6 additional tests. Module outputs are compared with the cited public reference, every bound displayed example value and diagram sample is checked, and the rebuilt report is compared byte for byte with the issued page. The source records and commands are given in the evidence register.

No mesh, time step or convergence iteration is used by this closed-form calculation. Array agreement checks arithmetic consistency, rather than independent physical validation.

8 Findings, limitations and recommendations

The published pressure calculation is reproduced when its inputs and gravity are used. Application is limited by the constant-density, static-fluid model and by caller-managed units.

Recommendations

Record the pressure datum with every input and result; this follows the surface-plus-increment relation in the theory source. Before a component acceptance decision, supply its rating and applicable design basis, and compare that rating with the pressure demand. Acceptance remains NOT_EVALUATED in their absence.

Obtain a density profile when temperature, salinity or compressibility makes density vary with depth. Integrate that profile using a suitable model, as required by the variable-density equilibrium relation. Accelerating fluids, flow losses, transients and gas columns require additional governing physics.

Add finite-value, unit and datum checks at the calling interface before operational use. Retain independent conversion references before changing the legacy pressure multiplier: the existing tests preserve worksheet compatibility and round-trip consistency, rather than qualification to a metrology reference. No production coefficient is changed by this analysis.

10 Not covered

Hull displacement, buoyancy centres, trim and intact stability are handled outside this fluid-column module. They are excluded from the present assessment.

Dam-force integration, structural resistance, wave loading, station keeping and installation sea-state envelopes are not calculated. Orientation tolerances, rigging and forecast thresholds have no supplied basis in this static pressure example and are not assigned.
