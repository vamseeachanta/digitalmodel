1. Executive Summary

The ANSYS module is a Python interface for preparing structural analysis inputs, reading result records and calculating supporting engineering quantities. Analysis is performed to determine the response of an open-ended elastic cylinder and the extent of the available software evidence; key results are summarized below.

  • The analytical bore response is available for comparison with a future numerical solution.
    • The bore equivalent stress is 135.5 MPa and radial displacement is 0.4995 mm under 10.0 MPa internal pressure. A design allowable is absent and strength acceptance remains NOT_EVALUATED. [Source B] [Source C]
  • The prepared meshes are available for a controlled numerical comparison.
    • Mesh preparation and analytical evaluation are demonstrated. Mesh convergence, native execution and component design acceptance remain NOT_EVALUATED in this analysis. [Source B] [Source E]

2. Introduction

The purpose, scope and status of the analysis are presented in this section. The main workflow is assessed from fixed cylinder inputs through mesh preparation and analytical response calculation.

Text inputs are generated for ANSYS Parametric Design Language. Material and section records, design-point tables, journal metadata and output listings are read by separate interfaces. Thickness, weld-linearization and fatigue calculations are supplied as supporting methods. Input generation is distinguished from solution execution and engineering acceptance. [Source D]

Not covered

Bolted joints, contact, thermal coupling, fatigue life, weld assessment, lifting plates, foundation plates and hydrodynamic load transfer are not evaluated here. Their interfaces are listed in the module knowledge pack; their physical assumptions and acceptance criteria require separate analyses. [Source D]

4. Analysis Methodology

The cylinder mesh and analytical response are computed by the module from the retained public basis. No licensed solver is started or queried.

The preparation function returns command text, nodes, connectivity, loaded faces, restraints and response positions. The reference function accepts decimal pressure, radius and axial position strings and returns radial, hoop, axial and equivalent stress, radial–axial shear stress, and radial and axial displacement. The fixed basis is enforced; this reference is not a general cylinder design interface. Implementation locators and the invocation are given in section 9.2. [Source B] [Source C]

Elasticity equations

For internal pressure p, inner radius a and outer radius b, the integration constants A and B are determined by the radial tractions. Radius r is measured from the axis; axial position y is measured from the restrained bottom edge. E is Young’s modulus and ν is Poisson’s ratio. The following equations are the open-end specialization of the axisymmetric elastic solution. [Source A] [Source C]

dσᵣ/dr + (σᵣ − σθ)/r = 0;
A = pa²/(b² − a²); B = Ab²;
σᵣ = A − B/r²; σθ = A + B/r²; σ𝓏 = τᵣ𝓏 = 0;
uᵣ = [(1 − ν)Ar + (1 + ν)B/r]/E; u𝓏 = −2νAy/E;
σᵥ = √(σᵣ² + σθ² − σᵣσθ).

The inner traction is σᵣ(a) = −p and the outer traction is σᵣ(b) = 0. The axial condition represents open ends with a compatible bottom displacement datum. End caps or an axial restraint over the full length change the axial stress and the equivalent stress. Equation coefficients are algebraic identities from the cited derivation; no design-standard coefficient is used. [Source A] [Source C]

7. Limitations

The limits of use are stated for the physical model and the software evidence. A numerical result outside this basis requires a separate definition and assessment.

Material response is linear, isotropic and isothermal. Plasticity, instability, fatigue, residual stress and temperature-dependent strength are not evaluated. The uncertain quantity for strength use is the applicable allowable stress; it is not inferred from elastic modulus. [Source A] [Source B]

The idealization has no heads, end-cap force, nozzle, weld, contact or local attachment. Stress concentration and end-restraint effects are not represented. The public elasticity source distinguishes axial boundary conditions and end effects; transfer to a physical cylinder requires an assessment of those conditions. [Source A] [Source D]

The reference function accepts the fixed example pressures and geometry only. Different pressures, wall dimensions and material constants are not supported by this reference interface. An unloaded analytical control is not evidence of solver completion. [Source B] [Source C]

General preparation helpers contain incomplete geometry and loading branches. The module knowledge pack identifies head and reinforcement-pad placeholders, uniform temperature loading in the pressure-wall helper, and absent external-pressure buckling assessment. Complete design-code coverage is not established. [Source D]

8. Recommendations

The next engineering actions are defined by the missing acceptance inputs and evidence. Use is restricted to analytical comparison and input inspection until those gaps are closed. [Source B] [Source D]

For strength assessment, supply the governing design-standard edition, material product form, temperature, allowable basis, joint quality and corrosion allowance. Recalculate the applicable section checks with that basis before accepting any component. A limit exceedance requires a revised section or an explicitly assessed operating restriction; no pressure rating is assigned here. [Source D]

For numerical acceptance, retain solved outputs for the prepared meshes, the unloaded control, boundary tractions and reactions, and compare the defined stresses and displacements with the analytical solution under the example’s numerical criteria. Record convergence and unresolved diagnostics before accepting a response. [Source B] [Source E]

For extended use, complete the missing geometry and loading branches and establish separate public benchmarks for each claimed physical feature. Prioritize the closed load path and end-restraint definition before adding nozzles, thermal gradients or contact. [Source A] [Source D]

10. Acronyms and Symbols

The terms and symbols used in the analysis are defined below. Stress and pressure use megapascals; length and displacement use millimetres.

ANSYS is the analysis software product. APDL means ANSYS Parametric Design Language. FEA means finite element analysis. MPa means megapascal; mm means millimetre. a and b are the inner and outer radii; t is wall thickness; L is axial length; p is internal pressure; r is radial position; y is axial position. E is Young’s modulus; ν is Poisson’s ratio; A and B are elastic integration constants. σᵣ, σθ and σ𝓏 are radial, hoop and axial normal stresses; τᵣ𝓏 is radial–axial shear stress; σᵥ is von Mises equivalent stress. uᵣ and u𝓏 are radial and axial displacements.

In the result legend, a result marked NOT_EVALUATED has no completed assessment against the stated engineering criterion. Analytical values are calculated values under the stated idealization, not an acceptance verdict.
