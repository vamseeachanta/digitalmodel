Vortex-induced vibration screening module analysis

Draft for maintainer review

1. Executive summary

The vortex-induced vibration screening module is a uniform-span current-screening calculation. Analysis is performed to determine modal lock-in flags and an indicative cross-flow fatigue life; the key results are summarized below. The documented public synthetic example is evaluated, and its results are not a field design basis. [4]

  • The synthetic example fails the cross-flow life comparison.
    • The calculated life is 10.8 years against the 60 year required life. Detailed response and fatigue assessment is the next engineering action before any operating acceptance. [4]
  • The scope of the decision is limited to screening.
    • 1 cross-flow mode and 1 in-line mode are flagged. In-line fatigue is not evaluated, and a separate in-line fatigue assessment is required before acceptance. [4]

2. Introduction

A straight, uniform tubular span in steady current is considered. Riser vibration screening is performed with analytical beam modes, a reduced-velocity current envelope and supplied modal stress. Water depth is not supplied; span length is the structural length and is not interpreted as water depth. [4]

The documented workflow accepts a configuration mapping, calls the analytical modal and fatigue components, and writes per-mode and summary comma-separated tables. A configuration echo is available through the engine entry point. The worked calculation calls the same workflow directly. [4] [5]

3. Design data

The input basis is taken from the public synthetic example. All physical properties, environmental properties, stress calibration and acceptance inputs are marked ASSUMED - to be confirmed for any application to a real span. [4]

The model uses 60.0 m span length, 0.2731 m outer diameter and 0.2287 m inner diameter. Pinned supports and 0 N effective tension are specified. The complete data register is given in Appendix A. [4]

A spatial current profile is accepted as an alternative to a scalar current. Only the minimum and maximum profile speeds are used; depth-dependent modal work and shear are not resolved. The effective beam mass and the effective mass ratio are separate inputs and are not checked for mutual consistency. [4]

Criteria and rationale

Required screening life is design duration multiplied by the design fatigue factor, giving 20 × 3 = 60 years. This is a supplied acceptance basis; it is not presented as a universal standard requirement. The equivalent annual damage limit is 0.01667 per year. [4]

Implemented inclusive reduced-velocity bands are 4 to 8 for cross-flow and 1 to 3.5 for in-line motion. These are implementation criteria read from the calculation source. Their independent physical qualification is NOT_EVALUATED. The associated standard references do not establish that these particular coefficients are qualified for a proposed installation. [4] [6]

4. Analysis methodology

Uniform beam modes are calculated before current lock-in and cross-flow fatigue are assessed. The first benchmark is the untensioned pinned-beam closed form. Added mass is included in the supplied mass per unit length; it is not calculated by this workflow. [1] [4]

For the untensioned uniform beam, the governing equation is m ∂²w/∂t² + EI ∂⁴w/∂x⁴ = 0. Here m is effective mass per length, w is transverse displacement, t is time, x is distance along the span, E is Young’s modulus and I is second moment of area. The small-deflection beam equation and dispersion relation are given in the open beam text. [1]

With zero deflection and zero bending moment at each pin, the sinusoidal mode has wave number nπ/L. Substitution into the cited dispersion relation gives fₙ = n²π/(2L²) √(EI/m), where fₙ is modal frequency in hertz, n is positive mode number and L is span length. For an annulus, I = ∫ y² dA = π(Dₒ⁴ − Dᵢ⁴)/64; Dₒ and Dᵢ are outer and inner diameter. Here y is distance from the section’s neutral axis and dA is an elemental cross-sectional area. The factor follows by integrating the circular section in polar coordinates. The worked example is confined to this zero-tension, pinned case. [1]

Reduced velocity is defined as current speed U divided by fₙDₒ. The dimensionless Reynolds number is U Dₒ/ν, with ν the kinematic viscosity. Reynolds number is the inertia-to-viscosity measure in the open fluid-mechanics teaching source. The module's reduced-velocity definition is a screening convention whose standard association remains NOT_EVALUATED here. [3] [4] [6]

The cross-flow amplitude relation, stability factor, piecewise shedding-frequency coefficients, alternative support constants and tension corrections are implemented approximations. Independent source qualification of their numerical coefficients is NOT_EVALUATED. Their outputs are reported as module screening estimates; no unpublished coefficient or licensed table value is reproduced. [4] [6]

Full stress range is twice the supplied stress amplitude at unit displacement ratio multiplied by the calculated displacement-to-diameter ratio. Annual cycles are frequency multiplied by exposure fraction and the duration of a year. Annual damage is the sum of cycle count divided by endurance at each stress range; screening life is its reciprocal. Linear cumulative damage and its limitations are described in the open fatigue text. The module’s stress-range convention and annualisation are implementation assumptions, not additional material qualification. [2] [4]

The workflow escalates when locked modes lack a stress calibration, the response exceeds its confidence criterion, excessive simultaneous modes are locked, checked frequencies do not cover shedding, or Reynolds number is outside its implemented window. Damage and life are then suppressed. The exact trigger constants remain source-qualification gaps and are not adopted as design limits. [4] [6]

5. Detailed results

The synthetic example is computed by calling the module and reading the emitted modal table. No licensed solver is used. The calculation is a narrow-band cross-flow screening estimate. [4]

The annular second moment of area is 0.00013877195 m⁴ and the first modal frequency is 0.174308 Hz. With 0.22 m/s current, the first-mode reduced velocity is 4.6215 and Reynolds number is 50489.1. The hand-calculation anchors and displayed-input frequency check are specified in Appendix C. [4] [5]

The first-mode displacement-to-diameter ratio is 0.5221; its stress range is 46.989 MPa. Annual cross-flow damage is 0.09283 per year, giving 10.8 years against the 60 year required life. The result is a failed screening comparison, and detailed response and fatigue assessment is required before any operating acceptance. [4]

The workflow checks 10 modes and flags 2 distinct locked modes. The in-line flag is unassessed for damage. Zero cross-flow damage in another case therefore does not demonstrate infinite total fatigue life. [4] [5]

6. Key findings

The supplied life requirement is not met by the public example under the module’s response assumptions. The result is suitable for identifying the need for further engineering assessment, with physical accuracy still dependent on input calibration and response-model qualification. [4]

Existing evidence includes analytical-component tests, input rejection and applicability tests, current-envelope tests, annualisation and fatigue-factor tests, output-location tests and deterministic example regression checks. The durable workflow test contains a pinned-beam first-frequency comparison. These records test implementation behaviour; measured-response benchmarking and corpus qualification remain absent from the documented validation basis. [4] [5]

7. Limitations

Detailed response prediction and a complete fatigue assessment are excluded from this screening scope. In-line fatigue, sheared-current modal energy, variable tension and geometry, nonlinear response, stress recovery, thickness effects and site-specific loading are not resolved by this workflow. [4]

The fatigue curve identifier F1 denotes the selected weld-detail curve in the implementation; seawater with cathodic protection is the selected environment. No claim of suitability for a particular weld or environment is made. The returned standards string omits a fatigue-standard edition, and exact edition and clause equivalence are NOT_EVALUATED. [4] [6]

Higher modes for alternative end supports use simplified scaling, and effective mass and mass ratio are supplied separately. The first flagged cross-flow mode is selected as critical; this is not a ranking by the largest damage. A passing cross-flow screening result is not an in-line fatigue acceptance. [4]

Not covered

The separate parametric screening adapter, legacy vibration workflows, hosted interfaces, full time-domain analysis, host motion, drift-off, recoil and weak-point assessment are not covered. An installation operating envelope is not evaluated because no installation basis is supplied. [4]

8. Recommendations

The failed synthetic comparison is addressed by a detailed response and fatigue assessment before operating acceptance. The open fatigue source identifies stress range, cycle history and the limitations of linear damage accumulation as essential to interpreting a fatigue estimate. [2] [4]

For application to a real span, geometry, effective mass, damping, stress calibration, exposure, weld category and acceptance basis are to be established from application data. In-line fatigue is to be included whenever its lock-in flag is set. A change in current, support condition or geometry is a trigger for reassessment; this report establishes no numerical operating restriction for an installation. [4]

The first qualification slice is the pinned, untensioned uniform span with supplied stress calibration. Measured or openly published response and fatigue benchmarks are needed before extending confidence to alternative boundaries, tension corrections or the implemented response coefficients. Source edition and clause mapping is to be completed before any standards-compliance claim. [1] [4] [6]

Appendix C. Calculation evidence and pre-flight

Hand-worked anchors cover Reynolds number, annular second moment, required life and full stress range. The first frequency is also recalculated from the displayed input precision. The report consistency checks compare every tagged worked-example value with a fresh module call and compare the rebuilt rounded page with the committed page. These checks establish arithmetic and rendering consistency, not empirical response accuracy.

The beam benchmark uses the displayed diameters without reducing their precision. Annual damage is compared with the inverse required life. Full fatigue qualification, independent response measurements, alternative support benchmarks and installation operating limits are NOT_EVALUATED. References resolve within this report and the numeric inputs retain their significant digits.
