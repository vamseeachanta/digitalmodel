Field development

Concept and economic screening

Draft for technical review

1. Executive Summary

The field development module is a screening tool for offshore host concepts, costs, layouts and production economics. Analysis is performed to determine how a documented illustrative field is ranked and evaluated. The key results are summarized below.

  • Concept shortlisting is supported under the stated heuristic assumptions.
    • Spar and Semi-submersible tie at 92.50 points for the documented example. The stable sort preserves LEGACY_HOST_TYPES order and selects Spar before Semi. This ordering does not establish an engineering preference. The water depth of 2,100.00 m lies within its implemented screening band of 1,200.00 to 3,000.00 m. This is a model applicability check, not a structural capacity check.
  • The calculated economic value is unsuitable for investment acceptance.
    • Pre-tax net present value is 16,045.82 million USD, above the zero-value comparison line. Scheduled production is 662.71 million barrels against the supplied reservoir size of 400.00 million barrels. The production discrepancy prevents acceptance of that economic value. The tied Semi-submersible alternative has pre-tax net present value 13,743.90 million USD under the same production basis.

2. Introduction

Offshore development choices are screened using water depth, reservoir size, access to existing infrastructure and fluid type. The selected host is passed into cost estimation and annual cash-flow evaluation. The calculation described here is the documented public workflow example, with its identifying label replaced by a neutral description.

The example is an illustration supplied with the software. It is not a measured field dataset or a published independent benchmark. Its numerical inputs are read directly from the documented example, and its results are obtained by calling the module. The source editions and precise calculation locators are recorded in §9.2.

3. Summary of decision controls

The screening output is used to organize further engineering evaluation. Acceptance is conditioned on a reconciled production forecast, independently established cost inputs and a verified fiscal basis.

A positive suitability score identifies a candidate for further consideration. It does not establish technical feasibility. A positive net present value only describes the discounted cash-flow balance under the supplied assumptions. The production-volume discrepancy in this example overrides that financial comparison.

The curve holds concept, capacity, costs, discount rate and field life fixed. It does not represent a price forecast. All plotted cases retain the unresolved production basis.

4. Design data and basis

The example is evaluated as an oil development with infrastructure distance unspecified. The local economic fallback is used without the optional external data package. The fiscal identifier is retained, but no tax adjustment is applied.

4.1 System representation

A floating spar host, production riser and seabed connection are shown schematically. The drawing identifies the physical system only; hull dimensions, mooring geometry, well count, pressure boundary conditions and metocean loads are not supplied.

4.2 Inputs and criteria

Water depth is 2,100.00 m; reservoir size is 400.00 million barrels; production capacity is 100,000 barrels per day; price is 75.00 USD per barrel; annual discount rate is 0.10 as a fraction; and field life is 30 years. These are illustrative assumptions, not verified field measurements. Table A supplies the input register.

The depth band is the host exclusion criterion implemented by the ranking model. The zero-value line follows the definition of net present value. Neither criterion is a code-of-practice acceptance limit. No standard-derived structural or installation criterion is claimed.

5. Theory and methodology

Host suitability is calculated using weighted depth, reservoir, distance and fluid scores. Cost estimates are then converted into an annual cash-flow sequence and discounted. The mathematical definitions below describe the implemented local calculation, whose source editions are recorded in §9.2.

5.1 Concept ranking and cost estimation

S = wdsd + wrsr + wlsl + wfsf

Here S is dimensionless suitability; s denotes a component score; w denotes its implemented weight; and subscripts d, r, l and f denote depth, reservoir size, infrastructure distance and fluid type. A host outside its hard depth band receives a zero score before the weighted sum. These are engineering heuristics, not calibrated probabilities. The weights are 0.40 for depth, 0.30 for reservoir size, 0.20 for distance and 0.10 for fluid type. Both tied hosts contribute 40 + 30 + 14 + 8.5 points. These coefficients are ASSUMED - to be confirmed; their independent empirical calibration is NOT_EVALUATED.

C = Cref(Q/Qref)a [1 + k max(0, d − dref)]
O = Oref(Q/Qref)bA(age)

C is standalone-host capital expenditure; O is annual operating expenditure; Q is daily production capacity; d is water depth; ref denotes the implemented reference condition; a and b are capacity exponents; k is the depth coefficient; and A is the age multiplier. The economic fallback uses the base cost estimates and the reference operating age. Abandonment expenditure is a fixed fraction of capital expenditure when no external estimate or override is available: 7% of capital expenditure. The fraction is ASSUMED - to be confirmed. The historical cost basis is recorded in the source; escalation, location adjustment and quotation accuracy are not established.

5.2 Production and discounted cash flow

qt = Qft;   ft = exp(−Dτ) after the plateau
Ft = Rt − Ot − Ct − At;
V = Σt Ft/(1 + r)t

q is daily production rate; f is the production multiplier; D is exponential decline rate; τ is elapsed time after the plateau in years; F is annual net cash flow; R is revenue; C is capital expenditure; O is operating expenditure; A is abandonment cost; r is annual discount rate; and V is net present value. The time index t includes the initial development period. Monetary cash flows are in million USD.

Supplying reservoir size activates automatic exponential decline in this workflow. The recovery factor is 0.15 and the plateau fraction is 60% of field life; both are ASSUMED - to be confirmed. The module's estimated ultimate recovery (EUR) is therefore 60.00 million barrels (400 × 0.15), while the 18-year plateau alone produces 657.00 million barrels. Because the plateau already exceeds EUR, 2.00 per year is the fallback constant, not a solved decline rate. The implemented plateau remains at capacity even when its cumulative volume exceeds the recovery target. Operating expenditure falls with the production multiplier; fixed operating costs are not separated. Capital spending overlaps early production, and abandonment is charged in the final year. Carbon cost is omitted because no carbon-price input is supplied.

6. Worked example results

The documented example is evaluated through the public workflow. Spar and Semi-submersible tie at 92.50 points. The stable sort preserves LEGACY_HOST_TYPES order and selects Spar before Semi; the ordered scores are retained in Table B. The tie alone provides no economic preference.

Calculated capital expenditure is 5,450.00 million USD. Reference annual operating expenditure is 175.00 million USD/year, and abandonment expenditure is 381.50 million USD. Pre-tax net present value is 16,045.82 million USD, above the zero-value comparison line. These estimates inherit the unverified cost calibration and the production discrepancy. The tied Semi-submersible alternative has pre-tax net present value 13,743.90 million USD; its costs are retained in Table D. Both economics calculations retain the same production limitation.

Scheduled production is 662.71 million barrels, exceeding the supplied reservoir size of 400.00 million barrels by 65.7% relative to that supplied size. Scheduled production is 11.0 times the module's EUR of 60.00 million barrels. Reservoir size is described as recoverable reserves by the ranking interface, while the economics calculation applies a further recovery-factor assumption. The inconsistent meaning and the uncapped plateau require reconciliation before this economic value is used for a development decision.

7. Validation evidence

The repository evidence is separated into software regression, illustrative examples and independent engineering validation. Workflow tests check input rejection, populated outputs, finite economic values, selected-host consistency and comparison ordering.

Cost tests check scaling, ordering and invalid-input handling. Concept tests exercise ranking and exclusion behaviour. Economics tests include mocked adapters and optional integration paths; their existence does not establish the accuracy of the external cost or fiscal models. Benchmark tests include explicitly synthetic records. The public reference catalogue supplies descriptive analogues, but no independent measured cash-flow comparison for this worked example is established.

Report verification compares the displayed values and plotted samples with fresh module returns. Independent literal cost and score assertions, a Decimal cash-flow calculation for all 11 prices, and a separate cash-flow reconstruction check the numerical basis. The reported production volume comes from that reconstruction, without private module cash-flow imports. Payback is year 3. This is a reproducibility check, not independent validation of reserves, costs or tax.

8. Key findings, limits and recommendations

The calculation supports comparative screening under explicit assumptions. Investment acceptance remains NOT_EVALUATED because production conservation, cost calibration and fiscal suitability are not established.

Reconcile the reserve definition and constrain cumulative production to an independently approved recoverable-volume forecast before interpreting economic value. Add a regression criterion that cumulative production does not exceed that forecast. This action follows the discrepancy in §6 and the production construction identified in §9.2.

Provide priced capital, operating and abandonment inputs with a stated currency date before financial use. Verify the selected fiscal calculation against the actual offshore regime; the local fallback does not apply tax, and the optional United States adapter selects an onshore implementation. Record the actual backend with each evaluation. These actions follow the adapter selection and cost fallback evidence in §9.2.

Reject an all-zero concept ranking before further evaluation. The single-concept workflow can still select the first listed host when every host is excluded; the comparison workflow explicitly rejects that condition. For tiebacks, pass the actual distance into a separately verified cost basis: the local economic fallback substitutes a fixed distance rather than forwarding the screening distance.

8.1 Not covered

Generalized onshore and offshore layout routing, cable and flowline sizing, rig capability, empirical concept probabilities, architecture statistics, schedule analytics, multi-axis concept screening and visualization are available as separate sub-areas. They are not evaluated here. Installation orientation, sea-state limits, rigging, forecast support, structural capacity and operational envelopes require other analyses and supplied design data. No installation limit is inferred from this economic example.
