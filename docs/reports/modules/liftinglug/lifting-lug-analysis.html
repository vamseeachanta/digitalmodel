1 Executive Summary

The lifting lug module is an analytical screen for a circular plate loaded through a pin. Analysis is performed to determine the governing bearing, net-tension and shear tear-out demand; key results are summarized below. The public example is assessed using the implemented strength factors, whose design-code applicability remains NOT_EVALUATED.

  • The example is held from design acceptance.
    • The shear tear-out utilization is 116.2 % against the 100.0 % screening limit; the exceedance is 16.2 % of the limit.
    • The bearing and net-tension screens pass; that outcome excludes welds, bending, fatigue and rigging adequacy.
  • A restriction for the unchanged example geometry is available for engineering review.
    • The most-loaded leg static tension is restricted to 361.4 kN for the same dynamic and skew factors. This rounded-down value passes only the implemented screens.
    • Material certification, load basis, connection resistance and code applicability are required before design release.

2 Introduction

A circular lifting lug is assessed under an already resolved in-plane sling tension. The main workflow is covered from load entry to the governing strength result and summary output.

The public functions accept numerical geometry and material inputs; the configuration interface also handles the skew-factor floor and writes a summary. The workflow is intended for preliminary screening. The published example is repository-authored; it is not an independent textbook benchmark or a field measurement (example guide).

Not covered

The interpolated lug atlas, whole-rigging statics, crane motion, splash-zone loads and finite-element padeye studies are outside this workflow. Vessel heading, a sea-state envelope and live forecast support are NOT_EVALUATED because the module has no hydrodynamic response or weather inputs. No installability statement is made.

4 Design Data and Basis

The lug and pin are idealised as a concentric, in-plane connection with equal resistance on the ligaments. The data are taken from the public example input; no material or fabrication record accompanies it.

Criteria and rationale

The implemented bearing, tension and shear factors are respectively 0.90, 0.60 and 0.40 times the supplied yield strength (implementation). They are reproduced to explain the software result. The repository calls them allowable-stress factors but identifies no edition, clause or table of the American Institute of Steel Construction (AISC) design provisions for them. Their suitability as design allowables is NOT_EVALUATED; no standard-compliance conclusion is drawn.

A utilization at or below 100.0 % passes a strength screen by the module's comparison. The entered skew factor is 1.1; the returned minimum is 1.1, and the governing factor is 1.1 (router output). The repository attributes the leg-count floor to Portable offshore units, April 2016, “Lift points – in-plane loads”, identified in the source register. The clause text has not been independently examined for this analysis. The example guide records applicability restrictions and directs marine-operation lifts to an independently assessed skew factor. Applying a numerical floor does not establish that scope is satisfied.

The assumed basis is axial in-plane loading with full participating plate thickness. The dynamic amplification and yield strength are ASSUMED - to be confirmed. The appendix data register identifies each evidence gap.

5 Methodology and Equations

The factored load is formed by multiplication, and nominal stresses are obtained from idealised resisting areas. The following equations reproduce the public module implementation, rather than a complete padeye design method.

P = Ps Ad K;   t = tp + 2tc;   r = D/2;   ℓ = R − r

P is the factored sling load and Ps is static tension in the most-loaded leg, both in kN. Ad is the dimensionless dynamic amplification factor. K is the larger of the entered skew factor and the implemented leg-count minimum. The plate thickness tp, each cheek thickness tc, total thickness t, hole diameter D, hole radius r, outer radius R and loaded ligament ℓ are in mm. The multiplier on cheek thickness represents the cheek on each side of the main plate.

σb = 1000P/(dt);   σt = 1000P/(2tℓ);   τ = 1000P/(2tℓ)

The pin diameter d is in mm. Bearing stress σb, net-tension stress σt and shear stress τ are in MPa. The unit conversion is from kN to N; force in N divided by area in mm² gives MPa. The net and tear-out expressions use the same idealised area, so their demands are equal in this implementation.

Sb = abFy;   St = atFy;   Ss = asFy;   u = demand/allowable

Fy is the entered yield strength in MPa; ab, at and as are the implementation factors stated in the basis section. Sb, St and Ss are the resulting nominal stress limits in MPa. Utilization u is dimensionless and is displayed as a percentage. The largest utilization governs. A below-minimum entered skew factor fails the overall screen even after the governing floor has been used for the load.

6 Worked Example and Results

The documented public configuration is evaluated by calling the module's public configuration interface. No example input or calculated stress is transcribed into the report.

The input static tension is 420.0 kN and the dynamic factor is 2.0; with the governing skew factor of 1.1, the returned design load is 924.0 kN. The calculated total thickness is 40.000 mm and the loaded ligament is 70.0 mm. The geometry, yield input and factors are listed with their sources in the appendix.

The shear tear-out screen fails at 116.2 % utilization against the 100.0 % limit. Its exceedance is 16.2 % of the limit. The summary reports a failed screen, governed by shear tear-out. The outcome is a rejection of the example under the implemented criteria, not a prediction of physical rupture or a complete design rejection.

With geometry and factors unchanged, the limiting static tension is calculated by dividing the example static tension by the unrounded governing utilization and rounding down to the displayed precision. At 361.4 kN the public interface returns a passing screen. This restriction is conditional on the implementation basis; attachment and operating acceptance remain NOT_EVALUATED.

7 Validation Evidence

Arithmetic and configuration behaviour are checked by the existing repository tests. Physical validation of this circular-lug screen is not established by those checks.

The existing workflow tests compare the example load, governing utilization and nominal stresses with hand-calculated expressions. They also exercise missing and invalid skew factors, unsupported sling counts, and below-minimum factors with otherwise passing strength results. Those tests demonstrate agreement with the coded formulas and the intended input guards.

The report rebuild tests compare every displayed example field with independent hand-calculated literals, compare all plotted cases with hand arithmetic and public-interface results, and check the rounded-down restriction and the next load increment. The report and change data use explicit LF line endings. Byte comparisons are checked under native text defaults and emulated Windows text defaults; a native Windows rerun remains required. No field-load measurements or destructive-test results are supplied with the workflow example. The wider repository contains finite-element padeye material, but no matched comparison to this example is established here; no numerical agreement with it is claimed.

8 Key Findings, Limits and Recommendations

The example is governed by the implemented shear tear-out limit, while evidence for design acceptance remains incomplete. The following actions are tied to the calculation and the limitations in the example guide.

  • Hold the example from release. Restrict the most-loaded leg static tension to 361.4 kN at the unchanged dynamic and skew factors for preliminary screening only. Obtain a complete connection assessment before treating any passing screen as an operating permission.
  • If the entered tension is retained, assess an increased participating thickness or loaded ligament, then repeat the strength, pin, weld and parent-plate checks. Cheek participation requires a load-transfer assessment.
  • Confirm the material, tolerances, effective thickness, extreme-centre-of-gravity tension and amplification basis. Confirm the governing standard and edition, including skew scope, before selecting design criteria.
  • Provide separate checks for out-of-plane bending, pin bending and clearance effects, welds, parent structure, fatigue and fracture. These resistances are not returned by the current module.
  • Validate positive finite geometry, force and material inputs before direct function use. The current low-level functions do not comprehensively reject non-finite values, non-positive pin diameter or negative design force.

A proposed next development slice is a single documented finite-input contract shared by the public functions and configuration interface, followed by an independent circular-lug benchmark with an explicit design-code basis. Until that evidence is available, design-code applicability and physical validation remain NOT_EVALUATED.

9 References and Use

The calculation basis is bounded by publicly inspectable repository records. An implementation citation establishes what is computed; it does not establish an external standard's requirements.

9.1 Inputs, outputs and interpretation

The documented numerical load helper accepts static leg force, dynamic amplification and skew factor. The numerical strength helper accepts design force, total thickness, pin and hole diameters, outer radius and yield strength, and returns named demand, allowable, utilization and pass fields. The configuration interface accepts main and cheek thicknesses separately, requires an explicit skew factor, optionally enforces a sling-count floor, and returns the governing result and overall screening status.

The configuration interface writes a JavaScript Object Notation summary to the requested directory. Its payload includes input and governing skew factors, the minimum and source, the below-minimum flag, design load, total thickness, component checks and the governing utilization. The returned summary path is added after writing, so that path is absent from the file itself. The text label naming standards in the payload is not evidence of design compliance.

9.2 Source register and reproducibility locators
