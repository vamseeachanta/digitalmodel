1 Executive summary

The riser reference database is a catalogue of configuration geometry, stack-up references, rig-interface references and design-criteria citations. Analysis is performed to determine its use as an engineering input source, and the key results are summarized below.

  • Public configuration geometry can be retrieved and compared with its documented source.
    • The public drilling-riser example returns a water depth of 8,202 ft, an outer diameter of 21.00 in and a wall thickness of 0.874 in. The largest difference from its source is 0.0 % before display rounding.
  • A drilling or connected operating envelope is not established by these records.
    • Tension, joint travel, host motions and environmental loading are not calculated by the database. Do not connect on the basis of catalogue geometry.

2 Introduction

The database interface and its engineering boundary are described in this section. Riser analysis is addressed through the suitability of reference inputs, rather than through a response calculation.

The main workflow is the retrieval of a public drilling-riser configuration from the configuration catalogue. Its water depth is an illustrative input, not a maximum supported depth or a demonstrated equipment rating. The public software example is treated as assumed geometry for design use.

Reference handles for historical stack-ups and rig interfaces are included in the inventory. Their underlying private records, hardware capability values and fatigue-curve parameters are not reproduced. Public source and implementation records are listed in Section 9.2.

3 Design data

The available input data and missing physical basis are identified in this section. Data status is retained separately from the existence of a catalogue record.

The lookup inputs are a database directory and an exact configuration key. The returned record contains the configuration identifier, riser type, source locator, water depth, outer diameter, nominal wall thickness, material-grade label, analysis-kind label and notes. Labels describe the source configuration; they do not establish material certification or completion of the named analysis.

3.1 Field, site and host

The public example is used without attribution to a field, site or host. A site-specific water-depth datum, host offset limit and host motion response amplitude operators are not supplied by the catalogue lookup.

3.2 Riser and host interface

The overall geometry is represented by the schematic in Figure 2. The generic arrangement identifies missing interfaces and is not a joint-by-joint assembly definition.

3.3 Contents, pressure and metocean

Contents and motion-related fields are present in the source example, but they are not returned as configuration-table fields. Pressure, mud-weight cases, site current profiles, wave spectra and host motion operators are not established by this database example.

3.4 Criteria and load-case basis

The reference-data target benchmark is exact agreement with the public source in the catalogue's native units. It is used because catalogue generation is a unit conversion and transcription task; no engineering response is approximated in that comparison.

Unknown keys are required to raise a lookup error, and missing numeric cells are required to remain missing. These requirements follow the documented loader contract in Section 9.2; they prevent an unsupported match or missing measurement from becoming an input.

The comparison contains the public configuration retrieval only. A physical load-case matrix is not evaluated: site, host, operating-mode and component criteria are required before still-water, current-dominated or wave-dominated cases are defined.

4 Analysis methodology

The reference-data calculation and the relationship to downstream riser methods are described in this section. The database is called directly; no structural or hydrodynamic response calculation is substituted for it.

4.1 Reference-data workflow

The public interface loads the known tables after checking their file fingerprints, column layouts and record keys. A configuration is requested by its exact key, and a typed record is returned. A missing or malformed table prevents that load from completing.

Source example → catalogue generation → manifest-bound tables → database lookup → engineering input selection. The public example is read independently and compared with the returned record before report quantities are formatted.

Length conversion is expressed as length in feet equals length in metres divided by metres per foot. Diameter and wall thickness in inches equal the returned millimetres divided by millimetres per inch. Exact conversion definitions are taken from the seed specification and checked against the public units reference.

Percentage difference = 100 × (returned value − source value) ÷ source value.

The source value is the reference for each comparison. The percentage scale is a mathematical definition, and zero difference is the data-agreement target. No physical acceptance coefficient is assumed.

4.2 Meaning of the criteria references

The getter interfaces identify fatigue, stress and joint-angle criteria with citation metadata. Their conceptual relations are described below from the interface documentation; the relations are not evaluated in this example.

Dallow = 1 / FD; Δσlocal = Kσ Δσnominal; σallow = fy Sy.

Allowable fatigue damage Dallow is related to design fatigue factor FD. Local stress range Δσlocal is nominal stress range Δσnominal multiplied by stress concentration factor Kσ. Allowable equivalent stress σallow is specified minimum yield strength Sy multiplied by design factor fy. The getter citation editions and clause titles are identified in Section 9.1.

A direct getter validates its citation record and fails if the required record cannot be resolved. Citation resolution establishes the metadata contract used by the software; it does not independently establish the correctness or applicability of a licensed criterion. Numeric criteria and licensed table values are not published here.

4.3 Physical analysis sequence

A physical riser assessment is bounded by the missing data in Appendix A. The first calculation is a still-water weight, buoyancy and tension balance against an independent hand calculation; current and then waves with current follow only after that basis is established.

Dynamic analysis requires the actual host motion operators, wave directions and irregular-sea realisations. A geometry record or prescribed regular heave alone does not establish dynamic response. Centralisation, lateral supports, travel stops and buoyancy zones remain undefined in the schematic.

5.2 Validation evidence held

Automated repository checks are provided for typed lookup, exact-key rejection, missing files, changed fingerprints, column changes, duplicate keys and file-size bounds. Additional checks cover catalogue source coverage, record joins, reference-consumer compatibility, citation metadata, override behaviour and edition consistency.

The report comparison adds direct source-to-record agreement and retention of missing depth. The checks and their scope are identified in Section 9.2 and the evaluated comparison criteria are shown in Appendix B. These records establish software and data-contract evidence; they do not provide a measured or solver-derived physical benchmark for this configuration.

5.3 Physical response status

The physical response quantities are not returned by the database. Their evaluation status is retained below to prevent catalogue coverage from being interpreted as an operating envelope.

  • Still-water tension and buoyancy: not evaluated; data needed: joint tally, component weights, contents and buoyancy.
  • Statics with current and operating envelopes: not evaluated; data needed: environmental profiles, component criteria and host offsets.
  • Dynamics, stroke and angles: not evaluated; data needed: host motion operators, component stiffness, travel limits and wave basis.
  • Drift-off: not evaluated; data needed: watch circle, current basis and loss-of-position response. Do not connect on this basis.
  • Recoil: not evaluated; data needed: closure curves.
  • Weak point analysis: not performed.
  • Vortex-induced vibration and fatigue: not evaluated; data needed: stress histories, hydrodynamic basis, detail geometry and qualified fatigue criteria.

6 Key findings

The engineering use of the demonstrated records is summarised in this section. Conclusions are limited to the public lookup and its stated comparison criterion.

  • Configuration geometry is retrievable through a documented public interface.
    • The worked comparison meets exact agreement with the public source in native catalogue units.
  • Reference availability and physical suitability remain separate findings.
    • Stack-up and rig records contain reference handles; they are not a qualified component tally or proof of tensioner capability.
  • Physical connection and drilling limits are not established.
    • No allowable offset, sea-state boundary, effective-tension profile or travel margin is calculated by the demonstrated interface.

7 Limitations

The data and calculation boundaries are stated in this section. Acceptance for a physical design is conditioned on the evidence identified in the data register.

A matching file fingerprint establishes consistency with the supplied manifest, not trusted authorship, freshness or physical plausibility. Numeric conversion does not apply universal finiteness, sign, range or unit checks; those checks remain necessary at the engineering input boundary.

The database does not supply a selected host, complete joint stack-up, code-qualified material strength, site loading or calculated response. The public configuration is a software example, not a measured installation or an independently benchmarked physical case.

The default database location is relative to a source checkout. Availability through a packaged installation is not established; a caller can supply an explicit database directory. The helper that aggregates citations can return an empty mapping with a warning when resolution fails; an empty citation mapping is not authority to accept a criterion.

7.1 Not covered

The other catalogue families and private reference resolution are outside the worked workflow. These include flexible, steel catenary, steel lazy-wave, top-tensioned and lift-screen configurations, detailed stack-up-to-rig selection, licensed fatigue parameters and mode-specific integrity criteria.

8 Recommendations

The next engineering actions are identified in this section. Their priority follows the difference between reference retrieval and physical operating qualification.

  • Use the demonstrated interface for controlled reference-data retrieval.
    • Retain the source record and units, check the manifest, and reject unknown keys or missing required quantities before a calculation, following the documented loader contract.
  • Establish a physical basis before a connection decision.
    • Obtain the host-specific motion operators, watch circle, component tally, weights, buoyancy, contents, stiffness and joint travel limits listed in Appendix A. Do not connect on the basis of this catalogue example.
  • Qualify criteria at their intended point of use.
    • Resolve each required citation, establish edition and clause applicability, and reject a missing criterion. The direct getter contract and its resolution checks are identified in Section 9.2.
  • Establish an independent physical benchmark before reporting envelopes.
    • Compare still-water weight, buoyancy and tension with hand calculations, then introduce current and host-driven dynamics as set out in Section 4. Retain demand, limit and governing case for each physical result.

9 References

The public units basis and repository evidence are identified in this section. Standards identifiers are retained to explain the citation interfaces; their numeric criteria are not adopted for a physical calculation.

9.1 Public units and criteria descriptions

National Institute of Standards and Technology, Special Publication 811, Guide for the Use of the International System of Units, 2008 edition, Appendix B.3, Use of conversion factors, and Appendix B.8, Factors for Units Listed Alphabetically, entries foot and inch. Public conversion definitions support the display units; no physical design allowance is derived from this reference.

The getter documentation identifies DNV-OS-F201, Dynamic Risers, 2010 edition, Section 5, design fatigue factors; DNV-RP-C203, Fatigue Design of Offshore Steel Structures, 2021 edition, Section 2, stress concentration factors; API RP 16Q, Design, Selection, Operation and Maintenance of Marine Drilling Riser Systems, first edition, 1993, Section 3.3, tension factors; and API STD 2RD, Dynamic Risers for Floating Production Systems, second edition, 2013, Section 5, dynamic riser design criteria. These are implementation citation descriptions, not independent clause-content verification. The implemented edition labels are checked against repository records; they are not assertions about the latest available editions.
