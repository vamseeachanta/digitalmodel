    1. Executive Summary

    The reservoir module is a static oil-volume calculator and well-log display package. Analysis is performed to determine oil initially in place and the change in recoverable volume for specified recovery fractions; key results are summarized below.

    • The public teaching example supports calculation review.
      • Oil initially in place is 42,898,636.8 stock-tank barrels. This is the physical upper bound for recovery from the stated volume.
      • Recoverable volume is 9,437,700.1 stock-tank barrels at the stated primary recovery fraction and 12,869,591.0 stock-tank barrels at the raised fraction.
      • The increase is 3,431,890.9 stock-tank barrels; the recovery fractions are scenario inputs rather than demonstrated reservoir performance.
    • Field use remains conditional on evidence.
      • Arithmetic regression evidence is available. Field accuracy, a commercial reserves classification and a production schedule are not established.
      • Confirm mapped pay, fluid data and a recovery basis before applying a recovery scenario to a development decision.

    Source: the retained teaching example, current scalar calculation and open reserves reference; see Detailed Results and References.

    2. Introduction

    The public scalar workflow is assessed for a reservoir engineer reviewing an early volumetric estimate. The retained teaching problem is used to demonstrate the callable interface with a documented numerical basis.

    The original teaching page is unavailable during source checking. Its published repository brief and matching regression fixture are used as the retained example record; independent confirmation of the original statement is not established. The open reserves reference supports the equations rather than the example inputs.

    The assessment covers a calculation, not an actual field. No measured reservoir inputs or licensed calculation programs are used.

    3. System and Workflow

    The original oil volume is calculated from mapped area, net pay, porosity, initial oil saturation and the initial oil formation volume factor. Recovery scenarios are applied to that stock-tank volume.

    The public input record is named VolumetricInputs. The callable functions ooip_stb, recoverable_stb and incremental_recovery_stb return scalar stock-tank volumes. A stock-tank barrel is abbreviated STB in equations and chart units.

    Mapped area and net pay describe the bulk rock volume. Porosity identifies the pore fraction, oil saturation identifies the oil-filled pore fraction, and the formation volume factor relates reservoir barrels to stock-tank barrels. No pressure distribution, transmissibility, flow boundary or computational mesh is solved.

    3.1 Not covered

    The stratigraphic display interface creates gamma-ray, resistivity, density-neutron and facies tracks aligned on formation tops. Its available checks use synthetic data and mocked plotting objects. Geological correlation accuracy, fixed facies colour identity and rendered appearance are not established by those checks.

    Material balance, pressure-transient analysis, production forecasting and the separately packaged reservoir framework are outside this calculation. Broader historical examples request package exports that are absent from the current public initializer; those examples are not treated as executable evidence.

    4. Data and Basis

    The retained teaching brief supplies the input basis. Arithmetic is evaluated in field units; the oil formation volume factor is supplied at the same initial reservoir condition as the saturation estimate.

    The example area is 1,200.0 acres and net pay is 40.000 ft. Porosity is 0.18 and initial oil saturation is 0.80, both fractions. The initial oil formation volume factor is 1.25 reservoir barrels per stock-tank barrel. Primary and raised recovery fractions are 0.22 and 0.30. Source: retained teaching brief, Given table; input lineage is recorded in the appendix.

    4.1 Theory and criteria

    N = C A h φ So / Boi; V(R) = N R; ΔV = N(Rraised − Rprimary).

    N is original oil in place (STB), C is the acre-foot conversion coefficient (barrels per acre-foot), A is area (acres), h is net pay (ft), φ is porosity (fraction), So is initial oil saturation (fraction), Boi is initial oil formation volume factor (reservoir barrels per STB), R is recovery fraction, V is recoverable volume (STB), and ΔV is incremental recoverable volume (STB).

    The implemented conversion coefficient is 7,758.0 barrels per acre-foot. Its stated precision and the volumetric and recovery equations match the open reserves reference, Volumetric estimation subsection, revision identified in References. The increment equation is the difference of the two recovery-volume calculations.

    Finite nonnegative area and pay, fractions between zero and unity, and a positive formation volume factor define the caller input domain. For a physical recovery fraction, the recoverable volume is bounded by the original oil in place; this follows directly from its definition. It is not a recovery-performance acceptance criterion. The scalar module does not enforce these checks.

    Uniform effective properties are assumed by the scalar representation. A homogeneous depiction is marked ASSUMED - to be confirmed for field use. A commercial recovery target, uncertainty range and field validation criterion are NOT_EVALUATED because no field basis is supplied.

    5. Detailed Results

    The documented teaching inputs are passed to the public volumetric functions. Each displayed example result is populated from the returned scalar rather than entered as report text.

    Original oil in place is 42,898,636.8 STB. The primary scenario returns 9,437,700.1 STB at recovery fraction 0.22. The raised scenario returns 12,869,591.0 STB at recovery fraction 0.30. Incremental recovery is 3,431,890.9 STB.

    Both scenarios lie below the oil-in-place bound of 42,898,636.8 STB. The difference is the additional volume implied by the selected fractions, with no time or cost assigned. Source: retained teaching inputs and the scalar volumetric functions; the matching regression cases are recorded in the appendix.

    6. Recovery Sensitivity

    The recoverable volume is varied against recovery fraction while the teaching oil volume is held fixed. Every sampled volume is calculated through the public recovery function.

    The straight line expresses multiplication by a selected fraction. No sweep efficiency, relative permeability, well spacing or depletion history is inferred from it. The chosen recovery scenarios require a separate reservoir-performance basis before field adoption.

    7. Validation Evidence

    Current repository checks are used to assess arithmetic and interface behaviour. The recorded execution contains 23 passing cases and 30 skipped cases, with no failed cases or collection errors; the complete scoped outcome record is identified in the reference register.

    The volumetric checks cover a known-volume product, the direction of formation-volume-factor sensitivity, recovery multiplication and recovery increments. The teaching example checks oil in place and both primary and incremental recovery. Comparison is made to the repository's expected arithmetic values; it does not measure agreement against a field survey or an independent reservoir simulation.

    Plotting checks use mocked graphics. Historical framework cases are skipped because the required exports are absent from the package initializer. Skipped cases provide no evidence for production forecasting or material balance.

    No measured reservoir benchmark is present in the scoped evidence set. Field validation is NOT_EVALUATED. The documentation rebuild checks bind displayed values and chart samples to current module calls; they establish consistency of this report, not geological accuracy.

    8. Limits and Recommendations

    The scalar model is limited to a supplied static oil-bearing volume. Recovery fractions are user inputs; field performance and commercial classification are not calculated.

    8.1 Limits of use

    Heterogeneity, connected pay, free gas, fluid-property variation, aquifer support and time-dependent recovery are not resolved. A single effective saturation is insufficient to establish connected movable oil. Invalid fractions, nonfinite values or a nonpositive divisor are not rejected by the module; the calculation can return an unphysical value or raise an arithmetic error.

    The public worked example demonstrates the arithmetic for its supplied inputs only. Uncertainty, production rates, operating constraints and commercial viability are NOT_EVALUATED. The input source is a retained teaching brief, with the original online statement unavailable.

    8.2 Recommendations

    Before field use, establish the mapped connected pay, representative porosity and saturation, and laboratory fluid-volume basis. Confirm the recovery fraction against reservoir-drive evidence and performance data. These actions follow the open reference's distinction between volumetric inventory and recovery efficiency.

    Add caller validation for finite values, physical fractions and a positive divisor before using the scalar result in an engineering decision. Reject an incremental-recovery claim when its raised fraction is below the starting fraction. These recommendations follow the implemented arithmetic and fraction definition.

    A proposed first extension is a validated input wrapper with boundary tests and a documented geological data register. Retain the current nominal arithmetic regression cases. Qualify recovery and stratigraphic interpretation separately against evidence suited to their intended use.
