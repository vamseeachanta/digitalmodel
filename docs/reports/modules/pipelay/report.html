Pipelay module analysis

Draft for client review

1. Executive Summary

Pipelay is an analytical pipeline installation screening module. Analysis is performed to determine static suspended geometry, bending strain and assumed lay-rate restrictions; key results are summarized below.

  • The public J-lay example meets the assumed sagbend strain limit.
    • Strain utilisation is 65.1 % against the assumed limit of 3000.0 µm/m. The example is suitable for reproducing the static screen, not for authorising installation.
  • The public reel-lay example meets its assumed core strain limit.
    • Strain utilisation is 85.3 % against the assumed limit of 20000.0 µm/m. The straightener response remains an input assumption.

Vessel orientation, station-keeping tolerance and an installation sea-state envelope are NOT_EVALUATED. The assumed joining cutoff of 2.5 m significant wave height does not establish a safe operating limit. Public J-lay example; Public reel-lay example; Analytical basis.

2. Introduction

The supported workflow is defined as static J-lay and reel-lay screening. The intended use is preliminary input checking and transparent reproduction of analytical quantities before a separately qualified installation analysis.

The public router accepts a configuration mapping, selects the lay method, rejects invalid design inputs, writes a strict JavaScript Object Notation (JSON) summary and returns the augmented mapping. The direct calculation functions return result mappings without writing files. Implemented interface.

J-lay geometry is solved using depth and the angle from vertical. Supplied top tension is a consistency check, not an independent means of tuning curvature at a fixed depth and angle. Reel-lay screening uses the innermost wrap radius and a prescribed fraction of curvature removal. Analytical basis.

3. Summary

The detailed screening controls are presented as strain utilisation and assumed wave-height cutoffs. A combined installation envelope is not established because period, heading and dynamic component demands are absent.

The J-lay rate is controlled by the lower of the supplied laying and joining wave-height limits. The cutoff is 2.5 m; equality is permitted by the rate rule. Abandonment and recovery capacity is not assessed by that rule. Analytical basis; Public J-lay example.

A live forecast, wave-period envelope, orientation tolerance and forecast-support trigger are NOT_EVALUATED. Operating instructions require a qualified vessel response model and approved criteria before a forecast service or heading restriction is specified. Evidence register.

4. Design Data

The design basis is taken from the repository’s public illustrative inputs. Pipe contents are excluded, and all material values and selected limits remain ASSUMED - to be confirmed for an installation.

4.1 Physical system and inputs

Lengths are supplied in metres, force in kilonewtons, density in kilograms per cubic metre and strain as a dimensionless fraction. Report strains are expressed in micrometres per metre. The complete pipe data register is retained in the appendix. Vessel, crane, rigging and response-amplitude data are not supplied. Public J-lay example; Public reel-lay example.

4.2 Criteria and rationale

The strain screen compares computed bending strain with the explicitly supplied example limit. The reference overbend radius uses the supplied strain limit of 2000.0 µm/m; it is a geometric reference, not a model of a fitted stinger. Public J-lay example; Analytical basis.

J-lay tension closure is checked using the returned relative tolerance of 0.1 %. This input-consistency check prevents a depth, angle and force combination that violates the assumed static equilibrium. Finite positive submerged weight and a nonzero near-vertical angle are required. Input plausibility bounds detect unit mistakes; they are not design allowables. Analytical basis.

No standard-derived acceptance limit is applied. The implementation’s inherited standards references lack qualified editions and clauses for this use; standards compliance is NOT_EVALUATED. Evidence register.

5. Methodology

The geometry is derived from static equilibrium of an inextensible flexible line with uniform submerged weight and a horizontal touchdown tangent. Bending strain is then estimated from the catenary curvature.

Let D be steel outside diameter, t steel wall thickness, c radial coating thickness, and Dᵢ and D꜀ the bore and coated diameters. Areas Aₛ, A꜀ and A𝑑 denote steel, coating and displaced cross-section. Densities ρₛ, ρ꜀ and ρ𝑤 denote steel, coating and water; g is the gravitational acceleration convention retained by the implementation.

Dᵢ = D − 2t; D꜀ = D + 2c; Aₛ = π(D² − Dᵢ²)/4;
A꜀ = π(D꜀² − D²)/4; A𝑑 = πD꜀²/4; w = g(ρₛAₛ + ρ꜀A꜀ − ρ𝑤A𝑑).

The annular areas and empty-pipe buoyancy expression are defined by the analytical basis. Its gravity convention is retained without a new environmental assumption. Analytical basis.

Let h be water depth, θ top angle from vertical, T top resultant force, H horizontal force, a catenary parameter, x horizontal touchdown offset, z height above seabed and s suspended arc length. Consistent force units are used.

H = T sin θ; T = H + wh; a = H/w = h sin θ/(1 − sin θ);
z = a[cosh(x/a) − 1]; x = a acosh(1 + h/a); s = √(h² + 2ah).

Let κ be touchdown curvature, ε bending strain, E Young’s modulus and σ the elastic stress proxy. The minimum reference radius Rₘᵢₙ follows from an assumed maximum strain εₘₐₓ.

κ = 1/a; ε = Dκ/2; σ = Eε; Rₘᵢₙ = D/(2εₘₐₓ).

These equations and their numerical constants are geometric identities derived in the analytical basis, not values copied from a licensed standard. Axial extension, flexural stiffness in equilibrium, contents pressure, contact, hydrodynamic forcing and material plasticity are excluded. The elastic proxy is not a combined stress check. Analytical basis.

For reel-lay, let R be core radius, η the assumed fraction of curvature removed, κᵣ residual curvature, L the measurement span and δ its small-slope midspan offset.

εᵣₑₑₗ = D/(2R); κᵣ = (1 − η)/R; εᵣ = Dκᵣ/2; δ ≈ κᵣL²/8.

The straightener fraction is prescribed rather than predicted from cyclic material response. Perfect removal produces zero curvature and an unbounded residual radius encoded as null in JSON. Analytical basis.

For the assumed rate rule, Hₛ denotes significant wave height, v₀ calm-water rate, b degradation per unit wave height and Hₛ,ₗᵢₘ the supplied cutoff. The rate is zero above the cutoff; otherwise v = max(0, v₀ − bHₛ). J-lay uses the lower laying and joining cutoff; reel-lay uses the laying cutoff. Analytical basis.

6. Detailed Results

The example results are obtained by calling the public router on the documented input files. Only the output destination is redirected; engineering inputs are retained.

J-lay worked example

The calculated radius and top tension follow from the supplied depth and angle. The detailed values are retained in the appendix using the module’s returned precision and engineering display units.

At depth 1500.0 m and top angle 3.0 degrees from vertical, the top tension is 2624.9 kN and the touchdown radius is 82.8 m. Touchdown offset is 301.7 m and suspended length is 1580.7 m. Public J-lay example; Analytical basis.

Bending strain is 1954.4 µm/m against the assumed limit of 3000.0 µm/m; utilisation is 65.1 %. The elastic bending stress proxy is 404.6 MPa. Public J-lay example; Analytical basis.

The sagbend strain screen passes against the assumed limit. The supplied tension closes within the input-consistency tolerance. The elastic stress proxy is below the example’s specified minimum yield strength of 448.0 MPa; this observation does not assess axial stress, local buckling or installation acceptance. Analytical basis; Public J-lay example.

Reel-lay example

The core strain is 17069.0 µm/m against the assumed limit of 20000.0 µm/m. The residual span offset is 112.5 mm over the supplied span of 12.0 m. The full component values are retained in the appendix. Public reel-lay example; Analytical basis.

The core strain screen passes against the assumed plastic-strain limit. The residual offset is calculated using the supplied straightener fraction and span; no residual-offset acceptance criterion is supplied, so its engineering acceptance is NOT_EVALUATED. Analytical basis; Public reel-lay example.

7. Validation

Repository evidence is provided by independent arithmetic anchors and analytical regression assertions. These records check implementation behaviour; field validation and native installation qualification are NOT_EVALUATED.

The documented anchors cover submerged weight, catenary parameter, top tension, strain, offset and suspended length. Reel checks cover core strain, residual curvature, radius, strain and span offset. The anchor comparison uses the analytical basis’s relative tolerance, equal to the tension-closure tolerance displayed in the design basis. Analytical basis; Regression evidence.

Boundary tests reject unsupported methods, missing inputs, unknown keys, impossible annuli, non-finite values, non-positive submerged weight and inconsistent force closure before summary output. Regression assertions also cover perfect straightening, unrounded strain acceptance, the joining cutoff and strict summary readback. Regression evidence.

The report rebuild test compares all displayed numerical bindings with fresh router returns, checks that worked-example numerical text has a binding and compares the committed HTML with a deterministic rebuild. These checks establish reproducibility of the presentation, not independent physical qualification. Regression evidence.

8. Key Findings, Limitations and Recommendations

The public examples meet the selected strain screens under the stated assumptions. Engineering release is restricted by missing operating conditions, acceptance criteria and independent installation evidence.

Use the J-lay calculation to check static input consistency and preliminary curvature. Confirm pipe contents, coating tolerances, material certificates and the governing edition-qualified criteria before extending the screen to an installation assessment. Obtain a qualified response analysis for vessel motion, waves, current, axial load, touchdown contact, local buckling and fatigue. The evidence register identifies these installation-evidence gaps. Evidence register.

For an exceeded J-lay strain criterion, increase the touchdown radius by selecting a consistent depth, angle and force combination, then reassess the full installation model. For an exceeded reel criterion, increase the core radius or revise the pipe selection against qualified material limits. These actions follow the inverse radius relation for strain; a passing screen alone does not release the installation. Analytical basis.

Obtain measured or qualified straightener response and an approved residual-offset criterion before judging reel-lay residual curvature. Retain the rate cutoffs as planning assumptions until joined-up vessel and joining constraints are demonstrated; no forecast-support sea state is assigned. Analytical basis; Evidence register.

Not covered

Supporting S-lay stress estimates and departure-angle tables, native model generation, dynamic response, diffraction, fatigue, cyclic plasticity, ovalisation, abandonment and recovery, station keeping and forecast services are outside this analytical workflow report. The supporting S-lay depth-table regression retains an expected failure because its angle expression is independent of depth. Restrict that table to non-design inspection until a depth-dependent formulation and independent reference cases are provided. Regression evidence; Evidence register.
