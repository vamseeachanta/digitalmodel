1. Executive Summary

Floating wind concept screening is a preliminary calculation of buoyancy, intact stability, rigid-body natural periods and steady mooring offset. Analysis is performed to determine which parametric concepts meet the supplied screening limits; key results are summarized below.

  • A shortlist is obtained from the public sizing example.
    • 7 of 12 variants meet all supplied proxy limits, including the minimum metacentric height and dynamic amplification limit. This result permits further concept assessment; design acceptance is not established.
  • The principal semi-submersible study is screened against the supplied load cases.
    • 4 of 6 semi-submersibles pass. The same scope and acceptance qualifications are retained in the detailed results.
  • An installation decision is not established.
    • Heading tolerance, significant-wave-height operating limits and forecast triggers are not evaluated. The motion proxy does not use wave height, and no installation system is represented.

2. Introduction

The module is used to reduce a set of floating-wind concepts to candidates for further analysis. The calculation is separated from the evidence needed to accept a particular structure.

Semi-submersible, spar, tension-leg and barge archetypes are represented by parametric models. Displacement and ballast are determined at a specified draft; small-angle stability and uncoupled heave and pitch periods are then calculated. Variant grids, optional linear mooring offset, trade-space ranking and standalone concept data sheets are provided. Source: Floater equations and assumptions, Screening equations and acceptance logic and Trade-space implementation.

The main workflow in this report is the documented public repository sizing example. Its semi-submersible sweep is treated in detail; the accompanying spar and barge cases are retained for coverage. These are generic example geometries and assumed loading, rather than a reconstruction of a published reference support platform. Source: Public sizing example.

Not covered

Cost sensitivities, reliability and availability adjustments, scaling, learning, collaboration and maturity scoring are available as separate overlays. They are not applied to the physical shortlist in this report. Tendon sizing, fatigue, ultimate strength, dynamic cables, aerodynamic control, installation rigging and coupled wave response are not evaluated here. Source: Package capability exports, Economic calculation and Maturity scoring.

4. Design Data

Geometry, topside mass and site cases are read from the public sizing example. All example design inputs are classified as ASSUMED - to be confirmed; their adoption for a real structure is not established. The complete data register is provided in the appendix. Source: Public sizing example.

Physical model and boundary conditions

Columns are treated as identical cylinders, evenly spaced on a ring above a submerged pontoon volume. The keel is the vertical datum and the still-water plane is at the specified draft. Vertical hydrostatic restoring is calculated from waterplane area. Pitch inertia is assembled from lumped steel, ballast and topside masses with a horizontal spread term. A linear horizontal mooring spring is used only for steady offset; it is not coupled into the vertical natural periods. No mesh is used in the closed-form screen. Source: Floater equations and assumptions and Screening equations and acceptance logic.

Inputs and outputs

A topside mass model, a non-empty load-case list, archetype geometry, sweep values and screening criteria are supplied. Dimensions and masses are positive; pontoon height is less than draft. The example uses a full-factorial combination of the listed sweep values. Additional input guards and assumed defaults are identified in the data register. Source: Floater equations and assumptions and Screening equations and acceptance logic.

The screen returns properties, component demand and limit records, feasibility and an overall verdict for every variant. The sizing workflow returns concept and variant counts plus a passing shortlist, ordered by the returned governing margin; optional JSON output contains that summary. Rejected variants are available from the full screen result, rather than the shortlist summary. No physical response history or line-tension time series is returned. Source: Screening equations and acceptance logic and Public sizing workflow.

Criteria and rationale

The metacentric-height floor identifies weak initial restoring; the motion-amplification ceiling screens proximity to resonance. The excluded period band screens overlap with the assumed energetic wave band, and the offset ceiling screens a steady watch circle. Their numeric values are example inputs; standard compliance, damaged stability, heel limits, freeboard and line strength are not established. Source: Public sizing example and Screening equations and acceptance logic.

Sea-water density is 1,025.0 kg/m³ and gravitational acceleration is 9.80665 m/s². These are implementation constants; environmental calibration is not supplied. Added-mass, centroid and damping coefficients remain assumed model inputs rather than measured properties. Source: Floater equations and assumptions.

5. Methodology

The main equations are applied to each geometry and then screened across the supplied load cases. The formulae below describe the implemented model; the source records are linked at each step.

Hydrostatics and mass balance

A = πD²/4; V = nA(T − h) + Vₚ; Aw = nA
Iw = nπD⁴/64 + nAR²/2; BM = Iw/V; GM = KB + BM − KG
KB = [Vₚh/2 + nA(T − h)(T + h)/2]/V
m = ρV; mb = m − ms − mt; KG = Σmᵢzᵢ/Σmᵢ

The column count is n, column diameter is D, column radius is R, draft is T, pontoon height is h, and submerged pontoon volume is Vₚ. V is displaced volume, A is one column area, Aw is waterplane area and Iw is its second moment about a horizontal centroidal axis. KB, BM, KG and GM are centre of buoyancy, metacentric radius, centre of gravity and metacentric height above the keel as applicable. Density is ρ. Displaced mass is m; ballast, steel and topside masses are mb, ms and mt. A component centroid is zᵢ. The summation symbol denotes addition over all mass components. Source: Floater equations and assumptions.

Negative required ballast is clamped to zero and the concept is marked infeasible. The topside centroid is a mass-weighted rotor and nacelle at hub height plus a uniform tower at its midpoint. Ballast is placed at a fixed low fraction of draft, and the steel centroid is a specified fraction of draft. Free-surface ballast effects are not included. Source: Floater equations and assumptions.

Natural periods and motion proxy

Th = 2π√[m(1 + ch)/(ρgAw)]
Ip = Σmᵢ(zᵢ − KG)² + mR²/2
Tp,n = 2π√[Ip(1 + cp)/(ρgVGM)]
r = Tn/Tw; Q = 1/√[(1 − r²)² + (2ζr)²]

Heave and pitch natural periods are Th and Tp,n. Pitch inertia is Ip; ch and cp are added-mass and added-inertia ratios; g is gravitational acceleration. The forcing-to-natural frequency ratio is r, natural period is Tn, peak wave period is Tw, damping ratio is ζ and dimensionless dynamic amplification is Q. The motion check uses the largest heave or pitch amplification across all load cases. Wave height is stored but is not used in this calculation. Source: Floater equations and assumptions and Screening equations and acceptance logic.

The inertia expression above applies to the semi-submersible. The spar and barge use their own horizontal mass-spread terms. A non-positive metacentric height produces an infinite pitch period; the motion proxy returns a finite placeholder for non-finite periods, so feasibility and stability are assessed separately. Tendon-stabilised periods require a separate stiffness basis. Source: Floater equations and assumptions and Screening equations and acceptance logic.

Offset and aggregation

x = F/k; acceptance = feasible AND all applicable checks pass

Steady offset is x, steady horizontal load is F and linear horizontal stiffness is k. The mooring check is omitted when either stiffness or allowable offset is absent. The current example supplies both. Motion and offset retain the worst load-case result; stability and modal checks are independent of the load-case label. Source: Screening equations and acceptance logic.

The returned governing check is selected using the smallest raw signed margin. Metres, seconds and dimensionless margins are mixed by that implementation; the reported governing label is not a physically normalized utilization ranking. Each demand and limit is therefore presented separately. Source: Screening equations and acceptance logic.

6. Detailed Results

The configured proxy limits are applied to every example variant. The original example geometry is retained as the worked semi-submersible case; no input is tuned to obtain a passing result.

Worked semi-submersible

At the original example geometry, displacement is 15,155.7 t, required ballast is 8,875.7 t and metacentric height is 17.4 m. Heave and pitch natural periods are 18.0 s and 33.6 s. The maximum motion amplification is 1.5 against a limit of 2.0; steady offset is 7.5 m against 30.0 m. Source: Public sizing example and Floater equations and assumptions.

The complete semi-submersible sweep returns 4 passes from 6 variants. The rejected variants fail the motion proxy at the supplied longer-period sea state. They are excluded from the shortlist pending an independent response calculation; geometry and mass distribution are re-assessed if the motion limit remains exceeded. Source: Screening equations and acceptance logic and the per-variant appendix.

7. Validation

Repository evidence is classified by the quantity actually checked. Algebraic identities and regression tests are distinguished from independent validation of a complete floating structure.

The existing floater tests check mass closure, the metacentric-height identity, the circular-waterplane metacentric radius, unstable and infeasible cases, geometry rejection and broad period plausibility. The screening tests check the optional linear offset, modal rejection and verdict aggregation. The trade-space and reporting tests exercise ranking and rendering interfaces. Source: Existing module tests.

For the spar, the independent circular-waterplane reduction BM = D²/(16T) is compared with the returned metacentric radius in the existing tests. For offset, the independent static spring relation x = F/k is checked. These comparisons address algebraic quantities; the reference values are not model-test or field measurements. Source: Existing module tests.

The committed public sizing example is a reproducible software example. Its geometry and coefficients are not established as an exact published support-platform definition. The inspected floater tests check algebraic relationships and plausible ranges; independent hydrodynamic and coupled-response comparison is not established by those tests. No new independent benchmark or measurement is introduced by this report. Source: Public sizing example, Existing module tests and Solver preparation implementation.

The report rebuild test independently calls the public screen and sizing workflow, compares every registered example value, checks all displayed rounding and chart limits, and verifies byte-for-byte reproduction. A changed module result changes the report. These are reproducibility checks, not physical validation. Source: Report numerical checks.

8. Key Findings, Limitations and Recommendations

The shortlist is conditional on the assumed input and criterion set. Further assessment is required before a physical concept is selected for design or an installation is authorized.

Key findings

The workflow closes the mass balance and applies separate stability, period, amplification and offset checks. The semi-submersible and spar results identify candidates for further work; barge modal rejection identifies a mismatch with the supplied period band. Source: Public sizing workflow and Screening equations and acceptance logic.

Limits of use

Small-angle initial stability is represented; large heel, damaged stability, reserve buoyancy, freeboard, steel strength, tendon capacity, ballast tank free surfaces and fatigue are not evaluated. Added mass, inertia and damping are coefficient approximations. No wave-excitation amplitude, directional response, aerodynamic damping or controller coupling is calculated. Source: Floater equations and assumptions and Screening equations and acceptance logic.

A horizontal stiffness is not checked for positivity by the screening criterion model, and an empty load-case list is not a supported calculation basis. A positive stiffness, a non-empty case set and an ordered finite period band are required as supplied engineering inputs before use. Feasibility also checks ballast mass rather than ballast storage capacity. Source: Screening equations and acceptance logic.

Solver preparation supplies primitive meshes for spar and barge concepts, while column-stabilised concepts require supplied meshes. Dispatch status records intent and does not establish a completed solve. Native model loading, response-unit reconciliation and independent coupled results remain not evaluated. Source: Solver preparation implementation.

Recommendations

Replace assumed masses, centroids, geometry, coefficients and site cases with an agreed design basis before a shortlist is used for concept selection. Assess ballast capacity and free surfaces, then compare hydrostatics and natural periods with an independent calculation and measured or published response evidence. Source: Floater equations and assumptions and Existing module tests.

Retain the motion-rejected semi-submersibles and band-rejected barges outside the shortlist until the motion and period-band exceedances are resolved by a change to geometry or by a substantiated response and site criterion. Review each component separately; avoid interpreting the mixed-unit governing margin as a utilization. Source: Screening equations and acceptance logic.

Supply a complete geometry mesh and a versioned response basis for shortlisted concepts before coupled analysis. For installation planning, define the installation system, headings, directional sea states and component limits, then determine the operating envelope and forecast triggers in a separate analysis. Source: Solver preparation implementation and the missing-data register.
