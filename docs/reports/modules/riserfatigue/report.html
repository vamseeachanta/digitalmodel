1. Executive Summary

The riser fatigue module is a supplied-response fatigue screening calculation for steel riser locations. Analysis is performed to determine combined wave and vortex-induced vibration fatigue damage, and the key results are summarized below. [1][2]

  • The public synthetic example is assessed against its explicit design fatigue factor.
    • The governing location is the touchdown zone, with design-life damage 0.0134 against allowable damage 0.1; the screening criterion is met. [1][2]
    • The governing unfactored fatigue life is 1.86e+03 years against a required life of 250 years; this is a screening estimate from supplied stresses. [2]
  • The evidence supports checking damage arithmetic and locating the governing supplied response.
    • A connected operating envelope and whether a riser stands remain NOT_EVALUATED; host motion, loads, stack-up and structural response are not established by this calculation. [2]
    • Design acceptance requires a qualified stress basis and an independent published benchmark; neither is established by the synthetic example or the existing regression checks. [3]

2. Introduction

A steel catenary or steel lazy-wave riser is represented by supplied stress ranges at selected locations. Riser fatigue analysis is performed after stresses and cycle exposure have been determined upstream. Water depth and a site-specific configuration are not defined by this example. [1][2]

The main workflow accumulates wave-histogram damage and narrow-band vortex-induced vibration damage, selects the largest total damage and returns a screening status. The separate touchdown assessment accepts a histogram and returns corrected ranges, per-bin damage, annual damage and fatigue life. A text-report helper is also available. [2]

3. Design Data

The public repository example is used as the calculation basis. Its section properties, stress histograms and vibration exposure are synthetic inputs rather than measurements. [1]

3.1 Field, host and riser

Field, water depth, host class, response amplitude operators, offsets and a dimensional stack-up are not supplied. The schematic shows physical locations only; curvature, component length, soil restraint and support stiffness are not determined. [1][2]

Section diameters and wall thicknesses are retained in millimetres because the fatigue interface uses millimetres and megapascal stress ranges. The outer diameter and material label are informational in this workflow; wall thickness affects only the wave thickness correction. No stress is derived from pressure, curvature or material strength. [2]

3.2 Contents, metocean and supplied responses

Contents density, pressure, current profiles, waves and host-motion histories are not held. The supplied histograms replace the upstream response calculation; there is no current-dominated or wave-dominated operating envelope. Tables A, C and D in the appendix hold all numeric example inputs. [1][2]

3.3 Criteria and rationale

The design life is 25 years and the explicit example design fatigue factor is 10, giving allowable design-life damage 0.1. These are example choices, marked ASSUMED - to be confirmed for any design application. The factor reduces permissible accumulated damage to reserve fatigue life; it is not a calculated material property or a demonstrated site-specific requirement. [1][2]

The selected stress–life detail category is F1 in seawater with cathodic protection. This is an example selection, not a verified weld classification. The implemented acceptance criterion is design-life damage at or below the inverse of the fatigue factor. The same condition is expressed as unfactored life at or above required life. [1][2]

The load-case matrix consists of supplied wave bins and narrow-band vibration cases at each location. Their exposure and stress ranges are stated in the appendix; no sea-state probabilities or host-motion cases are inferred from them. [1]

4. Analysis Methodology

Fatigue damage is calculated from supplied stresses through the shared stress–life and linear damage engine. Geometry and loading are not solved by the fatigue module. [2][4]

The equation basis is the cumulative-damage criterion and stress–life method in DNV-RP-C203, October 2011, clauses 2.2 and 2.4.3. Period and cycle-exposure scaling follow unit conversion; life, usage and margin are algebraic rearrangements of that criterion. No new standard coefficient or fatigue-factor selection is established. [8]

4.1 Calculation boundary and theory

Sc = S Ks max(1, t / tr)k

For wave bins, S is the supplied stress range, Ks is the stress concentration factor, t is wall thickness, tr is reference thickness and k is the class-specific thickness exponent. Sc is the evaluated stress range. The correction is neutral below reference thickness. The reference thickness used by the touchdown interface is 25.000 mm. The exponent is selected from the repository tables for the detail category unless explicitly supplied to the touchdown interface. [2][4][8]

N(Sc) = Nk (Sc / Sk)−m; Dp = Σ nj / Nj

N is allowable cycles, Nk is knee cycles, Sk is knee stress and m is the active branch slope. The shared curve implementation anchors both branches at the knee; the second branch is continuous there rather than independently anchored to a rounded tabular intercept. nj is applied cycles in bin j, Nj is its capacity and Dp is damage over the histogram period. Standard coefficients are consumed from the implementation and are not reproduced here as independently checked standard values. [4][8]

Da = Dp / H; Dw = T Da; nv = f e Y T; D = Dw + Dv

H is histogram period in years, T is design life in years, Da is annual wave damage, Dw and Dv are wave and vibration damage over the same design life, f is vibration frequency in hertz, e is the annual exposure fraction, Y is seconds per year, and nv is vibration cycles. The implementation uses 3.16e+07 seconds per year as a calendar conversion, not an environmental exposure. Exposure given in hours is converted to a year fraction by the same convention. [1][2][8]

L = T / D; Dallow = 1 / F; U = D F; M = L / (T F)

L is unfactored life, F is the design fatigue factor, Dallow is allowable damage, U is fatigue usage and M is the fatigue-factor life margin. The criterion is U ≤ 1, equivalently M ≥ 1, for positive damage. The inverse and unity thresholds are definitions of the implemented screening check, not independently selected environmental limits. [2][8]

4.2 Structural benchmark and dynamic basis

A static hand calculation followed by still-water, current, then wave-and-current response is required upstream for a physical riser analysis. That sequence is not performed here. Effective tension, wall tension, foam extent, base uplift and host response amplitude operators are NOT_EVALUATED because the corresponding data and solver interface are absent from this example. The supplied-stress fatigue calculation is a screening estimate; it is not a result on either a regular-wave proxy or response-operator-driven host motion. [1][2]

Wave stress ranges receive the concentration and thickness corrections. Vibration stress ranges are passed directly to the stress–life engine. Vibration inputs therefore require a documented basis stating whether all needed local corrections are already included. [2]

5. Detailed Results

The combined fatigue screening results are returned by the module for the public synthetic example. Only supplied-response fatigue is evaluated; results are not established for the upstream structural stages. [1][2]

5.1 Statics, current and operating envelopes

Statics against a hand calculation, statics with current and connected operating envelopes are NOT_EVALUATED. Geometry, weight, buoyancy, pressure, tension and motion inputs are required. No allowable offset is established. [1][2]

5.2 Dynamics, drift-off, recoil and weak point

Dynamic stroke, tension and angles are NOT_EVALUATED. Stop strikes and travel limits are not assessed. Drift-off is NOT_EVALUATED; current histories, a drift model and structural load histories would be needed for a separate response assessment. Drilling-riser operability is not applicable to this supplied-stress fatigue screen. Recoil and weak-point response are outside its calculation boundary; no operating instruction is issued. [1][2]

5.3 Vortex-induced vibration and fatigue

The touchdown zone governs with wave damage 0.0121 and vibration damage 0.0013, giving total design-life damage 0.0134 against allowable damage 0.1. The screening criterion is met. [2]

The governing unfactored life is 1.86e+03 years and its fatigue-factor margin is 7.45 against the unity minimum. These are screening estimates from supplied stresses, not predictions of actual service life. Table B gives each location and Table E gives every damage contribution. [2]

5.4 Validation evidence held

Existing tests check a single-bin hand expression, fatigue-factor verdict changes, thickness effects, detail-category and environment selection, lowercase-category normalization, report fields, unequal input lengths, explicit and cited fatigue factors, and combination of wave and vibration damage. The documented example is also exercised through the public engine. [3]

The single-bin test checks allowable cycles and damage against an expression using the repository's encoded parameters. This is useful arithmetic regression evidence, but the constants share the implementation's source basis. No independent published benchmark or measured riser response is established by these checks. Test execution evidence is stated separately from engineering qualification in the appendix. [3][4]

6. Key Findings

The governing supplied response is identified and its combined damage is below the explicit example limit. The conclusion is confined to the provided stress and exposure basis. [1][2]

The governing location is the touchdown zone, with design-life damage 0.0134 against allowable damage 0.1; the screening criterion is met. The governing unfactored fatigue life is 1.86e+03 years against a required life of 250 years; this is a screening estimate from supplied stresses. Whether a production riser stands and how far a host remains connected are NOT_EVALUATED. [2]

7. Limitations

The calculation is limited to damage accumulation from supplied responses. Qualification of geometry, weld detail, exposure and loading remains outside the module's demonstrated evidence. [1][2]

The standards basis in the source is mixed: the assessment metadata names the 2021 edition of DNV-RP-C203, while the shared numeric table provenance and class thickness lookup cite the 2011 edition. The edition mapping is documented in the repository, but no licensed clause text is checked here. The fatigue factor in this example is explicit; its selection is not a standard-derived requirement for an actual riser. [2][4][5]

The direct histogram interface checks matching array shapes and positive histogram period and fatigue factor, but a complete contract for finite, nonnegative, nonempty inputs and valid geometry is not enforced by that interface. The report generator rejects missing, non-finite and inconsistent screening evidence; that check does not qualify external response data. All external inputs still require engineering qualification. The material designation is informational. [2]

7.1 Not covered

Stress extraction, cycle counting from histories, vortex-induced response prediction, spectral response, host motion, soil interaction, installation fatigue, corrosion progression and crack growth are not covered. The text-report helper and fatigue-factor citation resolution are described as interfaces, not used as substitutes for independent engineering evidence. An omitted fatigue factor in the combined workflow requires a resolvable citation context; the direct touchdown interface has a default instead. [2]

8. Recommendations

Design use is conditional on resolving the input basis and standards applicability. The arithmetic screening result alone is insufficient to establish operational acceptance. [2][3][4]

  • Establish a dimensioned stack-up and a static hand benchmark, then supply qualified current, wave and host-motion stress histories with support, stop and soil models before setting an operating envelope. The module accepts only the resulting stress basis. [2]
  • Document stress-range units, cycle-period basis, local corrections, weld category, environment and fatigue-factor selection. Confirm that vibration ranges include the corrections omitted by their adapter. The synthetic example is retained only as a regression case. [1][2]
  • Reconcile the governing standard edition and clause applicability against an authorized source, then establish an independent published or measured benchmark for the intended application. Existing tests remain arithmetic regression evidence. [3][4][5]
  • For any future result exceeding allowable design-life damage, withhold acceptance and reduce the qualified stress or exposure basis through an assessed design or operating change. Recalculate the combined damage until it satisfies the same fatigue-factor criterion; no generic stress-reduction percentage or inspection interval is inferred here. [2]

10. Acronyms and Symbols

The abbreviations and symbols used in the calculation are defined below. All damage, usage, exposure-fraction and margin quantities are dimensionless. [2][4]

VIV means vortex-induced vibration. SCF means stress concentration factor. DFF means design fatigue factor. S–N means stress range versus allowable cycle count. RAO means response amplitude operator. MPa means megapascal; mm means millimetre; Hz means cycles per second. DNV identifies the standards publisher. API is the material-specification publisher named in the example; the grade label is not used to calculate stress–life capacity. QA means quality assurance.

S is supplied stress range; Sc is evaluated range; Ks is the concentration factor; t and tr are wall and reference thickness; k is the thickness exponent; N and n are allowable and applied cycles; subscript j identifies a bin; subscript k identifies a knee; m is stress–life slope; H and T are histogram period and design life; f is vibration frequency; e is exposure fraction; Y is seconds per year; D is combined design-life damage; subscripts p, a, w and v identify period, annual, wave and vibration quantities; L is unfactored life; F is the fatigue factor; U is usage; M is fatigue-factor margin. NOT_EVALUATED means that the required assessment has not been performed, rather than a zero demand.

C. Calculation reproduction and QA pre-flight

The report is rebuilt by calling the documented combined workflow on the public example with only its output destination redirected. The touchdown interface is called separately on the same wave inputs to obtain the allowable damage and period basis. The rebuild test compares all numeric output rows and per-location results against direct module calls, checks every displayed numeric field against the embedded record, and checks deterministic reproduction of this HTML and its change record. [6]

From the repository root, the following POSIX-shell command runs the report and supporting fatigue checks with the declared numerical and test dependency bounds. Other shells require PYTHONPATH to be set to src before running uv. The --no-project option does not load project constraints, so those bounds are supplied explicitly, including NumPy below version 2. An unconstrained cached environment does not establish supported-dependency verification. [6]

PYTHONPATH=src uv run --no-project --with 'pytest>=7.4.3,<9.0.0' --with 'numpy>=1.24.0,<2.0.0' --with 'pandas>=2.0.0,<3.0.0' --with 'pylife>=2.2,<3.0' --with 'pydantic>=2.7.0,<3.0.0' --with 'rainflow>=3.2.0,<4.0.0' --with 'pyyaml>=6.0.0,<7.0.0' python -m pytest tests/riser_fatigue/test_module_report.py tests/riser_fatigue/test_touchdown.py tests/riser_fatigue/test_resolve_dff.py -q -rA -p no:cacheprovider

The command checks arithmetic, report reproduction and interaction logic. Its result does not establish physical qualification or native browser and platform behavior. Test scratch can be directed outside the checkout by appending --basetemp with a new disposable directory.

The chart plots total design-life damage by location with the allowable damage returned by the touchdown interface. Its ordinate starts at zero and its upper extent includes both demands and the limit. No operating boundary, stress limit or service-life probability is inferred from the chart. [2][6]

The structural QA pre-flight is incomplete: no still-water hand benchmark, effective-tension comparison, current-response benchmark, mesh convergence or host-motion check is available in this example. Existing arithmetic tests and report rebuild tests are distinct from those missing engineering checks. No independent published benchmark is established. [1][3][6]
