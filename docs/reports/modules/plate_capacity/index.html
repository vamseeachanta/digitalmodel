Plate capacity module analysis

Simply supported elastic plate buckling with an axial bar verification appendix.

Summary

The synthetic plate has an elastic critical stress of 43.4 MPa, normalised slenderness 2.4 and elastic utilisation 46.1 %. These values describe the stated perfect-plate model; they do not establish code resistance or acceptance. Confirm boundary conditions, material properties, imperfections and a governing resistance method before design use.

Method and equation sources

Four simply supported edges, uniform uniaxial compression along a and free transverse in-plane expansion are assumed. MIT 2.080J (2013), Lecture 11, Section 11.2, Eqs. 11.15–11.17 give N_cr=k*pi²*D/b² and k=min over positive integers m of (m*b/a+a/(m*b))². The transverse mode has one half-wave. The continuous minimum is at m=a/b; the two adjacent positive integers bracket the discrete minimum. For a long plate k approaches 4.0. Lecture 4, Eq. 4.60 gives D=E*t³/[12*(1-nu²)], hence sigma_cr=N_cr/t=k*pi²*E/[12*(1-nu²)]*(t/b)².

Geometric slenderness is defined here as b/t. Normalised slenderness is defined here as sqrt(f_y/sigma_cr), a nondimensional comparison of input yield stress to the elastic critical stress. Utilisation is defined here as applied compressive stress divided by sigma_cr. Neither definition supplies a standard resistance curve, reduction factor or acceptance limit.

Limits of use and NOT_EVALUATED checks

The plate is geometrically perfect, thin, homogeneous, isotropic and linearly elastic. Positive finite E and dimensions and -1<nu<0.5 are enforced; these bounds follow from positive elastic bulk and shear moduli in MIT Lecture 4, Eqs. 4.14 and 4.16. Thinness and elastic applicability must be established independently.

  • NOT_EVALUATED: DNV-RP-C201 design resistance, partial factors, effective widths, interaction and imperfection reductions. An applicable licensed edition and clause basis are not present in the calculations folder; no licensed values are reproduced.
  • NOT_EVALUATED: yielding, residual stress, corrosion, stiffeners, biaxial compression, shear, lateral restraint and post-buckling reserve. Required models and evidence are absent.
  • NOT_EVALUATED: a physical test, external solver comparison or project acceptance. The calculation is a mathematical elastic screen only.
  • NOT_EVALUATED: historical scenario scripts outside the calculations folder; those scripts are not independent numerical references and are not imported.

Verification appendix: axial bar model

The public fea_model entry accepts axial_bar with elements and a signed tip_load. It assembles a one-dimensional chain fixed at the left node and loaded at the right node; there are no interior applied loads. This analytical fixture does not start an external solver. MIT Lecture 4, Eq. 4.25 gives N=EA*epsilon and Lecture 2, Eq. 2.5 gives epsilon=du/dx. For a prismatic element these give k=EA/L, K_e=k[[1,-1],[-1,1]], and delta=P*L/(EA). Two elements in series carry the same force, so k_eq=k1*k2/(k1+k2).
