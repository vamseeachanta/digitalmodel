Draft analytical example. No operational clearance is established.

1 Summary

The constant operator returns an amplitude of 6.0 m from RAO 3.0 m/m and wave amplitude 2.0 m. The synthetic peak example gives variance 2.1 m², RMS 1.5 m and significant single amplitude 2.9 m.

The response contracts and independent arithmetic are evaluated. Geometry-specific motion prediction and operating acceptance are NOT_EVALUATED. Supply a qualified RAO and assess spectral convergence before applying the workflow.

2 Scope and capability

The numerical public entry points are regular_wave_response and spectral_response in the RAO-analysis package. The existing RAOAnalysis plotting class remains available. Legacy plotting scripts are not numerical response solvers.

This analysis covers regular-wave superposition, spectral transfer, finite-band statistics and explicit input contracts. Related module reports cover diffraction (#3222), spectral hydrodynamics (#3227) and OrcaWave spectral reporting (#3233).

4 Method and equation sources

At a common phase origin, eta(t)=sum a_j cos(omega_j t+theta_j) and x(t)=sum R_j a_j cos(omega_j t+theta_j-phi_j) for lag phases. Positive lead uses +phi_j. Orcina's RAO theory defines the single-component equation; superposition follows from linearity.

Independent Fourier components give S_r(omega)=|R(omega)|² S_w(omega). Integrate over the supplied angular-frequency band: m0=integral S_r d omega, RMS=sqrt(m0), significant single amplitude=2 sqrt(m0). The latter is half the corresponding significant double amplitude; the highest-third interpretation requires narrow-band Gaussian peaks.

The modified Pierson–Moskowitz formula is S=(5/16) Hs² omega_p⁴ omega⁻⁵ exp[-(5/4)(omega_p/omega)⁴]. JONSWAP multiplies this shape by gamma raised to exp[-(omega/omega_p-1)²/(2 sigma²)], with sigma=7/100 below and 9/100 above the peak. The source is Waves in Ocean Engineering (1991), pp107–108, identified and reproduced in the public Orcina wave-spectrum documentation. The separate parameter page confirms the two widths. The textbook itself was not accessed.

The synthetic heave-like curve is explicitly defined as R=peak_gain*exp[-((omega-omega_p)/peak_width)²]. It is an illustrative peak, with no mass, damping or hydrodynamic excitation model.

For hydrostatics, WAMIT User Manual v7.5 section 3.1 gives C33=rho*g*A, C44=rho*g*Ixx+rho*g*V*zB-m*g*zG, and the corresponding Iyy pitch term. With equilibrium mass rho*V, these reduce to the centered-waterplane equations checked by the adapter tests. Density and gravity are required inputs.

6 Numerical evidence and tail check

The hand result Hs²/16 is 1.0 m². A separate raw Pierson–Moskowitz curve is integrated without normalization. Substitution u=(omega_p/omega)⁴ gives m0(0,W)=Hs²/16 exp[-(5/4)(omega_p/W)⁴]. The full grid loses less than or equal to 0.1 % of infinite-band variance; the numerical relative tolerance is 2.0e-04, bounded from the analytic tail and quadrature error in the value-test docstring.

Truncating that same raw curve at 2.0 rad/s loses 17.7 % of variance. This diagnostic addresses the tail concern in #2613; no existing generator defaults are changed. Zeroth-moment convergence does not establish convergence of higher moments or zero-crossing period.

Tests check the peak-response integral by separate scalar Simpson quadrature, the constant case by hand arithmetic, the raw Pierson–Moskowitz integral against its antiderivative and hydrostatics against a tracked unit-box fixture. They fail on missing required files and never silently skip the new value checks.

7 Input and output contracts

Angular-frequency axes are rad/s and ascend strictly. Frequency axes are Hz and period axes seconds; periods descend to preserve increasing omega. Omega=2*pi*f=2*pi/T. Regular-wave phase is explicitly degrees or radians, independent of rotational amplitude units. Positive lag delays the response crest. Translation output is m for m/m RAOs; deg/m and rad/m RAOs retain their respective response units.

Spectral density is one-sided per rad/s. Transform density with its Jacobian before converting from Hz or period axes. A complex RAO enters through its squared magnitude. Non-monotonic grids, duplicate points, mismatched lengths, nonfinite values and negative density raise ValueError. No sorting, interpolation, extrapolation or default phase inference is performed.

8 Limits of use and NOT_EVALUATED

  • Geometry-specific RAOs, heading coupling, nonlinear response and forward speed: NOT_EVALUATED.
  • Licensed solver execution, native solver import, physical benchmarking and native export-schema acceptance: NOT_EVALUATED.
  • Extreme response, peak-distribution fit, higher spectral moments and operating limits: NOT_EVALUATED.
  • Hydrostatic adapter record: centered symmetric waterplane, zero horizontal centre offsets, equilibrium mass rho*V; finite heel, off-centre coupling and free-surface correction: NOT_EVALUATED.
  • The optional hydrostatic mapping is a separate SI calculation record. It does not qualify legacy vessel defaults or their native-unit conversion.

9 Way forward

Supply qualified complex RAOs with an explicit phase origin, response units and axis convention. Extend and refine the frequency grid for every statistic used. Confirm the narrow-band assumption before interpreting the significant single amplitude as a highest-third peak measure. Obtain a separate native-schema and solver comparison before transferring these records to an operational model.

Rebuild with PYTHONPATH=src python scripts/reporting/build_rao_analysis_report.py. Use --output to select an external rebuild directory. Run python -m pytest tests/hydrodynamics/rao_analysis/test_module_report.py with the source import path and declared dependencies. Numerical tests use unrounded computed values; displayed quantities use one decimal place.
