1 Executive Summary

The naval architecture module is a collection of callable calculations for floating-body geometry, hydrostatics, stability, resistance, motions, manoeuvring and longitudinal strength. Analysis is performed to determine the supported preliminary calculation workflow and its limits of use; key results are summarized below. The implementation and evidence records are identified in Section 9.2.

  • The public rectangular-tank calculation gives an effective initial metacentric height of $effective_gm_ft ft, above the neutral boundary of $neutral_gm_ft ft, with the source loading condition held fixed.
    • The free-surface correction is $correction_ft ft, reducing the uncorrected height by $reduction_pct %. The calculation supports a preliminary estimate of the static liquid-surface penalty; an operational stability margin is not established. Reference A, sections 3.3 and 4.8.3.
  • Use is limited to the supplied loading condition and an unrestricted rectangular slack surface at initial heel.
    • A complete righting-arm curve, downflooding information and applicable criteria are required before an operational loading decision is made. Section 8 defines the restriction.

2 Introduction

The module is assessed as a preliminary engineering calculation library. A documented scalar stability function is exercised directly, while the broader package is described from its existing interfaces and test records. No licensed analysis program is required for the worked example.

The principal workflow is the assessment of initial transverse stability after allowing for liquid motion inside a tank. Hydrostatic properties and the loading condition are supplied by the engineer; hull geometry and the mass distribution are not reconstructed by this function. This distinction controls the intended use of the result.

2.1 Not covered

Mesh ingestion, mesh hydrostatics and trim, box-hull loading analysis, supplied-table intact and damage screening, tank gauging, resistance transfer and prediction, propeller performance, rudder loads, turning-circle estimates, seakeeping, hull-girder strength, scantlings, submarine buoyancy, hull-form sizing, numerical integration and inertia estimates are available as separate sub-areas. Their callable interfaces and example records are identified in Section 9.2. A common engineering qualification across these sub-areas is not established by this report.

3 Input Controls

The input controls are established before the stability function is called. The result is interpreted as an initial-heel static correction within the stated tank model.

  • Confirm displacement, gravity height and transverse metacentre for the same loading condition. Liquid mass is already included in that condition.
  • Retain the native units at the calculation boundary: feet, long tons and mass density in slugs per cubic foot. A long ton is denoted LT in the source; it is not a metric tonne.
  • Use the free-surface length and breadth of the connected slack surface, rather than the gross tank dimensions where baffles, divisions, pocketing or irregular geometry alter that surface.
  • Record the liquid correction and corrected metacentric height separately. A positive corrected value does not replace the applicable intact-stability, damage-stability or operating criteria.
  • Confirm caller input validation. The scalar function performs arithmetic without rejecting nonphysical dimensions, inconsistent density units or a nonpositive displacement.

The free-surface model, sign criterion and data requirements are taken from Reference A, sections 3.3 and 4.8.3–4.8.4. The physical arrangement and sensitivity are shown below.

5 Theory and Calculation Method

The free-surface penalty is represented as a virtual rise of the centre of gravity. The liquid moves transversely as the hull heels, reducing the initial restoring moment without changing the displacement.

5.1 Equations and symbols

i = L B³ / 12; C = ρ g i / (Δ c); GMeffective = KM − KG − C.

Here i is the transverse second moment of the liquid surface in ft⁴; L and B are its length and breadth in ft; ρ is fuel mass density in slug/ft³; g is gravitational acceleration in ft/s²; Δ is displacement in long tons; c converts long tons to pounds-force; C is the free-surface correction in ft; K is the keel datum, G the gravity centre and M the transverse metacentre. KM and KG are their heights above K in ft. The rectangular coefficient and correction are sourced to Reference A, sections 4.8.3 and 4.8.3.1, printed pages 4-29–4-30.

The module uses gravitational acceleration $g_ft_s2 ft/s² and a conversion of $lb_per_long_ton lb per long ton. These constants are checked against the same edition's Equations and Conversion Factors, Miscellaneous Conversions. The liquid-surface coefficient is geometric, rather than a fitted material coefficient.

5.2 Calculation sequence

The source inputs are read, their finite positive values and unit conventions are checked, and the documented free-surface function is called. Its ordered outputs are the correction and effective metacentric height. An uncorrected comparison is obtained with the correction suppressed. Every point in Figure B is obtained from another call to the same function, with only surface breadth changed. The callable example and reproduction command are given in Section 9.2.

7 Validation Evidence

The existing evidence is assessed by comparator type. Arithmetic regression is distinguished from physical validation and from acceptance of an operational loading condition.

7.1 Existing regression and benchmark records

The repository holds textbook-labelled tests and input vectors for buoyancy, gravity shifts, inclining, righting-arm corrections, free surface, resistance and submarine buoyancy. The selected stability test checks both the free-surface penalty and the corrected metacentric height against its stored expectations. Those expectations are repository transcriptions; the textbook labels are not treated as independent proof of each numerical value. The present public example is taken directly from the cited problem and is also checked against the governing equation.

Mesh benchmark tests compare analytic parabolic-hull volume and wetted area with generated geometry. Loading-analysis tests exercise box-hull equilibrium and strength calculations. The resistance reproduction record explicitly identifies approximate reference values as unverified; that record does not qualify the resistance method. These evidence locations are listed in Section 9.2.

7.2 Interpretation of evidence

The report rebuild compares every embedded input, returned example result and sensitivity point with independent hand calculations of the rectangular-surface correction and corrected metacentric height, using the cited source constants at the worked condition and every sensitivity point. The displayed rounding is checked. The accompanying quality checks inspect section structure, source records and offline interactions. The retained check record is described in Section 10.

No measured roll response, inclining survey or approved stability booklet is supplied for this example. Agreement with arithmetic and stored regression expectations therefore does not establish performance of an actual hull, dynamic liquid motion, regulatory compliance or operational acceptance.

8 Key Findings and Limits of Use

The static initial-surface penalty is calculated for the public tank model. The correction increases with the cube of the connected free-surface breadth, as defined by the cited rectangular second-moment relation.

The effective initial metacentric height remains above the neutral boundary for the illustrated breadth range. This finding applies only to the fixed public loading condition. The boundary supplies no margin against large-angle heel, flooding, wind heeling, waves or cargo movement.

The simple surface formula requires revision where a roof or bottom clips the liquid surface, the tank is not rectangular, the liquid is restricted by internal divisions, or the surface shape varies materially with heel. Dynamic sloshing is not represented. The source distinguishes static and dynamic effects in Reference A, sections 4.8.1–4.8.3.

Units are explicit but not enforced by a dimensional type system in the scalar function. The simple buoyancy helpers also choose fresh-water arithmetic for any water selector other than the exact salt-water selector. Input qualification is therefore required at the calling boundary. Signed trim and longitudinal origins differ between some package interfaces; those conventions require reconciliation before results are exchanged between them.

9 Recommendations and References

The preliminary result is used to identify the liquid-surface penalty and the further evidence required for an operating decision. The following recommendations follow from the model boundaries.

9.1 Recommendations

Confirm the loading survey, liquid density, connected slack-surface dimensions and tank divisions for each intended condition. Obtain the applicable minimum stability margin, downflooding information and complete righting-arm curve before an operating limit is assigned. These items complete the basis identified in Reference A, sections 3.3, 4.4 and 4.8.

Use a supplied-table stability calculation with qualified hydrostatic and cross-curve data when a full loading condition is assessed. Reconcile coordinate and trim conventions at that interface. Validate each engineering criterion against the applicable edition before a design conclusion is issued.

Where the liquid-surface penalty governs a future condition, assess longitudinal compartment divisions or a controlled filling arrangement against the complete loading and stability calculation. The rationale is the breadth dependence and mitigation discussion in Reference A, section 4.8.4. A target filling percentage or retrofit requirement is not assigned without the missing tank geometry and operational criteria.
