KCS calm-water hull resistance — RANS/VOF verification

Calm-water resistance of the KRISO Container Ship at Fn 0.26, computed with OpenFOAM interFoam and gated against the Gothenburg-2000/Tokyo-2005 towing-tank referent. Four acceptance criteria were fixed against published values before any result existed; all four currently fail, at 3-9%. The dominant error found during this work was not in the flow solution but in force integration.

Objective

Purpose and scope of this calculation.

Objective

Establish whether this OpenFOAM setup reproduces a published towing-tank total-resistance coefficient closely enough to be used for client hull work, and quantify the residual error.

Scope: KCS bare hull, fixed even keel, no rudder, model scale Lpp 7.2786 m, Fn 0.26.Scope: three systematically refined grids for a grid-convergence estimate.Out of scope: sinkage and trim, propulsion, appendages, full-scale extrapolation.Out of scope: any client geometry — the case builder and geometry path do not yet exist.

All four acceptance gates fail. The coefficients below are computed results, not validated ones, and must not be used for design.

Analysis methodology

Governing relations, shown explicitly so a reviewer can check the arithmetic by hand.

Non-dimensionalisation

Forces are divided by dynamic pressure times wetted area, which removes size and speed so hulls of different scale are comparable. The total splits exactly into a pressure and a viscous part; that identity is what lets the two be gated separately.

Ct = RT / (½ ρ U² S)
(1)
RTTotal resistance, full hullN
ρWater densitykg/m³
UModel speedm/s
SPublished wetted surfacem²

Cp and Cv use the same S, so Ct = Cp + Cv holds identically.

Cf = 0.075 / (log10 Re − 2)²
(2)
ReReynolds number, U·Lpp/ν–

ITTC-1957 correlation line — the flat-plate yardstick Cv is judged against.

Force integration on a two-phase field — the controlling detail

The hull patch spans the waterline: 9.4216 m² of half-hull surface, of which only 4.8601 m² (51.6%) is submerged. Integrating shear over the whole patch at a constant water density applies ρwater to dry topsides. Measured here, that supplied 62.3% of the reported viscous force and put Ct +113.79% over the experiment. Using the VOF density field instead weights each face by its own phase fraction.

R = ∫S ρ(α) τw dA ,   ρ(α) = α ρw + (1−α) ρa
(3)
αWater volume fraction on the face–
τwKinematic wall shear stressm²/s²

OpenFOAM: rho rho, not rho rhoInf. Correcting this moved Cv from +141.40% to −8.71% on the same solution.

Free-surface solution

The computed wave field shows a bow crest, divergent and transverse wave systems inside the Kelvin wedge, and a stern system — qualitatively correct for a displacement hull at Fn 0.26. This is the strongest available evidence that the flow solution itself is sound.

Boundary layer and wake

Velocity deficit at mid-draft and on a longitudinal cut. The boundary layer hugs the hull and the wake recovers within roughly one ship length aft. Peak eddy viscosity in the boundary layer is nut/ν = 167, normal for this Reynolds number.

Hull pressure distribution

Dynamic pressure on the submerged hull, projected onto the profile plane. Stagnation at the bow, suction over the forward shoulder, recovery aft — consistent with the near-correct pressure coefficient.

Convergence and averaging

Local time stepping marches to a pseudo-steady fixed point. Iterative uncertainty is bounded by the oscillation envelope of the force signal over a trailing window, per ITTC 7.5-03-01-01 §4.1. The medium grid's window means agree to 0.02% from iteration ~9,000 onward, giving UI = 0.012% of the mean — negligible against the grid-to-grid difference.

UI = ½ ( SU − SL )
(4)
SU, SLMaximum and minimum of the oscillating signal over the windowN

Grid convergence

Coarse and medium grids differ by 5.58% in Ct, against a 1.5% criterion. The fine grid was stopped by its convergence rule at iteration 9,011 while its running mean was still descending 35%, so its value is not converged and no three-level observed order of accuracy can be computed.

Qualitative flow features

Kelvin wave pattern, boundary-layer development and stern pressure recovery are all physically plausible. Peak boundary-layer eddy viscosity nut/ν = 167 is in the expected range. No evidence of a defective flow solution was found.
