3. Analysis methodology

The plate is represented with PLANE182 elements and plane stress with thickness, KEYOPT(3) = 3. The hole is subtracted from the plate area. The bottom edge at y = 0 is restrained, and 50,000 N in global +Y is divided equally among nodes selected on the upper-hole arc by the generator's radial band. No pin, contact pair or friction model is included.

The native solution is performed with Ansys Mechanical APDL 2026 R1.01, build 26.1, update UP20260202, Windows x64, one-core SMP and sparse direct solution. Equivalent stress is extracted from the all-node result using FULL graphics, AVPRIN = 0 and global coordinate/result systems. The reported peak is nodal averaged; it is not an unaveraged integration-point maximum.

Support force is summed from restrained-node RF components and compared with the independent PRRSOL listing. FSUM is retained separately as an element-force diagnostic. Equilibrium is evaluated from the vector sum of support reaction and applied force, against 0.1% of 50,000 N = 50 N. The archived two-mesh criterion detects peak changes beyond combined printed resolution, 0.0001 MPa; it is a diagnostic sensitivity criterion, not a continuum error estimate.

The initial native pair exposed an extraction error: FSUM produced approximately +50,000 N while PRRSOL support reactions totaled −50,000 N. Treating FSUM as a signed support reaction produced a false 100,000 N balance residual. The corrected NODE RF extraction was verified by two fresh native runs. Stress values, peak nodes and element/node counts are identical between original and corrected receipt entries. 5. Validation status and limitations

Padeye convergence and native qualification remain false in the archived assessment. Sizing below the inherited allowable and force balance below 50 N are necessary checks for this example; they do not resolve load-distribution sensitivity, the shape warning or the missing physical comparator.

The earlier native continuation required a reviewed comparison criterion and a nonsingular reported quantity before a padeye solve. The subsequent captures used frozen diagnostic criteria, but a nonsingular reported quantity was not established. That recorded prerequisite remains unmet; the captures are diagnostic evidence and cannot be treated as fulfillment of the earlier qualification route. The fixed/free base corners introduce an additional singularity risk that has not been bounded. The new addendum will require a reviewed restraint/quantity screen before another stress campaign, with preparation-only load-transfer work allowed before that gate.

The public receipt records an independent audit of 24 artifact hashes across the original and corrected captures. Its pure dictionary assessor separately reports evidence binding unverified; that field does not invalidate the external hash audit or elevate the model to qualified status. Raw logs and operator records remain locally retained. Off-host backup and publication of raw artifacts are not established.

Material certification, pin clearance/compliance, contact pressure, friction, weld capacity, buckling, out-of-plane load transfer and a qualified physical reference dataset are not established. Therefore a lifting capacity and the physical error of this model cannot be calculated from the present evidence. Acceptance for such use will depend on an approved physical model and corresponding validation evidence.

Operational context for the solver-consolidation scope: recorded fleet inventory covered four of seven registered machines directly. Access to three was unavailable in the recorded attempts. Quarantined and partially removed worktrees remain subject to salvage and unique-work checks; remote deletion readiness is not established. These earlier inventory observations remain the recorded coverage; the new ANSYS captures did not expand it. Preserved branches and drafts are retained evidence, not completed integrations.

6. Conclusions and way forward

The next recommended step is the prescribed-pressure study addendum. It proposes a sinusoidal upper-hole pressure law, explicit discrete-resultant normalization, independent applied-force and moment checks, three prescribed meshes and zero default shape-warning violations. Its new 1% finite-mesh sensitivity target will apply only to the proposed case; the current failed study will retain its original criteria and disposition.

After review and scope approval, tests will precede implementation, a bounded native pressure-transfer probe will precede the mesh campaign, and complete diagnostics will be retained whether the campaign passes or fails. A passing numerical screening study will not itself authorize physical pin-contact claims or golden/registry promotion.
