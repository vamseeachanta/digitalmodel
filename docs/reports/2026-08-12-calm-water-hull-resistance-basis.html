Objective

Purpose and scope of this calculation.

Objective

Calm-water resistance is the towing power a hull demands at a given speed in still water, and it is the base on which every added-resistance, self-propulsion and powering estimate is built. This calculation establishes the analysis basis for predicting it by free-surface CFD: it pins the reference condition the eventual computed coefficient will be judged against, states the governing relations explicitly, and fixes the acceptance gates and their failure modes in advance of any solver hour.

Hull: KCS at 1/31.6, bare, fixed even keelCondition: Fr = 0.26, Re = 1.4×10⁷, still waterMethod: interFoam VOF towing, LTS pseudo-steadyDeliverable: model-scale coefficient onlyStatus: basis and method — no results

This report states no resistance result, and the omission is the point. No CFD run has been executed for this analysis; nothing has ever been launched on the CFD host. An analysis basis is a legitimate and standard deliverable in its own right — it is what makes the eventual result reviewable rather than merely reported — but it must not be read as a preliminary answer. Every field in Section 4 is pending, and no number anywhere in this document is a prediction of resistance.

The basis is now primary-sourced, and was not always. Two earlier plan revisions for this work were rejected in adversarial review, both for the same root cause: reference-condition provenance. Revision 1 gated a body fixed in heave and pitch against a free-to-sink-and-trim measurement, a 4.5 % condition offset spent before a cell was meshed. Revision 2 corrected the referent, then re-scored a with-rudder study against a bare-hull reference inside the very table rebutting the first finding. The referent used here was subsequently retrieved field by field from the workshops that published the data, and it moves the gate centre again — from 3.55×10⁻³ to 3.56×10⁻³, and the residuary by 1.4 %. Where a number in this report replaces an earlier one, Section 5 says so.

Analysis methodology

Governing relations, shown explicitly so a reviewer can check the arithmetic by hand.

Resistance decomposition and the coefficient definition

What a towing tank and a CFD run both produce is a force. What is gated is a dimensionless coefficient, formed on the wetted surface and the tow speed. Because the coefficient carries S in its denominator, a reference Ct quoted without the area, attitude and appendage state it was reduced on is not a number that can be gated against at any tolerance — which is precisely how the two earlier revisions of this work went wrong. Two decompositions appear below and they are not the same thing. Equation (2) is a bookkeeping split: Cf is supplied by a correlation line, so Cr is whatever remains. Equation (3) is a physical split the solver actually computes, integrating pressure and viscous traction over the hull patch separately. The validation strategy in Section 3.8 turns on the difference.

Ct = RT½ ρ S U²

SWetted surface, full hull, static orientationm²
UTow speedm/s

On a half domain the integrated force is doubled before this is formed, while S remains the full-hull area. Omitting the doubling is a clean factor of two and it does not announce itself.

(2)

CrResiduary coefficient — the remainder–
Ct = Cp + Cv
(3)

CvComputed viscous-force coefficient–

Equation (3) holds exactly and identically, which is why the component gates cannot be fully independent of the total. Section 3.8 states the resulting detection floor rather than leaving it to be discovered after a run.

The ITTC-57 model–ship correlation line

Cf is not a measurement. It is a correlation line adopted by the ITTC in 1957, and the workshop reduced its own experimental data with it — the published CF0 = 2.83×10⁻³ agrees with 2.832045×10⁻³ recomputed here. Two consequences follow, and both are stated because an earlier revision of this analysis got them wrong. First, Cr = Ct − Cf is a derived quantity carrying the line’s assumption; it is analytical, never validated. Second, there is no published, independently measured KCS pressure or residuary coefficient at all. The workshop defines its CR as CT − CF0, and Shen’s tabulated CP is footnoted as CT − CF. A previous revision cited their agreement as independent corroboration of the arithmetic. It is not corroboration — it is a definition reproducing itself. The only measured resistance datum in the whole referent is Ct.

Cf = 0.075(log10 Re − 2)²
(4)
ReReynolds number of the model–

At Re = 1.4×10⁷ this returns 2.832045×10⁻³, so the referent’s residuary is 7.2796×10⁻⁴ — 20.4 % of the total. That fraction is the reason the free surface must be resolved rather than approximated.

Froude and Reynolds scaling

A model cannot satisfy Froude and Reynolds similitude at once: matching Fr fixes the speed, which then fixes Re far below full scale. The decomposition of Section 3.1 exists to work around that, by scaling the wave-making part on Fr and correcting the friction part on Re. This analysis stops at the model-scale coefficient — there is no full-scale extrapolation and no powering estimate here. The viscosity is set so that the stated speed and length reproduce the stated Re = 1.4×10⁷, which reproduces the workshop’s own data reduction by construction.

Fr = U√g Lpp
(5)
gGravitational accelerationm/s²
LppLength between perpendicularsm
Re = U Lppν
(6)

U = 2.1962 m/s and Lpp = 7.2786 m at Re = 1.4×10⁷ give ν = 1.14180×10⁻⁶ m²/s. The tank temperature is not published, so whether 1.4×10⁷ is exact or nominally rounded is unknown; it does not matter, because the reference reduction used the same figure.

Free-surface capture — interFoam VOF

interFoam solves one momentum equation for a mixture of two incompressible phases and transports a phase fraction α with an artificial compression term that keeps the interface from smearing across the mesh. The hull is towed by imposing a uniform inlet velocity on a fixed body, so the wave system develops in the frame of the hull. At Fr = 0.26 the residuary is 20.4 % of the total, so the free surface is not a refinement — a solution that computes no wave field returns pure friction, Ct = Cf = 2.832×10⁻³, which is 20.4 % below the referent and fails the gate by nearly sevenfold. That degenerate case is the known-negative control the test suite is required to reject.

∂α∂t + ∇·(αU) + ∇·[α(1 − α)Ur] = 0
(7)
αWater volume fraction, 1 in water, 0 in air–
UMixture velocity fieldm/s
UrInterface compression velocitym/s
ρ = α ρw + (1 − α)ρa
(8)
ρwWater densitykg/m³
ρaAir densitykg/m³

Because density is a field rather than a constant, the forces function object must be told which ρ to normalise on. Leaving the density source implicit in a two-phase run is how a factor slips in unnoticed, so rhoInf is stated explicitly.

Local time stepping — why not a Courant-limited transient

This is the choice that makes the run feasible, so it is argued rather than asserted. A time-accurate interFoam run advances the whole domain on one step, and that step must satisfy the Courant condition in every cell simultaneously — so it is set by the smallest cell anywhere in the mesh. In a resistance mesh the wall-normal cells near the hull are three to four orders of magnitude smaller than the free-stream cells, while the tow speed is the same 2.2 m/s everywhere, which drives the global step to order 10⁻⁴ s. The physics being waited on is slow by comparison: the Kelvin wave system has to establish itself over several hull lengths, order 10 s of physical time. The product is 10⁵ to 10⁶ global steps, and almost every one of them is rate-limited by boundary-layer cells that contribute nothing to the timescale being resolved.

Local time stepping breaks that coupling by discarding the one thing not needed here — time accuracy. Calm-water resistance is a steady quantity; the transient path to it is of no interest. Under ddtSchemes localEuler each cell advances on its own pseudo-time step sized by its own local Courant limit, so small cells relax quickly in pseudo-time without holding the rest of the domain back, and the field marches to the steady state in tens of thousands of iterations rather than hundreds of thousands of time-accurate steps. Published practice for exactly this configuration (interFoam, LTS, static mesh, SST) is 20 000–40 000 iterations; this analysis budgets 25 000 with the force mean taken over the final 4 000.

Two consequences are carried deliberately. The iteration history is not a physical time series, so nothing may be read from it except convergence — the averaging window and its iterative scatter are recorded for that reason. And an accidental regression to a transient ddt scheme would not fail loudly; it would simply cost an order of magnitude more, which is why localEuler is pinned by a test rather than left to the case files.

Δti = Comax di|Ui|
(9)
ΔtiPseudo-time step, local to cell is
diCell size along the local flow directionm
ComaxCourant limit: maxCo 10, maxAlphaCo 5–

In a global-step transient the same expression is evaluated over the whole mesh and the minimum is taken. LTS keeps the per-cell value; that single change is the difference between a run of hours and a run of weeks.

Force extraction and the computed decomposition

The OpenFOAM forces function object integrates pressure and viscous contributions over the named patch and writes them separately. Keeping them separate is what turns one gated number into two constrained by different references: the pressure coefficient carries the wave-making, the viscous coefficient carries the skin friction, and a wall-treatment defect or an unresolved wave field shows up in one of them even when the total happens to look right.

Cp = Fp,x½ ρ S U² , Cv = Fv,x½ ρ S U²
(10)
Fp,xStreamwise pressure force on the hull patchN
Fv,xStreamwise viscous force on the hull patchN

Both are doubled for the half domain before normalisation, on the full-hull S = 9.4379 m².

Decomposition, execution and the host

The case decomposes onto 8 ranks, hierarchical with n (2 2 2). The repository’s own scaling benchmark measures 1.00 / 0.886 / 0.607 / 0.454 efficiency at 1 / 2 / 4 / 8 ranks and a regression to 0.193 at 16, so 8 is the practical ceiling — but that measurement is from a different (sloshing) case and is carried in Section 2 as an assumption, not as a property of this one.

Two execution facts belong in the method because they bound it. First, the mesh pipeline is not a fixed short sequence: the DTCHull Allrun needs surfaceFeatureExtract, blockMesh, six topoSet / refineMesh pairs, snappyHexMesh, restore0Dir, and redistributePar / renumberMesh around the parallel solve. A runner that emits only mesh-then-solve cannot produce this case on 8 ranks at all. Second, the existing runner drives each stage as a single subprocess with a two-hour default timeout and contains no mpirun, so an execution-capability change necessarily precedes any production solve. Note also that a fail-closed wall-clock preflight and a detached long run are in tension: once the command is wrapped in setsid nohup the timeout no longer bounds the solve, so the preflight is a configuration-consistency check, not a safety property, and should be described as one.

Validation criteria — the gates, and how each can fail

Four criteria are pre-committed here, before any result exists, because a gate chosen after seeing the number is not a gate. Each is stated with the way it can fail; a criterion nothing can fail is the defect that rejected an earlier revision of this work, where the residuary check was an algebraic re-expression of the total and could not fail while the total passed.

V1: |Ct,CFD − 3.56×10⁻³|3.56×10⁻³ ≤ 0.03
(11)

How it fails. An under-resolved or absent wave field — the degenerate no-wave solution sits at −20.4 %, failing by 6.8×. A mesh too coarse to resolve the bow and stern wave systems. Or a run whose attitude, appendage state or normalising area does not match the referent tuple, which is a provenance failure rather than a physics one and is guarded by a fixture-row assertion. Why 3 % and not 5 or 10. 3 % of Ct is 1.07×10⁻⁴, already 14.7 % of the residuary the case exists to compute; the issue’s original 10 % would let that quantity be wrong by half. Below the 1.73 % uncertainty floor the gate would start failing correct work.

V2a: |Cp,CFD − Cr,ref|Cr,ref ≤ 0.15
(12)

What makes it independent. Cp,CFD is integrated by the solver, not obtained by subtracting a correlation line from the gated total — so unlike a Cr = Ct − Cf check, this one can fail while V1 passes. How it fails. Compensating errors: an over-predicted friction offsetting an under-predicted wave field. A worked pair — Cv at +4.0 % and Cp at −15.7 % — reproduces the referent Ct to within 0.05 % while failing V2a. What is contested. Cr,ref = 7.2796×10⁻⁴ is itself derived, so the independence is on the CFD side only. And the published deviations of computed pressure coefficients from this reference span −7.9 % to −2.7 % — entirely one-sided — so a symmetric ±15 % band is not symmetric evidence. That asymmetry is an open item, recorded rather than resolved.

V2b: |Cv,CFD − Cf(Re)|Cf(Re) ≤ 0.05
(13)

How it fails. Wall treatment. The DTCHull tutorial ships nutkRoughWallFunction with Ks uniform 100e-6 — a 100 µm sand-grain roughness. Inherited unexamined onto a smooth towing-tank model it inflates skin friction, and V2b is the criterion that catches it without depending on whether the pressure side happens to compensate. Published condition-matched grids give Cv/Cf within 1.2 %, so 5 % leaves margin while still catching the defect. Note Cv,CFD and the ITTC-57 line are not the same decomposition, so the tolerance absorbs some definitional slack as well as physical error.

V3: |Ct,fine − Ct,coarse|Ct,fine ≤ 0.03
(14)

What it tests. Self-consistency between two mesh levels separated by rG = √2, refining downward from the production mesh. V1 is deliberately not required on both levels: on a two-point study the coarse level’s agreement with the experiment is not the property under test. How it fails. The answer is mesh-dependent, in which case no validation claim stands regardless of which level happens to match. What is contested. 3 % here is several times the grid-uncertainty scale the closest published analogue produces, so a pair differing by 2.9 % would pass while implying a fine-grid error larger than the budget V1 was built from. Either the threshold belongs nearer the grid-uncertainty scale, or a near-miss must re-open the V1 tolerance. Recorded as open.

Cv at +5.0 % , Cp at −15.0 % ⇒ Ct at +0.91 %
(15)

The detection floor, stated rather than discovered. Because Ct = Cp + Cv holds identically, a compensating pair sitting exactly on both component boundaries lands at +0.91 % on the total — inside V1, and passing all three criteria at once. So the decomposition gate cannot detect a compensating pair whose net effect on Ct is below roughly 1 %. That is the limit of what these criteria can prove, and it is a property of the arithmetic, not of the mesh.

Empirical cross-check — unavailable

An independent empirical prediction would ordinarily sit here as a sanity bound on the CFD result. This repository’s Holtrop–Mennen implementation cannot supply one and is not used to produce any number in this report. The defect is filed and reproduced as issue #2020: a Series 60 form and a tanker — hulls differing by 68 % in waterline length and 33 % in block coefficient — return total resistance coefficients agreeing to 4 parts in 10⁵. Hull-form dependence has collapsed out of the total somewhere in the chain. Its own declared fixture vectors fail by −31.5 % and +50.6 % against a ±15 % tolerance, and the form-factor row fails its band as well; no test loaded the fixture, which is why none of this was caught. Using an unvalidated empirical method to corroborate a CFD result would only move the unverified claim one level down, so the cross-check is reported as unavailable and carries no weight until #2020 is repaired (the implementation is owned by #1682).

Way forward

What each additional input unlocks, ordered by how much it changes the answer.

Stage 0 — commit the referent as a machine-readable fixture

No CFD. The referent tuple is transcribed into a committed fixture with a per-field provenance marker and its workshop citation, together with the free-condition series from the other lineage so that a future free-to-sink-and-trim criterion has something correct to gate against. The fixture schema requires attitude, appendage state and wetted surface on every row, which is what makes it structurally impossible to load a free-condition value into a fixed-condition gate — the failure that rejected the first revision.

Required
InputThe retrieved referent evidence — already committed
EffortTranscription only; nothing left to retrieve
Returns
UnlocksGate centres fixed and testable before any solve
GuardsCondition-mismatch regression, by schema rather than by care

Stage 1 — execution capability, then a tutorial reproduction

The first thing to establish is that a long parallel solve can run at all: MPI ranks, detached execution that survives a deliberate mid-solve disconnect, a staged mesh pipeline that can express the six-pass refinement loop, and a preflight that refuses a solve whose estimated wall-clock exceeds its configured budget. Then the DTCHull tutorial is reproduced unmodified — no repository code in the loop — to prove the toolchain end to end and to take the first measurement of the per-cell-iteration rate on this box. That measurement is expected to change the schedule, possibly upward.

Stage 2 — the emitter, test-first

A resistance case emitted from a typed configuration, ported from the tutorial as frozen literal templates. The test that matters asserts a declared-deviation diff in both directions: every intended deviation from the tutorial is present, and no unintended one exists. An accidental drop during the port then fails a test in seconds rather than producing a divergent solve days later.

Stage 3 — Wigley smoke case, after a real geometry repair

A cheap re-parameterisation exercise with no validation claim. The shipped Wigley STL is not closed, and the closure work is larger than it first appears: beyond 4 illegal triangles there are 392 edges connected to a single face, so de-duplication alone leaves the surface open and a closure gate would still fail. Capping the open lid is part of the stage or the stage does not pass.

Stage 4 — KCS geometry and mesh

The largest schedule risk, and deliberately late so that it is not paid for twice. The workshop publishes IGES/STP; a watertight STL at 1/31.6 must be produced and verified against the published particulars — waterline length, beam, draught, displacement and wetted surface — before any cell is generated. Verification before meshing is what makes a bad tessellation cost minutes instead of a multi-day solve.

Stage 5 — solve, and populate Section 4

One Froude number, two mesh levels, 25 000 LTS iterations each with the force mean over the final 4 000 and its iterative scatter recorded. Extract Ct, Cp and Cv; evaluate V1, V2a, V2b and the two-level criterion. This is the stage that turns every pending field in this report into a result — and the only one that can.
