2. Introduction

Two synthetic corroded pipeline sections are assessed to demonstrate the relationship between remaining strength, measurement sufficiency and an operating decision. No measured or site-specific data are used. The purpose is to identify the next engineering action; this demonstration does not establish fitness for an actual operating asset.

3.5 Criteria

Allowable-to-demand ratio ≥ 1 (100.0%) with no applicability flag is the current-strength criterion. The method applicability limits are applied without reproducing their licensed values (References 1–3). The assessment defaults require at least 15 valid readings and a minimum of 5 degraded cells resolving a local flaw. These are configurable engineering criteria informed by inspection practice, not code mandates. A boundary minimum requires extended coverage. General-loss averaging is not applied to either bounded local patch. Section A life is projected to the wall at which its reduced local-loss rating reaches design pressure. The flaw length, background wall, bulging factor, material and loads are held fixed; lateral growth is not modelled. Applicability is checked along this projection. Other failure modes or six-method ratings may limit life earlier. Section B retains the point-minimum pressure-wall projection. Neither projection is operating authorization. Re-inspection at half the projected life is an assessment assumption; zero means immediate action. Interim operation is a decision for the maintainer of the pipeline record when measurements are inadequate.

3.6 Method

The methods and precedence rules are stated in Section 4.

3.7 Load cases

Two sections are assessed by six strength methods each. Each section also receives a point-minimum pressure-wall comparison, a simplified Level 2 local-loss assessment and three corrosion-rate cases: half, nominal and double. Pressure and axial membrane stress are evaluated separately; no combined-load qualification is implied.

4. Analysis Methodology

B31G uses maximum depth and the full axial window with a parabolic loss area; long flaws use the infinite-length branch. The relative-depth limit is applied before disposition. Method source: Reference 1.

Modified B31G uses the same depth/window with a modified area factor and two-branch bulging correction. RSTRENG instead searches all axial subsegments using trapezoidal lost area and returns the lowest capacity; both retain the original-family depth limit. Method source: Reference 1; their numeric evidence is limited to previous calculation comparisons.

RSTRENG-2D projects each axial row to its greatest depth before applying the same effective-area calculation. It equals RSTRENG by construction and does not establish two-dimensional interaction capacity. Method source: Reference 1.

DNV-RP-F101 uses tensile strength, maximum depth and length correction for a single longitudinal defect. The allowable pressure applies modelling and operating factors to capacity. Method source: Reference 2.

Circumferential net-section balance reduces axial membrane capacity in proportion to lost circumferential area. The extent comparison uses the supplied axial length and joint factors; exceeding that comparison does not invalidate the subsequent net-section balance. Through-wall loss raises an applicability flag. This is not a burst-pressure or combined-loading calculation. Method source: Reference 3.

The point-minimum comparison uses the required pressure wall (Reference 4) as a diagnostic, not an intervention or API Level 1 criterion. Both sections are local metal loss: Section A is a single bounded patch, not general loss around the pipe. The Level 2 calculation uses the wall away from the flaw, after uniform loss and future allowance, as the reference wall in the simplified local-loss relation (Reference 3). The full-wall background establishes that reference for these synthetic grids. The reduced rating uses the uncorroded pressure rating obtained by inverting the linear pressure-wall calculation (Reference 4), then applies the remaining-strength reduction. The local-loss length equals the count of degraded axial rows times the index spacing, whereas the six-method maximum-depth calculations use the full window. This difference is retained explicitly. A shell parameter outside the supported range raises an applicability flag.

The validation record reproduces the cited DNV worked-example capacities to better than 0.001%. That accuracy claim does not extend to all methods: the B31G published comparisons have coarser reported tolerances, and Modified B31G and RSTRENG are checked only against previous calculations. No published example values are reproduced here.

Assumed decision precedence: escalate, then take more measurements, then re-rate, then accept. Any applicability flag, escalation, repair or replacement takes priority over inadequate sampling. Otherwise an under-sampled result requires more measurements; a de-rating retains both pressure and axial restrictions. Acceptance or monitoring gives accept only with positive remaining life. Other outcomes give escalate. The pressure reduction uses only pressure ratios and the corrected local-loss pressure rating, never an axial ratio. The reduced rating participates in the strength decision even when the remaining strength factor is below its allowable value.

6. Key Findings

Section A, 24 in line: DNV-RP-F101 governs the six-method comparison at 1,165 psi versus 800 psi, a ratio of 145.6% against 100.0%. The reduced local-loss rating is 1,022 psi against 800 psi, a ratio of 127.7%. The lower pressure rating participates in the decision. The local profile contains 6 degraded cells. Field data is adequate; the strength-factor comparison requires formal evaluation. The final decision is escalate; strength alone does not resolve measurement adequacy.

Section B, 12.75 in line: B31G governs the six-method comparison at 2,724 psi versus 1,000 psi, a ratio of 272.4% against 100.0%. The reduced local-loss rating is 2,889 psi against 1,000 psi, a ratio of 288.9%. The lower pressure rating participates in the decision. The local profile contains 2 degraded cells. At least 5 degraded cells are required by the assessment sampling criterion. The final decision is take more measurements; strength alone does not resolve measurement adequacy.

7. Limitations

The grids are synthetic and bound single defect windows. Colony interaction, mill-tolerance correction, crack-like defects, bending, external pressure and combined loading are not assessed. Corrosion rates are declared assumptions rather than evidence from repeat inspection. Remaining life uses the Section A fixed-geometry reduced-rating route and the Section B pressure-wall route; it does not predict the future crossing of every six-method capacity curve. RSTRENG-2D equals RSTRENG by construction; Modified B31G and RSTRENG have no external validation beyond previous calculation comparisons. The corrected Level 2 reference wall does not establish complete edition-matched qualification to Reference 3; the bulging relation remains simplified. A strength ratio above unity does not establish measurement adequacy or authorize operation. Section B spacing and supplementary loads and material strength require confirmation for any physical application.
