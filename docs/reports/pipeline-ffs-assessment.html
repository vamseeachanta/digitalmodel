2. Introduction

Two synthetic corroded pipeline sections are assessed to demonstrate the relationship between remaining strength, measurement sufficiency and an operating decision. No measured or site-specific data are used. The purpose is to identify the next engineering action; this demonstration does not establish fitness for an actual operating asset.

3.5 Criteria

Allowable-to-demand ratio ≥ 1 (100.0%) with no applicability flag is the current-strength criterion. Original-family depth loss is limited to 80.0%; the tensile-strength method to 85.0%; through-wall loss requires escalation. The measurement defaults are assumed, based on previous experience: at least 15 readings, general-loss coefficient of variation ≤ 10.0%, at least 5 degraded cells for a local profile, and 0.020 in measurement tolerance. A boundary minimum requires extended coverage. A remaining strength factor below 0.50 requires higher-level assessment. Remaining life is the linear time to the required pressure wall, zero when already below it. Re-inspection at half that time is assumed, based on previous experience; zero means immediate action. Under-sampled data do not support operation, regardless of the calculated strength.

3.6 Method

The methods and precedence rules are stated in Section 4.

3.7 Load cases

Two sections are assessed by six strength methods each. Each section also receives Level 1 and Level 2 thickness assessments and three corrosion-rate cases: half, nominal and double. Pressure and axial membrane stress are evaluated separately; no combined-load qualification is implied.

4. Analysis Methodology

B31G uses maximum depth and the full axial window with a parabolic loss area; long flaws use the infinite-length branch. The relative-depth limit is applied before disposition. Method source: Reference 1.

Modified B31G uses the same depth/window with a modified area factor and two-branch bulging correction. RSTRENG instead searches all axial subsegments using trapezoidal lost area and returns the lowest capacity; both retain the original-family depth limit. Method source: Reference 1; their numeric evidence is limited to regression anchors.

RSTRENG-2D projects each axial row to its greatest depth before applying the same effective-area calculation. It equals RSTRENG by construction and does not establish two-dimensional interaction capacity. Method source: Reference 1.

DNV-RP-F101 uses tensile strength, maximum depth and length correction for a single longitudinal defect. The allowable pressure multiplies capacity by the assumed modelling and operating factors; partial-safety-factor tables are not used. Method source: Reference 2.

Circumferential net-section balance reduces axial membrane capacity in proportion to lost circumferential area. The extent comparison uses the supplied axial length and joint factors; exceeding that comparison does not invalidate the subsequent net-section balance. Through-wall loss raises an applicability flag. This is not a burst-pressure or combined-loading calculation. Method source: Reference 3.

Level 1 compares minimum remaining wall with required pressure wall using the assumed pressure-design factors (Reference 4). Level 2 uses area-average wall for general loss or a simplified local-loss bulging relation (Reference 3). The local-loss length equals the count of degraded axial rows times the index spacing, whereas the six-method maximum-depth calculations use the full window. This difference is retained explicitly. The local-loss shell parameter is bounded at 20; a used value outside that range raises an applicability flag.

The validation record reproduces the cited DNV worked-example capacities to better than 0.001%. That accuracy claim does not extend to all methods: the B31G published comparisons have coarser reported tolerances, and Modified B31G and RSTRENG are regression-anchored only. No published example values are reproduced here.

Decision precedence: any applicability flag gives escalate. Otherwise an under-sampled result gives take more measurements, whatever the strength result. With sufficient data, escalation, repair or replacement from either assessment gives escalate; a de-rating gives re-rate with both pressure and axial restrictions retained. Acceptance or monitoring gives accept only with positive remaining life at least as long as the assumed re-inspection interval. Other outcomes give escalate. The pressure reduction uses only pressure ratios and the supplemental pressure rating, never an axial ratio.

6. Key Findings

Section A, 24 in line: DNV-RP-F101 governs the current-demand comparison at 1165 psi versus 800 psi, a ratio of 145.6% against 100.0%. General-loss variation is 23.3% against the 10.0% limit. The final decision is take more measurements; strength alone does not resolve measurement adequacy.

Section B, 12.75 in line: B31G governs the current-demand comparison at 2724 psi versus 1000 psi, a ratio of 272.4% against 100.0%. The local profile contains 2 degraded cells against the minimum of 5. The final decision is take more measurements; strength alone does not resolve measurement adequacy.

7. Limitations

The grids are synthetic and bound single defect windows. Colony interaction, mill-tolerance correction, crack-like defects, bending, external pressure and combined loading are not assessed. Corrosion rates are declared assumptions rather than evidence from repeat inspection. Remaining life refers only to the required pressure-wall criterion and does not predict the future crossing of every six-method capacity curve. RSTRENG-2D equals RSTRENG by construction; Modified B31G and RSTRENG have no external validation beyond regression anchors. The Level 2 relations are simplified; edition-matched API 579 Part 5 qualification is not established. A strength ratio above unity does not overcome deficient wall or inadequate measurements. Section B spacing and supplementary loads and material strength require confirmation for any physical application.
