Difference-frequency QTF variance: confirmed and corrected

Issue 2617. The unmodified repository function at c5e4ac33 returns 50.005840 kN² against a fixed-seed Monte Carlo variance of 24.926308 kN²: a ratio of 2.006147. The corrected function returns 24.952483 kN², within 0.1051% of Monte Carlo against the 1% criterion. All inputs are synthetic. This comparison qualifies the documented numerical cases, not a vessel or mooring design.

Physical convention and correction

The reference convention is the full difference-frequency load in Orcina's second-order load theory: F(t) = Re sum_ij Q_ij a_i a_j exp(i[(omega_i-omega_j)t+phi_i-phi_j]). The complete difference-frequency QTF is Hermitian; a_i = sqrt(2 S_i dw).

Two ordered terms form one real sinusoid with amplitude 2 a_i a_j |Q_ij|. Its variance is (2 a_i a_j |Q_ij|)^2/2 = 8 S_i S_j dw^2 |Q_ij|^2. Different unordered pairs have zero covariance under independent uniform phases, including pairs sharing one component. The variance sum therefore visits only i<j. The old loop visits each off-diagonal pair twice and includes constant i=j mean loads as fluctuating variance.

The implementation retains factor eight and visits each unique pair once. Newman's positive-lag calculation is retained; lag zero is set to zero to exclude mean load for this discrete fixed-amplitude sea. Discrete variance is sum(S_sv)*dw with full bin weights, including the final lag. A trapezoidal integral of these discrete bins would incorrectly halve an occupied final bin, as demonstrated by the bichromatic test.

Narrow-band Monte Carlo reference

The generated sea uses the repository JONSWAP function: Hs=2 m, Tp=7.853982 s, gamma=10, omega=linspace(0.2,1.224,2049) rad/s, dw=0.0005 rad/s. The synthetic diagonal drift coefficient and every Newman QTF entry equal 10 kN/m². The initial code is called directly before production edits; its source SHA-256 is retained in the compact metrics record.

With seed 2617, 2048 independent phase vectors are drawn uniformly on [0,2π). Each record spans 12566.370614 s at 8192 equally spaced times. The complex wave envelope is evaluated by FFT, and F(t)=10*|z(t)|^2 is formed in the time domain; this reference never evaluates a pair-variance sum. The carrier frequency cancels in the squared magnitude. Difference frequencies lie on exact DFT bins; Nyquist=2.048 rad/s exceeds the largest difference frequency of 1.024 rad/s.

The record force mean is removed by np.var. Sampling uncertainty is estimated from the 2048 independent record variances, not the correlated time samples: 1.96*std(record_variances)/sqrt(2048)/mean(record_variances). The resulting 95% confidence half-width is 0.29836%, below the requested 1% sampling error. Exact sparse tests independently guard the diagonal correction, which is small on this fine grid.

Bichromatic and linear surge checks

The two-component case uses frequencies 0.7 and 0.8 rad/s, amplitudes 1 m and 0.5 m, and a constant QTF of 10 kN/m². Its single beat force has amplitude 10 kN and exact variance 50 kN². The unmodified function returns 312.5 kN²; the time history returns 50.0 kN². An additional Hermitian complex-pair case, Q12=3+4i, has exact variance 12.5 kN². Single-component and diagonal-only QTFs give zero fluctuation; their mean load remains separate.

The linear surge equation is 10000*x'' + 200*x' + 100*x = F(t), with force in kN and displacement in m. Natural frequency is 0.1 rad/s and damping ratio is 0.1. The frequency-domain prediction calls the actual full-QTF function after weighting each QTF entry by |1/(k-m*mu²+i*c*mu)|. This is equivalent to filtering the force variance by |H|².

Four fixed-seed random-phase bichromatic histories are integrated using SciPy signal.lsim with piecewise-linear force interpolation, 256 samples per beat cycle, 64 cycles discarded and 128 complete cycles retained. The discarded interval reduces homogeneous transient amplitude below 4e-18 of its initial amplitude. Standard deviation removes the static offset. Complete-cycle averaging eliminates random-phase window error for this one-beat case; the four measured standard deviations differ by less than 1e-10 m.

Caller audit and behavior change

A repository-wide text search, including tracked documentation, examples and notebooks, finds no production call site. The package re-exports the symbol in src/digitalmodel/orcawave/__init__.py (import and __all__). The sole pre-existing direct caller is tests/orcawave/test_drift_forces.py::TestFullQTF.test_full_qtf_positive_std; its fixture is updated to a complete QTF on a uniform grid and its assertion requires positive fluctuation.

New direct callers in tests/orcawave/test_drift_force_variance.py are narrow_band_reference, surge_oscillator_reference, test_bichromatic_pair_variance_excludes_double_count, test_diagonal_qtf_has_mean_load_but_zero_fluctuation, and test_newman_full_agree_with_full_endpoint_bin_weights. The Monte Carlo and oscillator tests reach the function through the first two helpers.

External consumers are not inventoried. Their fine-grid full-QTF standard deviations will decrease by approximately 1/sqrt(2); sparse/diagonal cases can change more. Newman's zero-lag bin changes from a positive value to zero. Both public signatures and return types are preserved. Complete Hermitian matrices and uniform increasing integration grids are prerequisites; validation of malformed, triangular-only or nonuniform inputs is unchanged. Nonlinear mooring response, directional seas and licensed solvers are not evaluated.
