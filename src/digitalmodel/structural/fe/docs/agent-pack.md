## 1. Purpose and scope

The package builds beam-model inputs and routes them to the in-process `ElasticBucklingSolver` and `VonMisesSolver`, then writes CSV results and replaces the input subsection with a summary. [src/digitalmodel/structural/fe/model_builder.py:L28-L43] [src/digitalmodel/structural/fe/elastic_buckling_workflow.py:L21-L68] [src/digitalmodel/structural/fe/von_mises_workflow.py:L20-L49]

The engine dispatches `basename: elastic_buckling` and `basename: von_mises` to these routers; both durable workflow entries specify `runtime: offline`. [src/digitalmodel/engine.py:L533-L542] [docs/registry/workflows.yaml:L389-L404]

The routing registry marks the containing structural domain `stable`; this is domain metadata, not evidence of general beam-model accuracy. [docs/registry/module-routing.yaml:L525-L530]

The implemented element has transverse-displacement and rotation DOFs. The stress route applies `x` loads to the transverse DOF and `y` loads to the rotation DOF as moments. Its `axial_stress` is E times transverse displacement over L, which has no physical meaning as an axial stress. Bending uses element-average curvature and under-predicts end-moment peaks (non-conservative because the reported bending component is 25.0 % below the 11.0 MPa root peak in E2); `max_displacement` mixes translation and rotation. E2/E4 stresses and safety factors must not be used as engineering results (S37–S42). [src/digitalmodel/structural/fe/model_builder.py:L97-L110] [src/digitalmodel/infrastructure/base_solvers/structural/elements/beam_element.py:L15-L19] [src/digitalmodel/infrastructure/base_solvers/structural/elements/beam_element.py:L143-L150] [src/digitalmodel/infrastructure/base_solvers/structural/stress/von_mises.py:L178-L189] [src/digitalmodel/infrastructure/base_solvers/structural/elements/beam_element.py:L206-L216] [src/digitalmodel/infrastructure/base_solvers/structural/elements/beam_element.py:L240-L265] [src/digitalmodel/infrastructure/base_solvers/structural/stress/von_mises.py:L310-L326]

The demonstrated buckling scope is a uniform +x-oriented member only: each element must have `node2.x > node1.x` and equal y coordinates (`c = 1`, `s = 0`). Any other orientation, including −x, is outside that scope. Numbering E1 from x = 0 to x = −3.0 m gives 60839.4 N against Euler 1865355.2 N (96.7 % relative error); rotating E1 onto +y gives 89076.7 N (95.2 % relative error). Both complete without `euler_tolerance`; supplying the E1 tolerance of 5.0 % rejects both. Stiffness is transformed but geometric stiffness is not (S44). The direction of the error is not bounded for other cases. [src/digitalmodel/infrastructure/base_solvers/structural/elements/beam_element.py:L133-L152] [src/digitalmodel/infrastructure/base_solvers/structural/elements/beam_element.py:L155-L168] [src/digitalmodel/infrastructure/base_solvers/structural/buckling/elastic_buckling.py:L184-L199] [src/digitalmodel/structural/fe/elastic_buckling_workflow.py:L43-L51]

The E2 x-load of −1000.0 N produces 1.3 mm transverse tip displacement and 83.3 MPa reported maximum stress, with a reported safety factor of 5.4. A y magnitude of 1000.0 acts as a moment and reports 50.0 MPa. The axial term is physically meaningless; the element-average bending component is 8.2 MPa versus an 11.0 MPa end peak, 25.0 % low. E2/E4 stresses and safety factors must not be used as engineering results. Buckling use is restricted to the demonstrated +x-oriented member, with `node2.x > node1.x` and equal y for every element; any other orientation, including −x, is outside that scope. The −x and +y E1 cases are incorrect and complete unless a sufficiently tight `euler_tolerance` is supplied (S38–S44).
