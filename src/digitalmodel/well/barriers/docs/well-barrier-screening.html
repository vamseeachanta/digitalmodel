Permanent well barriers and leak-path screening

digitalmodel.well.barriers provides a bounded permanent-envelope screen and a directed leak-path ranking. All example well inputs are synthetic. No field calibration, current-standard compliance or quantitative release risk is established. The two calculations are independent: a valve probability is never assigned to permanent cement.

Permanent-barrier envelope

Supply each FlowZone with a BarrierCriteria: required count, minimum plug length in measured-depth metres, minimum coverage above the zone in measured-depth metres, and the criterion source/edition/clause. All criteria are caller inputs; no standard-derived selector is supplied. Hole type, foundations, annular verification and jurisdiction affect selection.

A PermanentBarrier represents an entire isolating envelope. Its externally verified cross-section includes all annuli and formation contact (rock to rock). Placement, long-term material suitability, bonding and test evidence require engineering assessment; a Boolean assertion is not a substitute for those records. Attach their stable references in evidence.

The screen compares plug length with its minimum, cemented length above inflow with required above-zone coverage, and formation integrity with the governing future pressure at each barrier base. Both pressures use MPa at that base, with the same datum. Coverage is max(0, min(base MD, zone top MD) - top MD); an uncemented gap above inflow contributes nothing, and an envelope with zero coverage is rejected even when the supplied minimum is zero. The pressure margin must be strictly positive. The caller supplies future recharge, hydrostatic translation and the governing formation-strength evidence. Separate envelopes require separate independence groups and no MD overlap in this simplified model. A continuous combined barrier or shared annular job requires assessment outside this screen.

Gas migration and sustained casing pressure are observations: true or unknown holds the screen for investigation. A false observation does not establish an absence of leakage over the abandonment lifetime. Each result retains the selected criteria, rejected-barrier reasons and citation sidecars.

Leak-path graph and probability

Nodes represent sources, cavities (annuli, tubing, wellhead voids) and sinks. Each directed LeakConnection identifies a WBE and a failure event. Opposite flow requires a separate connection. Source pressures, flow direction and cavity connectivity are supplied assumptions; no pressure-network solver verifies them. Connections into sources and out of sinks are rejected, including inactive failure-mode connections. Sources cannot serve as intermediate cavities.

An inactive failure-mode route is omitted. An open valve without closure credit needs its own open-conduit route; adding a closed-leak event does not automatically add that route. Different modes of one element must receive distinct event IDs unless they are deliberately the same physical event. Each WBE has one consistent state and closure demand throughout the scenario; each WBE/mode pair has one event ID even when used on several connections. Assigning independent IDs to repeated appearances of the same physical failure would understate shared-event probability. Topology completeness and omitted failure modes require external review.

PFDavg = lambda_DU [1/h] * proof_test_interval [h] / 2
P(path) = product of distinct event probabilities on that path
P(sink | source demand) = Boolean union of minimal event sets
ranking score = P(sink | source demand) * supplied consequence weight

The model assumes constant DU rates, full proof-test coverage, negligible restoration time and uniformly timed demands. The upper rate times interval must be at most 0.1 for unfailed events. Products of average PFDs additionally assume independent random test phases; synchronized testing can produce a different joint average. Permanently abandoned barriers without proof testing cannot use this formula as a lifetime leak probability.

Simple paths are enumerated, duplicate failure sets and supersets absorbed, and their union evaluated by conditioning on shared events. For paths AB and AC, the result is qA*(qB+qC-qB*qC), rather than a sum of path probabilities. Each minimal set retains one representative geometric route and its own PFD. Traversal and graph limits raise an error rather than return a partial ranking.

Known failed elements use known_failed=True and probability 1. Their rate band and proof-test interval are retained as input metadata but are not evaluated; an observation of failure supersedes the rate approximation. Unknown data must not be entered as zero. Distinct event IDs require an explicit independence_basis. Repeated IDs remain shared. Distinct events with the same declared common-cause group are rejected; a specified common failure can instead be represented by one event shared across affected routes. No beta model, hidden dependencies or test correlations are inferred.

Failure data and consequences

Supply each failure mode with a RateBand: bounds in 1/h, a source and an uncertainty explanation in basis. No handbook rate or mode fraction is embedded. The example uses synthetic sensitivity inputs and makes no population-data claim. Supplied data require qualification for well equipment, permanent cement, degradation and the failure mode.

Each sink receives a fluid, release-scenario pressure in MPa and H2S mole fraction. A required ConsequencePolicy supplies comparison weights and pressure/sour thresholds. No threshold or weight is a standard default. The output separates probability, severity, class, and score. Scores are ordinal comparison aids without loss units; differences and ratios depend on the chosen policy. Sensitivity bands hold consequence weights fixed. No annual frequency, release mass, exposure, ignition, dispersion or acceptance limit is calculated.

Worked example

PYTHONPATH=src uv run python examples/well/barrier_screening.py

Two separate synthetic 120 m MD envelopes are located at 1800–1920 m and 1600–1720 m above a zone beginning at 2000 m. Their selected coverage minimum is 80 m; their cemented coverage is 120 m each. These are illustrative caller criteria with no standard-derived acceptance values. Base formation integrity 35 MPa exceeds governing base pressure 30 MPa by 5 MPa. With the assumed placement and full-cross-section verification and no observed anomalies, the supplied two-envelope screen is met.

The leak comparison uses two series closed valves in either well. The first has the same confirmed leakage anomaly in both cases. Its probability is 1. For the remaining valve, the synthetic closed-leak rate band is [4.0, 5.0, 6.0]e-7/h and the test interval is 800 h, giving a nominal PFD of 2.0e-4 with bounds 1.6e-4 and 2.4e-4. The graph probability is therefore identical. The high-pressure sour-gas example uses 50 MPa and 0.02 H2S mole fraction; the water injector uses 1 MPa and zero H2S. Synthetic policy inputs use a 10 MPa pressure threshold and 0.01 sour threshold, gas weight 3, water weight 1, pressure multiplier 5 and sour multiplier 10.

Acceptance for a real well requires current applicable criteria, qualified formation/placement/cross-section evidence, validated failure data, dependency assessment and consequence analysis. The output alone does not establish any of these. Explorer and website integrations in other repositories remain follow-up work.
